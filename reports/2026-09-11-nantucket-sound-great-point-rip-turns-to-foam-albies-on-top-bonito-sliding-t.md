# Great Point Rip Turns to Foam: Albies on Top, Bonito Sliding Through Below
*The new moon tide stacked bait against every rip from Great Point to Monomoy, and for once the fluke story is happening here, not somewhere else.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — September 11** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

Stood on the point Tuesday morning watching the rip off Great Point turn white in patches — not the usual current break, but albies working through it in tight, fast pushes, three and four at a time, then gone. A few minutes later something bigger rolled under one of those boils, slower, heavier, and didn't come back up. That's the read on the Sound right now: albies and bonito mixed together on top, bass working the same bait underneath, and it's been building like that for the better part of a week.

The new moon that hit September 10 gave this whole stretch of water the hardest tidal push we've seen since spring, and it's doing exactly what a strong new-moon tide is supposed to do this time of year — squeezing bunker schools tight against structure. From Monomoy up through Chatham's inlets and along the Sound-side beaches, bait that had been loose and scattered got pinned against the current lines, and the stripers noticed. We flagged this pattern last week, said the bunker would keep tightening under the building moon and the bass would start showing real commitment instead of just picking at stragglers — and that's precisely what's happening now. This isn't the scattered, half-interested feeding of late August. Fish are committing.

Here's the part of the story that's actually ours right now: the keeper-grade fluke bite that's thinned out in a lot of the mid-Atlantic and even parts of the Cape has shifted into Nantucket Sound and out onto the Shoals. While places like the Jersey surf and the back bays down south are stuck with mostly shorts as the bigger fish stage offshore for the fall exit, the bigger fluke here are still very much inshore-adjacent, holding on the edges of Nantucket Shoals in 40 to 60 feet where the bottom transitions from sand to broken structure. Boats working the drift with 4-inch white or pink Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a squid strip, have been finding fish to 6 and 7 pounds mixed in with the usual keeper-and-up grade. Slow drift, bounce it right off bottom, and don't be afraid to work the drift twice if the current's soft — the bigger fish are sitting tighter to structure than the average August fluke.

The albie and bonito run itself has gone from a tease to a real pattern over the last several days, and given where we sit geographically — east of the Buzzards Bay and Rhode Island push that's been carrying this whole run — the Sound is right in the middle of it rather than waiting for it to arrive. Great Point on the ebb has been the most consistent visual show: bait balls of bay anchovies and silversides getting pinned against the rip, albies crashing through in fast, tight schools, bonito mixed in but running a beat slower and staying a little deeper in the boil. Half-ounce Deadly Dick and Albie Snax in bone or pink/white have been getting bit, but the window is short — first hour of the ebb, then it goes quiet until the bait regroups. Monomoy's east-side rips have been showing the same thing on a smaller scale, more sporadic, worth a look if Great Point is crowded or slack.

Bass are still mostly a bycatch story on top of the albie bite, but they're a real one. Fish in the 28- to 34-inch range have been coming out from under the same boils that are holding albies, especially early and late in the day when the light's low and the bunker pods bunch up tighter against the beach. Working a white or bone SP Minnow through the edge of a bait pod, or a 1-ounce bucktail with a white curl-tail trailer dragged slow along the bottom of the rip, has been the better play than trying to match the albie speed with epoxy jigs. Bluefish are in the mix too — nothing derby-sized locally, but snapper blues are thick in the harbors and back creeks around Nantucket and Chatham, which is usually the tell that bigger blues aren't far behind as the water keeps cooling.

Looking at the next few days: the tide's still carrying good energy off that new moon, and as long as the wind stays out of the south or southwest and doesn't blow the rips apart, this pattern should hold or even sharpen. A hard northeast blow would be the thing to watch — it tends to scatter the bait that's currently pinned so nicely against Great Point and Monomoy, and the albie bite in particular goes from consistent to frustrating fast when that happens. If the forecast holds clean, the smart plan is early mornings on the ebb at Great Point for albies and bonito, then a shift out to the Shoals midday for fluke while the tide's manageable, and back to the beach edges at last light for bass working the bunker. Keep an eye on Horseshoe Shoal too — it's been quieter than Great Point this week but has a way of turning on with almost no warning once the bait finds it. That's the kind of spot worth a check on the way past, even on a day you've already got a plan.
