# Niantic Fleet Riding Bunker Schools Into Twotree — Porgies Steady, Albie Watch Heats Up
*New moon current finally kicked the bass bite loose at the rips, but the fluke and the first false albacore are still make-you-wait propositions.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 14** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Ran into Captain Ray at the Niantic dock Tuesday morning loading coolers, and he didn't even let me get the question out before he said it — "Danny, the bunker are everywhere in the bay right now, and the bass finally found them." That's been the talk up and down the fleet all week. The peanut bunker showed up thick in Niantic Bay and pushed right up into the river mouth, and where there's bait like that stacked tight, the stripers don't stay quiet for long.

We flagged this last week — said the building tidal flow into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what happened. Twotree Island Channel has been the money spot on the last two hours of the outgoing, with schoolie-to-mid-30s bass working the bait against the current seam. Guys are doing well on 5-inch white Gulp swimming mullets rigged on half-ounce bucktails, cast up-current and swung through the seam so it swims naturally instead of dragging. A few of the drift boat regulars have also been dropping bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs right off the edge of the channel where it drops to 15-18 feet, and that's been picking off the bigger fish that are hanging back off the schoolies.

Hole-in-the-Wall has been more of a dawn-and-dusk deal — the current rips through there hard on a big tide and the bass use it to ambush bait getting funneled through. If you can time a trip for first light on the last of the incoming, that's when it's been happening. Bluefish are riding shotgun on the same bunker pattern. Adult blues to maybe 4-5 pounds have been mixing in with the bass schools out toward Black Point, and inside the Niantic River the snapper blues are thick — good news for anybody trying to get a kid hooked on this sport, because those little guys will hit anything shiny on a light spinning rod.

Porgy fishing has been the steady, reliable story all week, no surprises, no excuses needed. Black Point and McCook Point rockpiles are still holding good numbers in that 20-25 foot range, and the party boats out of Niantic are putting anywhere from decent to real good bags together on sandworms and squid strips fished tight to bottom with a two-hook bottom rig. That bite doesn't care much about the tide stage — it's been productive on both the push and the drop, which makes it the easy call when the wind's blowing and you don't want to fight current for stripers.

Sea bass are doing what they do this time of year — improving gradually rather than exploding, which is honestly the pattern I'd expect heading toward fall. Guys scouting the deeper structure off Black Point and out toward the reef pieces are finding keeper-class fish mixed with a lot of shorts, so it's worth culling through but not a guaranteed limit trip yet. Squid strips and clam on a high-low rig has been the go-to, dropped right on the rock edges in 25-35 feet.

Fluke I've got to be straight with you — it's been a mixed bag at best in this end of the Sound. New London reading around 69 degrees, cooler than the western Sound, and that's kept the flatfish scattered rather than concentrated. A few boats drifting the sandy stretches off Niantic Bay and out toward the mouth of the river have scratched out keepers on Gulp swimming mullet and bucktail combos, but nobody's filling coolers. If fluke is your main target this week, I'd treat it as a bonus fish while you're really out there for bass or porgy, not the primary mission.

Now here's what's got the dock buzzing beyond the bass — there's real talk of bluefin pushing unusually far into the eastern Sound this month, which if it holds up would be a notable thing for this stretch of water. I haven't had it confirmed on my own beat yet, so I'm not going to oversell it, but it's the kind of development worth watching closely over the next week. Same with albies and bonito — the broader region between Montauk and Buzzards Bay is primed for a first sighting any day now given the warm water and bait density, and if that pattern extends into eastern Long Island Sound the way it sometimes does late in August, Twotree Channel and the rips off Black Point would be the first places I'd expect to hear about it.

Looking ahead, if this bunker pattern holds and the tide keeps building through the week, I'd keep working Twotree on the outgoing and Hole-in-the-Wall at first light — that's been the most consistent one-two punch for bass. Porgy guys, just keep doing what you're doing at Black Point and McCook, that bite isn't going anywhere. And keep half an eye on the horizon for anything busting bait on the surface — if the albies do show up in this corner of the Sound, it won't be subtle.
