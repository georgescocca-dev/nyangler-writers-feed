# Pine Point Blues Shred Mackerel While Biddeford Pool Bass Hold Through the Incoming
*Bluefish are running the coast right now — the bass are still here too, but you've got to work the tide to find them.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — August 31** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

Mackerel showed up thick off Pine Point this week, and the blues found them fast. Guys working the jetty and the beach in front of the Pine Point Fishermen's Co-op were cutting off wire leaders left and right — 3- to 6-pound bluefish coming through in waves, tearing mackerel schools apart on the surface hard enough that you could see it from the parking lot. That's the pattern right now up and down this stretch of coast: bluefish are the sure thing, bass are the puzzle you've got to work a little harder to solve.

We said last week that the cooler, more stable Gulf of Maine water would keep this fishery dependable while the water south of us cooks off — and that's exactly what's playing out. Nobody up here is dealing with dead water or stressed fish. The bite's been steady, not spectacular, which after a summer like this one, most of the regulars will take.

Biddeford Pool is still doing what it does best on the incoming — stripers stacking in the channel as the tide floods over the flats and pushes bait into the Pool. The first two hours of the incoming, especially with a rising moon overhead building toward full later this week, has been the most consistent window on this whole beat. Fish to the high 20s, a few pushing 30, have come on Danny-style swimmers in olive-over-white and on Doc's Goofy Jigs bounced slow along the channel edge. Nothing crazy in numbers — two or three good fish a tide if you put your time in and fish the structure right, not just chuck and hope. The last hour of the incoming into the first of the high slack has been the sweet spot; once it turns and starts draining back out, the bite shuts down fast.

Prouts Neck has been a southwest-wind spot all week, and it delivered. That wind pushes bait up against the ledges and gets the current working right for fish holding tight to the rocks. Pencil poppers — the bigger Gibbs Polaris in white or yellow — worked the outside edges at dusk have been drawing bass in the 20s, along with the occasional bluefish crashing the same water. Cape Elizabeth's rocky stretch has fished similarly after dark, big swimming plugs worked slow through the wash, best on a dropping tide when the current pulls bait off the ledges.

Higgins Beach is where the bluefish have really taken over. Between the mackerel schools running the beach and warm, bait-rich water pushing up against the sand, the blues have been chopping bait right in the wash during the last two hours of daylight. Bring wire or heavy fluoro leader — 40-pound minimum — because these fish are not shy about cutting you off on topwater. Kastmasters and Deadly Dicks worked fast through the froth have been the ticket; slow it down and you'll just feed the school your lure. Anyone throwing a Whopper Plopper into that mess this week found out the hard way what a school of hungry blues does to soft plastic tails.

Mackerel themselves have been thick enough to jig right off the rocks at Wells Harbor and the Ogunquit breakwater — small Swedish Pimples or a mackerel tree will load you up in twenty minutes if you want live bait for the evening tide. A lot of the better bass this week came off live-lined macks fished on a fish-finder rig in the current seams outside Biddeford Pool, proof that when you've got the real thing swimming on your hook, the stripers still can't help themselves.

Flounder action has held up in the usual spots — Wells Harbor and the calmer water inside Cape Porpoise — bloodworms and clam bellies on a simple two-hook bottom rig working the mud on the last of the ebb. Nothing to write home about, but a fine way to fill a cooler on a slow tide when the surf isn't cooperating.

Cod remain mostly a boat story right now, party boats out of the deeper water off Kittery still finding fish on structure, but that's a different report than this beach-and-rock game.

Looking ahead, the moon builds through the week and that means more water moving, which should tighten up the bait and start pulling more bass into range at the Pool and along the Prouts Neck ledges. If the wind holds out of the southwest into the weekend, stick with the pattern — Biddeford Pool on the incoming, Prouts Neck and Cape Elizabeth at dusk on the outgoing. If it swings northeast and starts stacking swell on the open beaches, shift toward the harbors and let the flounder carry the day until it lays back down. Labor Day traffic is going to thicken the beaches, so an early alarm clock still buys you the best water with the fewest footprints in the sand.
