Here's the mechanism. Offshore buoy data has had significant wave heights running around 3.6 feet at an 8-second dominant period, with the mean wave direction coming out of the east-southeast. That's a quartering sea relative to the inlet's throat, and when swell approaches at that angle it doesn't push straight through — it sets up a longshore component that scours sand off the updrift (east) side of the channel and redeposits it on the downdrift flats inside. That combination — a tightened channel edge plus a strong thermal push of bunker and mullet funneling down the beach — is exactly the setup that concentrates predators instead of spreading them out. Spring tides are building into the October 6 full moon, which means more water moving faster through that narrowed cut on each tide change, and more current seam for stripers and bluefish to sit on without burning energy chasing bait across open sand.

Word from the surf and the jetty rocks this week has been consistent: slot-sized bass and a healthy run of bluefish, some pushing into chopper range, working the outgoing tide hardest at the Moriches Inlet mouth and along the Cupsogue side of the channel. The pattern anglers are reporting matches what the bathymetry would predict — fish sitting on the hard edge of that scour line, picking off bait getting swept out of the bay on the ebb rather than chasing it in open water. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, cast across the current and allowed to sink before a slow bottom hop, have been the go-to presentation on the bass. Bluefish are hitting the same water but responding better to something with flash and speed — diamond jigs or metal-lipped swimmers worked faster through the seam, since blues tend to want a retrieve that outruns a slower bucktail swing.

We flagged this pattern last week when we said bluefish would ride shotgun wherever bass were showing on the bunker and mullet pushes, and that's exactly what's playing out at the inlet now — the two species are trading blows in the same wash, sometimes in the same cast.

That pre-frontal window — likely the next 24 to 48 hours — is probably the better bet than fishing directly behind the front, when northwest wind usually cleans the water but also drops temperatures another notch and can shut down the bite for a day or two while fish adjust.
