# North Bar Under the New Moon: Bass Commit to Bunker, Derby Blue Tops 18 Pounds
*The tide that builds the fall run finally showed its hand at the Point, and the bluefish got there first.*

**MONTAUK, NY — September 11** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

There's a moment every September when Montauk stops staging and starts feeding, and this past week that switch got thrown. The new moon on the 10th delivered the hardest tidal push we've seen since spring, and every guide, mate, and surf rat on the Point has been talking about the same thing: bunker schools getting squeezed tight against the beaches from the Lighthouse all the way around to Fort Pond Bay, with predators finally committing instead of just picking at stragglers. We flagged this last week — said the building new-moon tide would intensify bass activity as bait got pushed harder through the rips — and that's exactly what unfolded. This isn't the peak of the fall run yet, but it's the first real down payment on it.

Water off the Point has settled around 69 degrees, which is right in that sweet band where bass, blues, and bait all want to occupy the same real estate. The bluefish got the headline this week — a fish pushing 18-plus pounds took top honors on the derby boards, and that's not a fluke catch, that's a signal. Blues that size don't show up unless there's a serious bait base to support them, and there is. Bunker pods have been thick in Block Island Sound and stacking against the boulder field off the Lighthouse on the last two hours of the ebb, with blues busting on top hard enough to see the spray from the bluffs. Anglers throwing bone or parrot-colored Hopkins jigs and 2-ounce diamond jigs into those blitzes have been getting bit almost every cast — no finesse required when blues are on bunker like this. Snapper blues, meanwhile, are thick in Montauk Harbor and Fort Pond Bay, a reliable sign that the adult run behind them is only going to get heavier as the water cools another few degrees.

Striped bass are the more interesting story right now because the quality is climbing even as the numbers stay moderate. The building tide has bass showing consistently on the North Bar during the last two hours of the incoming, holding tight to the sand eel and bunker pods getting pinned against the structure. Bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, white or chartreuse, cast across the current and swept along bottom with a slow lift-and-drop, have been drawing the better fish — mid-20s to low-30-inch class with a scattering of overslots mixed in. Shagwong Reef has been holding fish too, particularly on the top of the tide when the rip stands up and bait gets funneled through the boulder seams. Trollers working bunker spoons and umbrella rigs off Shagwong and out toward Frisco's have connected with some of the better bass of the week, several pushing well past the slot. Turtle Cove has been a livebait spot worth knowing — anglers soaking whole bunker on a fish-finder rig, weighted just enough to hold bottom in the current, have taken quality bass on the incoming as fish stage there before pushing around the Point on the tide change.

Fluke tell a different story, and it's worth being straight about it. The keeper-grade bite that was solid earlier in the summer has largely thinned out here, mirroring what's happening up and down the coast as the better fish move to deeper, cooler structure ahead of their fall exit. Drifting the usual grounds off the Point and into Block Island Sound in 40 to 60 feet is still producing fish, but the ratio has shifted hard toward shorts, with keepers coming more as a bonus than a target. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on bucktails still get bit, but anyone building a whole trip around fluke right now should adjust expectations — this bite is winding down, not building.

Porgies remain a dependable consolation prize. Fishing bloodworms and squid strips on standard porgy rigs around the rock piles off the Lighthouse and out toward Shagwong continues to produce steady action in 20 to 40 feet, a good option for anyone who wants bent rods without chasing blitzes. Blackfish season hasn't opened yet, but there's already chatter among the regulars about scouting structure for October, and the boulder fields that hold bass all summer will do double duty once that season starts.

What everyone's really waiting on is the albies, and the warm-water edge that's been fueling the run off Rhode Island and through Nantucket Sound is close enough now that it could show up off the Point any day. There's no confirmed showing yet at Montauk, so don't plan a trip around it, but Shagwong and the rips east of the Lighthouse are the first places they'll appear when the sand eels get pushed hard by that warm filament. Worth keeping a light spinning rod rigged with small epoxy jigs in the truck just in case.

Looking ahead, the tidal energy from this new moon will start easing over the next several days, which typically means the blitz-style feeding calms from its peak but doesn't shut off — the underlying bunker migration is still the driver, and that's not going anywhere soon. If there's only one window to fish this week, target the last two hours of the incoming at the North Bar or Shagwong, right as the tide stands up and pins bait against structure. Keep an eye on wind direction heading into the weekend; a clean easterly with moderate seas will keep bait pinned tight to the beaches and rips, while a hard blow out of the southwest could scatter things and push the better action offshore for a few tide cycles. Either way, this is the stretch of September that separates a good fall run from a legendary one, and right now the signs point toward the latter.
