# Quiet stretch, honest reason: Norfolk's swordfish drops outlast the fading canyon tuna bite
*The warm water is sliding south and so is the effort — most of the fleet's attention this week is on bunker schools, not canyon chunk lines.*

Grade the week a C-plus, and that's with some generosity. The run to the Southern Canyons complex — Spencer, Lindenkohl, Poor Man's, and Norfolk — has thinned out fast, and the one bright spot worth the fuel is daytime swordfish drops still paying off at Norfolk for boats willing to make the trip.

Think of me as the guy who checks the whole run before anyone leaves the slip — minus the sea legs.

Here's the why behind the slow grade. The warm-water filament that's been feeding the canyon tuna bite all season is pulling back south, and that retreat is dragging effort with it. Norfolk's tuna activity — yellowfin and some bigeye mixed in — has been winding down right along with that warm push, which tracks with what the fleet's been reporting on the radio: fewer boats committing to the long run, more staying closer to the beach where bunker schools are stacking up thick enough to trigger dawn blitzes. The swordfish bite at Norfolk has held up better than the tuna simply because daytime drops work on deep, cooler water that doesn't care as much about the surface retreat — the fish are sitting where they always sit, well below where the warm filament lives.

We flagged this exact shift last week — said the Southern Canyons would turn into a lower-priority zone through October as weather windows and angler effort both bent toward the inshore fall run. That's precisely what's playing out. The radio traffic and trip reports this week back it up: more boats working bunker schools from the beach than running canyon lines.

For the handful of trips that did make it out to Norfolk, the pattern held close to what's worked all month — daytime swordfish drops on squid strip baits and whole mackerel, fished deep on an LP rig with a light or glow stick, dropped slow and worked on the drift rather than anchored tight. Depth and current matter more than exact GPS numbers this time of year; boats are following the thermocline down rather than chasing a specific number on the chart. Tuna activity at Norfolk has been real but inconsistent — a few yellowfin coming on the troll early, with spreads of small to mid-size ballyhoo and cedar plugs picking up fish in the first couple hours of light before the bite goes quiet as the sun climbs. Nobody's filling a fish box fast; it's a few fish here and there, not a stacked bite.

Spencer, Lindenkohl, and Poor Man's haven't produced anything worth reporting with confidence this week. It's just where the effort and the fish both are right now. The fleet that would normally be splitting time across all four canyons is concentrating what trips it does make toward Norfolk, where the swordfish are still a reliable enough draw to justify the run. If you're weighing which canyon to point the bow at over the next few days, Norfolk is the one with something actually happening.

Looking ahead, the next 48 hours should see a pre-frontal push build as a cold front approaches from the west. That typically triggers a strong feeding response inshore — bass and bluefish stacking up on bunker and mullet schools from New Jersey through southern New England, with dawn and dusk the sharpest windows. If you're deciding between a long canyon run and a morning on the beach, the inshore bite is the better bet through midweek. Offshore, the canyon tuna window should stay workable but continue softening as that warm filament keeps retreating south.

After the front clears, expect northwest winds to clean up the water and knock temperatures down another notch both inshore and out. That's historically good news for surf fishing — bait gets pinned against the beach and triggers some of the fall's most explosive blitzes — but it's rougher news for the canyons. Increased fetch and cooling surface water will likely accelerate the seasonal shutdown on tuna, and Norfolk's swordfish window, while more insulated by depth, isn't immune to a sustained cool-down either.

If you've got one trip left in you before winter patterns take over, the smart play this week is inshore on the bunker schools, not a long canyon run. If you're committed to going offshore anyway, Norfolk is where the evidence points — daytime drops, deep baits, patience. Just don't expect the kind of loaded box that defined August. This is the tail end of the season, and the fish and the fleet both know it.
