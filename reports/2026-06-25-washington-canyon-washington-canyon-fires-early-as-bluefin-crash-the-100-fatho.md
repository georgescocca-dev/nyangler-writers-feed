# Washington Canyon fires early as bluefin crash the 100-fathom line
*Big fish are already here, and the water's telling us why this could be the best June in years.*

**WASHINGTON CANYON — June 25** — _by Capt. Chris "Wash" Morrison, Zone Writer_

The Wash is doing what it does best — producing fish when everyone else is still waiting for summer to show up. We've got bluefin tuna pushing triple digits already stacked on the 100-fathom line, and the water structure is setting up exactly like I want to see it in late June.

That giant fish that came over the rail last week wasn't a fluke. The bluefin are here early and they're feeding hard. I'm seeing consistent action from the northeast wall down to the southern fingers, with the best bite happening where the 600-foot contour meets that hard drop to 1200. The fish are sitting in that thermal layer between 60 and 80 feet, right where the cool canyon water meets the Gulf Stream push.

Here's what's working: chunking with fresh butterfish on 8/0 circles, fished on 80-pound fluorocarbon leaders about 60 feet down. The key is keeping your chunks in that temperature break — too shallow and you're in the warm stuff where nothing's holding, too deep and you're below the bait. I'm also seeing boats score on the troll with cedar plugs and small feathers, but the chunk bite has been more consistent.

The yellowfin are mixed in with the bluefin, mostly smaller fish in the 40 to 60-pound range, but they're aggressive. Same depth, same structure, but they're hitting the smaller baits harder. Squid strips on 6/0 hooks have been money, especially on the drift when you can work them through that thermocline.

What's got me excited is the bait situation. We're seeing good concentrations of butterfish and squid right where they should be this time of year, pushed up against the canyon walls by the current. The northeast corner has been particularly loaded — that's where the eddy sets up when the Gulf Stream pushes hard against the shelf, and it's been a bait magnet all week.

The mahi are starting to show too, mostly smaller fish under the grass lines that are setting up along the temperature breaks. Nothing huge yet, but they're there. I picked up a couple nice ones yesterday working the edges with small jigs and live ballyhoo. The wahoo bite has been spotty — a few nice fish on high-speed trolling with Ilanders, but you've got to cover water to find them.

Water temps are sitting right where I want them for this time of year. The canyon's holding that cool 68 to 70-degree water while the Stream edge is pushing 74 to 76. That gradient is what's keeping everything stacked up and feeding. The clarity has been excellent — that deep blue canyon water that tells you the fish are going to be there.

Current has been moderate but steady, which is perfect for chunking. Too much current and your baits get swept away from the structure; too little and the bait doesn't get concentrated. We're in that sweet spot where you can hold your position and work the water properly.

The white marlin are starting to make their presence known too. Nothing consistent yet, but I'm seeing more bait balls getting worked on the surface, and that's usually the first sign that the billfish are moving in. Give it another week or two and we should be into some real action.

Looking ahead, the new moon this weekend should fire things up even more. The darker nights always seem to concentrate the bait better, and with the water temperatures where they are, I'm expecting the bite to get even better. The long-range forecast shows stable conditions through the holiday weekend, which means we should have good fishing right through the Fourth of July.

If you're planning a trip out here, bring your A-game. The fish are here, but they're not stupid. Fresh bait, sharp hooks, and proper presentations are going to make the difference between a good day and a great one. The Washington is showing why it's always been my favorite piece of water — when conditions line up right, there's nowhere else I'd rather be.
