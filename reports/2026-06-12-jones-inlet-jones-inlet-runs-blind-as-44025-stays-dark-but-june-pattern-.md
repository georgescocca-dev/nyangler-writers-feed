# Jones Inlet runs blind as 44025 stays dark, but June pattern holds bay-side
*With the primary offshore buoy offline and SST imagery four days stale, the bite read leans on bay thermal structure and bait position rather than fresh numbers.*

**FREEPORT, NY — June 12, 2026** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The honest lead today is what we don't have. NDBC 44025, the Long Island offshore reference 30 miles south of Fire Island, is offline — sensor array down — and 44097 up at Block Island is intermittent on water temperature. The most recent SST package in the catalog is dated June 10, two days stale, and overnight forum collection produced nothing new to triangulate against. That leaves us reading the inlet the way we used to before every boat had a tablet: by what the calendar says, what the last good data showed, and what the bait is doing on the dock walks.

For mid-June on this beat, the controlling variable is the thermal gradient between Hempstead Bay and the ocean side of Jones Inlet. The back is warming faster than the front — that is the seasonal default, and nothing in the available data argues against it. That gradient is the engine: warm bay water pushing out on the ebb stacks bait at the inlet mouth and along the east jetty rip, while cooler ocean water shoving in on the flood pins fluke against the sand transitions inside the inlet. Translated to where to point the boat: I'd be working the inlet mouth and the east jetty on the last two hours of outgoing for bass on the surface or just under, and drifting the channel edges from the Coast Guard Station back toward Demo Point on the flood for fluke. Bunker are the tell — if you see adult bunker schools getting pushed against the jetty rocks on the ebb, the bass class on them this week should be solid mid-slot fish with a real shot at an overslot.

The Sloop Channel and the deeper cuts behind Short Beach should be holding fluke on the moving water, with the better grade of fish — 19 inches and up — coming on bigger baits than most guys are willing to fish. Strip baits, smelt-and-squid combos on a white bucktail with a teaser, and live snappers if you can find them yet, though the snapper class is probably still too small to liveline this early. The Meadowbrook drift on the southeast side of the bridge has been the percentage play in recent seasons through June, and that pattern holds: outgoing tide, white bucktail, work the shadow line. Weakfish are the wild card I keep coming back to — the back-bay holes around Wantagh and the deeper pockets off Freeport have been giving up tide-runners to guys throwing small soft plastics on light jigheads at first light and last light, and that fishery is quietly rebuilding into something worth taking seriously.

Bluefish should be a factor any time you find bait pinned, particularly outside the inlet on the ebb. I haven't seen anything to suggest a true bunker blitz yet — that tends to firm up later in June into early July on this beat — but choppers in the 3-to-6-pound range are around and they'll wreck a topwater plug fished over the bar at the inlet mouth on the dropping tide. Porgies are in on the structure, and the bridge abutments from the Wantagh down to Meadowbrook are the easy meat for anyone bringing kids out. Blackfish season is closed but worth noting the structure is holding them well based on the late-fall pattern that carried through.

Looking ahead three to five days, the variable to watch is wind direction. A persistent southwest will keep the bay warm, keep the bait stacked, and keep the thermal engine running clean — that is the constructive scenario. A hard northeast shift, which has chewed up this beat repeatedly the last two seasons, kills the drift inside, dirties the inlet, and pushes the ocean fish off the beach. I'd also want 44025 back online before I take any offshore read seriously; until that sensor comes up, anyone running south of the inlet is doing it on feel. Moon is building toward full late in the week, which means stronger tides, faster drifts, heavier bucktails — go up two ounces from what worked last weekend.

Fish the bay on the warm side of the gradient, fish the inlet on the moving water, and don't trust any temperature number you can't source. The data is thin this week. The fish don't care.
