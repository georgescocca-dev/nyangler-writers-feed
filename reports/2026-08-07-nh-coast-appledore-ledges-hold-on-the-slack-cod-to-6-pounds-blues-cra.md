# Appledore Ledges Hold on the Slack: Cod to 6 Pounds, Blues Crash the Piscataqua
*Thirteen miles of coastline doesn't sound like much until you've fished it right — this week the Shoals gave up cod on the drop-off and the peanut bunker finally pulled blues into Hampton Harbor.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 7** — _by Cole Robinson, Zone Writer_

People hear thirteen miles of coastline and figure there's nothing here worth trailering a boat for. I've got a 20-foot Lund and I fish this water like it's a hundred and thirty. Between the Piscataqua current lines, the Hampton marsh, and six miles of open water out to the Shoals, there's more structure and more species turning over out here in a week than most guys see all season on a bigger stretch of coast. You just have to know where the bottom breaks and when the tide wants to hand it to you.

This week that meant being off White Island before 5 a.m., anchored up on the ledge in 70 feet with the tide just starting to slack. Cod have been mixed in with haddock out there most mornings, fish running 3 to 6 pounds with a couple pushing 8 coming over the rail on diamond jigs worked slow off bottom — nothing fancy, just a gentle lift-and-drop through the last hour of the ebb and the first hour of the new tide. Clam bait on a high-low rig still works if the jig bite goes quiet, especially once the sun gets up and the fish slide tighter to structure. I won't oversell it — it's been a mixed week, not a banner one. Some mornings the ledge produces a limit in an hour, other mornings you work three drifts for a handful of keepers and call it. That's high summer at the Shoals. The water up top has warmed enough that the fish are holding deeper and tighter to rock than they were in June, so if you're marking bait but not marking fish, drop another ten feet before you give up on a spot.

Mackerel have shown up in decent numbers around Appledore and Star Island, schools stacking on the sounder in 30 to 40 feet mid-morning. A three-hook Christmas tree rig with no weight needed will fill a bucket fast, and honestly that's been half the reason to run out there — fresh mackerel strips are outfishing frozen clam on the bigger cod when you can get them.

Closer to shore, the striper story has been the better one all week. Cooler Gulf of Maine water keeps these fish comfortable and feeding through more daylight hours than you'll find south of the Cape right now, and it's showing in the Piscataqua. The current seams below the Memorial Bridge and down through Portsmouth Harbor have been holding fish on both the last of the outgoing and the first push of the incoming — bucktails in white or chartreuse, 1 to 1.5 ounce, worked across the seam and let it swing. Live mackerel on a fish-finder rig has also been quietly productive for the bigger fish holding deeper in the channel near the Route 1 bridge pilings.

Hampton Harbor is where the week turned interesting. We flagged the peanut bunker pattern building a few weeks back, said it would spread and pull cocktail blues into the back bays — and that's exactly what happened. Peanuts have packed into the marsh creeks off Hampton and Seabrook, and bluefish have followed them in on the last two hours of the incoming, blowing up on the surface right at the mouth where the harbor narrows. Small metal — half-ounce Deadly Dicks, Kastmasters — thrown right into the boils has been the move, and there's enough size mixed in with the snappers now that it's worth the drive even if you're not running a boat. Stripers are working the same bait but sitting a little deeper and later, picking off peanuts on the dropping tide closer to the bridge.

Rye Harbor and Odiorne Point have been steadier than exciting — flounder still coming off the mud bottom on the incoming with worms fished tight to the bottom in 15 to 20 feet, nothing to write home about but a reliable meal if the Shoals run gets blown out. Great Boars Head has had a few solid mornings for surfcasters working the bar right at first light on the last of the outgoing, stripers sliding off the sand as bait gets pushed out with the tide — small swimmers or a live eel if you can find one has been the ticket.

Weather's held stable and that's kept this whole pattern intact — no big blow to scatter the bait, no dirty water pushing fish off the structure. If that high holds through the coming week I'd expect more of the same: groundfish mixed and honest at the Shoals, stripers working the Piscataqua tides, and those blues staying thick in Hampton Harbor as long as the peanut bunker keep holding in the marsh. If a system rolls through and stirs up the harbor, I'd shift effort back out to the Shoals ledges where depth buys you some insulation from a couple dirty days inshore. Either way, there's enough going on in these thirteen miles to keep a small boat busy every morning this week.
