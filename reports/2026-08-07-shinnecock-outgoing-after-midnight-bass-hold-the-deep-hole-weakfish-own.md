# Outgoing After Midnight: Bass Hold the Deep Hole, Weakfish Own the Bridge Lights
*With the ocean pushing mid-70s water, the real action at Shinnecock starts when the sun goes down and the tide starts pulling.*

**SHINNECOCK INLET, NY — August 7** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

I had a decision to make Tuesday night: fish the inlet on the last two hours of the outgoing, or head over to Ponquogue and work the bridge lights on the tail of the incoming. I picked the inlet. Reason was simple — the ocean's sitting warm, mid-to-upper 70s out past the jetties, and that kind of water pushes the better bass to feed after dark when the surface layer cools a touch and the bait gets nervous. Daytime fishing out there right now is mostly blues and short fluke. The grown-up bass show up when the light's gone.

So I set up on the north side of the inlet, tight to the deep hole where the channel scours out against the rocks, and waited for the tide to really start pulling — that back half of the outgoing when the whole bay is draining past you. Livelined bunker on a fish-finder rig, no weight beyond what it takes to hold bottom in that current, and I let it swing naturally into the seam where the fast water meets the slower eddy behind the jetty rocks. Two bass in the mid-30-inch range came out of that spot in about ninety minutes, both fat, both clearly feeding on the bunker pods that have been holding just outside the inlet mouth all week. Nothing giant, but real fish, and they came on the same stretch of tide both nights I fished it. If you're going to gamble one night this week, that back-half outgoing at the inlet is where I'd put my money — just watch your footing on those rocks in the dark and carry a light you trust.

Ponquogue Bridge is still doing exactly what it does in August, and I mean that as a compliment. Weakfish are stacked around the pilings, feeding on the peanut bunker that's flooded into the bay over the last couple weeks. We flagged this pattern a while back — said the weakfish would track the peanut bunker into the bays through August, and that's precisely what's unfolding under those bridge lights right now. Half-ounce jig heads in pink or chartreuse, tipped with a Bass Assassin or a 3-inch Gulp minnow, worked slow along the current seams where the light meets the shadow line. Best window is the last hour of the incoming into the top of the tide, when the peanuts get pushed up against the structure and the weaks stack underneath them. I've had three- and four-pound weakfish come off there on consecutive nights, along with a fair number of cocktail blues mixed in chasing the same bait. It's not a guaranteed fish every cast, but it's consistent enough right now that I'd call it the most reliable bite in the whole system.

Fluke have slid off the shallow bay flats as that water's warmed up, and they're holding deeper now — the channel edges off Tiana and the deeper drifts toward the inlet mouth, 15 to 25 feet, where the bottom stays cooler and the current keeps things moving. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift, still the standard. It's been a mixed bag out there — plenty of shorts, a keeper here and there, nothing to write home about. If you want a legitimate limit trip you need to cover water and not get married to one drift.

Bluefish are scattered but present — snapper blues thick in the back bay off Tiana and around the canal mouth, adult blues chasing bunker schools just outside the inlet on the ocean side. Nothing blitz-worthy yet, but the bunker are there in numbers, and given how fast that peanut bait has moved into the system, I wouldn't be shocked to see the cocktail blue action spike hard in the bay within the next week or two.

The canal's fishing the way it always does this time of year — current-driven, and you need to time the locks. Best window is right as the locks start opening, that 30-45 minute stretch when everything gets concentrated and moving, and again about ninety minutes before they close for the night. Bucktails tipped with squid strip or Gulp minnows on simple one-hook rigs, because that rubble bottom will eat anything fancy. If you're planning a night session there, remember you need the Suffolk County Night Fishing Permit for the 10pm-6am window — daytime access is free and open, but after dark they check.

Looking ahead, the tide swings are building back up out of the recent slack stretch, which should sharpen the bite everywhere current concentrates bait — the inlet hole, the bridge pilings, the canal locks. If that holds through the weekend, I'd stay on the same script: inlet after midnight on the outgoing for bass, Ponquogue on the incoming for weaks. Watch the bunker schools outside the inlet too — if the blues commit to them the way they have the last few summers, that could turn into the best surface action of the month.
