# Marine Parkway Bridge after dark: the access point of the week as bunker stack up on the outgoing
*Full moon tides are squeezing bait toward the inlet, and the bridge lights are where the bass are cashing in.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — August 27** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one shot this week, make it the Marine Parkway Bridge on the back half of the outgoing, after the sun's down. That's the access point paying off right now, and it's not close. You can walk right out under the span from the Rockaway side, get your bucktail or eel down into the current seam where the pilings break the flow, and let the tide do the work. Boat guys are doing the same thing from anchor upcurrent of the structure, letting bunker chunks or a live eel swing back into the shadow line. Either way you get there, that shadow line under the bridge lights is holding the most consistent bass in the bay this week.

Why there, why now — it's the bunker. Peanut and adult bunker schools have been building in the bay all month, and they're finally thick enough that the bass are organizing around them instead of just picking off stragglers. We flagged this a week ago, that the outer bay would start showing bait funneling toward the inlet as the season turned, and that's exactly what's happening — pods of bunker getting squeezed through the Rockaway Inlet and Beach Channel corridor on every tide change, with bass, and now a few bigger bluefish, riding the tide right along with them. The moon builds to full tomorrow, and we said that would tighten the current and concentrate the bait even more. It has. The swings at Marine Parkway and Cross Bay Bridge have been noticeably stronger the last few days, and that's pulling fish up onto structure that would otherwise be scattered out on the flats.

Daytime, this is still a summer pattern, not a fall one — water in the bay is sitting warm, mid-70s on the surface, and the bass know it. They're not chasing bait across open flats in the heat of the day; they're holding tight to bridge structure, channel edges, and anything with shade and current, and they're feeding hardest early, late, and after dark. Don't expect a repeat of a spring blitz on a sunny August afternoon. Expect a solid, honest bite in the right window if you fish the tide correctly.

Fluke are the clearest sign the bay hasn't turned yet. They've been soft and scattered for weeks now, spread thin across the warm flats from Grassy Bay to the JFK side, and the counts are down from where they were in June. The fish that are still producing are sitting on channel edges and deeper structure where there's a cooler bottom layer — the drop-offs along Beach Channel, the edges of the Cross Bay Bridge channel, and the deeper cuts inside Rockaway Inlet on the last two hours of the outgoing. Four-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on a half-ounce bucktail, dragged slow right on bottom, are still drawing strikes, but you're working for keepers now, not culling through shorts. If fluke is your priority this week, fish the deep stuff on the moving tide and don't waste time on the shallow flats until the water cools.

Snapper blues are the one thing you can count on without much thinking. They're thick in every back bay channel and marina basin — Canarsie Pier, Motts Basin, the docks off Cross Bay Boulevard — and they'll hit small metal, Kastmasters, or a piece of spearing under a bobber pretty much any time of day. It's easy fishing, and it's a great way to get a kid or a first-timer bent up while the bigger stuff waits for better light. Mixed in with those snappers, especially on the evening tide near the inlet, cocktail blues in the 2 to 4 pound class are showing up on structure and around the bass, taking the same bunker chunks and swimming plugs.

Weakfish are around in their usual late-August rhythm — not a headline fishery, but a real one if you know where to look. Drifting bloodworms or small Gulp Sandworms on light jigs over the deeper mud in the main channel near Ruffle Bar, particularly on the tail of the outgoing, has been picking off a few keeper weakfish mixed in with the fluke drift. It's not a fish you're targeting exclusively, but it's worth having a lighter rig rigged up while you're working that channel edge.

For kayak access, Motts Basin and the marsh channels off Broad Channel are still the play — protected water, good current, and you can slip right up on bunker pods without spooking them the way a bigger boat will. Shore access at Breezy Point and along Rockaway Beach is quieter right now than it'll be in a month, but a few bass are working the surf line at night on swimmers and bucktails, especially around the jetties where there's a little structure to hold bait.

Looking ahead, the full moon tomorrow should keep pushing stronger tides through the weekend, and that's the thing to watch. If the bunker keep tightening up against the inlet the way they have been, the next step is the first real fall-style blitz — not this week, but the outer bay and Rockaway Inlet are exactly where it'll show first when it does. Fish the moving water, fish the structure, and keep an eye on the birds working the inlet mouth on the outgoing. That's your tell.
