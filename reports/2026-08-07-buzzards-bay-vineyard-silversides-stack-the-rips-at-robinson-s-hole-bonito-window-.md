# Silversides Stack the Rips at Robinson's Hole — Bonito Window Cracking Open
*Bait's balled up tight in every hole from Woods Hole to Menemsha, and the water's finally warm enough to make you believe the first bonito are close.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — August 7** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Silversides are stacking up thick in Robinson's Hole right now — I mean pinned against the rocks on the west side, flashing like a school of nervous dimes every time the current turns. That's the tell I wait for all summer. When the bait gets that nervous, tight, and silvery in the rip, something's coming to eat it, and this week it's mostly bluefish and the occasional big striper, but the water's finally sitting in that window where bonito show up without much warning.

We flagged this exact stretch a few days back — said the Vineyard rips would be in the first wave once RI breachways got going, ahead of Block and Montauk. I haven't had a confirmed bonito in the boat yet, and I'm not going to tell you I have when I haven't, but the ingredients are all sitting on the table. Squid showing in the deeper holes at Quick's and Robinson's, silversides balled in the current seams, and surface temps that finally feel right on the back of your hand instead of just warm-ish. If you fish this water every week like I do, you learn to trust that gut feeling before the first fish actually shows, and my gut says we're days away, not weeks.

In the meantime the striper bite has been honest work, not a gimme. Best fish have come off the west end of Cuttyhunk on the last two hours of the outgoing, working live eels and swimming plugs along the rockpile edges in 15 to 25 feet. Nothing giant — mostly 26 to 34 inches — but a steady pick if you're willing to drift it twice. Night tide has been better than day tide, which tracks with what everyone south of the Cape is seeing this time of year: fish sliding deep and feeding after dark while the surface stays warm through the afternoon. If you want a shot at something heavier, fish the last of the outgoing into the start of the flood at Woods Hole itself, right in the throat where the current squeezes — bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig, weighted just enough to hold bottom in that whitewater. That's producing the occasional overslot fish for guys willing to sit through a lot of nothing.

Bluefish have been the most dependable thing going, and they're riding peanut bunker just like everybody predicted they would once that bait pushed further into the bay. Menemsha Basin and the rips off Lucas Shoal have both had these hard-charging pods of cocktail blues, 2 to 4 pounds, blowing up on top in short, violent windows — usually 20 minutes, then gone. Small metal — half-ounce Deadly Dicks or a 3-inch Kastmaster — thrown right into the boil and ripped back fast is about as complicated as it needs to be. If you're marking bait suspended mid-column on the sounder around the Elizabeth Islands, don't overthink it, just cast into the commotion.

Fluke have quietly been the most consistent producer of the week, which always happens right before the albie run gets everyone's attention and nobody talks about the bottom fish anymore. Best drifts have been in 20 to 35 feet off the north side of Naushon and through the channel edges toward Quick's Hole, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the last of the ebb. Keeper-sized fish, 18 to 22 inches, mixed with a lot of shorts you'll have to sort through. Scup have been thick around the Woods Hole drawbridge pilings and the rocks off Nonamesset — squid strips or clam on a light jig will keep a rod bent for the kids while you wait on the bigger stuff.

Tide-wise, we're coming off the last new moon push, and the currents through these holes have been running hard and clean, which is exactly what you want once the albies actually show — nothing kills an albie bite faster than slack, muddy water. Wind's been out of the southwest most afternoons, which stacks a little chop into Robinson's Hole by 2 PM, so mornings have fished cleaner across the board.

Looking ahead, if the pattern holds — stable high pressure, bait staying pinned in these rips, and surface temps easing another degree or two — I'd expect the first bonito to show at Robinson's Hole or off the Cuttyhunk side within the week, with false albacore trailing a few days to a week behind them like they always do. That's the sequence I've watched play out for fifteen seasons here, and nothing about this year looks different so far. My plan for the next few days is simple: first light at Robinson's Hole on the peanut-bunker/silverside pattern, small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dicks rigged and ready in case something with a torpedo body and a sickle tail shows up in that white water. Until then, work the stripers at night, keep the bluefish rod loaded with metal, and don't ignore the fluke bite just because everyone's staring at the horizon waiting on albies. That's how you miss a good week.
