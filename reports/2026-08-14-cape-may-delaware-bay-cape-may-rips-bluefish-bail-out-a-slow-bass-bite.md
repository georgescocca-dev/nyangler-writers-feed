# Cape May Rips: Bluefish Bail Out a Slow Bass Bite
*Stripers are ghosts at the Rips this week, but building tide off the new moon is turning on bluefish and fluke where it counts.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 14** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

I'll give the Rips a C this week, and that's being generous. Bass are around, but they're not stacked up and they're not stupid. If you fished the Rips proper — the stretch running off Cape May Point out toward the tip where the ocean current collides with the outgoing bay water — you had to work for anything with stripes on it. August does this. The water's warm, the bait's scattered thin instead of balled up tight, and the bass that are here are more interested in holding deep and sulking than chasing anything on the surface. That's not me making excuses. That's just what mid-August looks like on this piece of water most years, and this year's no different.

What's saved the week has been bluefish, and they've saved it hard. Chopper blues, three to six pounds, have been riding the same bunker pods that are pushing bass activity up the coast from here to Rhode Island — we're just not getting the blitz version of it that they're seeing off Narragansett and Buzzards. What we're getting is a solid, dependable bite on the ebb, best in that first two hours when the tide starts pulling hard and the rip lines actually form up. Metal — Deadly Dick or a 2-ounce Hopkins — worked fast through the froth has been money, and if you want to slow down and work the edges, bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig sitting just off bottom in 30 to 40 feet has been picking off the bigger singles that don't want to chase. If you're bass hunting and getting blues instead, don't fight it. Keep a rod rigged for both and let the day tell you which one's biting.

We flagged this last week — said the building tide into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what happened. The two days since the new moon have brought noticeably harder current through the Rips and around Cape May Point, and that extra push is what's been concentrating the bait and giving the bluefish something to work with. It's also what's kept the fluke bite alive over at Hereford Inlet, which honestly has been the more consistent story this week if you're after something for the cooler.

Hereford's fluke bite has been fair to good on the channel drop-offs along the inlet, particularly where the deeper cuts fall off the sand flats near the jetty. The bay's run warm this summer, pushing into the mid-70s in the shallows, and that's shoved a lot of the bait — and the weakfish and croaker that were feeding on it — off the flats and into the cooler, current-scrubbed channel edges. Best fluke window has been the last two hours of the ebb, when that warm flat water empties out and drags bait with it right into the strike zone. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom in 12 to 18 feet, have out-produced bare bucktails two to one. Nothing giant — most fish 16 to 19 inches — but a few keepers mixed in most trips, which for mid-August is a fine trade.

Across in the Bay, the black drum run is long buttoned up — that's a May story and everybody who's called the shack this week asking about drum gets the same answer, come back in spring. What's left over there is a scattered weakfish bite, mostly at night or first light, working the deeper channel edges off Fortescue and down toward Brandywine Shoal. Same story as Hereford — the warm surface water has pushed the tide-sensitive fish down into 20 to 25 feet where the current keeps things moving and the water stays a few degrees cooler. Bucktails tipped with Fishbites or a chunk of squid, bounced slow along the bottom on the last of the incoming, has been the play for the weakfish that are there. It's not a bite you build a whole trip around right now, but if you're already out drifting for something else, drop one down.

Looking ahead, I don't expect the Rips to turn into a bass blitz overnight — that's not really our pattern down here in August anyway, the way it can be further up the coast. What I do expect is the tide push to stay strong through the rest of this week as we build off the new moon, which should keep the bluefish honest and keep that fluke window at Hereford productive on the ebb. If I had one morning to fish, I'd be at the Rips at first light working the early ebb with metal for blues, then sliding over to Hereford by mid-morning to hit that channel edge on the tail of the tide. Two different bites, two different baits, but both riding the same current that's finally doing some work for us again.
