# Mackerel Push 40-Inch Bass Onto Duxbury Rips While Deer Island Pays Off on the Ebb
*Big bass are riding mackerel schools south of the harbor while Boston's own structure keeps grinding out slot fish and flounder on the tide change.*

**BOSTON HARBOR, MA — July 13** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

I had a choice to make this week — run the harbor structure I know like the back of my hand, or chase word of mackerel schools pushing big bass onto the rips south toward Duxbury and Kingston Bay. I split the week, and I'm glad I did, because both ends of my water are doing different things right now and a smart angler needs to know which one to play on a given morning.

Start with the harbor, because that's home base. Deer Island Light on the last two hours of the ebb has been the most consistent thing I've fished all month. The current rips hard off that ledge as the flow pulls out of President Roads, and bass are stacking on the downcurrent side waiting for whatever gets swept off the flat. I've been bouncing 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of squid along the bottom edge of that drop, letting the current do the work and just walking the rod tip to keep contact. Slot fish, mostly 28 to 34 inches, with the occasional better one mixed in when the tide really starts ripping in the last hour before dead low. Spectacle Island's north ledge is fishing the same way — soft plastics, 6-inch RonZ in bone or pearl, worked slow along that drop on the same stage of tide.

Flounder are still around on the Deer Island flats and up into the Winthrop side, but you have to anchor up and fish it properly — spreader rigs with squid strips or bloodworm, fished dead slow on the last of the incoming when the water's pushing warm, cloudy bait-carrying current up over the sand. It's not a fast fishery this time of year, but it's a real one if you're patient and willing to sit still for an hour.

Now the part that had me running south. Word came out of the Duxbury and Kingston Bay area that mackerel schools have moved in thick, and where mackerel go in July, the bigger bass follow. I heard of a fish pushing 49 inches taken off the South Shore on a mackerel-matched presentation, and that's not a fluke-sized outlier — that's a signal that the bigger year classes are keying on that bait right now. I ran down to fish the Scituate ledges and the mouth of Duxbury Bay on a live-lined mackerel under a balloon, drifting the edge where the bay current meets the open water on the last of the ebb. That's when the bait gets pushed out and the bigger fish set up to ambush it. It's not a numbers game down there — you might get one good bite in three hours — but when it comes it's a fish worth the drive.

Nahant and up toward Gloucester and Cape Ann, the rocky shore is holding fish the way it always does this time of year, but the bite has been more of a dawn-and-dusk program than an all-day affair. Warm surface water midday is pushing the bass down and out, and the better window has been the last two hours of light with topwater plugs and soft-plastic swimbaits worked tight to the rocks off Nahant's east side. Bluefish have shown up mixed in with the bass along that stretch, which tells me the bait's thick enough to hold two different predators on the same structure — a good sign for August.

Cod and haddock guys working the Cape Ann bottom have had a mixed bag of it — some solid drifts over the traditional humps outside Gloucester on diamond jigs and clam-baited hooks, other days slow when the current's been light and the fish scatter off the structure. That fishery is always going to be feast-or-famine in midsummer; I wouldn't plan a whole trip around it right now without a backup plan for stripers inshore.

Be honest with yourself about this stretch of July — it's not the wide-open blitz fishing of May, and it's not going to be until the fall run gets going. What you've got right now is structure fishing and bait-matching. Find where the mackerel are holding, find the tide stage that concentrates current on a ledge or a rip, and be there when it happens rather than fishing all day and hoping.

Looking ahead, if the mackerel schools stay put around Duxbury and Kingston Bay, that's where I'd spend my better mornings this coming week — live-lined mackerel on the last of the ebb, working the edge where the bay drains into open water. Inside the harbor, Deer Island and Spectacle Island will keep producing slot fish on bucktails through the tide changes regardless of what's happening outside. And up on the North Shore, I'd fish Nahant hard in that first hour of light before the boat traffic and the sun push everything off the rocks. Watch the bait — where the mackerel schools sit is going to tell you more this week than any chart I can hand you.
