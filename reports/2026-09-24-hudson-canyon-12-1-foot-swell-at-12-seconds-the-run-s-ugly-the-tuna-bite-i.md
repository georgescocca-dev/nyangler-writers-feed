# 12.1-Foot Swell at 12 Seconds: The Run's Ugly, the Tuna Bite Isn't
*A long-period groundswell is making the ride to the canyon a grind, but the warm filament pushing bluefin and yellowfin onto trolled spreads hasn't budged in weeks.*

What I do have is the data telling you what's waiting on the other side of it.

And what's waiting is a tuna bite that's held together for weeks now, which for late September is genuinely unusual in a good way. The Hudson-Wilmington warm filament that's been anchored over this stretch of water is still doing exactly what it's been doing since early this month — pulling bluefin and yellowfin up onto trolled spreads and jig drops along the edge of the canyon and the surrounding structure near the 100-fathom line. We flagged two weeks ago that any shift in that filament's position was the single biggest variable to watch for this zone, and it simply hasn't moved. That's the whole story right now: stable water, stable bite, and the only real obstacle is getting a boat out there through a sea state that's currently running double digits.

On the troll, the pattern that's been working is bunker-imitation lures — think green machines, spreader bars, and cedar plugs mixed with actual bunker-profile skirts — pulled at 6 to 7 knots across the temperature break where the filament's warmer water meets the surrounding canyon water. Yellowfin have been the more consistent player in that mix, running mid-30s to mid-40s in the fleet chatter, with bluefin mixed in on the cooler edges rather than sitting dead-center in the warmest water. That's consistent with what we called out a week and a half ago — the bluefin tend to hold on the flanks of a filament like this rather than stack inside it, and that's still the read. If you're marking bait and warm water on the meter but the trolling bite goes quiet, don't abandon the spot — drop a jig or a knife-jig rigged bait down through the thermocline instead. Boats working that vertical presentation over structure near the 100-fathom line have reportedly connected on bigger bluefin that weren't showing much interest in the spread up top.

Mahi have been a lighter presence in the reports coming back this week, mostly incidental fish picked up around any floating debris or weed lines crossing the warm side of the break — nothing to plan a whole trip around, but worth a long-line teaser or a naked ballyhoo out the back if you're already trolling. Wahoo and white marlin haven't been showing up with any real consistency in what's coming back from this stretch, which tracks with a filament pattern that's more of a tuna magnet than a full pelagic buffet right now.

The bigger question for the next several days isn't the fish, it's the sea state. That 12-second period swell out of the southeast is a groundswell signature, likely the tail of weather that's already passed through somewhere else, and those tend to ease gradually rather than drop off a cliff. If it backs down into single digits by the weekend — which is the more likely scenario given how these long-period swells typically decay — the window opens back up for a full troll-and-jig day without punishing the ride out. If it holds in the 8-to-10-foot range, it's still runnable for a lot of the canyon fleet, just slower going and a rougher drift once you're on station.

The filament itself is the thing to actually watch heading into next week. September closing out with any real cold front pushing through the region could start to tighten this pattern up — that's the same read we had going into this stretch, and it's still the case. A stable warm filament in late September is a gift this time of year, not a guarantee it stays put through October. For now, the call is straightforward: if the swell backs off even a little, the bite at the canyon edge and along the 100-fathom line has earned the trip. Watch the water temp break on your plotter once you're out there, work both the troll and the vertical jig, and don't be surprised if the biggest fish come off the cooler side of that line rather than dead in the warm center.
