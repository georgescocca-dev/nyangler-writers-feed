# Montauk bass bite fires as water temps hit 68 degrees at the Point
*Eels after dark producing 40-pound class stripers while diamond jigs work the rips.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 24** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is absolutely on fire right now, and it's all about the water temperature finally hitting that magic 68-degree mark. After weeks of cooler water holding fish back, we've got the thermal conditions that make Montauk the striper capital of the world.

The big bass are here in numbers I haven't seen this early in the season for years. We're talking legitimate 40-pound class fish coming over the rail after sunset on live eels fished tight to the boulder fields. The technique is classic Montauk — fishfinder rig with a 6/0 circle hook, just enough weight to get down, and let that eel work in the current along the rocks. The key is fishing the last two hours of the outgoing tide when the water really starts to move.

Diamond jigs are absolutely crushing it in the rips during the day. Two to four-ounce AVA 007s in white or chartreuse, worked fast through the water column, are producing steady action with bass in the 20 to 30-pound range. The technique that's working is dropping to the bottom, then working the jig with sharp lifts about three feet off the deck. Don't let it hit bottom again — that's where you lose them.

The water clarity has been exceptional, which is why the bucktail bite has been so consistent. One-ounce white bucktails tipped with Uncle Josh pork rind or a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet are working the structure perfectly. Fish them slow along the bottom, especially around the North Bar on the incoming tide.

What's really driving this bite is the bait situation. Sand eels are thick throughout the area, and that's what these bass are keyed in on. The squid that pushed through a few weeks back have settled into their summer pattern, but it's the sand eels that are keeping fish in the area. Bunker are still scarce, which is actually working in our favor — when bass don't have those big easy meals, they stay aggressive on smaller baits.

The fluke fishing has been a pleasant surprise. The south side drops in 30 to 40 feet are producing steady action with fish to six pounds. White Gulp on chartreuse bucktails, dragged slow on the drift, is the go-to rig. The key is fishing the edges where the current creates those little eddies — that's where the fluke are ambushing bait.

Porgies are showing up in good numbers around the rocky structure, though they're not everywhere like they used to be. Green crabs on high-low rigs in 20 to 35 feet are producing steady action, with some nice fish in the two-pound range.

The bluefish situation is interesting — we're seeing those chunky little cocktail blues in the one to two-pound range, which is always a great sign for the overall health of the fishery. They're hitting small metals and bucktails, and they're a blast on light tackle.

Conditions this week have been nearly perfect. Light winds, clean water, and that 68-degree temperature reading is exactly what we want to see. The new moon earlier this week spread fish out a bit, but now that we're building toward the full moon Friday, expect those big tides to really get things moving.

Looking ahead, this full moon is going to create some serious current flow. I'm watching for the inlet to really drain at sunset — that's when we might see the first real push of weakfish. The water temperature is right, the bait is there, and those big tides should trigger some serious feeding activity. If you're planning to fish the Point this weekend, focus on the last two hours of the outgoing tide with live eels, and don't overlook the incoming tide around the North Bar with bucktails. This is shaping up to be one of those classic Montauk summers.
