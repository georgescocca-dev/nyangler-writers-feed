# Bass crash the Norwalk rocks as bait pods stack on spring tide surge
*Schoolies to 32 inches hitting bucktails and soft plastics in the wash around the islands.*

**NORWALK ISLANDS, CT — June 12** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

The spring tide surge that peaked Tuesday night did exactly what I expected — it flushed massive bait pods out of the harbors and stacked them against the rocky structure around the Norwalk Islands. By Wednesday morning, the bass were all over it.

I've been watching this pattern develop for two weeks now. Water temps finally hit that magic 68-degree mark in the shallows, and the spearing have been thick along the Connecticut shoreline from Stamford to New Haven. When you get spring tides moving that much bait around structure, the bass can't resist.

The action has been best on the back side of the islands where the current creates those perfect eddies. I'm talking about the rocky points on Sheffield and Chimon islands specifically. Fish are holding in 12 to 20 feet, right where the current breaks around the boulders. White and chartreuse 3/4-ounce bucktails with 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets have been deadly. Cast uptide and let it swing through the rocks — that's when they hammer it.

The bite window has been narrow but intense. First light to about 8 AM, then again from 6 PM to dark. During the day, you need to work deeper — out to the 30-foot contour where the thermocline is setting up. The fish are there, but they're more finicky. Switch to lighter jigs, maybe 1/2-ounce, and work them slower.

Port Jefferson has been producing too, especially around the ferry terminal pilings on the incoming tide. The structure there creates the same kind of current breaks that hold bait. I've been hearing about keeper fluke mixed in with the bass action — nothing huge, but solid 18 to 20-inch fish on the sandy patches between the rocks.

What's got me really excited is the bait situation. The spearing schools are getting thicker every day, and I'm starting to see some adult bunker moving through. That's early for bunker, but the water temps are running about two degrees warmer than normal for mid-June. When those bunker schools really establish, that's when the big bass show up.

The Stratford Shoal area has been hit or miss. The rip is running hard with these spring tides — maybe too hard for consistent fishing. But when you time it right, around the tide changes, there are fish there. Bigger fish too. I heard about a couple of 30-plus-inch bass coming off the shoal this week, but you need to be able to handle the current.

Porgies have been a pleasant surprise. The Middle Ground Light area has been loaded with them — big ones, 12 to 14 inches. They're in 25 to 35 feet, and they're hungry. Small hooks, size 6 or 8, with pieces of clam or squid. It's been a great backup when the bass bite slows down.

Looking ahead, we're moving into neap tides this weekend, which should calm things down and let the fish settle into more predictable patterns. The full moon is still a week away, so we've got some stable conditions coming. I'm watching for the water temps to push into the low 70s — that's when the summer pattern really kicks in and the fish spread out more.

The key right now is mobility. Don't get stuck on one spot. The bait is moving with these tides, and the fish are following. Keep your eyes open for bird activity and surface disturbances. When you see the terns diving, get there fast. This bite won't last forever, but while it's on, it's as good as it gets in the central Sound.
