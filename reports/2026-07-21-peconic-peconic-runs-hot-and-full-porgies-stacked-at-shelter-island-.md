# Peconic runs hot and full — porgies stacked at Shelter Island, fluke steady
*The bay's ahead of the Sound again this July, and the bass have gone nocturnal while the porgies and fluke carry the daylight hours.*

**GREENPORT, NY — July 21** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

Every year around this time I remind people that the Peconic doesn't play by the Sound's clock, and every year it proves me right. Kings Point is sitting at 70.9, New London's still down at 69.4 — but our bay warmed weeks ago and it shows. The water off Shelter Island has that thick, green, bait-rich look it gets in high summer, and the porgies have settled into their usual haunts like they never left.

Coecles Harbor and the flats off Big Ram and Little Ram are holding porgies in the 15 to 22 foot range, and they're not shy about it. Sandworms and clam snouts on a high-low rig, small hooks, light sinkers — you don't need to overthink porgy fishing here, you just need to find the sandy patches between the mussel beds and drop straight down on the last of the incoming or the first two hours of the outgoing. Same story off Jennings Point and the mouth of Coecles — bottom's holding scup in numbers, with a scattering of keeper blackfish mixed in on the rockier stretches now that the season's open. The tog bite isn't the fall show yet, but a few three and four pound fish have come off the reef edges near Shelter Island's western shore on green crab, fished slow on the slack.

Fluke are exactly where they should be. We said last week the Peconic fluke bite would carry through summer, and here we are — it has. The channels are the story: North Race, the cut off Orient, and the deeper water running past Jennings Point are all holding fish in the 20 to 32 foot range. Drifting bucktails tipped with Gulp — white or pink swimming mullet, 3/4 to 1 ounce depending on current — is still outproducing bait-only rigs, especially on the last two hours of the ebb when the channel water is moving with some authority. Sizes are mixed, plenty of fish in the 16 to 18 inch range with enough 20-inchers mixed in to keep it interesting. Nothing spectacular this week, but steady — the kind of bite where a few hours on the right tide fills a cooler without much drama.

Striped bass have gone the way they usually go once the water settles into summer pattern — quiet through the middle of the day, then switching on hard around dusk and staying active well after dark. Plum Gut is still the anchor spot for this zone, and with the first-quarter neap settling in, the current through the Gut and the North Race has softened up noticeably. That's actually good news for presentation — instead of fighting a screaming rip, you can work a bucktail or a swim bait more deliberately across the edges. I've had better luck lately casting a 1.5-ounce bucktail across the deep side of the channel, letting it sink clean off the drop, and working it back slow along bottom rather than ripping it through the current. Orient Point's outer edge has been holding fish the same way — slot-sized bass mostly, with the occasional overslot mixed in on bunker schools that have been drifting through Gardiners Bay.

Speaking of which, bluefish have shown up in real numbers through Gardiners Bay and around Orient. These aren't the little snappers we saw in June — these are jumbo blues, slashing through bunker pods in open water during daylight, which is unusual enough to notice. If you find the birds working over a bait ball off Orient or the eastern side of Shelter Island, don't overthink your presentation — a bucktail or a diamond jig worked through the melee will get bit almost every cast. Just watch your fingers on the release, these fish are angry and healthy.

Weakfish have been the quiet surprise. We flagged last week that the weakfish bite would rebuild on calm, warm evenings, and that's exactly what's happened around Three Mile Harbor and the deeper holes in Noyack Bay. Nothing like the old-timer weakfish runs, but enough action after dark on bucktails and soft plastics — pink and white five-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets on light jig heads — to make an evening trip worthwhile if you're patient and fishing the last of the tide into full dark.

Looking ahead, the neap tide should keep softening through midweek before the tides build back toward the coming full moon, which will get things moving again through the Gut and the Race. If the current stays gentle, I'd lean into slower presentations for bonus fluke and blackfish over the next several days — this is the window for it. Once the tides build back up, I expect the bass to tighten back onto the hard rip lines and the bluefish to keep pushing bait through the bay as long as this settled weather holds. If I had one evening to fish this week, I'd be on the Gut at last light with a bucktail in hand, watching for that first swirl in the current seam.
