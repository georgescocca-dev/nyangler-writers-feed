# Montauk jigging explodes as sunset trips fire — bass to 26 pounds on the Point
*Diamond jigs crushing bass on the Lighthouse grounds while porgies stack thick in 30 feet.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 19** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is alive. Diamond jigging has absolutely exploded this week, with bass to 26 pounds coming over the rail on the Lighthouse grounds and captains adding sunset trips to keep up with demand. When the boats are booking extra runs, you know something special is happening out there.

The action is concentrated on the south side drops in 25 to 40 feet, where the water has settled into that magic 67-degree range that gets everything feeding. The Viking fleet has been crushing them on the traditional Montauk diamond jig program — 3 to 6-ounce AVA 007s and 017s in white, yellow, and chartreuse, worked vertically through the water column on the drift. The key is letting the jig flutter down through the middle of the water column, then ripping it back up with sharp, aggressive jerks. These aren't bottom fish right now — they're suspended and feeding aggressively.

What's driving this bite is the thermal setup we've got working. The shelf water has climbed to 67 degrees while Block Island Sound is still sitting at 62 — that 5-degree gradient is stacking bait along the Point's underwater structure like a dinner bell. The peanut bunker are thick, and when you combine that with the approaching full moon tides, you get bass that are feeding with serious intent.

The porgy bite has been equally impressive, with jumbo fish in the 2 to 3-pound class coming from the same grounds. High-low rigs with size 2 hooks and small pieces of squid or clam are doing the damage. The beauty of this setup is you can target both species on the same drift — drop the diamond jig for bass, then switch to the porgy rig when the action slows.

Bluefish are mixed into the action, which tells you the bait concentration is real. When you've got bass, blues, and porgies all feeding in the same area, that's the Point working the way it's supposed to work. The structure is funneling everything together, and the fish are taking advantage.

Surf fishing has been productive on the incoming tide, particularly around Turtle Cove and the North Bar. The northwest wind we've been getting has been cleaning up the water and creating that perfect surf condition where bass move in tight to feed. Bucktails with white Gulp trailers and live eels on fishfinder rigs have been the go-to presentations. The key is fishing the last two hours of the incoming, when the current pushes bait against the structure.

The water clarity has been excellent despite the 5-foot seas we've been dealing with. That southwest groundswell is finally backing down, and conditions are becoming more manageable for smaller boats. The Block Island run is still sporty with 6-foot seas, but the Point itself is fishable if you know how to read the water.

Looking ahead, this full moon cycle should keep the action hot through the weekend. The big tides will flush more bait out of the back bays and estuaries, and that means more feeding opportunities for the bass. The sunset trips that captains are adding aren't just a gimmick — they're responding to fish that are feeding aggressively in low light conditions.

The northwest wind pattern we're in should continue to clean up the water and keep conditions favorable. When you combine good water, concentrated bait, and aggressive fish, you get the kind of fishing that makes Montauk legendary. This is why we call it the striper capital of the world — when conditions align like this, there's nowhere else quite like the Point.
