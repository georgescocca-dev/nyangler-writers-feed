# Stellwagen gravel in 110: cod stacked tight, bluefin working the mackerel schools topside
*Cold, green water holding on the Bank has the groundfish steady on bottom and giant bluefin pinning bait on the surface — a two-fer week out of Gloucester.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — August 9** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

One-ten feet on the meter, gravel edge on the southwest corner of Stellwagen Bank, current running slack to the first hour of the flood — that's where we found the cod Tuesday morning, and they were sitting right where they should be this time of August. Jigging diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid, working them off bottom in six-foot lifts, and getting solid thumps from fish in the 8-to-15-pound class, a few better mixed in. Nothing epic, but steady. You drop, you get bit, you move a quarter mile when it goes quiet, you get bit again. That's how gravel fishing works out here — it's not a pile, it's a pattern, and this week the pattern held.

Haddock have been on the mud, like always, but I'll be honest — it's been a mixed bag in the deeper basin area of the Bank. We worked the transition zone where the gravel rolls off into softer bottom, around 130 to 150 feet, and found pockets of decent-sized haddock, 3 to 6 pounds, mixed with a fair number of shorts we had to cull through. Clam bait on a standard haddock rig, small hooks, fishing tight to bottom on the anchor. Some drifts produced, some didn't. If you're headed out for haddock specifically, I'd fish two or three spots in that basin transition before committing — don't burn the whole tide in one hole hoping it turns on.

Now the real story this week is topside. The water on the Bank has stayed cold and green through this whole stretch — no warm intrusion pushing in from the south, no signal of that changing — and that cold, nutrient-rich water has kept the bait thick. Mackerel schools have been balled up hard over the deeper structure off the drop-off, and the bluefin have been right on them. We flagged this building pattern last week, said the bluefin show would keep growing as long as that cold water pattern held, and that's exactly what's happened — more fish showing daily, more consistent surface activity, birds working the same real estate as the tuna.

We've had multiple hookups on the troll running spreader bars and green machines just off that drop-off in the 150-to-200-foot range where the bottom starts to fall away, mackerel balled up thick on the sounder in that mid-water column. When we mark a school getting worked from below, that's when we've been pitching stickbaits and pencil poppers into the commotion — topwater eats on giant bluefin never get old, and we've had a few blow up right at the boat that'll keep you up at night replaying it. Not every school stays up long enough to get a bait to it, and not every pass produces, but the fish are there in numbers we haven't seen yet this summer, and that's worth noting heading into the back half of August.

Beyond this immediate area, we don't have any confirmed reports to pass along this week — nothing solid enough to put a number or a location behind. When something firms up, it'll be in the column.

The moon's building toward new on the 12th, and that means tidal range is about to sharpen up across the whole Bank. For groundfish, that usually means shorter, more defined bite windows — the fish feed hard on the push and shut down on the slack, so timing your drift to the tide change matters more than it did during the slower neap water we just came off. For the bluefin, more current usually means better bait concentration along the drop-off, pinning those mackerel schools tighter against structure instead of letting them spread out over open water.
