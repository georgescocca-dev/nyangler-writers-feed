# Captree dock talk: fluke sliding to the inlet, blues chewing bunker, bass still on hold
*The ocean's been sloppy all week and it's pushed the whole program — fluke, blues, and the first fall stirrings — into the inlet and the bay.*

**CAPTREE, NY — August 31** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Stood at the scale house Sunday morning listening to a mate off one of the party boats tell anybody who'd listen that the ocean's been "lumpy as a bad mattress" all week, and he wasn't wrong. Wind swell's been running three to four feet with a short five-second period out front of the inlet, and that kind of chop doesn't destroy a bite so much as it moves it. Fish don't like getting tossed around any more than we do, so the whole program this week — fluke, blues, even the first whispers of fall bass — has been sliding out of the open ocean and tucking into the inlet, the bay channels, and anywhere there's a lee.

Water's still warm, low 70s out at the buoy line and not much cooler inside the bay, and that warmth is exactly what's been pushing fluke off the flats and into the deeper, better-oxygenated water around the Robert Moses bridges and the main inlet channel. We flagged this pattern last week — said fluke wouldn't collapse, just shift into structure and deeper channels through September before the real fall pullout — and that's precisely what the fleet's finding. It's not a bang-bang bite anymore, but there are still keeper-class fish stacking on the drop-offs.

The fluke bite right now is a patience game. Best water has been 25 to 35 feet on the flood tide around the east and west Robert Moses bridges, with 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails dragged dead slow along the bottom. A few boats working the deeper edge of the Fire Island Inlet channel, closer to Democrat Point, have been picking off fish up to 5 pounds mixed in with a lot of shorts, so you're culling more than you're keeping most trips. The pattern holds through the last two hours of the incoming and the top of the tide — once it starts ripping out hard, the fluke tuck tight to structure and go quiet.

Bluefish have been the one thing you can set a watch by. They're chewing on bunker schools that have been building along the beach and pushing into the inlet mouth, and the size has been solid — 3 to 6 pounds, with the occasional bigger cow mixed in when a school gets pinned against the Democrat Point jetty. Diamond jigs, white bucktails, or just chunked bunker on a fish-finder rig fished on the bottom near working birds has been the simplest way to connect. This is textbook — blues track bunker density, plain and simple, and with bait still thick along the south shore beaches there's no reason to expect that to slow down before Labor Day.

Striped bass are the one piece of this puzzle everybody's asking about and nobody's got a real answer for yet. They're around — a few slot fish have come off the bridge pilings at Robert Moses on bucktails worked at night, and the bay side has produced some schoolies up in the Patchogue and Sayville flats on bloodworms and small swimming plugs — but this is a holding pattern, not the fall run. The bait hasn't concentrated enough yet to trigger a real push. That usually doesn't happen until nighttime temps start dropping hard, and we're not there. Mid-September is the number to watch, not this week.

Weakfish have been mostly a memory this time of year in the bay, though a few incidental fish have shown up mixed in with the fluke around channel edges — worth a mention if you're jigging soft plastics and feel something with a little more give in the fight than a fluke usually offers. Porgies are still there for anyone who wants a bend in the rod without overthinking it — the humps off Democrat Point and the deeper holes inside the inlet have been giving up decent numbers on sandworms and clam bellies, bottom rig, incoming tide. Blackfish season's still off in the distance, but a few boats have been marking fish already stacking up on the rockier structure near the inlet jetties, which is worth remembering come October.

Looking ahead, the moon's building toward full over the next several days, and that's going to matter. More tidal push means more current to concentrate bait, and if the bunker schools tighten up the way they have from Raritan Bay on up the coast, the blues should stay locked in right where they are — working the surf line off Fire Island and the inlet mouth. The ocean chop should ease some by midweek if the wind pattern backs off like it's forecast to, and that would open the beach fluke and surf bass bite back up a little, especially at first light around Democrat Point and the Fire Island beaches west of the lighthouse.

If you've only got one shot this weekend, work the last two hours of the flood at the Robert Moses bridges for fluke, then swing over to the inlet mouth at first light or last light for the blues working bunker. Keep an eye on the surf for any sign of albies or bonito showing in the wash — the water's warm enough right now that it wouldn't shock anybody at the dock if one turned up before Labor Day. Nobody's seen one yet. But the setup's there.
