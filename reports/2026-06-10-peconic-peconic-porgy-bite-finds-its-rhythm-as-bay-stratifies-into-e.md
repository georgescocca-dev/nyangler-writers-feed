# Peconic porgy bite finds its rhythm as bay stratifies into early summer pattern
*With the analyst feed thin on live buoy numbers, seasonal thermal structure and tide-stage discipline are driving the Shelter Island scup grounds.*

**GREENPORT, NY — June 10, 2026** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The oceanographic picture on the Peconics this week comes with a caveat: the Dr. Fish analyst feed is running light on live buoy data, with offshore stations 44017, 44039 and 44040 offline and wind readings missing from the nearshore network. The SST package out of the pipeline is current through June 8. What we can say with confidence is that the bay system has fully cleared its spring transition. Surface temperatures inside the Peconic and Gardiners estuary are running well ahead of the Sound and the ocean side of Montauk, which is the seasonal norm — this is a shallow, semi-enclosed basin that absorbs solar gain fast once we get past Memorial Day. By the second week of June the inside water is typically pushing into the mid-60s while Plum Gut and the rips off Orient still drag along five to eight degrees cooler on a strong flood.

That thermal gradient is the single most important variable on this beat right now. It means the scup are locked into their early-summer pattern on the structure between Shelter Island and the Greenlawns, the weakfish have finally moved up into the deeper holes off Jessup's and the Cedars in fishable numbers, and the bass game has split — the early-season Orient and Plum Gut fish that were behind schedule a couple of weeks ago should now be feeding in earnest on the cooler outside water, while the resident slot-class fish are working the harbor mouths at Three Mile and Noyack on low-light tides. The fluke are stacking in the channel edges in 18 to 30 feet, but the bigger fish are still on the deeper sand south of Gardiners.

The porgy picture deserves a closer read. This spring has not been the carpet-bombing scup run we've seen in past Mays — fish have been there, but spottier, and the size structure has been the headline. Quality 16- to 19-inch jumbos are showing on the right drops near Shelter Island and over the rockier humps on the flood, but you have to commit to a chum line and let it work for ten or fifteen minutes before the bite consolidates. The crowd-pleaser spots like the Middle Grounds and the Greenlawns have been inconsistent depending on tide and boat pressure, and a flat-calm day with no current is the worst combination out there right now. Run-and-gun drifting is not the game this week; anchor on the up-current edge of structure, build a slick, and let the tide do the work.

Weakfish have shown up on schedule and in better numbers than the last couple of seasons would have suggested. The classic pattern is holding — drifts through the deeper soft-bottom areas in 18 to 25 feet on a moving tide, with the bite firing best on the last two hours of the flood into the top. Pink and chartreuse plastics on a half-ounce head are doing the work, and there have been enough 3- to 4-pound fish in the mix to make targeted trips worthwhile. The bycatch story is what tells you the bay is healthy: weakfish coming up in the scup chum line, the occasional bass nosing in, and bluefish starting to show on the surface in Noyack and the western end of the Peconic proper.

The next three to five days are about wind and moon. We're working into the back half of the lunar cycle, which means more moderate tides and longer slack windows — that favors the scup anchor game and hurts the fluke drifters. If the southwest fills in the way it usually does by mid-June, the chop on the Peconic in the afternoon will push the better fishing into the morning, and the lee of Shelter Island becomes the high-percentage zone. Watch for the inside bay water to crack 68 degrees by the weekend; once that happens, the weakfish bite typically peaks for a two-week window before the bigger fish slide back toward the ocean.

I'd be on the water at first light, anchored on the up-tide side of a known scup hump near Shelter Island with a fresh log of clam chum, and I'd save the last hour of the flood for a drift through the weakfish water. The bay is doing exactly what it's supposed to do in the second week of June. The anglers who match tide stage to species and stop fighting the current will fill coolers.
