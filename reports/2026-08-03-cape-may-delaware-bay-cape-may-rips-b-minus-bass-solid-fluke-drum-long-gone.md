# Cape May Rips: B-Minus Bass, Solid Fluke, Drum Long Gone
*The rips are fishable on the right water but you're working for every bass — fluke are the honest bite right now.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 3** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

I'll grade the Rips a B-minus this week, and that's being generous to the bass. Fluke earn the only A on my card. If you want the truth instead of a sales pitch: the striper bite out front has gone the way it always does first week of August — fish holding deep on structure through the day, only showing themselves in a tight window at first light and again after the sun's off the water. Anybody telling you different either hasn't been out or is selling you a charter.

Water out at the buoy off the Bight is sitting around 75-76 degrees with a low swell running, nothing that's shutting things down but enough lump that I'm picking my days on the outer rips carefully. Inside, where the bay water dumps out through Cape May Inlet and around the Point, temps are pushing into the mid-to-upper 70s — that's warm enough that the stripers want no part of open water at noon. They're stacked on the deep edges of the Rips itself, the humps off the Point, and the pilings under the Cape May-Lewes Ferry terminal, sitting tight to structure and current seams. My best fish this week — a 34-incher — came at 5:40 in the morning on a live eel, slow-trolled along the north edge of the Rips on the last hour of the incoming, right where the sand gives way to the rock pile in about 25 feet. After that window closed, it was quiet until dusk.

Here's the thing about the tide right now — we're sliding into a neap cycle over the next couple days, and that's going to soften the current through the Rips noticeably. Less water moving means the bait doesn't get pinned as hard against structure, and the bass get lazier about chasing it. If you fish the Rips this weekend, don't expect the kind of rip you saw during the new or full moon push — plan on lighter leadheads, slower presentations, and patience. I'd rather fish the two hours around the tide change than blind-cast through a slack, weak-moving tide with nothing showing on the surface.

Fluke, though — fluke are doing exactly what they're supposed to be doing in the first week of August, and this is where I'd point anybody who wants a fish box instead of a story. We're drifting 30 to 50 feet on the outer edge of the Rips and along the Hereford Inlet channel, dragging 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets, chartreuse and white both working depending on the light. Doormat-class fish are showing — we had a 6-pounder off Hereford on Tuesday's outgoing, and shorts have been thick enough that you're culling through a dozen fish to find your keepers. The bite's been best on the back half of the ebb, when the bait gets pulled out of the inlet and pinned against the channel edge in that 20-30 foot stretch. If fluke is your priority this week, that's your spot and that's your tide.

Bluefish are following the bunker pattern everybody up the coast is seeing — pods pushing into the mouth of Hereford Inlet and stacking up along the jetty rocks on the Wildwood side. Nothing blitz-scale down here yet, but enough fish that a bucktail or a Kastmaster thrown into a bait ball at first light will get you bit fast. Small to mid-size blues mostly, 3 to 5 pounds, but they'll wreck your leader and your morning in the best way.

Now for the honest part nobody wants to hear in August: the Delaware Bay drum run is done. That's not a slow week, that's just the calendar — black drum is a May fishery down here, Fortescue and the Cohansey mouth were producing fish up to 40 pounds back in the spring on fresh clam and now that window's closed until next year. Anybody asking me about drum this week, I'm telling them the same thing my father told guys who asked him in August thirty years ago — wrong season, come back in the spring.

What the bay is holding onto is weakfish, and that's a pattern worth chasing if you've got a bay boat. The back channels up toward Fortescue and into the grass-lined creeks are holding weakfish tight to the edges on the tide change, with warm bay water in the mid-70s keeping them actively feeding rather than sulking. Bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, worked slow along the channel drop, have been the ticket — nothing giant, mostly 16 to 20 inches, but a legitimate bite worth running for if the ocean's blown out.

Looking at the week ahead — we flagged this coming, and here it is: with tides softening into the neap through the 5th and 6th, expect the bass to compress even harder into the deepest, coolest structure and the strongest current hours. That means night eeling at the Rips and around the ferry terminal pilings becomes the play, not daytime jigging. I'd also keep an eye on the wreck and rock-pile structure off the Point — triggerfish have a habit of building through August as the water peaks, and the pieces close to shore are worth a bucktail drop if you're already out there for sea bass. No promises on numbers yet, but the pattern's right for it. My plan this week is simple: fluke in the mornings on the moving tide, bass at first and last light on structure, and I'm not wasting a trip on the Rips during a dead slack neap tide when I could be filling a cooler with doormats instead.
