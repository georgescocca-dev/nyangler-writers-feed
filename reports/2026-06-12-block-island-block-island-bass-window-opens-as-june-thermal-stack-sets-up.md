# Block Island bass window opens as June thermal stack sets up over the Ledge
*With 44097 reporting intermittently and 44025 dark, the read is by inference — but the seasonal thermal gradient is on schedule and bait is staged.*

**BLOCK ISLAND, RI — June 12, 2026** — _by Jack Sullivan, Zone Writer_

The primary offshore reference for this beat is compromised this morning. Buoy 44025 south of Long Island is offline, and 44097 southeast of Block has been intermittent on the water-temperature channel, leaving the published SST package from June 10 as the cleanest snapshot available. That package and the seasonal trajectory through early June put the surface layer around the island in the low-to-mid 60s, with the Southwest Ledge sitting near the leading edge of the thermal front that separates warmer Block Island Sound water inside from the cooler, more oxygenated slug pushing up the shelf edge. Wind has been workable, seas inside Point Judith have laid down through the week, and the outside has not had the kind of sustained southwest blow that piles up surface chop on the Ledge and kills the drift. In short: the engine is built, even if one of the gauges is out.

What that means for this beat is straightforward. June 12 is the front edge of the trophy bass window at Block. The migration class — the genuine cows, fish that wintered off the Chesapeake mouth and the Hudson Canyon edge — is arriving now to settle into the cool, oxygenated rip water they come here for. The Southwest Ledge is the headline structure, and the North Rip is the secondary play depending on wind angle. The fish that show up first are not slot fish. They are overslot and trophy class, and they are going to set up on the down-current side of the Ledge in 60 to 90 feet, working the bait that the tide rivers off the structure. Live eels on a three-way, or three- to five-ounce bucktails tipped with pork or a slim soft plastic, fished right on the edge of the drop — that is the technique that matches the water this week.

On the ground, the porgy and sea bass picture inside Narragansett Bay and around Newport has been steady since the seabass opener, with the better grade of fish holding on the harder structure off Brenton Reef and the lower Bay ledges rather than the soft mud. Fluke reports from the south side of the island have been uneven — a familiar early-summer pattern where the tide is still running too hard on the new and full phases to hold a controlled drift on the deeper edges, and the better fish are coming on the slack-to-building stages closer to the beach in 30 to 45 feet. The drift problem is real and it is geometric, not a bait issue: when the southwest wind opposes the outgoing, the boats spin and the rigs hang. Pick your tide.

The blackfish bite around the wind farm towers and the natural Block boulder fields continues to be a structure story rather than a temperature story, and that fishery is shoulder-season anyway. The signal worth tracking right now is bass. Bait — sand eels on the south side, bunker pods working up from Point Judith, squid still around on the warmer nights — is in position. The water is in the band stripers want. The fish are here or arriving within days.

Looking ahead three to five days, the controlling variables are wind direction and the moon. We are building toward the full moon later in the month, which means tides are going to get bigger before they get smaller, and the Ledge will fish best on the back side of the bigger stages — the last two hours of the ebb into the first of the flood after dark is the classic Southwest Ledge trophy window, and that window gets sharper as the moon fills. If the forecast southwest holds light, the night eel bite should turn on hard by the weekend. A hard onshore blow would push the surface layer warmer and shove the bait tight to the beach, which would shift the program toward the North Rip on the dropping tide. I would be on the Ledge at slack-to-ebb after dark before I would be anywhere else on this beat this week.

The data gaps are real and worth naming — without 44025 and with 44097 unreliable, the precise SST number on the Ledge today is an inference rather than a reading. But the seasonal clock, the bait position, and the structure are all telling the same story. The first serious bass of the Block Island summer are on the board.
