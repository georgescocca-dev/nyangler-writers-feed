# Monhegan Ledges on the Ebb: Pollock Topside, Bass Chasing Pogies Into the Bay
*Mackerel showed thick off Damariscove this week, and where the mackerel go, the bass and pollock follow — but the groundfish still make you work for it.*

**BOOTHBAY HARBOR, ME — July 17** — _by Rusty Pelletier, Zone Writer_

Forty-some years back, my father had a rule about mid-July: don't leave the dock till you've seen what the gulls are doing over Damariscove. If they're diving, the mackerel are up, and if the mackerel are up, everything else that eats is close behind. I still follow that rule. This week the birds were working hard off the Cove and again out toward Monhegan, and sure enough, the whole food chain lined up behind them — pollock on top, bass working the edges, and the groundfish holding deep where they always do this time of year.

Monhegan's south side has been the most reliable piece of water in the zone. Around White Head and down toward the ledges off Manana, pollock have been busting bait on the surface in the gray light before seven, then dropping again once the sun gets up. A 2 to 3-ounce diamond jig worked with a quick, erratic retrieve has taken fish steady — nothing huge, mostly 3 to 6 pounds, but enough for a fast morning. Striped bass are mixed into the same water, especially on the back half of the ebb when the current pulls bait off the ledges into deeper cuts. Live mackerel on a fish-finder rig, or a big white or bone Slug-Go worked slow along bottom, has been drawing bass up to the high 20s, occasional fish pushing 34, 35 inches. It's not a five-fish morning every time out — some days you find three or four good ones and call it, other days the bite shuts off the minute the tide turns. That's the honest version.

Eastern Egg Rock has had puffins working the surface most mornings, and where there's bait thick enough to hold puffins, there are usually bass cruising underneath looking for the same forage. Fly guys throwing olive-and-white Clousers into the boils around the rock have connected on schoolie-to-mid-slot bass, best in the last two hours of the ebb when the current stacks bait against the rock's east side.

Inside the bay, the pattern's a little different. Pogies — menhaden — have started pushing into the western passages of Penobscot Bay, and the bass are following them in rather than staying out on the open ledges. I've had good reports of fish working the mouth of the St. George River and up around the Muscle Ridge Channel on the incoming tide, right where the current funnels bait against the ledges. A big paddle-tail swimbait in bunker colors, fished slow and deep on the drift, has out-produced topwater most mornings, though the first hour of light with a popper has been worth staying up for if you don't mind a slower pick.

Groundfish are still down where they belong — the deep channels of Penobscot Bay, particularly the Fox Islands Thorofare and the stretch of water between Vinalhaven and North Haven, 100 to 140 feet on the sounder. Cod and haddock are there, but it's not stack-'em-up fishing like it was thirty years ago. You have to find the right piece of bottom — a hard edge, a hump, a spot where the current breaks — and jig it properly, or soak clams on a bottom rig and be patient. Haddock especially have been running small this year, plenty of fish just under the line that need to go back. When you do connect with a keeper cod, it's usually in that 5 to 10-pound range, occasionally better, taken on a diamond jig worked tight to bottom on the last hour of the ebb when the current slacks enough to hold your jig in the strike zone.

Down around Deer Isle and Stonington, the mackerel schools have been thick enough in the Thorofare to fill a bucket in twenty minutes with small feathered jigs — good bait for the bass runs, good eating too if you like them fresh off the grill. Striped bass around the ledges off Isle au Haut have been working the last of the ebb, and a tube-and-worm rig trolled slow along the drop-offs has taken some quality fish, a few pushing 36 inches, though the bite there has been more about timing than volume — miss the tide stage by an hour and you're fishing empty water.

Bluefish remain scattered this far east — a few showing off Boothbay in the warmer surface water near the harbor mouth, nothing like the blitzes you'd see farther south, but worth a cast if you're already rigged for bass with wire or a heavier leader.

Looking ahead, I expect the mackerel to hold through the rest of July, which means the bass and pollock ought to stay tucked in around the same structure — Monhegan's ledges, Egg Rock, the passages into the bay. If the pogies keep pushing west into Penobscot proper, I'd shift more time to the river mouths and the Muscle Ridge Channel rather than fighting open water for scattered fish. For groundfish, if the weather holds and you can get out to the deeper channels between Vinalhaven and North Haven, the cod and haddock are there for anyone willing to work the bottom instead of just drifting over it. It's a working man's bite right now — steady if you put in the time, not a gimme, and that's about right for mid-July on this water.
