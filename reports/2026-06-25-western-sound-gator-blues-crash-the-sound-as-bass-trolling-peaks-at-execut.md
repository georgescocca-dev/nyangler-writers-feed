# Gator blues crash the Sound as bass trolling peaks at Execution Rocks
*Big stripers to 50 pounds on Mojos over the ledge while cocktail blues blitz butterfish in the shallows.*

**EXECUTION ROCKS, NY — June 25** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The Western Sound is firing on all cylinders right now, and I'm seeing the kind of fishing that makes you remember why you fell in love with these waters in the first place. Water temps have climbed past the magic 70-degree mark in most spots, and that's triggering the summer patterns I've been waiting for all season.

The big news is the bass trolling bite at Execution Rocks. I've been watching boats work the ledge structure there, and when the outgoing tide gets moving, those fish turn on like someone flipped a switch. The pattern is simple but deadly effective: large Mojos dragged over the drop-off in 35 to 50 feet of water. The key is timing it with moving water — the morning slack produces nothing, but once that ebb starts pulling, you're in business. I'm hearing about multiple 40-inch-plus fish coming over the rails, including one monster that went an estimated 50 pounds. That's the kind of fish that makes grown men weep.

The Stepping Stones area is producing similar results, especially on the flood tide when bait gets pushed against the structure. Bunker spoons in the 3 to 4-ounce range are working well here, particularly the chrome and blue patterns. The fish are staging deep during the day — 45 to 60 feet — but they'll come up to chase if you put the lure in their face.

Now here's where it gets interesting for the light tackle crowd: the bluefish invasion has begun in earnest. These aren't the cocktail blues we've been dealing with — I'm talking about legitimate gator blues in the 8 to 12-pound class that are absolutely demolishing baitfish in the shallows. Matinecock Point has been a hotbed, with schools of butterfish getting hammered in 15 to 25 feet of water. Small metal jigs — 1 to 2-ounce Hopkins and Ava diamonds — are all you need. Cast into the melee and hang on.

The fluke bite has been more challenging, but there are fish to be had if you know where to look. The grass beds between Oyster Bay and Lloyd's Neck are holding some decent fish, though you'll sort through plenty of shorts to find a keeper. The productive pattern has been 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets, worked slow on the drift. The fish are relating to the bottom structure, so you need to stay in contact. I'm seeing the best action on the incoming tide when the current pushes bait into the shallows.

Porgies have made their presence known around the rocky structure near Cold Spring Harbor. Standard porgy rigs baited with clam are producing steady action, with fish running 1 to 2 pounds. It's not glamorous fishing, but it's reliable, and these fish make excellent table fare.

The bait situation is what's really driving everything right now. Peanut bunker are thick in the harbors and bays, which is unusual for this time of year — normally we see adult bunker spawning in June, not the juveniles. But the fish don't care about our expectations. Spearing are also abundant along the shorelines, particularly around dock structures and marina areas.

Looking at the bigger picture, we're entering that sweet spot of summer fishing where multiple species are active simultaneously. The new moon on Friday is going to create some serious tidal movement, and I expect that to fire up both the bass trolling and the bluefish blitzing. The key is being flexible — if the trolling slows down, switch to casting metals for the blues. If the blues move off, try drifting for fluke.

One thing I'm keeping an eye on is the water temperature gradient between the Sound and the back bays. The harbors are running 4 to 5 degrees warmer than the main body of water, and that differential is creating some interesting fishing opportunities. Bass are using these cooler pockets as staging areas during the heat of the day, then moving shallow to feed at dawn and dusk.

For the week ahead, I'm watching the weather pattern closely. We've got stable conditions forecast, which should keep the fishing consistent. The full moon cycle is approaching, and that typically means big tides and active fish. I'd focus on the major structure — Execution Rocks, Stepping Stones, the Throgs Neck Bridge pilings — during the peak tidal flow periods. Early morning and late afternoon will be your best bets for surface activity.

The summer pattern is officially here, folks. Water's warm, bait's abundant, and the fish are feeding aggressively. This is what we wait all year for in the Western Sound.
