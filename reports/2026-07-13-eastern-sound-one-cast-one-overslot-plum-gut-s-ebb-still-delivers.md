# One Cast, One Overslot: Plum Gut's Ebb Still Delivers
*Cooler eastern water is holding big bass tight to the current seams while the west side of the Sound cooks — here's where to find them.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 13** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

3:40 a.m., chest-deep on the rocks below Plum Gut, current screaming through at what felt like five knots dead against the moon. First cast with a 2-ounce Danny plug, swing it across the seam where the white water folds into the slick — one thump, then the rod loads over and doesn't stop. Forty inches, high thirties on the scale I didn't have with me, and it came off the third rock out from the point where the rip pinches down hardest. That's the fish that tells you everything you need to know about this week: the big girls are still stacked on the structure, they're just not going to make it easy.

Here's the deal on temperature right now — it's the whole story for the Eastern Sound. New London's reading in the mid-60s, while Kings Point on the west side is pushing low 70s. That gradient is real and it's working in our favor. Cooler, oxygen-rich water moving hard through The Race and Plum Gut is exactly what big migratory bass want in July — they don't like warm, sluggish water, and they sure don't like getting pushed shallow when it's 70-plus. So while the western Sound bass are getting comfortable in the back bays, our fish are staying tight to current edges, rips, and the deep gut itself, feeding hard on the tide changes and laying up in the eddies in between.

The pattern that's producing: fish the last two hours of the ebb through Plum Gut and The Race, working bucktails — 1.5 to 2 ounces, bare or dressed with a white or chartreuse trailer — cast across the seam and let it sink clean off the edge before you start a slow, deliberate bottom retrieve. Don't rush it. These fish are ambushing bait that's getting swept off the ledge, not chasing anything down. At night I'm still going heavier and louder — Danny plugs, big pencils, and swimmers worked across the boil lines off Fishers Island and the Orient Point rip. Two solid slot fish and that overslot came off the rocks this week, all within the same two-hour window on the back half of the ebb. Daytime has been tougher and more technical — the fish are there, but you're working for one good bite instead of three.

Bluefish have been the reliable action when the bass go quiet. Jumbo class — eight to twelve pounds, some bigger — are chopping through bunker pods off Orient Point and along the north side of Fishers, especially on the last of the incoming when bait gets pushed up against the beach. Poppers and 3-ounce Hopkins spoons worked fast through the boils have been drawing vicious strikes. If you see the surface nervous and gulls working low, don't wait — that window can close in twenty minutes.

Fluke fishing has been a grind but productive if you put in the drift time. Fishers Island Sound, the sand and gravel patches south of the ferry channel, 20 to 35 feet, drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of squid. Nothing huge — most fish going 16 to 19 inches — but enough keepers coming over the rail to make it worth the sea time, especially on the tail end of the outgoing when the current slows enough to keep bottom contact clean.

Porgies are thick around the rock piles off Plum Gut and the reef structure south of Fishers. High-low rigs with bloodworms or clam strips, fished on the bottom during slack water when the current backs off enough to hold position. This has been the most consistent bite of the week — not glamorous, but if you want fish in the cooler with minimal drama, this is it.

Weakfish are still around but thin — a few keeper-class fish mixed in with the porgy catches on bait, nothing worth targeting specifically. If the water stays on the cooler side through the rest of July, I wouldn't be surprised to see a better push of them show up, but I'm not betting the trip on it.

Looking ahead — if this gradient holds, with the west side running warm and the east staying cool off New London and through the Gut, I expect the pattern to keep producing right through the next lunar push. The rips are the whole game right now. If I've got one shot this week, I'm walking the rocks below Plum Gut on the last two hours of the ebb, throwing a bucktail deep into the seam before first light, and I'm not leaving until the current goes slack. That's where the big fish are living, and that first cast at 3:40 a.m. proved it's not a fluke pattern — it's the program.
