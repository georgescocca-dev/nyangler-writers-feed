# New moon springs push Rockaway Inlet fluke and set up a sunset bass window
*Big tides are flushing warm bay water out the inlet, dragging bait — and the fluke and stripers that eat it — right to the mouth.*

**BROAD CHANNEL, NY — July 10** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

We're sitting on a new moon right now, and if you've fished the bay this week you already felt it — the tide's got some shoulder to it. Spring tides build fast around a new moon, and Jamaica Bay doesn't mess around when the exchange gets big. I watched the water rip past the Motts Basin markers on Tuesday's outgoing like it had somewhere to be. That kind of current does two things for us this time of year: it flushes the hot, stagnant bay water out through Rockaway Inlet, and it drags every peanut bunker, spearing, and grass shrimp along with it. The bay itself has been running warm — properly warm, upper 70s in the back channels — which is fine for bunker but starts to push bigger fluke and stripers toward better-oxygenated water. That's exactly what's been happening at the inlet and out along the ocean side of Breezy Point, where upwelling has been holding the ocean a good six, seven degrees cooler than the bay. Fish don't love soup water in July, so they're voting with their fins.

Looking ahead, the new moon springs peak this weekend, and the PM flood is lining up with sunset Friday through Monday — that's the window I'd circle. When a big incoming tide crests right as the light goes flat, bass that have been sulking deep all day slide up onto the flats and rips to feed before full dark. It's the first real evening window we've had since the June full moon, and with this much water moving, the bait's going to be pushed hard into places like the Marine Parkway Bridge pilings and the Breezer's Rip off Fort Tilden. Wind's been mild out of the southwest, nothing to complain about, and if it stays that way through the weekend, conditions line up about as well as they're going to.

Fluke have been the more reliable story the last couple weeks, and the story's about location, not numbers. The deep holes in the bay — Bergen Basin, the channel off Ruffle Bar — have gone quiet as that water's warmed up too much for a fluke's taste. The better fish have shifted toward Rockaway Inlet itself, working the channel edges off the Coast Guard station and out past the Rockaway rocks, plus the deeper cuts along the ocean side where that cooler water's pushing in. Outgoing tide is the move — drift white or pink 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails right along the channel drop, 15 to 25 feet, and don't be shy about bouncing bottom hard through the top of the drift before it flattens. A guy I fish with put a 6-pounder in the box off the inlet mouth last week doing exactly that, bucktail tipped with a strip of squid for insurance. It's not every drift — you might make six passes and only get bit on two — but when the tide's ripping through that inlet on the outgoing, that's where I'd spend my afternoon.

Bluefish have been the wildcard, and honestly the more fun fish right now. Schoolie to mid-size blues have been showing in surface-churning pods off Breezy Point and the Rockaway Beach jetties on the last two hours of the incoming, chasing peanut bunker that are getting pushed out of the bay by all this tidal movement. Poppers and metal — Ava 27s, deadly dicks — worked fast on top will get you into them. Nothing citation-sized, but it's steady, and it's a good bet if the bass don't cooperate.

Stripers themselves have been more spread out than showing up in numbers. The Marine Parkway Bridge is still the most consistent spot in the zone — bass staging on the outgoing in that current seam under the span, holding 10 to 20 feet down. Live bunker on a fishfinder rig, or bucktails bounced right off the bottom structure, have been the ticket, mostly at first light and again right at dusk. Daytime's been slow with this bright sun and warm water pushing fish deep and lockjawed. If you're grinding the middle of the day expecting a repeat of spring, you're going to be disappointed — I've had afternoons out there with nothing to show for three hours of drifting.

Porgies are doing their reliable summer thing at Canarsie Pier and around the Cross Bay Bridge pilings — small hooks, sandworm or clam, bottom rigs, nothing fancy, and they're biting on both tides, which makes them a solid backup plan for anyone bringing kids or just wanting bent rods without the guesswork. Weakfish, I'll be straight with you — quiet. Water's warm enough, has been for weeks, but nobody I know has connected on a weakfish in the bay this season worth mentioning. Warm water alone isn't doing it; there's something else missing, and until that changes I wouldn't plan a trip around them.

My plan for the weekend: fish the Friday and Saturday PM flood at Marine Parkway Bridge for bass as the light drops, with the inlet fluke drift as my daytime move on the outgoing. If the bass don't show at the bridge, I've got Breezy Point blues as the fallback — that bite's been dependable enough to save an evening. Big tides, warm bay, cooler ocean edge — that's the setup this week, and it's worth building your trip around the tide chart before you build it around the calendar.
