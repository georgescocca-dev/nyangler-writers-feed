# Ran the night shift on Wilmington for bigeye — three bites, two in the boat, and a plan to do it again
*Stable high pressure and a warming eddy edge turned the chunk bite on after dark, while daytime trolling picked at yellowfin along the break.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 20** — _by Capt. Dana Cooper, Zone Writer_

I made the call Wednesday to skip the troll spread and run straight to the chunk grounds after dark, and it paid for the fuel plus a little extra. We'd been picking at yellowfin trolling the temp break most of the week — decent fish, but the numbers weren't there to justify burning that much diesel every trip. So when the reports started stacking up on bigeye holding under the chunk slicks overnight, I figured the smarter play was to run out in the afternoon, set up on the northeast edge of the canyon around 200 fathoms, and let the sun go down on us.

Glad we did it. We anchored up on a hard temperature break where the Gulf Stream eddy water is pushing warm and clean against the canyon wall — you could see it in the color change alone, that deep cobalt against the greener inshore water. Butterfish chunks on a slow, steady flow, no big globs, just a consistent trickle to keep the slick going without feeding them full. First bite came about ninety minutes after dark, second one an hour after that. Lost one at the boat on a hook pull, but we put two bigeye in the box in the 140 to 160-pound range. That's a good night by any measure, and it confirms what we were hearing — the bigeye have been sitting under those slicks consistently this week, not just a one-off.

Daytime yellowfin trolling has been more of a grind. We're still picking them off the same water — the temp break running along the 100-fathom line on the west side of Wilmington, where the eddy has set up a nice defined edge. Spreads with green machines and small Ilanders in the 6 to 8-inch range, worked well off the shotgun and long rigger positions, have been drawing strikes, but you're working for every fish. Two or three keeper yellowfin on a six-hour troll is about average right now — not bad, not the kind of numbers where you stop counting. If you want volume, the chunk bite after dark on bigeye has been the better use of your time and fuel this week.

Wahoo have shown up sporadically on the high-speed gear when we've run wire off the corners of the canyon, but nothing consistent enough to build a whole trip around. Mahi are scattered around any floating weed or debris — we picked up a pair of nice bulls, mid-teens, off a weed line about six miles short of the canyon on the way out. Worth a five-minute detour if you see color or grass, but don't expect a stacked-up school this time of year here. It's opportunistic fishing, not a target.

White marlin have been mostly a daytime troll story, and the numbers are building the way they typically do this time of July. We had two flag-raisers on naked ballyhoo skipping off the left long and short riggers — one pitch bait connection, one that just wouldn't commit. Nothing to write home about yet, but it lines up with what we expected heading into late July, and it's the kind of thing where patience pays. We flagged this a week ago — said the billfish migration would keep building through the month — and that's exactly the direction it's trending. Not a banner week, but more shots than we had two weeks back.

Water's been stable, which is the biggest factor in all of this. High pressure has held for going on ten days now, and that's let the eddy structure mature instead of getting torn apart by wind and swell. The break we've been working sits roughly along 38°25'N, 74°10'W give or take a mile depending on the day, right where the canyon wall drops from 100 to 300 fathoms fast. That kind of bottom contour paired with a stable temperature edge is exactly the setup that holds bait, and holding bait holds tuna.

Bluefin have stayed in the mix too, mostly smaller class fish inside the recreational slot, caught incidentally while working the yellowfin spread or on the troll transition running out to the canyon. Nothing worth a dedicated trip on its own right now, but a nice bonus if you connect.

Looking ahead, I don't see a reason for this to fall apart in the next several days. The high pressure pattern isn't showing signs of breaking, and that eddy edge has had time to organize instead of getting pushed around. If it holds through the weekend, I'd keep running the same play — troll the break during daylight for yellowfin and any wahoo that want to show up, then set up on the chunk after dark for bigeye. If the wind swings and starts mixing that break apart, I'd expect the yellowfin bite to get patchy fast, and I'd lean even harder into the night chunk since bigeye tend to hold deeper and care less about surface chop. Either way, I'm planning on running back out this week — the ratio of fuel burned to fish in the box has finally tilted in our favor, and I want to see if it holds.
