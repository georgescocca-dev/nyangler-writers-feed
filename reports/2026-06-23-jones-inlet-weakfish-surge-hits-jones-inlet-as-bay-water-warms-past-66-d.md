# Weakfish surge hits Jones Inlet as bay water warms past 66 degrees
*Trophy weakies crashing bunker schools while fluke action heats up on the Meadowbrook drift.*

**JONES INLET, NY — June 23** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The weakfish are back, and they're making a statement. After years of hoping and waiting, we're seeing legitimate tide runners pushing into Jones Inlet and the back bays as water temps climb past the magic 66-degree mark. I'm talking real weakfish here — not the scattered schoolies we've been picking at, but honest 5- to 7-pound fish that'll make your drag sing.

The action's been best on the incoming tide around the inlet mouth, especially that first hour when the flood really starts moving. Fresh bunker chunks on fishfinder rigs are doing the damage, but here's the kicker — these fish are also hitting 9-inch soft plastic sand eels worked slow along the bottom. The key is getting that bait right in the wash where the current breaks over the bar. I've been anchoring just inside the inlet on the north side, casting out toward the channel edge where the water drops from 8 to 15 feet.

What's driving this bite is the temperature gradient between the bay and ocean water. Bay temps are running a solid 4 degrees warmer than the ocean side, and that thermal barrier is stacking bait on every flood tide. The weakies are following the bunker schools right up into the shallows, and when they feed, they feed hard.

The fluke fishing has been solid too, especially around the Meadowbrook Bridge area where boats have been packing in on the southeast side. The drift's been perfect with this southwest flow, and guys are picking steady action on white bucktails tipped with squid strips or fresh smelt. Most fish are running 15 to 17 inches, but there's been enough keepers mixed in to make it worthwhile. The trick is keeping your rig moving — these fish want action, not a dead stick on the bottom.

Out at Jones Beach, the surf's been producing stripers on the evening bite. Diamond jigs with green tails are the ticket, worked right in the wash as the waves break over the sandbar. The fish are sitting shallow, right on that drop-off before the trough, and they're keyed on sand eels. Low light is critical — I'm talking that last hour before sunset when the water starts to calm.

The bunker schools are thick right now, and that's what's driving everything. Massive pods of peanuts and half-size bunker are getting pushed around by the tides, and every predator in the system is taking advantage. I've been seeing birds working constantly from the inlet all the way east toward the Robert Moses Bridge.

Looking ahead, we've got a new moon Friday which means spring tides and serious current. That's going to flush even more bait out of the back bays, and I expect the weakfish bite to go off. The inlet's going to be the place to be on the outgoing, especially that evening drain when the water really starts moving. If you've been waiting for the weakies to return to Jones Inlet, this is your week.
