# Fluke bite fires as bay water hits 68 degrees — jumbo blues crash the bunker schools
*Late June heat wave stacks bait in the inlets as summer patterns lock in from Sandy Hook to Cape May.*

**MANASQUAN, NJ — June 24** — _by Joey Napolitano, Zone Writer_

The water finally woke up this week, and so did the fish. Bay temps spiked to 68 degrees while the ocean side is holding steady at 64 — that 4-degree gradient is pure gold for anyone who knows how to read it. The thermocline set up perfect at 30 feet, and every inlet from Manasquan to Barnegat is stacking bait like a cafeteria line.

Fluke fishing exploded overnight. I'm talking keeper-class fish, not the dinks we've been picking at all month. The Dream Boat Challenge weigh-ins tell the story — 8.95-pound doormat came out of Jersey waters this week, and that's no fluke. The fish are following the sand eels, and those baitfish are concentrated where the temperature breaks are sharpest. One day you're on them, next day they've moved two miles down the beach. That's summer fluke fishing — you chase the bait, not the structure.

Best action is coming on 3/8-ounce jig heads with white Gulp Swimming Mullets, dragged slow on the drift. The key is staying in that 25-to-35-foot zone where the cooler bay water meets the warmer ocean push. Outgoing tide at dawn has been money — that's when the gradient is strongest and the bait gets flushed out of the back bays right into the waiting mouths.

Raritan Bay is absolutely loaded with stripers right now. The bunker schools are so thick you can smell them from shore, and everything with teeth is on them. Forty-inch-plus bass are common, and the bluefin are mixing in too. I'm not kidding — inshore bluefin in Raritan Bay in June. Find the bunker balls and you'll find the circus. Live peanuts on a fishfinder rig, or if you want to have some fun, throw metals at the breaking fish.

The bluefish showed up in force this week, especially along the Monmouth County beaches. These aren't the snappers — we're talking 5-to-7-pound choppers that'll test your drag and your wire leader. They're hitting everything from spoons to poppers, but the real action is on the jigs. One-and-a-half-ounce albie jigs in white or chartreuse, worked fast through the schools.

Manasquan Inlet has been spectacular for squid — yeah, squid in June. The cooler water we had earlier this spring pushed them inshore, and they're still hanging around the mid-range depths. Azori squid jigs are the ticket, and you're getting both bait-sized squid and jumbo calamari. The headboats are running dedicated squid trips, which tells you everything about how good this bite is.

Sea bass season is winding down — we go from 10-fish limits to 1-fish limits starting July 1, so get out there now if you want to fill the freezer. The Axel Carlson reef area has been producing limits of 14-inch-plus fish on clams and jigs. Drop and reel when they're stacked in the water column, but switch to bottom bouncing when they're tight to structure.

Looking ahead, this full moon Friday means spring tides and big water movement. That's going to flush even more bait out of the back bays and fire up the inlet fishing. The weakfish should start showing in the evening outgoing tides — they always follow the big moon phases. Water temps are climbing toward 70, which means the summer patterns are locking in hard. The fluke bite should only get better as more fish push out of the bays toward their offshore summer haunts.

The gradient is everything right now. Find where the bay water meets the ocean water, find where the bait is stacked, and you'll find the fish. It's that simple and that complicated. This is Jersey fishing at its finest — when the water warms up and the bait moves, everything else follows.
