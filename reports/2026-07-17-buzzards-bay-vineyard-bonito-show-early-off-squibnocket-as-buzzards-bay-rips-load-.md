# First bonito scouts reported off Squibnocket as Buzzards Bay rips load up with bass and blues
*The albies are still weeks away, but the bait's stacking in the holes and the first scattered bonito reports of the season are coming off the Vineyard's south shore.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — July 17** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Mid-July on this stretch of water is a waiting game, and this week the water gave us a hint of what's coming. We've had a stretch of settled southwest wind — nothing gale-force, just a steady 10 to 15 that's kept Vineyard Sound and the Elizabeth Islands chain from getting stirred up. That matters because clean water in the rips means the bait holds tight instead of getting scattered, and right now the bait is thick — peanut bunker pushing through Quick's Hole on the last of the ebb, silversides balling up along the Menemsha jetty at first light. The moon's working toward the new phase later this month, and as those tides build toward bigger swings, you're going to see more current volume ripping through Robinson's Hole and the Hole itself at Woods Hole — that's when the predators really start using the seams instead of just cruising them.

This week, with tides still moderate heading into the new moon build, the pattern has been about fishing the transition — the last two hours of the ebb and the first hour of the flood, when the current is strong enough to hold bait against structure but not so ripping that you can't get a lure down into the strike zone. That's exactly where the fish have been eating.

Striped bass have been the bread and butter all week, and they're sitting where you'd expect them to in mid-July — deep in the current seams off Cuttyhunk's western end, along the rocks at Tarpaulin Cove, and working the drop-off on the north side of Nashawena. Guys drifting bucktails — 1.5 to 2-ounce, white or olive, tipped with a strip of squid — through the deeper cuts on the outgoing tide have been connecting with slot fish in the high 20s to low 30s, with the occasional overslot mixed in around dawn. The retrieve that's working is Skinner's old standby: cast across the seam, let it sink dead to the bottom, then work it back slow with a lift-drop cadence that mimics a stunned baitfish rolling out of the current. Live eels fished on the drift through the Hole at night have also been productive for the bigger fish — that's still the most consistent way to find a 40-incher in this stretch, and July nights with a slack high around midnight have been the sweet spot.

Bluefish have been aggressive and reliable, which is typical for this time of year — schools ripping through Quick's Hole and the rip off West Chop chewing through anything silver. Poppers and metal — Deadly Dicks, Kastmasters in the 1-ounce range — worked fast on top during the first hour of daylight have been drawing consistent strikes. Nothing subtle about it: if you see the surface nervous with bait getting pushed, that's a blitz window, and it doesn't last long, usually 20 minutes before the school moves off with the current.

Fluke fishing has been steady but not spectacular — the kind of bite where you have to put in drift time to find keeper numbers. Buzzards Bay proper, especially the flats off Wings Neck and the deeper holes inside Hadley Harbor, have given up fluke to 5 pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets rigged on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom on the last of the incoming when the water's had a chance to warm through the afternoon sun. Scup have been thick around the Woods Hole draw bridge pilings and off the Cuttyhunk town dock — small stuff mostly, but if you want a bent rod for the kids, squid strips on a high-low rig in 15 to 20 feet has been about as close to a sure thing as this zone offers right now.

Now for the bonito — and this is the real story of the week. We've had scattered reports of the first bonito showing off Squibnocket and working the rip lines south of Menemsha on the tail end of the ebb, chasing sand eels that have been thick in that current for the last ten days. It's early, and it's not consistent yet — one solid run doesn't mean the school's parked, and I'd treat this as the opening notice rather than the invitation. If you want to chase them, small metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Deadly Dicks or Hogy epoxy jigs — cast into the rip and retrieved fast has been the ticket for the handful of fish that have come to hand. No albies yet, and I wouldn't expect them for another several weeks — that run typically doesn't fire up in earnest until the water starts easing off its summer peak in late August into September, when the bait gets pushed hard by the first cool nights. But the sand eel showing this early, combined with the bonito arriving ahead of schedule, tells me the bait cycle is running a little quicker than average this year. Worth filing away.

Looking ahead, as we build toward the new moon, expect the tidal current in Robinson's Hole and the Woods Hole passage to strengthen noticeably — bigger swings mean more water moving, which concentrates bait tighter against the ledges and makes those rips fishable for a longer window each tide, not just the peak hour. If the southwest wind holds and doesn't turn onshore and chop up the south side of the Vineyard, I'd spend my time working the bonito bite off Squibnocket on the dropping tide, with a backup plan of running to Cuttyhunk for bass if that scouting trip comes up empty. Either way, keep an eye on the bait — right now it's telling you more than any forecast will.
