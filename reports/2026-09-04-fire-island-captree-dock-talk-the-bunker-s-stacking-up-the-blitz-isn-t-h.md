# Captree dock talk: the bunker's stacking up, the blitz isn't here yet
*Fluke are sliding toward the ocean side, blues are chewing on the bait schools, and the bass are staged and patient — for now.*

**CAPTREE, NY — September 4** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Stood at the fuel dock Tuesday morning listening to a mate off one of the party boats tell anybody who'd listen that he'd never seen so much bunker sitting in the bay this early without stripers tearing through it. That's the whole story of this week in one sentence. The bait is here — thick pods of bunker rolling off Nicoll Bay, more pushing in through the inlet on the last of the flood — and the bass know it. They're just not committing yet. Water's still holding in that mid-70s range, warm enough to keep fish lazy and scattered rather than balled up and blitzing. That changes fast once the nights start cooling off, and with the moon building toward full on the 6th and 7th, the tide swings are already getting stronger. We flagged the bunker build last week as the trigger to watch for the first real fall blitz, and it's still building — the pieces are lining up, they just haven't clicked yet. My money says within the next week to ten days somebody's phone lights up with a report out of the bay or right off the beach that makes everybody drop what they're doing.

In the meantime, the fishing hasn't been bad, it's just been a patience game. Fluke are doing exactly what they do this time of year — thinning out of the back bay and the inlet and sliding toward the ocean-side structure ahead of the fall run. The Robert Moses bridges are still giving up keeper fish, but the numbers are down from August. Best drift has been 20 to 30 feet on the outgoing, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails tight to the pilings and the rip lines where the current stacks bait against structure. If you want a shot at a bigger fish, work the deeper edge off Democrat Point where the inlet dumps into the ocean — that's the kind of structure the bigger doormats are staging on before they push out. Chunk shedder crab or squid strips on the bucktail if the Gulp isn't getting bit.

Striped bass are the story everybody's watching. They're in the bay, staged on the bunker rather than actively chasing it, which means the bite is there but it's not easy — you have to be in the right spot at the right moment. Dawn and dusk have been the windows, especially the last two hours of the incoming at Democrat Point and along the Fire Island Inlet seam where the current pushes bait against the rocks. Snag a live bunker off the surface schools and free-line it back into the current, or chunk fresh bunker on a fishfinder rig and let it sit on bottom in 15 to 25 feet. Patience pays — a lot of guys are marking bait all day and getting one or two real windows where the bass actually turn on. Nobody's complaining about the size of what's coming over the rail, just the consistency.

Bluefish have been the most dependable thing going, and that tracks with what's happening basin-wide right now — warm water's kept them aggressive everywhere from Montauk down through here. They're showing up wherever the bunker schools are thick, which this week has meant the inlet mouth and the stretch just outside Democrat Point. Diamond jigs worked with a fast retrieve or bunker spoons trolled behind the boat have been getting bit hard. If you're marking bunker on the finder and seeing birds working, don't overthink it — cast into the edge of the school, not the middle, and let the jig flutter down before you start working it.

Porgies have quietly been solid around the Robert Moses bridge pilings and the deeper holes off Captree, 15 to 20 feet, high-low rigs with bloodworms or clam strips. It's not glamorous fishing but it's been steady, and it's a good option on a day when the wind's up and the inlet's got some chop to it — which it has, honestly, more days than not this week. The ocean buoy off the coast has been reading close to 4-foot seas on a short period, choppy enough that the open beach hasn't been fishing well and the surf guys have mostly stayed home or worked the calmer stretches inside the inlet. Weakfish are turning up as incidental catches in the bay channels around dusk on bucktails and soft plastics — nothing to build a trip around, but worth noting if you're working the same water for bass and get a surprise.

Looking ahead, the wind and the moon are the two things to watch. If the wind lays down through the weekend, that's your window to be on the water for the bass — work the incoming at Democrat Point early, then slide into the bridges for fluke as the tide turns. If a front comes through and knocks the overnight temps down even a few degrees, don't be surprised if the bunker schools tighten up fast and the first real blitz breaks loose somewhere along this stretch. Keep the rods rigged for both scenarios — a bucktail for structure fish, a snagging outfit for the bait schools — because when this bay decides to turn on, it doesn't give you much warning.
