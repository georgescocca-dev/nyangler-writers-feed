# New moon springs load up Plum Gut as porgies stack thick around Shelter Island
*Warm bay water is pushing fluke into the channels and cuts while bigger tides sharpen the bass bite at first light.*

**GREENPORT, NY — July 10** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

Last week the Peconic did what it does every July — it got warm fast and quiet, with light southerly wind most days and barely a ripple on the bay proper by mid-morning. That kind of calm is good for the eyes but it lets the sun cook the shallows, and by Wednesday the flats off Southold and the upper bay near Riverhead were pushing into the mid-to-upper 70s. That's too warm for fluke to sit comfortably, and true to form they slid out of the sand flats and into the channels — Jockey Creek, the mouth of Greenport Harbor, the deeper cuts around Shelter Island — chasing the cooler, moving water. We're seeing the same pattern that's been reported bay-wide this summer: warm surface water shoves fluke off the flats and into anything with current and depth. In our case that means the North Race, the Gut, and the deep water off Cedar Point.

This week the moon is the story. We're rolling into a new moon with peak spring tide alignment hitting July 11-13 — bigger swings, faster water, stronger push through Plum Gut and the Shelter Island cuts. That's not a minor detail out here. Plum Gut and Orient Point live and die on current, and when the tide really moves, the bass and blues stack up in the eddies behind the rips instead of scattering. I'd plan around the moving tide, not fight it — mornings on the last of the outgoing at the Gut, then shift to Shelter Island structure as the tide slacks and porgies settle back onto the bottom to feed. Water's clean out there right now, no runoff issues, which is one less thing to worry about.

Porgy fishing is doing exactly what it should this time of year — this is peak season and Shelter Island is earning its reputation again. Working the drop-offs around Big Ram, Little Ram, and the rock piles off Cedar Point in 15 to 25 feet, high-low rigs baited with sandworms or clam are filling coolers on the last two hours of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing. Porgies aren't picky right now but they do want you anchored tight to structure — drift too far off the rocks and you're just soaking bait over sand. If you want bigger fish, fish deeper water off Little Ram in the 20-25 foot range early, before the boat traffic picks up.

Fluke has been a mixed bag but productive if you go where the water's moving. The channel edges at Jockey Creek and the deeper stretch running out of Greenport Harbor have given up keepers to 4 and 5 pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets fished on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the outgoing. Shelter Island's North Ferry cut has also been worth a drift — that current funnels bait through a tight lane and the fluke sit right on the bottom waiting for it. Nothing has been easy — you're covering ground and picking through short fish to find keepers — but the channel bite has held up better than the open bay, which has gone quiet as the flats warmed.

Striped bass are still very much a Plum Gut and Orient Point story. The early morning bite — first light through about two hours after sunrise — has been the most reliable window, with schoolies to low-20-inch fish and the occasional better bass mixed in, taken on diamond jigs worked through the rip and bucktails bounced along bottom where the Gut narrows. Live eels after dark have also produced around Orient Point structure for guys willing to fish the tide change at night, which given the new moon's stronger current should be worth the effort this week — moving water at night tends to wake up the bigger fish that ignore daytime traffic. Gardiners Bay has scattered bass too, mostly smaller fish chasing bait pods, but nothing concentrated enough yet to call a pattern.

Bluefish have shown up as snapper blues in the harbors — Three Mile Harbor and Sag Harbor both have kids catching them off the docks on small metal spoons — and there are cocktail-sized blues mixed in with the bass activity out toward Gardiners Bay, chopping bait on the surface in the early morning calm. Weakfish, I'll be honest, remain a rumor out here. Water's warm enough on paper but I haven't heard of a legitimate weakfish catch in the bay in weeks, and I'm not going to tell you to go looking for a fish that isn't showing. Blackfish season's closed right now anyway, so that's off the table until fall.

Looking ahead, the spring tide peak this weekend should sharpen the Plum Gut bass bite — faster water means more bait getting pushed through the funnel, and bass know it. I'd fish the last two hours of the outgoing Saturday and Sunday morning and watch for diving birds working the rip. If the current gets too strong to fish comfortably, Shelter Island's porgies are the fallback — that bite doesn't care what the moon's doing as long as you're anchored on structure. And keep half an eye toward Orient — with the warm plume pushing east along the south shore this month, it wouldn't shock me to hear the first false albacore or bonito reports trickle into Gardiners Bay before the month's out. Nothing confirmed yet, but the water's right for it.
