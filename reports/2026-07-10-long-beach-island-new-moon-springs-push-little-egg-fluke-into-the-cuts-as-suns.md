# New moon springs push Little Egg fluke into the cuts as sunset stripers return to Barnegat Light
*Bay water cooked into the high 70s and shoved the fluke out — the ocean side and the inlets are holding the action while the weakfish stay a no-show.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — July 10** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what it does every July when we get a stretch of sun and slack air — it cooked. Great Bay's shallow flats and the back stretches up toward the Mullica pushed into the high 70s, and that shut the door on the grass-flat fluke bite fast. I had two guys tell me they marked bait and structure all over the meadows off Graveling Point and couldn't buy a bite past nine in the morning. That's not bad luck, that's biology — fluke don't like soup, and once the shallow water gets that warm they slide for the channels, the deeper cuts, and eventually the inlets where there's still some exchange with the ocean. We had a couple NE wind days midweek that helped push cooler water — I'm talking mid-70s pushing into the low 70s — through Barnegat and Little Egg inlets, and that's exactly where the better fluke have been sitting since.

This week the moon flips new on the 10th, which means spring tides are building right now and peak through Saturday into Monday. Bigger swings at Barnegat Light and Little Egg — more water moving means more current in the cuts, which means bait gets flushed and predators get organized around it. That also reopens a PM window we haven't had since the June full moon: the flood tide stacking up right around sunset. Low light, moving water, and warm surf temps — that's the recipe that's been missing on the striper side for weeks, and it's back starting tonight through Monday.

Fluke-wise, the shift to the inlet and ocean side is the story right now. Little Egg Inlet on the outgoing, working the drop-off just inside the throat in 14 to 18 feet, has been the most consistent stretch — 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along bottom, picking up keepers in the 19 to 22 inch range with the occasional better fish mixed in. Same story off the Beach Haven side channels and the deeper bends up toward Ship Bottom — anywhere there's current scouring a defined edge. Bay flats are mostly done for now unless you're fishing early, first light, before the sun gets on the water. Don't waste a midday tide on the meadows this week — go to the moving water.

Stripers have been a grind through late June, mostly schoolie-sized fish scattered thin, but the pattern for this weekend into Monday is legitimate. With the spring tide flood lining up against sunset, I'd be at Barnegat Light working the rocks on the last hour of light into dark — white bucktails, needlefish, or a darter worked slow across the seam where the jetty current meets the open water. Holgate's north end rip has been holding fish too, mostly on the last two hours of the incoming, with some bigger fish mixed into the schoolies — nothing giant, but 28 to 34 inch fish have shown up enough that it's worth the walk. If the wind stays light through the weekend, that PM flood-into-dark window at both spots is where I'd put my time. If it kicks up out of the east and blows the surf out, I'd shift to Little Egg on the last of the outgoing instead — dirtier water there tends to concentrate fish tight to structure rather than scatter them.

Bluefish have been riding shotgun with the stripers, chopping mullet in the wash at Holgate and off the Ship Bottom beaches on calm mornings — nothing huge, mostly 2 to 4 pounders, but they'll hit the same bucktails and metal you're throwing for bass, so bring a wire leader or expect to lose gear.

Kingfish are doing their steady, unglamorous thing in the surf at Holgate and the south end beaches — bloodworm and Fishbites on a high-low rig, small sinkers, fished tight to the wash on the bottom stages of the tide. Not exciting, but it's been dependable, and it's a good one to hand a kid a rod for while you work a plug rod on the same beach.

Black drum are basically done for the season up in the Mullica and around Graveling Point — that bite peaked back in May and by now it's stragglers only, so I wouldn't plan a trip around them.

And I'll say this plainly because I think honesty matters more than hype: weakfish have been a ghost. Water temps in the Mullica and Great Bay have been in the range where weakfish are supposed to show, and they just haven't. I've heard it from enough people now that I don't think it's a fluke of reporting — the temperature threshold that used to be a reliable trigger isn't producing fish the way it used to. If you want to try, dusk on soft plastics — 4-inch pearl or chartreuse paddletails — around the deeper holes off Graveling Point is still the right idea. Just don't build your week around it.

Looking ahead: the spring tide peak Saturday through Monday is the best striper window we've had since the June full moon, and if the wind cooperates I'd fish the sunset flood at Barnegat Light first, Holgate second. Fluke will keep favoring the inlet and ocean-side channels as long as the bay stays warm, so work the moving water on the outgoing and don't bother with the flats past mid-morning. If the bass don't show at the jetty, my backup plan is always the same this time of year — Little Egg on the last of the outgoing with a bucktail, because something's usually willing to eat there even on an off night.
