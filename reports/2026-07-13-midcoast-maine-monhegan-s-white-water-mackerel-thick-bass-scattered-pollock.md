# Monhegan's white water: mackerel thick, bass scattered, pollock still holding deep off the ledges
*A mid-July mixed bag from Boothbay to Isle au Haut — mackerel everywhere, stripers making you work, and the groundfish still down where they belong.*

**BOOTHBAY HARBOR, ME — July 13** — _by Rusty Pelletier, Zone Writer_

Forty-some years ago my father told me something I still repeat to sternmen who complain the fish aren't where they were last week: "The water don't lie, but it don't hurry either." I was hauling traps off Damariscove at the time, maybe nineteen years old, convinced the cod had vanished off the face of the earth because we hadn't seen one in a month. Three weeks later we ran into a pile of them stacked on a hard bottom patch we'd run over a hundred times and never marked. Same water. Same boat. We just hadn't been patient enough to let the season tell us where to look. I think about that every July, because this time of year Midcoast Maine never gives it up easy — it makes you go find it.

This week that lesson's holding true. The mackerel showed up thick — and I mean thick, the kind of showing where you can see the surface nervous half a mile off Ocean Point and again out past the Cuckolds — but the striped bass have been a different animal, scattered and moody, biting in windows instead of all day long. If you fished the last of the ebb at Damariscotta River mouth or down around the Thread of Life, you probably had a couple decent stripers, mid-20s to low-30s, on a white or chartreuse soft plastic swim shad worked slow along the current seam. But fish the wrong hour and you could soak bait for two hours and see nothing but mackerel stealing your sandworms.

The mackerel bite itself has been the most reliable thing going. Off Squirrel Island, Ocean Point, and out toward The Cuckolds, jigging small silver Swedish Pimples or mackerel trees in 20 to 40 feet is filling coolers fast on the last two hours of the incoming tide, when the bait pushes up against the ledges. Good news for anyone chasing bass too, because live or fresh-dead mackerel fished on a fish-finder rig has been out-producing plugs at spots like the Thread of Life and the mouth of the Sheepscot, where the current runs hard enough that a plug gets pushed around before it ever settles.

Out at Monhegan, the pattern I like this time of year is holding: fish the ebb along the ledges off White Head and down toward Manana, working the edge where the white water breaks off the rock into deeper green. Stripers have been mixed in with pollock right on the surface, chasing bait balls pushed up by the current, and if you've got a light spinning rod rigged with a 1-ounce diamond jig or a Kastmaster, you can work both species without changing tackle. The pollock have run smaller than I'd like — schoolies mostly, 16 to 20 inches — but there's enough of them to keep a rod bent while you wait for something with more shoulders to show.

Eastern Egg Rock has had bait balls stacked up tight most mornings, which means the puffins are working and so are the bass underneath them — but you've got to be there early, right around first light on the last of the flood, before the wind builds and pushes the bait down. By nine or ten the birds thin out and so does the bite. I had one morning out there last week where three casts with a Doc's Sand Eel got three fish, all in the 20-inch class, then nothing for the next ninety minutes. That's July for you — short windows, good if you time them, frustrating if you don't.

Further out into Penobscot Bay, the groundfish are exactly where they should be this time of year — deep, and staying that way with this warm surface water pushing them down. The channel between Vinalhaven and North Haven, and the deeper humps out toward Isle au Haut Bay, have been holding cod and a few haddock in 90 to 120 feet. Jigging diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid, or working a bottom rig with clam, has produced steady but not spectacular results — a few keeper cod in the 24 to 28-inch range mixed with a lot of shorts you've got to sort through. It's not the kind of bite that fills a fish box in an hour, but if you've got the patience to work the tide change slow, there's fish down there worth the trip.

Bluefish have been scarce so far, just the occasional slammer mixed in with mackerel schools off Boothbay, nothing consistent enough to plan a trip around yet. Deer Isle Thorofare and Merchant's Row have been quieter than I'd expect for mid-July — the current's there, the structure's there, but the bait hasn't concentrated the way it usually does by now. I'd keep an eye on that over the next couple weeks, because when it turns on out there it turns on fast.

Looking ahead, if the wind stays out of the southwest and the water keeps warming the way it has, I expect the bass to tighten up their pattern by the weekend — probably pushing harder into the river mouths on the last two hours of the ebb, chasing that mackerel bait downstream. That's where I'd put my time if I only had one tide to fish. The groundfish out in the bay aren't going anywhere; that's a slow, steady bite you can count on right through August as long as you fish it deep and patient. Monhegan will keep rewarding early risers who fish the ebb off the western ledges. Nothing complicated about any of it — just old water doing what old water does, in its own time.
