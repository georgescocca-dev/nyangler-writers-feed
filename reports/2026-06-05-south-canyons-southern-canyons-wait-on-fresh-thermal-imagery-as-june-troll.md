# Southern canyons wait on fresh thermal imagery as June troll window opens
*With SST products stalled at June 3 and no live buoy feed in hand, canyon strategy hinges on the last known edge positions and the calendar.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — June 5** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

The honest truth about the south canyon grounds this morning is that the picture is partially obscured. The most recent SST package in the catalog is dated June 3, and there has been no June 4 or June 5 imagery pushed through the pipeline yet. Offshore buoy readings did not make it into today's analyst brief either, so anything I tell you about the Spencer-to-Norfolk corridor has to be reasoned from the last clean look at the water rather than a fresh snapshot. That last look, from the May 31 through June 3 sequence, had the warm side of the shelf-slope front sitting in the high 60s to around 70 on the inshore lip of Spencer and Lindenkohl, with the blue water pushing up into the notches as the Gulf Stream meander to the south kept feeding warm filaments north and west. Sea state and wind are not in today's data set, so anyone running tomorrow needs to pull their own NDBC 44009 and 44025 readings before they untie.

What that means for the beat is straightforward. June 5 is right in the front edge of the yellowfin troll window for the southern canyons, and the fish that show up first almost always set up on the 100-fathom curve where the temperature break stacks against structure — the east wall of Spencer, the southwest corner of Lindenkohl, and the deeper fingers off Poor Man's. With the last imagery showing the warm push leaning into the canyon heads rather than washing over the top, I would be running to the 500-fathom line first, working the temperature gradient back inshore until the trolling spread tells you where the bait column is sitting. Bigeye are a dawn-and-dusk proposition this early, and the water has not been warm enough long enough to expect a real wahoo or mahi pick on the high-flyers and lobster pots yet, though a stray gaffer dolphin around any floating debris is not out of the question.

The pattern that was developing through the end of May had the cleanest blue water and the most consistent life pinned to Lindenkohl and the stretch between Lindenkohl and Spencer. Norfolk, further south and east out of Ocean City, was showing the more aggressive thermal contrast on the June 2 and June 3 charts — a tighter break, more chlorophyll edge — but it is a longer run and you want a confirmed report or fresh imagery before you commit to that fuel burn. Poor Man's has been the swing canyon, sometimes lighting up when the closer grounds are flat, and it deserves a look on the way home if you have the daylight.

Looking ahead three to five days, the variables I am watching are the return of SST imagery — once the June 4 and June 5 composites land, the edge positions will either confirm that warm push is holding into the canyon heads or show it has slid back off to the southeast, which would push the bite further offshore and reset the run. The other piece is the moon. We are working away from the full and into the last quarter through the back half of the week, which historically lines up with the first real chunking windows on overnight trips once water hits the low 70s consistently. If the next imagery cycle shows 70-plus on the inshore side of the break at Spencer or Lindenkohl, that is the trigger to start planning overnighters rather than day-troll missions.

I'd treat the next 48 hours as reconnaissance. Run the troll, mark your temperature breaks by hand, log bait shows and any life on the surface, and bring that intel back so the next trip can be fished on real information rather than a three-day-old chart. The canyons are opening up. The data just has not caught up to the calendar yet.
