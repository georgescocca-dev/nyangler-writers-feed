# Halfway Rock on the Slack: Bass Staged on Pogies, Groundfish Holding
*The bay's bait is thickening ahead of the full moon, and the ledges are giving up steady fish to anyone willing to work the tide right.*

**PORTLAND, ME — September 4** — _by Pashmi Cooper, Zone Writer_

Halfway Rock wears its early-September look this week — flat granite standing out of a near-slack tide, gulls working nervous, tight-balled pogie schools off the northeast corner instead of diving hard and committed. That hesitation, more than any single catch, tells you where the bay sits right now. The bait is here and building. The predators know it. But nobody's fully committed to the feed yet, and that's the pattern to understand before you plan your next few days on the water.

Water around the outer islands has held in that comfortable late-summer range, and pogies — menhaden, if you want the textbook name — have been pushing into the bay in thickening pods, staging off Jewell Island, the back side of Peaks, and up toward the mouth of the Presumpscot. Striped bass are stacked on that bait rather than blitzing it, which is exactly what you'd expect with water still carrying summer warmth and the moon still four days from full. We flagged this continuity call a week ago, and it held — the bay's late-summer rhythm carried straight through, with the same ledges fishing the same tide windows they did seven days back. That's useful information: it means the milk run from last week is still the milk run this week.

The moon builds toward full on the 6th and 7th, and that's the mechanism worth watching closely. Bigger tidal swings mean more current push across the ledges, which concentrates bait against structure instead of letting it drift loose across open water. When that happens here, the bass stop shadowing pogie schools passively and start working current seams hard — river mouths on the dropping tide, the down-current side of ledges on the last two hours of ebb. If the pattern holds the way it usually does this time of year, expect the next real uptick in bass aggression to show up right around that full moon push, not before it.

For now, the better bass have been coming off the ledges around Jewell and Cliff Island on live pogies fished under a slip float, or on white and pink soft-plastic swimbaits — 6- to 8-inch paddle tails on a 3/4-ounce jighead — worked slow along the drop-offs on the last hour of the ebb. Fish in the mid-20s to low 30-inch range have been the rule, with a scattering of bigger fish pushed up tight to structure at first light before the sun gets on the water. Bluefish have been mixed into the same schools, which is no surprise given how warm and forage-rich the whole coast has run this summer — expect that to stay strong through Labor Day week, with choppers hitting mackerel-imitation jigs and diamond jigs worked with a fast, erratic retrieve right through the same pogie balls the bass are shadowing.

Groundfish have been the more dependable story of the week, if less dramatic. Halfway Rock continues to hold cod and haddock in 90 to 120 feet on the down-current side, and the bite there has been steady rather than spectacular — diamond jigs and bait-tipped jigs working best on the slack around the tide change, when boats can hold position over structure without fighting a hard drift. Haddock in the 3- to 6-pound range have been coming up consistently on clam-baited hooks fished just off bottom, with the occasional keeper cod mixed in on bigger diamond jigs worked with a slow lift-and-drop. The ledges off Bailey Island and out toward Cape Small have produced similarly — not a stacked bite, but a reliable one for boats willing to fish the tide change patiently rather than drift through it.

Mackerel, which the bay hard back in May, have thinned considerably as a target species — what's left now is mostly forage for the bass and blues rather than a dedicated bite, though small pods still show up on Sabiki rigs around the harbor moorings early in the morning if you need fresh bait for the ledges.

Looking at the next several days, the setup favors patience over aggression. Wind has stayed manageable, which has kept water clarity decent around the outer islands — not gin-clear, but fishable, with enough visibility for the bass to key on bait balls without getting spooked by dirty water. If the forecast holds calm into the weekend, the building tidal push from the approaching full moon should tighten those pogie schools further, and that's when the bass typically stop shadowing and start feeding hard. The window to watch is the two days bracketing the full moon itself — the 6th and 7th — fishing the last two hours of the ebb at river mouths and ledge structure where current concentrates bait.

If there's only one trip in the next few days, start at first light on the down-current side of a ledge like Jewell or the backside of Peaks, working live pogies or large swimbaits through the last of the outgoing water. Keep a jig rod rigged for haddock as a backup if the bass stay quiet — Halfway Rock has been giving up fish reliably enough on the slack that it's a solid fallback rather than a consolation prize. The bay isn't blitzing yet, but the pieces — bait, moon, tide — are lining up the way they usually do right before it does.
