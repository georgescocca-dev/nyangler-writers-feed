# New moon springs load the Race and Plum Gut as fluke push to the rips
*Bigger tides, tighter current lines, and the first bonito chatter off Fishers Island — here's where the bite's stacking this week.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 8** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Last week the Sound got worked over by a stretch of NE wind that upwelled the ocean side of the Race and Plum Gut — cooler, cleaner water shoved in against the warmer stuff sitting in the bays. That's a big deal out here because it sharpened the temperature break right where the current lines set up. I felt it in the water column off Valiant Rock: cold on the bottom, warmer skin water on top, and bait pinned right at that seam. Meanwhile the bay side kept cooking — Great South Bay style temps pushing toward 79 on the flats — which is exactly why fluke have been bailing out of the back bays and running for the inlets and the Sound-side channels where that upwelled 71-degree water is stacking up. That shift isn't subtle. If you were still working shallow bay structure last week, you were probably fishing empty water.

This week the moon's the story. We're building into new moon springs, peaking July 11-13, which means the biggest tides of the month are about to rip through the Race, Plum Gut, and the Fishers Island rips. More current means more bait getting flushed off the structure and stacked in the eddies — that's where I'm putting my time, not out in the main current lane. The other piece: a PM flood-sunset window is realigning with the high stand as these springs build, the first one since the June full moon. That's prime striper time — low light, big water movement, bait pushed hard against structure. Expect the bite windows to compress around that sunset-to-dark flood rather than spreading out over a slow tide like we've had the past couple weeks.

Bass fishing has been real but you have to work for it. I've been running the backside eddy at Plum Gut on the last two hours of the ebb, into the turn, throwing big Danny-style swimmers and Guppy plugs in bone and black — fish from 28 to low-40s inches coming off the bottom of the eddy where the current dumps out and slows. Depth's 30 to 55 feet there depending where you sit relative to the rip line. At the Race, I've had better luck drifting the downcurrent side of Valiant Rock at first light, live eels on a fish-finder rig, letting the current do the work instead of retrieving — that's a 40 to 70 foot bite with fish holding tight to the pinnacle structure where the eddy folds back on itself. North Dumpling's south side has been giving up a few too on bucktail-and-eel-strip combos worked slow on the last of the outgoing.

Blues have been the more consistent player right now — schools chopping bunker on top off Orient Point and along the Fishers Island north rip, especially on the morning outgoing when bait gets pushed out of the harbors. Bunker chunks on wire leader, or just blind-casting big poppers into the surface commotion, and you're in fish inside ten minutes. Nothing huge — mostly 3 to 6 pounds — but steady, and they'll save a slow bass morning.

Fluke are where the real story is this week, because of that bay-to-inlet shift. The Orient Point rip and the channel edges through Plum Gut have turned on for keeper-class fish — 18 to 22 inches, some pushing bigger — on white Gulp Swimming Mullets rigged on 3/4 to 1-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing when that cooler water is funneling through. I'm marking bait stacked at 35 to 45 feet right where the channel drops off, and that's exactly where the fluke are sitting, ambushing whatever gets swept off the shelf. If you're still poking around in the shallow bay grass, you're behind the fish at this point — go find the moving water.

Porgies have been a nice consistent backup on the days the bass and blues go quiet — sandworms and clam on high-low rigs over the rock piles off Fishers Island and around Bartlett Reef, 20 to 35 feet, and they've been thick enough to fill a bucket in an hour if you're anchored right on the structure.

Weakfish, though — I'm not going to pretend they're here. Water's been warm enough for weeks and I haven't heard of a single one caught in this zone. That threshold everyone points to clearly isn't enough on its own; something else needs to line up, and right now it hasn't. I wouldn't burn a tide targeting them.

The one thing I'm watching closely: bonito and false albies. The cold wall that usually pins them down around Block Island has broken down, and that corridor's spreading west toward Point Judith and Fishers Island Sound. It's early — this is a short window, typically gone by mid-July if it doesn't hold — but if you see diving birds and nervous water off the Fishers Island rip or the east end of the Race, have an epoxy jig or a small Deadly Dick ready. That's not a guarantee of a blitz, but the setup's better than it's been in a couple seasons.

Look-ahead: Friday through Monday is where I'd put my best effort. The new moon springs peak, the PM flood lines up with sunset, and that's the kind of current-and-light combo that gets big bass moving out of the eddies to feed. I'd start at Plum Gut on the evening flood with a black Danny plug, and if that's quiet, slide over to the Race at first light on the eel drift. Fluke guys — work the inlet edges on the outgoing, not the bay flats. And keep one rod rigged light for albies if you're running past Fishers Island — this window won't last.
