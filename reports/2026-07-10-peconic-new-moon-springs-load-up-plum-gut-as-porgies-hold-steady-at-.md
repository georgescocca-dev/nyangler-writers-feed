# New moon springs load up Plum Gut as porgies hold steady at Shelter Island
*Bay water's running warm and the fluke have pushed to the channels, but bigger tides this week could finally light up the sunset bass bite at Orient Point.*

**SHELTER ISLAND, NY — July 10** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

Last week the Peconic did what it always does in early July — it warmed up faster than anybody wanted it to. The bay proper has been running warm, similar to what we've seen out in Great South Bay pushing 78 degrees in the shallows, and that kind of heat doesn't sit well with fluke or bass that like their water with a little more oxygen in it. Wind was mostly out of the southwest through the middle of the week, which stacked warm water against the north shore of the bay and pushed the better fishing into the channels and the current-swept spots — Plum Gut, the Gut edges off Orient, the deeper cuts around Shelter Island. The moon's been waning down toward new, and that's set up this week's story.

We hit new moon right around today, which means spring tides building through the weekend — bigger swings, stronger current, and more water moving through Plum Gut and the Gut channels than we've seen in a few weeks. That's the kind of tide that wakes this whole zone up. The last real evening bite window we had was tied to the June full moon, and now we're getting the mirror image of it on the new moon side. Watch for the flood tide lining up with sunset Friday through Monday — that's when the current through Orient Point and the Gut really starts pushing bait, and the stripers that have been sitting deep and sulky in the heat tend to slide up onto the edges to feed. If you can only fish one evening this week, that's the window I'd pick.

Porgy fishing has been the one constant, and it's been good — genuinely good, not hype-good. Shelter Island continues to be the epicenter, same as every July. Anchor up on the drop-off outside Coecles Harbor, or work the rocky bottom off Cedar Island and the pilings near the North Ferry, in 15 to 25 feet. High-low rigs with size 6 or 8 hooks, sandworm or clam strips, right on bottom. These fish don't need finesse this time of year — they're stacked and hungry, and a five-minute soak will tell you if you're on them. Fish are running a good average size too, plenty of 12- to 14-inch scup mixed with shorts, so you're culling for the better ones rather than scratching for keepers.

Fluke have done exactly what warm bay water makes them do — they've abandoned the flats and pushed into the channels and current-scoured cuts where there's cooler, cleaner water moving through. Robins Island channel, the drop along Jessup's Neck, and the deeper edges of the Gut off Orient Point have all been holding fish. The pattern that's working is a slow drift on the outgoing tide, bouncing a bucktail-and-Gulp combination — white or pink swimming mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail — right along the bottom where the channel breaks meet the flat. Nothing spectacular, no doormats to report this week, but a mixed bag of keepers to 22 inches for guys willing to move around and find the moving water instead of anchoring on memory from June.

Striped bass have been the tougher story, and I'll be honest about it — the heat's had them off their game in the open bay. But Plum Gut and the rips off Orient Point, Jennys and Cedar Beach Rip on the Shelter Island side, have kept producing when the tide's running hard. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig tight to the current seams have been the most consistent producer, with eels after dark for the guys willing to put in the night hours. Bluefish have been mixed in wherever the bass are working bait, chopping up bunker schools that get funneled through the Gut on the stronger tide stages — bring wire leader if you're using bucktails, because the blues have been aggressive enough to bite you off on mono.

Weakfish, I'll say plainly, have been a no-show. The water's warm enough on paper, but warm water alone doesn't make weakfish show up — we've learned that the hard way the last few seasons. A few scattered reports out of Three Mile Harbor and up near Orient, nothing worth planning a trip around. If you stumble into one working the same spots as bass or fluke, consider it a bonus, not a target.

One more thing worth watching — there's early bonito and Spanish mackerel chatter starting to build out along the eastern inlets, and Orient Point sits right at the doorstep of that pattern. It's early, and I wouldn't build a whole trip around it yet, but if you're already out there working the Gut on the new moon tide with small metal or Deadly Dicks, keep an eye out. That window tends to widen fast once it starts.

Looking ahead, the new moon spring tide peaks Saturday into Monday, and that's where I'm putting my time — sunset flood at Plum Gut and the Orient Point rips, bunker chunks and eels for bass, wire leader ready for blues. If that current push doesn't produce, my backup plan is the same as it's been all month: anchor on the Shelter Island scup grounds, soak worms on bottom, and let the porgies save the day. They usually do.
