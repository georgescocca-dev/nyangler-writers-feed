# Bass Hold the Boulders, Bluefin Camp Southwest of the Light
*The Point is easing into its September posture early, with bass staged on structure, bluefin settled off the rips, and the first albie showing feels close.*

**MONTAUK, NY — August 27** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

There's a specific feel to Montauk in the last week of August, and anyone who's fished this rock long enough knows it when it arrives. The daytime crowds are thinning at the Point, the light's coming in lower and gold by six, and the water — 69.1 degrees off the Lighthouse — still carries summer's warmth but not its indifference. This isn't the fall run yet. It's the setup for it, and this year it looks like it's arriving a little ahead of the calendar.

Across most of the mid-Atlantic and southern New England right now, striped bass are stuck in that predictable late-August pattern — warm surface layers pushing fish down and into structure during daylight, real activity saved for the edges of light. Montauk's not exempt from that physics, but the difference here is what the structure actually is. From North Bar out to Shagwong Reef and the boulder fields off the Lighthouse itself, there's current, cold seams, and bait funneling past on every tide — and that's kept the surf bite alive when other stretches of coast have gone quiet in the heat.

The reports off the rocks this week have bass mixed with big bluefish, staging tight to structure and hard-running current edges rather than roaming open water. That's a pattern worth understanding, not just following: with the surface warm, stripers are using the boulder fields and rip lines as thermal breaks, holding low and picking off bait that gets disoriented in the wash. The building tide at North Bar has been the most consistent window — start fishing an hour before the top of the flood, work a bucktail (1.5 ounces, white or chartreuse) across the seam where the white water meets green, let it sink clean off the edge of the bar, and work it back with a slow, rhythmic bottom retrieve. That's the exact presentation putting bass in the sand at Turtle Cove and off the Lighthouse rocks right now — not fast retrieves, not surface plugs at midday, but patient bottom-contact work on the leading edge of moving water.

Offshore and just southwest of the Point, the more interesting story of the week has been school bluefin tuna holding in a tight, repeatable pattern rather than passing through. Boats working out of Montauk Harbor have found them stacked on sand eel and mackerel against a thermal edge, and the jig bite has been legitimate — UVT Chief soft plastics scaled from 1.5 up to 10 ounces depending on the depth the fish are sitting at, and 5-ounce pink Ronz working just as well when the fish are higher in the water column. The fact that this bite has held for more than a week now, rather than flashing and disappearing, suggests these fish have settled into real structure and bait rather than just riding through on a push of water. Barring a hard wind event that scatters the sand eels, there's no obvious reason this pattern breaks in the next several days.

Fluke fishing, by contrast, is the soft spot on the report card right now, and there's no point dressing it up. The warm, thinned-out water that's spreading fluke out across flats from here to Delaware Bay is doing the same thing inside Fort Pond Bay and the harbor — fish are there, but scattered, and catch rates have dropped everywhere except the channel edges and deeper structure holding a cooler bottom layer. If fluke is the priority, work the deeper drops in the harbor channel and along the edges of Fort Pond Bay on the last of the ebb, when a cooler layer settles along bottom and concentrates what fish are left. White or chartreuse Gulp on a bucktail, dragged slow, is still the right call — there's just less water worth covering with confidence than there was a month ago.

Bluefish have been the one constant nobody's arguing about. Snapper blues are thick in the harbor and Fort Pond Bay, and larger cocktail blues are mixing right into the striper catches on structure and on the troll off the south side beaches. If a rod's been sitting quiet, it's usually not staying that way long with blues around.

We flagged it last week and it's worth repeating: the warm-water signal running through the region — squid pushing into Rhode Island and Nantucket Sound, a sailfish sighting off Chatham — pointed toward the first bonito or false albacore showing at the Point any day now. Nothing's confirmed yet in the rips, but the bait is right and the water's right, and Montauk is almost always among the first spots in the region to get them. Anyone fishing the Point or the Elbow this week should have a small metal — a Deadly Dick or a Got-Cha — rigged and ready on a spare rod, because when they show, it's usually with no warning at all.

Looking ahead: the moon's building toward full, which means bigger tidal swings and sharper bite windows at North Bar, Shagwong, and the rip lines off the Lighthouse over the next several days. Expect the surf bass bite to get another push on the increased current, expect the bluefin to keep holding southwest of the Point as long as the bait stays put, and keep watching the boils east of the Lighthouse for the first sign of albie or bonito activity. If there's only one tide to fish this week, make it the building flood at North Bar in the last hour of daylight — that's where the structure, the current, and the bait are lining up most reliably right now.
