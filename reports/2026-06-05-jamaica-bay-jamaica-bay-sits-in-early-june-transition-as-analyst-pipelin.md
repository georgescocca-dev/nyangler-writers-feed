# Jamaica Bay sits in early-June transition as analyst pipeline runs dark
*With no live buoy or SST data in today's analyst feed, the bay's thermal lag versus the ocean remains the controlling variable for bass, fluke and weakfish positioning.*

**ROCKAWAY, NY — June 5, 2026** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The oceanographic desk came in thin this morning. The Dr. Fish analyst run is carrying no live buoy readings, no current tide-station data, and the SST package hasn't refreshed since June 3 — the most recent thermal imagery in the catalog is two days stale, and we have no verified surface temperature to attach to Ambrose, Rockaway, or the inside stations. That is the honest top of the report. What we can say with confidence, based on the early-June climatology of this estuary and the June 1–3 SST frames that did publish, is that the bay's interior is running meaningfully warmer than the inlet and the open ocean off Breezy. By the first week of June the back bay flats — the broad shallows behind Canarsie Pier, the marsh edges south of the Cross Bay Bridge — are typically pushing into the mid-60s while the inlet itself and the surf along Rockaway are still in the high 50s to low 60s. That thermal gradient is the engine right now.

What that gradient means for this beat is straightforward. Bait — bunker, spearing, the first real push of sand eels along the ocean side — stacks on the temperature break, and the bass stage on the structure that sits across it. The Marine Parkway Bridge is the obvious one: warmer water dumping out of the bay on the back half of the ebb, colder ocean water pressing in on the flood, and bridge abutments creating the eddy lines that hold fish on both sides of the swing. This is a slot-class window. The big April and early-May overslot bass that ate chunked bunker on the flats are largely gone or going; what's left and what's arriving is the 28-to-36-inch class working the bridge, the inlet rips off Breezy, and the deeper holes inside the bay on moving water. Fluke have opened up and the bay floor is warm enough — the channel edges from the Marine Parkway east toward the airport flats are the early-season play, drifting bucktail-and-teaser rigs in 12 to 22 feet on the last of the incoming.

On-the-ground intelligence supports the staging picture. The chunk bite that defined April in the back of the bay has clearly tapered, and the conversation has shifted to bucktails, soft plastics, and the first serious fluke drifts of the season. Weakfish are in the mix again — the bay has quietly held a real spring weakfish class the last couple of years, and the pattern of mixed bass, weaks, and short fluke on the same incoming tide drifts in 10 to 15 feet is exactly the early-June signature this estuary throws when the interior warms ahead of the ocean. The surf side is the laggard. Rockaway Beach from B 32nd east through the numbered streets is still a morning-and-dusk proposition with bunker schools nosing along the bar but no consistent daytime blitz yet; the water out front simply isn't warm enough to hold bait through the heat of the day.

Looking three to five days out, the variables to watch are wind direction and the moon. Any sustained southwest pushes bunker tight to the Rockaway beachfront and stacks them against the west jetty at Breezy on the outgoing, which is when the inlet turns on for shore casters working bucktails and soldiers off the rocks. A flip to onshore east winds will scour the surf, color up the inlet, and push the action inside the bay onto the bridge and the deeper channel edges. The bay interior will continue to warm faster than the ocean for at least another week, which means the thermal gradient at the inlet sharpens before it dulls — and sharper gradients hold more bait and more fish. I'd be watching the Marine Parkway on the last two hours of the outgoing into the bottom of the tide, particularly on the dawn slot, and I'd be running fluke drifts on the flood through the middle of the day.

Without a live SST refresh and without buoy numbers in hand, this is a report built on structure, season, and what the water has to be doing given the calendar. When the pipeline catches up I'll have hard numbers on the inlet-versus-interior break and we can sharpen the bridge call. Until then, fish the moving water, fish the edges, and respect that the bay is two weeks ahead of the ocean right now.
