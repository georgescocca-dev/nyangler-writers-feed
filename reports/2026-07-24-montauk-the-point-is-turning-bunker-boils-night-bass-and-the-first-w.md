# The Point Is Turning: Bunker Boils, Night Bass, and the First Whisper of Albies
*Late July at Montauk means the striper bite goes underground and something faster starts moving in off the back side — here's what's holding and what's coming.*

**MONTAUK, NY — July 24** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

There's a week every summer when Montauk stops being one fishery and starts being three or four stacked on top of each other, and this is that week. I've fished this Point since I was tall enough to lean into the rail at the Lighthouse, and I know the rhythm by feel now — the bass go quiet in the daylight, the blues turn the surface white, and somewhere out past the Elbow the first albie of the year is already circling, even if nobody's boated one yet.

Water at the Point is sitting around 69 degrees, which sounds modest until you remember what that number does out here. It's the number that starts waking up the false albacore and bonito schools working their way up from the Rhode Island and Block Island side. We haven't had a confirmed albie yet — I want to be straight with you on that, nobody I trust has boated one off the Point as of this morning — but the water's right, the bait's right, and the pattern is exactly what I flagged last week when I said the headboats out of the Viking dock should start seeing early sign within seven to ten days if this warmth held. It's held. I'd be shocked if we don't have a real report by the weekend, somewhere between the Point and Shagwong Reef, maybe out toward the Elbow where the current really rips.

For right now, the loudest story on the water is bunker and blues, and it is a story worth chasing. Pogy schools have been pinned tight against the beach from the Lighthouse down through Turtle Cove, and the bluefish have been relentless on them — proper blitz conditions, birds working low and diving, bunker exploding on the surface like popcorn. This is the pattern I called last week too: bunker-driven blitz action holding as the dominant visible story until the bait disperses or a wind shift breaks it up. Neither's happened yet. If you want blues, don't overthink it — a Hopkins or a 2-ounce Kastmaster fired into the boil, or if you want to keep tackle intact, a wire-leader popper worked fast across the top. They're not picky right now, they're just violent.

The bass, though — that's where it gets serious, and where you need to change your whole approach. Stripers have gone nocturnal on us hard, exactly the pattern you'd expect once surface temps push into this range. Daytime, the boulder fields off the North Bar and the rip lines off the Point have been mostly quiet, maybe a fish or two picked off deep on bucktails worked slow along bottom structure, but nothing to build a trip around. Night is a different animal entirely. Guys working the incoming tide after dark around the Point and down toward Shagwong have been connecting on real fish — eels swum slow and deep along the boulder edges, bucktails with a curl-tail trailer dropped right into the white water where the rip meets the calm. I had a report of a 38-pound fish taken off the south side structure on a black eel, fished on the building tide around 11 p.m., worked tight to the rocks where the current stacks bait against structure. That's the program right now — full dark, slack water avoided, current moving, eels or bucktails, patience.

Fluke have slid into that midsummer pattern everybody who's fished this water long enough recognizes — shorts everywhere in the shallows, but the keeper-class fish have pushed out to the deeper structure and channel edges where the water stays cooler. I'd be working the drift outside Frisbie's or along the deeper edges toward Fort Pond Bay on the stronger tide stages, white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a bucktail, 30 to 45 feet, dragged slow along bottom contours. You'll cull through a lot of shorts to find the keepers, but they're there if you're willing to grind for them.

Porgies have been steady and reliable off Shagwong Reef and around the harbor structure — bloodworms or clam on standard bottom rigs, nothing fancy, just consistent action if you want to fill a cooler without overthinking it. Blackfish season's still a ways off but I mention it because guys are already scouting structure for the fall, and that tells you something about where heads are turning.

Offshore, the canyon boats running out of Montauk have kept finding yellowfin and some bigeye in consistent numbers, and that program should hold as long as this high-pressure stretch stays put. It's a long run but the payoff's been worth it for boats willing to make the trip.

Looking ahead, I think this coming week is the pivot point of the whole summer out here. If the warm water holds and the bait stays thick, I expect confirmed albie and maybe bonito sign within the next few days — that's where I'd put my attention if speed and light tackle are your thing, working the rips off the Point and out toward the Elbow with small metal and epoxy jigs once they show. If you're chasing bass, stay committed to the night program — full dark, moving current, eels and bucktails tight to structure at the North Bar and down through Shagwong. And if the wind stays cooperative, don't sleep on the bunker blitz along the beach — it's been the most dependable show on the Point all week, and there's no sign yet it's ready to break up.
