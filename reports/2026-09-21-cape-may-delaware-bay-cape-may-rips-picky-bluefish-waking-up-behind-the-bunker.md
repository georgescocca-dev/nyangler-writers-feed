# Cape May Rips Picky, Bluefish Waking Up Behind the Bunker
*Weakfish and croaker are working the Delaware Bay flats while the drum run sits nine months out and the real fall push is still building.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 21** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Report card for the week: Cape May Rips — fishable but picky, better on the outgoing than the flood. Hereford Inlet — fluke fading fast but still worth a drift. Delaware Bay — weakfish and croaker steady on the flats, black drum long gone and not due back until May. That's the honest read heading into a Harvest Moon week that should start turning the bigger fall pattern on.

The why is straightforward. Delaware Bay is still sitting warm for late September, holding in that 69 to 70 degree band, which is keeping weakfish and stripers working the channel edges instead of pushing hard toward the mouth yet. Bunker schools are funneling past on the tide, thick enough that the blue claw guys and bottom bouncers on the lower bay flats are pulling kingfish mixed with croaker along with the weakfish, which tells you the whole system down there is running a touch behind schedule for the calendar. Upper bay water needs to ease off another few degrees before the real migratory bass show up in numbers, and that's likely a week-out proposition, not a today proposition.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the bass bite would build steadily as the bait kept clearing out, and the blues would show bigger behind the bunker like they always do this time of year. That's exactly what's setting up now. The Harvest Moon tides are bringing bigger swings on both ends, which pushes more water and more bait past the jetties and the inlet mouth in a shorter window, and that's when you get the aggressive stuff. Watch for bluefish blitzes at the Cape May jetties on the back half of the outgoing, right as that stronger moon-driven current really starts ripping. Bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig or a big Ava jig worked through the wash will get bit when those blues commit.

On the Rips themselves, the bite has been inconsistent enough that it's not a blanket good week. The best window has been the start of the outgoing, working bucktails and swim shads down along the Rips proper and dropping back toward Second Reef as the tide builds. Stripers are there but they're not stacked — a mix of shorts with the occasional keeper mixed in, mostly on white or chartreuse bucktails tipped with a strip of bunker belly, fished slow near bottom in 20 to 35 feet. Anybody working the flood tide up top has been getting picked apart by short bluefish and not much else. If you've only got one tide to fish the Rips this week, make it the first two hours of the outgoing off Cape May Point — that's when the bait gets pulled through and the bigger fish show interest.

Hereford Inlet fluke are still around but this is a fading bite, not a building one. The better fish have been coming off the north side drift near Two Mile Landing in 20 to 30 feet, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails dragged slow through the deeper holes on the last of the ebb. Numbers are down from a month ago and that's normal for late September — expect this window to close out over the next couple weeks as water temps slide.

Down in the bay, weakfish are giving up a respectable count for anglers working bloodworm and squid strips on bottom rigs along the channel edges near Fortescue and up toward Brandywine Shoal. These aren't the giant tiderunners of the spring drum season, but a mixed bag of weakfish, kingfish, and croaker on the flats has kept the bay boats busy, especially working the last two hours of the incoming when bait gets pushed up onto the shallower structure. Triggerfish have shown as scattered bycatch around the inshore structure closer to the mouth, nothing to plan a trip around but worth noting if you're bottom fishing squid there.

As for the drum — that conversation is closed for the year. The Delaware Bay black drum run is strictly a May event, and there's no reason to expect anything different. Anybody asking about drum right now needs to circle back around the first warm push of spring.

Looking at the next few days, the Harvest Moon tides should keep intensifying the bunker-driven activity at the jetties and inlet mouths, which means bluefish should keep getting bigger and more aggressive as heavier bunker schools funnel through on those bigger swings. The striper piece is the one to watch closely — if upper bay temps ease off even a couple degrees over the coming week, expect the first real wave of migratory bass to show shadowing bait tight to structure, the same pattern building up the coast right now. Best case, that push arrives on schedule and the Rips start producing more consistent fish on the outgoing by early next week. If the bay stays stubborn and warm, expect more of this same mixed, working-for-it bite — fishable, worth the trip, but nothing to oversell. Keep an eye on the tide charts around the jetties for the bigger outgoing swings this week — that's where the moon is going to do its work first.
