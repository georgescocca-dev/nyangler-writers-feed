# Captree dock talk: bunker's tightening, bass are starting to notice
*The bait's stacking up along the beach and inside the inlet, but the blitz everyone's waiting for isn't quite here yet — here's why, and where to be when it turns on.*

**CAPTREE, NY — September 7** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Stopped by the scale house at Captree yesterday afternoon and the talk wasn't about what came over the rail — it was about what was swimming past the boats on the way in. Bunker. Big, tight pods of them, rolling right along the beach front and pushing into the inlet on the last of the incoming, thick enough that a couple guys idling back from the ocean side said their sounder looked like a smear of black ink from the surface to twenty feet. That's the story of the week out here, and it's the story that's going to matter for the next several days.

The full moon tides that ran through September 6 and 7 did exactly what we flagged they'd do — they tightened those bunker schools up hard along the south shore, and the bass are starting to pay real attention instead of just picking at stragglers. We said this stretch of beach was one of the trigger zones for the first fall blitz, and the ingredients are sitting right there off Democrat Point and through the inlet mouth right now. What hasn't happened yet — and let's be straight about it — is the actual blitz. There's been scattered surface activity at first light, some boils inside the inlet on the last of the ebb, a few reports of stripers busting bunker off the beach near the lighthouse, but it's been patchy, not sustained. The bunker are staged. The bass are shadowing. The trigger — cooler nights, a building tide toward the new moon on the 10th — hasn't fully pulled the pin yet.

Offshore, the ocean's been a little junky for surf guys — buoy readings off the coast have been running close to 4 feet at a tight 5-second period, which is the kind of short, stacked swell that makes it tough to work a plug cleanly off the outer bar. That rough water has been pushing a lot of the good bites inside — into the inlet itself, along the Robert Moses bridge pilings, and onto the bay-side flats where the afternoon sun is warming the shallows on the tail of the ebb. If you're choosing between fighting whitewater on the beach or working the calmer inlet seam, the inlet's been the better bet this week.

Bluefish have been the most reliable player in all this. They're all over those bunker schools, and they don't need convincing the way the bass do. Chunking bunker off Democrat Point on the incoming has been steady, and early risers throwing poppers and darters into the surface wash at first light near the inlet mouth have been connecting on choppers in the 3- to 6-pound class, some bigger. If you want a bent rod this week without overthinking it, that's the play.

Striped bass have been there, but it's been a game of patience and timing more than a sure thing. The better fish are showing on bucktails and darters cast into the edges of bunker pods, worked on the incoming tide right where the inlet current meets the open water off the point. A few schoolies mixed with some better fish in the 28- to 34-inch range have come on white bucktails with a curly-tail trailer, fished slow along the bottom on that seam. Evening's been producing more than midday — the light bunker push tends to happen right around dusk when the wind lays down.

Fluke are winding down from the summer peak, which tracks with where we are in September, but the bay's still giving up keepers if you know where to look. The west side of the Robert Moses bridges, working the channel edge in 25 to 35 feet on the outgoing tide, has been the most consistent drift — white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, or live killies and spearing on a simple fishfinder rig for the guys who want the real thing. Catches have thinned from the June and July numbers, and a lot of the better fish are starting to slide toward the inlet on their way out to deeper water, so this window's closing. If fluke's the target, the next couple weeks are the tail end of it before the fish move out for good.

Weakfish have been an incidental catch on those same bridge drifts, mostly in the evening, nothing to build a trip around but worth keeping a rod rigged light in case one shows. Porgies are starting their fall build around the rock piles and reef structure inside the bay and near the inlet rocks — sandworms and clam on a simple hi-lo rig will get you into them if you want a change of pace and a meal.

Looking ahead, the tide building toward the new moon on September 10 is the number to watch. If the bunker keep tightening the way they have been and a few cooler nights knock the water temps down even a couple degrees, the conditions are lined up for that first real blitz to break loose — most likely at first light around Democrat Point and the inlet seam, or on an evening push through the bridges as the tide turns. Best case, it happens within the week and it's the kind of window where you don't want to be running late. If it slides past the new moon without breaking, don't panic — the bait's not going anywhere yet, and neither are the fish that are following it.
