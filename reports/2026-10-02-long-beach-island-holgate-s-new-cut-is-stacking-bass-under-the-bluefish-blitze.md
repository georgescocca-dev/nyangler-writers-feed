Expect that squeeze to keep intensifying through the weekend.

As for what's actually coming over the rail: the bass mix is mostly slot-range fish in the 26 to 32-inch window, with the occasional overslot showing up in the deeper troughs at first light, especially where that new Holgate cut dumps into the rip. The pattern holding up best: white or bunker-pattern Daiwa SP Minnows and Super Strike needlefish worked through the wash on the outgoing, with white bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range tipped with a white Fat Cow trailer doing work in the deeper slots where the current is really moving. When the blitz is visible and fish are busting on top, smaller metal — Deadly Dicks or similar tins — gets more bites than anything oversized; these fish are eating peanut bunker, not adult menhaden, so match that size.

At night, the bigger bass are reportedly holding tighter to the jetty rocks at Little Egg Inlet and Barnegat Light, and bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig with circle hooks are still the go-to for anyone willing to sit on the rocks after dark. That's producing the occasional bigger fish — low 30s to upper 30-inch class — out of a bite that's otherwise mostly slot-sized.

It's not a numbers bite on weakfish — more of a bonus when you're working bass water — so don't plan a trip around them, but keep one rod rigged light if you're in that system. Graveling Point is holding bait and some scattered bass activity in the shallower flats on the push of the tide, worth a look if the main beach gets crowded.

Honest read on the overall bite: it's good, not uniform. Some stretches of beach are blitzing hard for a tide change and going quiet; others are producing steady but unspectacular action all morning. The fish are there, but they're moving with the bait, and the bait is moving with the tide and the moon. If you can only fish one window this weekend, the two hours around the outgoing at dawn — particularly near any visible cut or rip — is where the data and the chatter both point.

Looking ahead, the next 48 hours should hold the current pattern as the moon builds and tidal range increases, likely tightening the bait pushes even further at the inlets. The one thing worth watching in the 72-hour window is any shift to a northeast wind — the first real frontal passage of the fall could mix the water column and pin bait hard against the beach, which has the potential to turn a good week into the first major blitz event of the season. Keep an eye on the forecast and a rod rigged for both scenarios.
