# Forget the fluke — the bunker jam at Jones Inlet is the real show
*Harvest Moon tides are stacking peanut bunker at the inlet mouth, and the jetty rats are cashing in while the bay boats chase a fluke bite that's running on fumes.*

**JONES BEACH, NY — September 21** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Half the guys at the shop this week are still rigging bucktails for Sloop Channel and talking about squeezing out one more fluke trip before the season shuts the door. I get it — the numbers have been decent for late September. But that's not where the smart money is right now, and if you've been down to the inlet at first light you already know why. The bunker are stacking up thick at the mouth of Jones Inlet, peanuts and full-grown pogies both, and every predator in the system is leaning on them hard. That's the pattern to fish this week, not the one everybody's clinging to out of habit.

Here's the setup. We're coming into the Harvest Moon, and those bigger tides are doing exactly what big fall tides do — they're pulling more bait through the inlet on a faster exchange and pinning it up against structure instead of letting it spread out over open water. Offshore the ocean's been messy, running better than a wave and a half of chop with short, choppy 4-to-5 second period seas, so the beachfront game outside the bar has been a slog. That rough water is pushing bait and fish off the open beach and funneling everything into the inlet itself — the jetties at Short Beach, the rocks on the west side of Jones Inlet, and the channel edge running out past the Coast Guard station. When the ocean's beat up like this, the inlet becomes the path of least resistance for bunker trying to get out, and stripers and blues know it.

We flagged this last week — said the bluefish pattern would intensify as more bait got flushed south and east, and that's exactly what's playing out at the inlet mouth right now. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay creeks off Freeport, but the meaningful blues — 3 to 6 pounds, some pushing bigger — are riding the bunker schools right at the jetty tips on the last two hours of the outgoing tide. Guys working white bucktails and bunker-colored Kastmasters off the Short Beach rocks have been connecting steady during that window, and when the blues get under a peanut bunker pod tight to the rocks it turns into workable topwater action for twenty, thirty minutes at a crack — poppers and darters both getting eaten.

Striped bass are mixing into the same blitzes but they're not committing wall-to-wall yet. The better bass have been coming on bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig right off the inlet rocks on the outgoing, and on trolled bunker spoons for the boats working the deeper cut just inside the inlet. Sizes are mixed — plenty of schoolies in the 20-inch class mixed with a scattering of slot fish pushing 30, and a few overslot bass reported off the jetty on live-lined bunker fished on wire near the bottom where the current runs hardest against the rocks. This should only build. The moon's doing the work now, pulling more bait and more current through the system, and that combination is what turns a scattered bite into a real blitz pattern inside another week or two.

Fluke are still around but the writing's on the wall. The bite off the Wantagh Bridge pilings and through the deeper holes in Sloop Channel has held better than expected for this late in September, with keepers still coming on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with a chunk of squid, worked slow on the drift in 15 to 20 feet on the last of the outgoing. But the fluke crowd on the bay is thinning by the day, and rightly so — that fishery's on borrowed time as water temps start easing off and everything shifts toward the bait-driven blitz pattern. If fluke is your only shot this weekend, fish it early in the tide before the current slacks and don't expect a banner day.

Weakfish are the sleeper story in the back bay right now. They've been showing better than they have in years through the deeper holes off Freeport and in the Sloop Channel drop-offs, mostly on chartreuse or pink 1/4-ounce jigheads tipped with a strip of squid, fished slow along bottom on the last of the ebb when the water's carrying the most bait past the holes. Nothing to build a whole trip around yet, but worth a few casts if you're already out there working the channel.

Looking ahead, the next few days should sharpen up as the Harvest Moon tides build toward full strength. Best window is still the last two hours of the outgoing at the inlet mouth, right at first light and again in the last hour before dark, when the bait gets squeezed hardest against the rocks and the current is moving with real authority. If the ocean lays down at all, the open beach east and west of the inlet could load up fast once bunker start spilling out past the bar — that's the move if it happens. Until then, park at the jetty, work the bunker pods as they push through, and don't waste your best tide of the week drifting the bay for a fluke bite that's already packing its bags.
