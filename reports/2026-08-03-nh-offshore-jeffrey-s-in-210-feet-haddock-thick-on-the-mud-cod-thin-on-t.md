# Jeffrey's in 210 feet: haddock thick on the mud, cod thin on the gravel edge
*Bait's stacking on the ledge, the haddock are holding tight to structure, and the first bluefin scouts showed up behind the boat this week.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 3** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Two hundred ten feet, straight mud, northeast corner of Jeffrey's Ledge — that's where I found the best marks of the week, a tight red smear sitting six to ten feet off bottom that turned into haddock as soon as the jigs hit it. That basin mud between Jeffrey's and the edge of Scantum holds haddock this time of year the way a magnet holds shavings, and this week it delivered. Diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid, worked in a slow lift-drop right on the meter mark, put good numbers of 18 to 22 inch haddock in the box on three of four trips out. No need to horse the retrieve — these fish are eating on the fall, and if you're ripping the jig too fast you're pulling it right past their nose.

Cod is the harder story right now. I worked the gravel edge on the western side of Jeffrey's — the transition where the mud gives way to hard bottom in about 165 to 185 feet — and found cod, but scattered, not stacked. A few fish in the 8 to 12 pound class came up on jigs, a couple better ones in the mid-teens on bait, but you had to grind for them. I made three separate drifts across the same stretch of gravel Tuesday before I found a pod worth stopping the boat for. That's typical August pattern on this ledge — the cod aren't gone, they're just spread thin and holding tighter to the harder structure than they were in June. If you're going after them, fish the up-current edge of the gravel where it breaks toward the mud, drop a cod rig with clam or herring belly, and be patient. Don't waste time on soft bottom expecting cod — that's haddock water, different fish, different address.

Pollock have been the wildcard and honestly some of the most fun fishing of the week. Mid-water marks over the deeper basins — 40 to 60 feet off bottom in 200-plus feet of water — turned into pollock blitzes on a few drifts, six and eight pound fish hitting jigs on the drop before they even got near bottom. If you see that kind of mid-water sign on the meter, don't bother sending the jig all the way down. Work it through that band and you'll save yourself the grind.

Mackerel showed up in numbers around Platts Bank and along the inshore edge of Jeffrey's this week, which is exactly the kind of bait signal that gets my attention this time of year. The Gulf of Maine water out here is running noticeably cooler than what they're dealing with south of the Cape, and that temperature difference is exactly why bait wants to be here — herring and mackerel pushing onto the ledge, stacking up where the cooler water holds. We flagged this a couple weeks back, said conditions looked set up to stay favorable for offshore runs, and that's held true — steady weather windows all week let us get out and cover ground instead of sitting at the dock watching a forecast fall apart.

And where there's bait building like this, the bluefin start paying attention. We had two separate sightings this week — one behind the boat off the southeast corner of Jeffrey's, one further out toward Tillies Bank — fish showing on the surface, working a bait ball, gone before anyone got a bait in the water. That's scouting behavior, not committed feeding, and I want to be straight with you about that. Nobody I've talked to at the dock has hooked up yet. But this is exactly how the pattern starts every year — bait concentrates on the cooler water of the ledge through late July into August, tuna show up to check it out, and by the back half of the month they're committing hard enough to troll or chunk consistently. If you want to get ahead of it, I'd run a spread of green machines and a couple of ballyhoo behind the boat while you're groundfishing the ledge anyway — costs you nothing, and if those scouts turn into feeders you want to already be dragging something through the water.

Tide-wise, we're sliding toward the neap around the 5th and 6th, which means current on the ledge edges will soften up some heading into next week. That's worth knowing for the gravel bite specifically — cod tend to key on current pushing bait across those hard edges, so with the tide easing off, I'd expect the gravel fishing to get a touch tougher before it gets better again on the next building tide. Haddock on the mud don't seem to care much either way; that bite's been steady regardless of tide stage, which tells me it's more about the bait holding in that basin than current strength.

Looking ahead, if the weather holds the way it's been holding, I'd put my time on the mud for numbers of haddock, work the gravel patiently for a mixed bag of cod, and keep a trolling spread out on every trip regardless of target — the tuna scouting isn't a guarantee of a bite yet, but the ingredients are lining up the way they do most years right before the real bluefin fishing turns on. I wouldn't book a dedicated tuna trip just yet. I would keep gear rigged and ready, because when these fish commit on Jeffrey's, it tends to happen fast.
