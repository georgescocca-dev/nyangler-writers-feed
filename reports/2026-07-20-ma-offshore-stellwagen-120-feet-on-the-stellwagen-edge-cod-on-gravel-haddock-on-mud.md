# 120 Feet on the Stellwagen Edge: Cod on Gravel, Haddock on Mud, Tuna Behind the Macks
*A steady bank week — groundfish holding true to structure while bluefin work the mackerel schools topside.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — July 20** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

One-eighteen feet on the sounder, Stellwagen Edge, gravel showing up hard and red under the transducer with just enough structure to hold cod tight to bottom. That's been my number all week when I want a fish in the box before I even think about running offshore to check on the tuna. The bank's doing what it always does this time of July — cod pinned to the hard gravel patches along the edge, haddock spread out on the softer mud to the north and west, and the whole thing sitting under a warm cap of surface water that's kept the meter honest on where the fish actually are.

Cod fishing has been a grind more than a blitz. We're picking away at market fish in the 24 to 30 inch range on the Edge and down around the Southwest Corner, jigging diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid or working clam bellies on a standard cod rig, 12 to 16 ounces depending on drift. You need to hit bottom and stay there — these fish aren't chasing anything up in the water column right now, they're sitting in the gravel troughs waiting on the tide to push bait past them. Best window has been the top of the tide through the first two hours of the drop, when the current's got some shape to it but hasn't started ripping.

Haddock's been the better story. Stellwagen Basin and the mud flats off Tillies Bank have been holding good numbers of haddock in the 3 to 6 pound class, sometimes better, on the classic mud-bottom presentation — bait rigs with a chunk of clam or a Berkley Gulp swimming mullet trailing behind, worked slow just off bottom in that 180 to 220 foot range. Haddock like the softer stuff, and with the bank's mud basins holding cooler, more stable water than the gravel edges this time of year, they've stacked in tighter than the cod have. If I'm picking one species to promise a rod-bender to a boat of guys who just want steady action, it's haddock on the mud right now, not cod on the gravel.

Pollock and hake have been mixed in as a bonus more than a target — pollock jumping the gravel edges chasing bait up off bottom, hake showing on the deeper mud alongside the haddock. Nothing to build a trip around, but a welcome fish in the cooler when they show.

Now the real conversation at the dock this week has been the tuna. We flagged this coming a week back — said the new moon tide would push bait and concentrate fish on the bank, sand eels first, mackerel behind them — and that's exactly the pattern that's set up. Mackerel schools have been thick over the bank, marking heavy on the meter in 60 to 90 feet, and where the macks are, the bluefin are working them from underneath along with the whales topside. It's not uncommon right now to be running the Edge for cod, look up, and see a humpback surfacing through a bait ball with tuna boiling the same patch of water thirty yards off. The recreational slot fishery — 27 inch to under 47 — has stayed steady since the trophy category closed on July 3rd, and boats working the mackerel schools with green machines and small spreader bars behind the boat, or jigging straight into the bait balls with stickbaits, have been connecting on schoolie-class bluefin consistently enough to call it a real pattern, not a lucky day.

Wind and current have cooperated. We're sliding into the first quarter neap around the 21st, which is going to soften the tide on the Edge for a few days — good news for the cod and haddock guys because a slower drift means better bottom contact and less scope to manage, but it also tends to spread the bait out a little, so don't be surprised if the tuna bite gets a touch more scattered before it tightens back up around the full moon push. That's the trade-off on the bank — hard tide concentrates everything into tight windows, soft tide gives you more time on the water but you're working harder to find the exact seam where the fish are stacked.

Honest read for the week ahead: if you want groundfish in the box, run the Edge and the Basin early, hit your numbers on the gravel and mud before the sun gets too high, and don't expect the cod to be easy — they've been fussy fish this July, biting in short windows tied to the tide change. If you want tuna, watch the surface for mackerel and whale activity together — that combination has been the single best indicator all week for where the bluefin are working. Weather's been stable and I don't see anything on the horizon to change that through the weekend, so it's a good stretch to get out there and cover both patterns in the same trip if your boat and your fuel budget allow it. I'd start on the gravel at first light, work the mud through the morning tide, and keep one eye on the horizon for bird and whale activity before running back in — that's how we've been stacking full coolers with a bonus tuna story to tell at the dock.
