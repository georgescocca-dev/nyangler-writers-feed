# Penfield's Ebb Tide Loads the Cows with Porgies While Bass Punch a Dawn-Only Clock
*Blackfish season's closed till September, but the reef structure from Cockenoe to the Cows is still paying off for anglers who fish the right tide.*

**GREENWICH, CT — August 17** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Every Monday morning I sit down with the tide chart and ask myself the same question: which reef is going to hand me the best two-hour window before the boat traffic turns the water into a washing machine. This week the answer was easy. Penfield Reef, last two hours of the ebb, working the down-current side in 15 to 22 feet. That's where the bait piles up as the tide sluices off the reef complex toward Fairfield, and right now that's where the porgies are stacked thick enough to make it worth the run.

Water's sitting in the low 70s across the western basin — Kings Month reading close to 71, still warm enough to keep everything comfortable but not so warm it's shutting fish down like it can in a stagnant harbor. That's the difference this year between the open reef structure and the back-bay mud flats: the current keeps things moving, and moving water on a reef is money in the bank.

Porgies on the Cows off Stamford have been the most consistent thing I've fished all month. Small pieces of green crab or clam on a hi-lo rig, 2 to 3 ounces of lead depending on how hard the current's running, right on the humps in 12 to 20 feet around the top of the flood into the first hour of the ebb. Nothing fancy — just patience and staying anchored tight to the structure instead of drifting off it. Sea bass are filling in behind them on the same pieces, squid strips working better than clam once the sun gets up and the water clears a little. Fish are running mixed sizes, plenty of shorts mixed with keepers in the 14 to 16-inch class, so it's a sort-through-them kind of day, not a fill-the-cooler kind of day. Honest bite, not a spectacular one.

Blackfish are off the table right now — Connecticut's summer closure runs through the end of August, so don't waste bait on the rockpiles at Cockenoe or the Norwalk Islands hoping for tog. That structure's better spent on sea bass anyway this time of year. Cockenoe Reef has been firing better on the incoming than the ebb lately, current pushing across the rockpile and funneling bait into the channel edge on the northeast corner. That's a good spot to probe with a bucktail and Gulp swimming mullet for fluke too, though I'll be straight with you — most of what's coming over the rail in the Sound right now is short. We're getting keepers mixed in off the sandy edges near Sheffield Island on the last of the outgoing, working white bucktails tipped with a 4-inch chartreuse Gulp in 18 to 25 feet, but you're culling through a lot of fish to find a dinner's worth.

Striped bass have been the toughest read of the week, and that's mostly a clock problem, not a fish problem. With the afternoons building into thunderstorm weather most days, the productive window has shrunk down to first light — I'm talking that first hour and a half before the sun really gets up and before the Saturday center consoles start running the flats off Greenwich Point. Peanut bunker are holding tight to the shoreline there, and bass are working them from the tip of the point out toward the Executive Club flats on the tail of the high tide, right before it turns. Small bucktails or a peanut bunker under a slip float have both worked, but once that sun climbs and the boat traffic picks up, those fish slide off the flat and go quiet. If you're fishing this weekend, get out before six or don't bother — the Cows and the reef structure around Norwalk get pounded by mid-morning and the bite shuts down accordingly.

We flagged this a couple weeks back — said the snapper blues stacking up in the back bays would be the tell that adult-class bluefish were building through late August, and that's exactly what's showing now. The mouth of the Norwalk River and the water off Cos Cob Harbor are throwing real 3 to 4-pound blues mixed in with the peanuts, snappers still thick enough in the coves that kids on the dock are catching them on bare hooks. That adult class should keep filling in as the month goes.

Looking ahead, tidal flow is building again toward the August 28 full moon, and that's usually when this whole system sharpens up — more push on the reefs, tighter bait concentrations, better current-driven windows at Penfield and Cockenoe both. If the afternoon storm pattern breaks even for a day or two, I'd put my money on an early morning troll off Penfield with fresh bunker chunks fished tight to the drop-off, targeting bigger bass working the current seam before the crowds. Until then, I'm sticking with the reef program — early tide, right structure, home before the boats show up.
