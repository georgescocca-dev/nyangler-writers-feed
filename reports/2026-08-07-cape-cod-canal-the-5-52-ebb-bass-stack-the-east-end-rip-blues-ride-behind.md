# The 5:52 ebb: bass stack the East End rip, blues ride behind
*Current's still soft off the neap, but the last two hours of every ebb are turning fish on both ends of the ditch.*

**SAGAMORE, MA — August 7** — _by Mike Devlin, Zone Writer_

5:52 this morning, East End, and the ebb hadn't even topped out yet when the first boil showed off the Bourne side riprap. That's the tell right now — you don't wait for max current on the Canal in early August, you fish the building stage, because the fish are already up on the seam before the flow ever gets to its strongest push. By 6:40, when the current was running a legitimate 3.5 to 4 knots east to west, there were three guys hooked up simultaneously off the Herring Run rocks, and that's about as good a sign as you'll get on a Friday morning in high summer.

We flagged this a few days back — said the bass bite here would stay tied to the strongest tidal exchange as the moon eased toward the next neap, and that's exactly the shape of things this week. Current's been on the soft side, nothing violent, which means the window of good fishing on each tide change is narrower than it was during the new moon push. You've got roughly ninety minutes on each end — the last hour and a half of the ebb, and the same on the flood — where the current has enough shoulder to push bait into the rip lines and hold fish in position. Miss that window and you're bouncing bucktails through slack water for nothing.

East End remains the better of the two right now. Fish it on the ebb from the power lines down toward the railroad bridge, working the seam where the fast water meets the eddy along the Sandwich side. Six-inch olive and white Slug-Go's on a light jighead, swung across the current and let to swim dead in the eddy line, have been drawing the better bass — nothing giant, mostly 26 to 32 inches, but a solid keeper-class fish mixed in most mornings. Bucktails in the 1.5-ounce range, bounced bottom on the drift-through, are picking up a few bigger fish holding deeper in the scour holes off the rocks. Bluefish are riding shotgun on the same tide, choppers up to eight or nine pounds hitting anything metal — Deadly Dicks and Hopkins in the 2-ounce range are getting hit the second they touch the rip.

West End is the trickier read this week. The current seams off the Mashnee side and down toward the Bourne Bridge abutment have been holding fish, but it's a shorter window — you want the last forty minutes of the flood specifically, when the water's pushing hard enough to stack bait against the rocks but before it goes full slack.
