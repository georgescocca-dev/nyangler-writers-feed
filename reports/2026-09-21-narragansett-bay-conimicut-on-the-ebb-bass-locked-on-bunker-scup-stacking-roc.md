# Conimicut on the ebb: bass locked on bunker, scup stacking Rocky Point ledges
*The Harvest Moon's stacking bait against the points from Conimicut to Nyatt, and the fleet on the Providence River piers says the stripers finally found it.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — September 21** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Walk the piers on the Providence River this week and the talk is all bunker. Not peanuts anymore — real adult menhaden, pushing up out of the East Passage and stacking against the flats off Conimicut Point and down toward Nyatt on the last two hours of the ebb. That's the story of the week in the upper bay, and it's a welcome one after a summer that had guys grinding for scattered fish. The Harvest Moon tides that peaked over the weekend put more water and more current through the bay than we've seen in a month, and that's exactly what moves bait. Bunker schools don't like slack water — they like to get pushed, and this week they got pushed hard, right into the laps of the stripers that have been shadowing them since bait started thickening up in early September.

Water's holding in the upper 60s through the bay, warmer down at the mouth where it's brushing against the Sound's leftover summer heat, cooler up around Conimicut and the Providence River flats where the tide does more work moving mud and less work holding warmth. That gradient matters — it's why the bigger fish have been sitting mid-bay around Prudence Island and Greenwich Bay's outer edge rather than pushing all the way up into the river. Nobody's complaining about the wind this week either — it's been light and out of the northwest most mornings, which keeps the upper bay clean instead of chalky, and clean water with big current is the combination that turns bunker schools into blitzes.

We flagged this a week back — said the albie bite at the bay's mouth would hold as long as the water stayed in the upper 60s, and that's exactly what's happening down around Beavertail and the East Passage entrance. Bonito are mixing in too, and the boats running out of the lower bay are still finding them on epoxy jigs and small metal off the surface pods. That's not core upper-bay water, but it's worth knowing the warm-water pattern that's been carrying the whole system is still intact — which is a big part of why the bass in the upper bay have stayed aggressive instead of shutting down the way they sometimes do once the calendar flips to fall.

Striped bass are the headline, and they're eating bunker two ways right now. Guys drifting live bunker on a fish-finder rig off Conimicut Point on the last of the ebb are getting quality fish, some over 30 inches, holding tight to the edge where the flat drops into the channel. If you don't want to chase bait, snag-and-drop is working too — treble a bunker out of the school, let it flutter down on a fresh circle hook, and hang on. The other pattern is top-water: peanut-sized schools getting run down by bluefish and bass together off Nyatt Point and along the Prudence Island shoreline in the last hour of daylight, with poppers and SP Minnows in bunker colors drawing violent strikes. That bite's been short but explosive — twenty, thirty minutes of chaos right at last light before it shuts off. If you're going to chase it, be there early and don't waste the window walking the beach looking for birds that already left.

Fluke are still around longer than they usually are this time of year, and that tracks with what the rest of southern New England is seeing — warmer water keeping them shallow and feeding into late September instead of sliding out toward deeper water. The better drifts have been in Greenwich Bay along the channel edges and off the mouth of the Providence River where the bottom transitions from mud to gravel. Four-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the last of the incoming, have been picking off keepers in the 18-20 inch range with the occasional better fish mixed in. It's not a banner bite — more of a fish-here, fish-there pattern — but it's better than what guys had a month ago, and worth a drift if bass aren't cooperating.

Scup are the bread-and-butter right now, and that hasn't changed. Rocky Point's ledges and the rock piles off Nyatt Point are stacked with them in 15 to 25 feet, biting best on the first two hours of the incoming when current starts moving bait past the structure. Squid strips or clam on a high-low rig with size 4 or 6 hooks is all you need — no finesse required, just get down to the rocks and hold bottom. Black sea bass are mixed in on some of the deeper structure around Prudence, though the counts are thinner than the scup — a fish here and there rather than a steady pick.

Looking ahead, the next few days should keep this pattern rolling as long as the wind stays out of the northwest and doesn't kick up out of the southeast and dirty up the upper bay. The tides are still working off the Harvest Moon push, so expect the bait to keep funneling against the points on the ebb through midweek. If there's only one window worth building a day around, it's the last two hours of the ebb at Conimicut or Nyatt Point, right as the light starts to fade — that's when the bunker get squeezed against structure and the bass stop being patient about it. Once this moon eases and the tides flatten out toward the weekend, expect the bite to spread out and get harder to predict, so the smart move is fishing the big water now while the current's still doing the work for you.
