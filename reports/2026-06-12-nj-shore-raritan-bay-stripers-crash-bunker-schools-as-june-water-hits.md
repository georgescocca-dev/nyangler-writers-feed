# Raritan Bay stripers crash bunker schools as June water hits prime temps
*Big bass feeding heavy on peanut bunker from Sandy Hook to Manasquan as bait migration peaks.*

**RARITAN BAY, NJ — June 12** — _by Joey Napolitano, Zone Writer_

The Raritan Bay is absolutely loaded right now, and I mean stacked with stripers from 28 to 40 inches that are feeding like they haven't eaten in weeks. Water temps hit that magic 68-degree mark this week, and the bunker schools are so thick you can smell them from the dock at Keyport.

I've been working the back bay from the Amboy channel markers to the Outerbridge, and it's been lights-out fishing on the moving water. These fish are crushing peanut bunker on fishfinder rigs — 3/0 circle hooks on 30-pound leader, just enough weight to get down in the 15 to 20 feet of water where the bait is stacked. The key is fishing the edges of the bunker schools, not right in the middle where the fish are spooked.

The bite has been best on the last two hours of the incoming and first hour of the ebb. That's when the current pushes the bait against the channel edges and the bass set up to ambush. I'm seeing guys limit out on keeper bass using live peanuts, but the chunk bait bite has been just as good. Fresh bunker chunks on a 6/0 circle hook, fished on a three-way rig with a 2-ounce sinker, is putting fish in the boat from dawn to about 10 AM.

What's really got me fired up is the size of these fish. We're not talking schoolies here — these are legitimate keeper bass with plenty of 32 to 36-inch fish mixed in. The biomass that moved into the bay this week is incredible, and it's all following the bunker migration that's been building since late May.

Down at Manasquan Inlet, the jetty bite has been solid for guys working the rocks on the outgoing tide. White bucktails with pork rind trailers in the 1 to 2-ounce range are getting hit hard in the 8 to 12 feet of water along the north jetty. The trick is keeping your jig moving — these fish want action, not a dead stick presentation.

The fluke bite has been decent but not spectacular. Shark River has been producing short fish on Gulp Swimming Mullets in white and chartreuse, but the keeper ratio is maybe one in ten. The better fluke action is outside in 25 to 30 feet, but you're competing with the sea bass that are absolutely crushing everything that hits bottom.

Speaking of sea bass, if you've got a New Jersey license, get out there now. The 10-fish limit runs through June 19, and these fish are stacked on every piece of structure from the Shrewsbury Rocks to the Axel Carlson Reef. Drop a clam or piece of squid on a high-low rig and you'll be bent over in seconds. I'm seeing guys boat 50 fish in a morning and keep their 10 best.

The weakfish bite has been spotty but promising. A few fish in the 16 to 20-inch range have been taken on small bucktails with Gulp baits in the back bays, especially around the Navesink River mouth on the evening tides. These fish are finicky — you need light tackle and a subtle presentation.

Looking at the moon phase, we're building toward a new moon on June 18, which means the spring tides are going to really move water through these inlets. That's when I expect the weakfish bite to really turn on, and the striper action should stay hot as long as the bunker schools hold in the bay.

The water clarity has been excellent — that gin-clear water that lets you see bottom in 15 feet. Combined with the perfect temperature gradient between the bay and ocean, we've got textbook conditions for a sustained bite.

Next week, I'm watching for the bunker to start moving south toward Barnegat Bay. When that happens, the Manasquan and Shark River inlets are going to be absolutely insane on the outgoing tides. Get your live bait game dialed in and be ready to run and gun with the schools.
