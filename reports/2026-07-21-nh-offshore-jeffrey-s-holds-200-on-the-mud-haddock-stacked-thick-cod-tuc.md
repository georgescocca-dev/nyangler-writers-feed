# Jeffrey's holds 200 on the mud: haddock stacked thick, cod tucked tight to the gravel edge
*A stable July high kept the ledge fishable all week, and the first scattered bluefin showed on sand eel patches north of Platts.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — July 21** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Two hundred and five feet on the north end of Jeffrey's Ledge, soft mud bottom, and the sounder painted haddock stacked eight to ten feet off the bottom in a wall that ran the length of two drifts. That's the number I want to see in late July — haddock don't hold tight to structure the way cod do, they sit up over the mud in loose schools and you catch them on the drift with a standard hi-lo rig, size 6/0 hooks, clam or squid strip, jigged slow off the bottom. We had a mixed bag of keeper haddock in the 22 to 26 inch class out there Tuesday, with the biggest fish coming right at the top of a slow drift as the tide started to turn.

Cod is a different game entirely, and this is where the bottom type matters more than anything else I can tell you. You want the gravel — the hard, broken bottom on the transitions where mud gives way to rock, particularly along the western edge of Jeffrey's where it drops from 180 into the deeper basin toward Scantum. That's where we found cod this week, not big numbers but quality fish, several in the 8 to 12 pound range, caught on diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid, worked right on bottom with short hops. Cod don't like to be pulled far off structure this time of year — keep the jig within a few feet of bottom and let the boat's drift do the work. Platts Bank produced similarly on its gravel patches, though it took more running around to find them concentrated versus just scattered singles.

Pollock were mixed in wherever there was current moving bait — we picked up a few nice pollock in the 8 to 15 pound class suspended mid-water over Jeffrey's, and those fish will eat a diamond jig worked faster and higher off bottom than you'd fish for cod. Hake showed up mostly at first light and last light, typical pattern, soft bites down deep in the basin areas where the mud runs 220 feet plus. Nothing to build a trip around but a welcome bonus in the box.

Mackerel have been thick enough this week that we didn't need to buy bait — small schools showing on the sounder in 40 to 60 feet as we ran out past Cape Ann, and a few passes with Sabiki rigs filled the tuna tubes before we ever got to the ledge. That mackerel, along with a good showing of sand eels working the surface north of Platts, is exactly what's pulling the first bluefin through. We had two fish come up on a spread trolled with green machines and a naked ballyhoo north of Platts Bank on Thursday, neither one stuck, but the sign was real — birds working, bait balled up tight, occasional boils. This is early for giants on the ledge; the real showing typically doesn't stack up here until September and into October when the big fish push through chasing herring and mackerel in earnest. What we're seeing now is scattered fish moving through on the sand eel bite, opportunistic, not settled in yet. If you want to put time into tuna right now, I'd stay mobile — work the edges where birds are diving rather than anchoring your hopes on one spot, and have both trolling gear and a couple of stand-up rods rigged with live mackerel ready if you find a workable pod.

We're coming off a new moon push into a first-quarter neap this week, and on the ledge that matters — less current means the drift over the mud slows down, which is actually a plus for haddock fishing because your bait stays in the strike zone longer instead of getting swept through too fast. Cod fishing on the gravel gets a little tougher with less current moving bait around, so I found myself working smaller pieces of structure more methodically rather than covering as much ground. Weather's been the real story behind the consistency — stable high pressure held through most of the week, seas running one to two feet on the ledge most days, which let us fish the outer grounds like Tillies Bank and the Stellwagen approaches without losing a day to weather. That's not something to take for granted in the Gulf of Maine in July.

Looking ahead, I'd keep working the mud on Jeffrey's for haddock while that pattern holds — it's been the most dependable fishery of the week by a good margin. For cod, stick to the gravel transitions and don't expect big numbers, just quality if you're patient and willing to move when a spot goes quiet. The tuna picture is still forming. If the sand eel patches hold through the weekend and mackerel stay thick along Cape Ann, I'd expect more scattered bluefin sign on the ledge, but it's too early to call it a pattern worth building a dedicated trip around just yet. Best case, that bait stays put through the neap and we start seeing fish stick on the troll rather than just showing and disappearing. If it scatters east toward Stellwagen like it sometimes does this time of year, I'll shift the search out that way and report back.
