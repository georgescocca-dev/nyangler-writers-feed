# Race rips fire as 52-degree water triggers bass blitz in eastern Sound
*Plum Gut and The Race delivering slot fish on bucktails as water temps hit the magic number.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — June 12** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The eastern Sound just flipped the switch. Water temps climbed from 50 to 52 degrees this week, and that two-degree jump changed everything. The Race is absolutely firing, and Plum Gut is producing consistent action on both flood and ebb tides.

I've been hammering The Race on the ebb current, working 3/4-ounce bucktails in white and chartreuse through the deep water where the current screams at 4 knots. The key is getting your jig down fast in that 100-foot zone before the rip grabs it. I'm running 30-pound braid straight to a 40-pound fluoro leader, no swivel — you need that direct connection when these fish hit in the current.

The bass are stacked in the eddies behind the main rip line. You'll see the surface boil, but the real action is 80 to 100 feet down where the current breaks over the bottom structure. I've been putting fish in the boat almost every drift when the tide's moving hard. Slot fish from 28 to 32 inches, with a few overs mixed in that go back.

Plum Gut is equally productive but requires different timing. The flood tide has been the money shot, especially the first two hours when the current builds from slack. I'm fishing the same bucktail setup but working shallower water, 40 to 60 feet. The bass are ambushing bait getting swept through the gut, so you want your jig bouncing bottom on a tight line.

What's driving this bite is simple — baitfish are moving with the warming water. Spearing are thick along the Orient Point shoreline, and I'm seeing peanut bunker getting flushed through both the Gut and The Race on the tide changes. The bass know it and they're positioned to feed.

The 52-degree mark seems to be the trigger point. Below 50, these fish were sluggish and scattered. Above 52, they're aggressive and predictable. I'm reading 52 to 53 degrees consistently now from Orient Point west to Fishers Island, with slightly cooler water on the Connecticut side.

Technique matters in this current. At The Race, I'm using a three-way rig with a 6-ounce bank sinker to hold bottom, then running an 18-inch leader to the bucktail. Let it bounce and sweep with the current — don't fight the drift. At Plum Gut, I'm power-drifting with the engine, bumping in and out of gear to control the presentation.

Bluefish are also in the mix, especially at The Race. Big gorilla blues that will test your drag and steal your bucktails if you're not careful. They're hitting the same jigs as the bass, so be ready for anything when that rod loads up.

Weakfish are starting to show in the deeper water off Fishers Island. Nothing consistent yet, but I'm marking fish on the finder in 60 to 80 feet. The full moon this Friday should trigger some serious tide movement — spring tides always get the bait moving and the predators feeding.

Looking ahead, this warming trend should continue the bite through the weekend. I'm watching for water temps to push toward 55 degrees, which typically brings the first real bunker schools into the Sound. When that happens, the surface action will explode. Until then, it's all about working the bottom with bucktails and reading the current.
