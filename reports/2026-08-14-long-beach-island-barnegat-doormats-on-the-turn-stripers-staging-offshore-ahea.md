# Barnegat Doormats on the Turn — Stripers Staging Offshore Ahead of the Run
*Trophy fluke are showing in the inlet current while early bass sign offshore hints the fall push is starting to load up.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — August 14** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

I ran the drone over the Holgate spit Monday morning before the wind came up, and the south end cut has moved again — not dramatically, but enough that the deep slough that's been running tight to the dune line for the past month has pushed about forty yards further offshore. That outer bar took a beating from the three-to-four foot swell we've had rolling through on a five-second period, and it's reshaping the whole south end. If you fished that inside trough last week and got nothing, don't blame the fish — the structure isn't where it was. I'd walk it at low light on the next low tide and re-map it before you commit a whole tide to it.

The real story this week isn't the surf, though — it's Barnegat Inlet. Fluke fishing through the inlet has stepped up in a real way, and I mean doormat-class fish mixed into the average catch, not just talk. Guys working the channel edges off the north side rocks and along the deeper cuts by the Coast Guard station are connecting on the moving tide — both stages, but the last two hours of the ebb into the bottom of the tide has been the window. Four- to six-inch Gulp swimming mullet in white or pink shad on three-quarter to one-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom where the current breaks over the humps, is the ticket. This mirrors what's been happening up in Raritan Bay all month — a genuine trophy fluke trend running down the Jersey coast, and a 12-plus pound doormat weighed at a shop up that way tells you the size class moving through right now is real. I've had two separate reports of fish over eight pounds out of the inlet in the last five days, which for mid-August is well above what we usually see this late in the season.

Back in the bay, the blowfish bite remains about as easy as fishing gets around here right now. Anchor up on the flats off Ship Bottom or in the deeper holes around Beach Haven, drop a piece of clam on a small hook under a sinker, and you'll have a bucket full inside an hour. It's not glamorous but it's a great way to get a kid or a first-timer hooked, and there's no shortage of them right now in Great Bay and up toward the Mullica flats.

Snapper blues have shown up thick in the back bay creeks — Graveling Point, the mouth of the Mullica, the marsh edges around Great Bay — and that's usually the tell that the adult bluefish class is close behind. I'm already seeing bigger blues mixed in on the ocean side, feeding on peanut bunker pushing along the beach on the outgoing. Small metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Deadly Dicks — worked fast through the wash at Holgate and the Ship Bottom jetties has been drawing strikes in the early morning hours before the sun gets high.

Now the piece I've been waiting on: striped bass. We flagged this last week — said building tidal flow into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what's playing out. I'm getting early sign of bass staging offshore of the island, holding on bait schools well outside the surf line, which is textbook pre-migration behavior. They're not in tight yet, and I wouldn't call it a bite you can plan a trip around from the beach, but boats working just outside Barnegat Light and off the north end are marking fish sitting under peanut bunker pods. This is exactly the kind of staging pattern that tends to build through the back half of August before it pushes bass tight to the beach for the fall run. If you're patient and willing to put in some scouting time, I'd be running the LRF or a slow-trolled bunker spoon outside the surf zone right now rather than pounding the beach — that's where the fish actually are.

Kingfish are still around on the ocean side sand — Beach Haven and the stretch south of Ship Bottom have been giving up a few on bloodworm or FishBites on a high-low rig fished tight to the wash, best on the incoming as the water warms through midday. Weakfish have gone quiet in the Mullica system, which tracks with the warmer water this time of year — I'd expect that bite to reawaken some in September as things cool.

Looking ahead: if this swell settles down by the weekend like the forecast suggests, I'd hit the reshaped Holgate cut on the dropping tide with a darter or a bucktail worked slow through that new trough — first crack at structure nobody's fished yet is worth the walk. The inlet fluke should keep producing as long as the tide keeps moving current over those channel humps, and I'd keep half an eye on the water outside the beach for signs those staging bass start tightening up. If the bait keeps pushing tight to the beach the way it has the last few days, don't be surprised if the first real surf blitz of the fall shows up sooner than the calendar says it should.
