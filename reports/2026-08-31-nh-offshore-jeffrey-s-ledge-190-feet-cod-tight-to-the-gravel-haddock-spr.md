# Jeffrey's Ledge, 190 feet: cod tight to the gravel, haddock spread thin on the mud
*The ledge is fishing like a normal late-August week — steady, not spectacular — while the fleet starts scouting for the first bluefin sign on top.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 31** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

One-ninety on the sounder, hard gravel kicking back a clean mark, and that's been the number all week on the north end of Jeffrey's Ledge. Cod are holding right where they should be for this time of year — tucked into the gravel patches between 170 and 210 feet, not stacked deep in big pods but scattered in twos and threes along the harder bottom where the current breaks over the rise. That's typical late-August behavior. The big pushes of fish haven't shown yet, and they won't until the water starts cooling into September, but there's enough there to make a drift worth your time if you're working bait tight to bottom.

Haddock have been the more consistent producer, and that's no surprise — they want the softer mud basins off the west side of the ledge and in the deeper cuts toward Scantum Basin, typically 220 to 260 feet. The mud holds more worms and small crustaceans this time of year, and haddock will sit on it in loose schools that move with the tide rather than hold to one rock pile. Jigging has worked better than bait on the haddock lately — a 6- to 8-ounce diamond jig or a haddock rig tipped with a small strip of squid, worked with a short lift-drop right off bottom. When you mark them thick on the screen, mark the spot and make repeat drifts; they'll school up and thin out through the day depending on tide stage.

We flagged last week that conditions would hold stable through this stretch, and that's exactly what's played out — no fronts pushing through, no big wind events, water staying clean and green rather than churned up. That stability is why the pattern hasn't shifted much drift to drift. Southwest wind has stayed light most mornings, building a bit in the afternoons, which means starting early and getting your drifts in before the chop picks up is still the smart play. Tide-wise, the moon's coming off full and the currents are running a little stronger than average, which has actually helped concentrate the haddock on the mud basins rather than let them spread out — good news for anyone marking fish and trying to stay on them.

Platts Bank has been quieter than the ledge itself this week, but it's still worth a look if you're already running that direction — pollock have been mixed in with the cod on the rocky high spots, hitting diamond jigs worked a little faster through the water column, 20 to 40 feet off bottom, rather than right on it. Pollock like a moving jig more than a bait sitting still, so if you're marking suspended fish on the meter, speed up the retrieve before you give up on the spot.

Tillies Bank has been mostly a mackerel show right now — thick schools of mackerel showing on top and mid-water, which is a good sign for bait but hasn't translated into a heavy groundfish bite out there yet this week. If you need fresh bait for cod or want to jig mackerel for the smoker, that's the spot, but for targeted cod and haddock work, the ledge and the Scantum mud have been the better bet.

On the bluefin front — not yet, and that's expected. October is when the giants show up on the ledge in numbers, working the bait pushed up along the drop-offs near Cape Ann and out toward Thatcher Island. Right now we're still a month-plus out from that pattern setting up in earnest. A few boats have reported single fish sign — birds working bait balls, the odd blast of surface activity — but nothing consistent enough to call it a bite. Worth keeping an eye on if you're running that way anyway, but don't plan a trip around it yet. The mackerel schools building on Tillies are usually the first tell that the tuna are getting interested, so that's the piece to watch over the next couple weeks.

For the next few days, expect more of the same: stable weather, light morning winds building through the afternoon, clean water, and a groundfish pattern that rewards precision over volume. If you're targeting cod, work the gravel edges on the north ledge in that 170-210 foot band and fish bait — herring or clam — right on bottom with just enough weight to hold in the current. If haddock's the target, the mud basins toward Scantum Basin are producing better numbers, and jigging is outfishing bait right now. Either way, get there before the afternoon breeze fills in, because the morning tide stage has been the more productive window all week. Nothing dramatic setting up in the short term, but nothing falling apart either — just a solid, seasonal Gulf of Maine week, and that's usually when the fishing rewards the guys who know exactly where to put the anchor.
