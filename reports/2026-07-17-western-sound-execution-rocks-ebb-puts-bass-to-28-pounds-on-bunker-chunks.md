# Execution Rocks Ebb Puts Bass to 28 Pounds on Bunker Chunks
*The bunker never left Matinecock, and the bass that are stacked under them aren't leaving either — but you've got to work the tide right to get to them.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — July 17** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Guy at the bait shop on City Island Avenue asked me Tuesday if the bass had "moved out" of the western Sound yet, like it was already August. I laughed at him. Told him to go stand on the rocks at Execution during the last two hours of the outgoing and tell me they moved out. He came back Thursday with a 28-pound fish and didn't say much else to me, just nodded. That's how it goes out here — everybody wants to declare the season over the second the calendar flips to July, and every year the Sound tells them otherwise.

Here's what's actually happening. Kings Point buoy is reading 71.2 degrees, and that's about right for what I'm feeling off the transom — warm, comfortable, bunker-holding water from the Bridge out to Matinecock. Head east toward New London and you're down into the upper 60s, which tells you the thermal gradient across this Sound right now runs steep, west to east. That matters because it's the western end — our end — that's carrying the mature, established bite. The bunker schools that piled up off Matinecock back in June haven't broken up. If anything they've thickened, pushed by the warmth, and everywhere there's a bunker school stacked deep, there's bass underneath it working the edges on the tide change.

Execution Rocks is still the most consistent spot I've got on the board. The rockpile there does what it's always done — it breaks the current on the outgoing and creates a seam bass use to ambush bait getting swept off the shoal. I'm anchoring up-current of the light on the last two hours of the ebb, dropping bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig with 3 ounces of lead to hold bottom in 20 to 25 feet, and letting it sit. Fresh bunker beats frozen right now, no question — if you can snag a few off the surface schools before you set up, do it. Guys running live bunker on a 5/0 circle hook, lightly weighted, drifted right through that seam, are getting the bigger fish — the 28-pounder and a couple in the low 30s came off live bait, not chunks.

Stepping Stones is still doing its spring-highway thing, just slower and more tide-dependent now that we're deep into summer. Early morning, right at the top of the tide before it turns, I've had better luck there than mid-day. Bucktails — the white 1.5-ounce with a white Gulp trailer — worked slow along bottom on the drift past the light have picked at slot fish, nothing huge, mostly 26 to 30 inches, but steady enough to call it a real pattern, not a fluke.

Fluke fishing has been the honest disappointment of the week. Hempstead Harbor and the flats off Manhasset Bay are holding fish, but they're short — lots of 15 and 16 inchers, a few keepers mixed in if you cover water. I fished four drifts off Prospect Point Wednesday and only boxed one fish over 18 inches. Chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets on a half-ounce bucktail, dragged dead slow on the last of the incoming, is still the right presentation — the fish just aren't sized up yet the way they were this time last year. If you want fluke for the table, I'd fish the deeper edges of Hempstead Harbor on a strong tide rather than wasting time on the flats right now.

Porgies have been the quiet bright spot. Execution Rocks and the rockpiles off Hart Island are loaded — small hooks, sandworms or clam, right on bottom in 15 to 25 feet, and you can fill a bucket in an hour if you've got the patience for the smaller fish mixed in. Good action for anyone bringing kids out, and a nice break from working bass structure all day.

Weakfish are showing in small numbers off Manhasset Bay and up into Little Neck — nothing to build a trip around, but if you're jigging bucktails for bass and connect with a weakfish instead, handle it gently and let it go. Numbers are still thin enough out here that I'd rather see them rebuild than end up on a stringer.

Bluefish have been a bit of a ghost this week compared to earlier in the season — I've heard of a few slammers working bait off Throgs Neck at first light, chopping bunker on top, but by the time I got out there Monday it had shut off. Worth a look at dawn if you're already headed that way, but I wouldn't plan a whole trip around it right now.

Looking ahead, I don't see a reason for that Matinecock bunker school to break up anytime soon — the water's holding warm, the bait's thick, and the bass under it have been consistent enough three days running that I'd call it more than luck. If the wind stays out of the south and doesn't churn things up, I'd fish Execution on the outgoing again this weekend, live bunker if you can get it, chunks if you can't. Keep an eye on the tide charts — the bite windows are tightening to that last two hours of ebb, and if you show up on the wrong stage of water you'll wonder what all the fuss was about.
