# Yellowfin blitz fires at the break as Gulf Stream eddy spins up warm water
*72-degree water at the canyon edge is stacking bait and firing up the best tuna bite in weeks.*

**WILMINGTON CANYON — June 24** — _by Capt. Dana Cooper, Zone Writer_

The Gulf Stream finally decided to cooperate this week, spinning off a beautiful warm eddy that's sitting right on the 100-fathom line and lighting up the yellowfin bite like I haven't seen since early May. Water temps jumped from the mid-60s to a solid 72 degrees at the break, and that 8-degree gradient is acting like a magnet for everything with fins.

I've been running the same pattern for three days straight — trolling the temperature break from the northeast corner down to the southern wall, working that sweet spot where the 72-degree blue water meets the cooler inshore stuff. The yellowfin are stacked up in that transition zone, and they're not being picky. Cedar plugs in natural and pink have been absolute money, especially the smaller 6-inch models pulled on the long riggers. The key is keeping your speed right around 7.5 knots and watching your fishfinder for that thermocline at 45 feet.

Tuesday morning was the standout trip. Hit the break at first light and had three yellowfin in the boat before 8 AM, all fish in the 40 to 60-pound class. The bite window was tight — maybe two hours of steady action — but when they turned on, it was game over. Ballyhoo on the flat lines produced the biggest fish, a beautiful 58-pounder that took a perfectly rigged bally behind a pink and white Islander. The wahoo showed up around 10 AM, typical pattern for this time of year. Lost two good fish to cutoffs before I switched to wire, then boated a nice 35-pounder on a blue and white Yo-Zuri Bonita.

The chunking bite has been solid too, especially on the evening trips. Once the trolling bite dies off around noon, I've been anchoring up on the 30-fathom line and putting out a slick. Fresh butterfish chunks on 8/0 circles have been the ticket, fished on 50-pound fluorocarbon leaders about 15 feet under the surface. The yellowfin come up in the slick just as the sun starts to drop, and they're aggressive. Had one evening where we put six fish in the boat in 90 minutes, all on chunks.

What's really got me excited is the bait situation. The water's been thick with juvenile mahi, those little chickens in the 2 to 5-pound range, and where you find baby mahi, the big pelagics aren't far behind. I'm seeing scattered weed lines holding decent fish too — nothing huge, but schoolies in the 8 to 12-pound class that are perfect for the dinner table. Small feathers and spoons worked through the weed have been producing steady action.

The bluefin situation is interesting. I'm marking fish on the bottom machine in 80 to 120 feet of water, but they're being stubborn. Had one morning where we raised three fish on the troll but couldn't get a hookup. They're there, just not feeding aggressively yet. I suspect once this new moon cycle kicks in and we get some stronger tidal movement, those fish are going to turn on.

Conditions have been about as good as you can ask for — light winds, 1-foot seas, and that gorgeous blue water pushed right up against the shelf. The ride out has been a pleasure, and with fuel prices where they are, that 90-minute run to the break doesn't hurt the wallet as much when you're burning minimal fuel in calm conditions.

Looking ahead to this weekend, I'm watching the weather closely. We've got a weak front approaching that might stir things up a bit, but nothing that should shut down the bite. If anything, a little wind might help concentrate the bait even tighter against that temperature break. The new moon is Friday, which should start pulling some stronger currents through the canyon and potentially fire up those bluefin that have been playing hard to get.

My game plan for the coming week is simple — keep working that temperature break while it's there, because these eddies don't last forever. When the Gulf Stream shifts, and it will, that 72-degree water could disappear as quickly as it showed up. Until then, I'm making hay while the sun shines and putting clients on some of the best yellowfin action we've seen all season.
