# Barnegat Bay fluke slide deep as the shallows hit 80 — bluefish keep kids busy, weakfish thin out
*Heat's got the flounder hugging channel edges and bridge structure while snapper blues save the trip for everybody else.*

**FORKED RIVER, NJ — July 31** — _by Gus Papadakis, Zone Writer_

I remember a summer back in the late '80s, right around this same stretch of July, when my old skipper on the Point Pleasant boats told me something I never forgot: "Gus, when the mud flats start steaming, the fluke go looking for the basement." We were anchored off Tices Shoal that morning, watching heat shimmer come up off the sedge at low tide, and sure enough the flounder we'd been picking off the flats all June had vanished. We found them that afternoon stacked in eighteen feet of water on the edge of Double Creek Channel, and I've chased that same pattern every scorching July since.

That's exactly where we are right now. The shallows in the back bay — up around Toms River, the flats off Good Luck Point, the skinny water inside Oyster Creek — are pushing close to 80 degrees under this stretch of sun and southwest wind, and the fluke have done what they always do when it gets like this: they've packed up and moved into the deeper channels and bridge structure where the water stays a few degrees more livable. Double Creek Channel has been the most consistent address, 15 to 22 feet on the drift, and the Route 37 bridge pilings on the Seaside side are holding fish tight to the current seams. Standard program — 3/4-ounce white bucktail tipped with a chunk of squid or a 4-inch chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullet, dragged slow along bottom on the last two hours of the ebb when the current's still got some shoulder to it. We're getting a mix of keepers and shorts, nothing blitz-style, but a patient drift will put two or three fish over 18 inches in the box for anybody willing to work the structure instead of just soaking bait in open water.

Weakfish have been the disappointment of the week, if I'm honest. Back in May those sedge banks down toward Barnegat Light were holding solid pods of tiderunners, but the heat's scattered them — I'm hearing about isolated pockets on the drop-offs near the Inlet channel edges, mostly at first light or the last hour of dusk when the water's cooled a touch, and they're not schooled up tight enough to call it a pattern yet. If you want a shot, work a 1/4-ounce chartreuse or pink jighead tipped with a strip of squid right on the lip where the channel drops from six feet to twelve, and be ready for it to be one or two fish and done.

What's actually saving trips right now is the bluefish. Snapper blues and small cocktail fish are thick throughout the bay — off the docks in Forked River, along the bulkheads at Barnegat Light, out at Tices Shoal — and they'll hit just about anything that flashes. Small Kastmasters, Deadly Dicks, or a piece of bunker on a snapper rig under a bobber is about as easy as fishing gets, and it's the best thing going for anybody with kids aboard who just want bent rods. We flagged this pattern last week and said stable, hot conditions would keep it running steady through the weekend — that's held up exactly as expected, and there's no reason to think it breaks unless the wind shifts hard out of the northeast.

Striped bass are around but they've gone the way they always do this time of year — deep and nocturnal. The water's simply too warm during daylight for them to feed comfortably, so the better fish are holding on structure in the deeper stretches near Barnegat Inlet and feeding mainly after dark or right on the tide change at dawn. Bunker chunks fished on the bottom near the Coast Guard station channel, or a live eel drifted through the inlet rips on the tail of the outgoing, have accounted for a few decent fish in the 28 to 34-inch range for guys willing to fish the graveyard shift. It's not a numbers game right now — it's one good bite a trip if you put in the time.

Blowfish are still there for anybody who wants a relaxed afternoon — sandy bottom pockets inside Barnegat Light and along the ICW near Waretown are holding good numbers on small pieces of clam or bloodworm fished on a two-hook bottom rig. Kingfish are mixing in on the same bottom, especially where there's a little sand and shell mix, so it pays to keep a rod rigged light while you're anchored up for blowfish.

Tides are easing off the full moon push toward neap over the next several days, which usually softens the current just enough to spread bait out rather than concentrate it — expect the fluke bite to stay steady rather than explosive, and the bluefish to keep doing what they're doing regardless. What this bay really needs is a passing front to knock a few degrees off those shallow flats; if we get one, even a glancing shot of rain and cooler air midweek, I'd expect the flounder to push back up onto Tices Shoal and the flats off Good Luck Point, and the weakfish bite to tighten back up on the drop-offs. Until then, fish the channels, fish the structure, and don't waste tide chasing fish that packed up and left the shallows for a reason.
