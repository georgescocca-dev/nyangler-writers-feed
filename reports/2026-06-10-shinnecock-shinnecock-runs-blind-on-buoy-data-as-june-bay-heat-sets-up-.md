# Shinnecock runs blind on buoy data as June bay heat sets up the night dropping tide
*With NDBC offshore stations dark and the SST package three days stale, inlet thermal differential remains the controlling variable for the after-dark bass window.*

**HAMPTON BAYS, NY — June 10** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The oceanographic picture this morning is thinner than I'd like. The Dr. Fish analyst flags multiple data gaps that hit this beat directly — wind fields at 44025 south of Fire Island and 44097 to the east are incomplete, and the offshore trio at 44017, 44039, and 44040 is offline, which means we have no hard read on the shelf approach or on what the southwest sea state is doing to the inlet mouth. The published SST package is dated June 8, two to three days behind real time, and there is no live inshore buoy reading I can put a number on for Shinnecock Bay itself today. What I can say with confidence from the seasonal arc: by the second week of June the back bay west of Ponquogue is running well into the mid-60s and pushing toward 70°F on the afternoon flood, while the ocean side of the inlet remains noticeably cooler — that thermal step across the jetties is the engine driving everything right now.

That differential is what matters for the beat. When the bay heats through the day and then dumps cooler-than-bay, warmer-than-ocean water out through the inlet on the drop, you get a conveyor belt of disoriented bait pinned against the rip lines off the points. Slot-class striped bass — the 28- to 31-inch fish that have been the bread and butter of the night fishery — stage on the ocean side of the east jetty and along the sand lobe just outside the west jetty, feeding into the outflow. Overslot fish are around but they are mostly a deeper, structure-oriented bite working the scour holes inside the throat. Fluke have moved off the spring channel edges and onto the flats from the Ponquogue Bridge west toward Tiana, and the weakfish window at the bridge pilings is now open on the slack-to-early-incoming after dark.

The inlet bite has clearly shifted into its true June pattern. Trolled and swum plugs worked low and slow on the outgoing — the classic Shinnecock night presentation — are doing the work on the slot fish, and that pattern has been consistent enough through late May and into early June that I'd treat it as the baseline rather than a hot tip. Daytime in the inlet is largely a bluefish and short-bass shuffle right now, with the better grade of bass holding off until the sun is off the water. Fluke drifters working the bay are finding keepers without monsters — fat, healthy two- to four-pound fish on the drift between markers along the Ponquogue channel edges and on the shallower humps west of the bridge, with bucktail-and-Gulp combinations outproducing straight squid strips on the harder-running tides.

Ponquogue Bridge itself is the spot I'd be watching most carefully over the next week. Weakfish numbers have been quietly building in the back of the bay since late May, and the bridge lights on a moving tide after dark are textbook ambush water once bay temperatures lock above 65°F. Small soft plastics on light jigheads, worked in the shadow lines, will tell you within twenty minutes whether the tiderunners have set up shop. The Shinnecock Canal remains the back door — bait and bass moving between the Peconic and the bay through the lock cycle are a real factor this time of year, and a westbound push of bunker through the canal typically lights up the north end of the bay first.

Looking ahead three to five days, the controlling variables are wind direction and the moon. Any sustained southwest will stack warmer surface water against the south shore and accelerate the bay's thermal climb, which is good for weakfish and fluke but can shut the daytime inlet bite down hard by mid-morning. A clocking northwest would flatten the inlet and extend the productive window on both sides of low slack. Watch for the bay to cross the 68°F threshold this week if it hasn't already — that is the trigger for weakfish to commit to the bridge in numbers, and for the first real push of snapper-chasing bluefish to show in the back. Until the offshore buoys come back online and the SST package catches up, I'm fishing the tide stage and the thermal logic rather than the screen.

The move this week is simple: be on the ocean side of the inlet for the last two hours of the drop into low slack after dark, be at the Ponquogue pilings on the early incoming, and drift the bay flats during daylight. The data is thin but the pattern is not.
