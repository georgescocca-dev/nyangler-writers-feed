# Great Point Rip Holds Its Breath — Bonito Close, Bass Never Left
*The Sound's still stacked with stripers on the structure while every rip chaser on the island keeps one eye east for that first bonito flash.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — August 8** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning, half-tide, I'm sitting on the north side of Great Point watching a tern wheel and drop twice, then just... quit. No follow-up birds, no nervous water, nothing. That's been the story of my bonito watch for two weeks now — little false alarms, single birds working bait that never turns into the real show. I've had my 7-weight rigged and ready in the rocket launcher so long the cork's starting to fade. Still no bonito in hand off the Point. But I'll tell you what — I don't think it's far off, and I'm not the only one holding my breath.

Here's why. The bonito push that's been working up through Rhode Island — Watch Hill, Sakonnet, that corridor — has already crossed into Buzzards Bay. That's the leading edge of the run, and it's pointed right at us. We flagged this last week, said the August 12 new moon would be the trigger that builds enough tidal current to push bait hard through the rips and finally bring bonito and albies into range out here, and that's exactly the mechanism setting up now. The moon's still building, current through Muskeget and the Broad Sound side of the Shoals is getting stronger every tide, and that's the kind of flow that concentrates sand eels and silversides right where the predators want them. If I had to bet on when the first bonito gets weighed at a Nantucket dock, I'd put my money on the back half of this week into the weekend, once that new moon current really starts ripping through Great Point and around Monomoy's elbow.

Until then, the bass have been doing all the work, and honestly, they've been worth the trip on their own. Nantucket Sound itself — Horseshoe Shoal, the edges around Wasque, the rips off the south side of the Shoals proper — has a solid keeper-and-short mix right now, and it's not subtle. These fish are sitting tight to structure, current-protected water, not out in the open exposed stuff. That's the tell. Bait's concentrating where there's current break and bottom relief, not out on the flat, featureless middle of the Shoals, and the bass know it. I've been running umbrella rigs and bunker spoons over Horseshoe on the last two hours of the ebb, marking fish stacked on the edges of the drop where the current stacks bait against the shoulder. Bucktails — the 2-ounce white ones I always default to out here — worked slow along bottom on the up-current side have been just as effective, especially for the shorts mixed in tight to the sandy humps.

What's not working right now is fluke on the open Nantucket Shoals grounds. I made the run out there twice this week hoping for a repeat of some of the better days we've had, and it's just underwhelming — a few undersized fish, nothing worth staying on. The contrast with the Sound couldn't be clearer. Out on the open Shoals there's no current protection, no structure to hold bait tight, so everything's spread thin and moving. Inside the Sound, on the other hand, structure and current do the concentrating for you. If you want fluke this week, don't waste the fuel running to the open Shoals — work the edges of the channels around Monomoy, the drop-offs near Chatham inlet, drifting Gulp on light bucktails, chartreuse and white both producing. Slower drifts on the last of the outgoing have been better than fighting a hard tide.

Scup fishing has quietly been solid too, and it's an easy one to overlook when everybody's chasing albies and bass. Anywhere there's hard structure in the Sound — rock piles, old wreckage, the rubble edges around Monomoy Point — you can anchor up, drop squid strips or clam on a simple two-hook bottom rig, and load a cooler in an hour if the tide's moving right. It's not glamorous but it's about as sure a thing as this Sound offers right now, and it's a good fallback for anybody who spent the morning watching empty water for bonito that never showed.

Wind's been the other factor worth mentioning — we've had a mostly manageable pattern this week, nothing that's blown the rips out, which has helped keep the Great Point and Monomoy current lines fishable on both the ebb and the early flood. If that holds through the weekend and the new moon keeps building tidal strength like it should, I think the pieces are close to lining up. Warmer water pushing up from the south, current strengthening through the rips, bait getting squeezed into tight seams — that's the recipe. It's not here yet. But every rip chaser I've talked to at the dock this week has the same look on his face: watching, waiting, rod rigged.

My plan for the coming days is simple. I'll keep working Great Point and the Monomoy rips on the moving tides, especially around the new moon push on the 11th and 12th, because if the bonito show up anywhere first, it's going to be in that kind of current. In the meantime, the bass on Horseshoe Shoal and the scup on the structure are paying the bills. If you're headed out this week, don't skip the sure thing chasing the maybe — but keep that light tackle rigged, because I don't think we're watching empty water much longer.
