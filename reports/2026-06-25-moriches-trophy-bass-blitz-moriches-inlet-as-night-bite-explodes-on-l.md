# Trophy bass blitz Moriches Inlet as night bite explodes on live spot
*Inlet's shifting bathymetry concentrates bait as summer patterns lock in across the system.*

**MORICHES, NY — June 25** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

The inlet's running like a machine right now, and the bass know it. What started as scattered reports of decent fish two weeks back has turned into a full-blown trophy bite that's got every serious striper hunter making the drive out here after dark. I'm talking fish in the 35- to 45-inch class, with enough 20-pounders mixed in to keep your drag singing.

The pattern's textbook Moriches: live spot fished tight to the inlet's north jetty on the outgoing tide, right where the channel cuts deepest against the rocks. The key depth is that 18- to 22-foot zone where the current creates a natural feeding lane. Fish a Carolina rig with a 6/0 circle hook, 3-foot fluorocarbon leader, and keep that spot swimming just off bottom. The bass are staging in that deeper water during the day, then moving up to ambush bait as the tide pulls out.

What's driving this bite is the inlet's unique hydraulics right now. June's spring tides have been scouring the channel deeper on the north side while depositing sand on the south — classic Moriches behavior after we had those northeast storms in May. That deeper cut is acting like a conveyor belt, funneling spearing, rainfish, and juvenile bunker right into the bass's wheelhouse. Water temperature in the inlet's holding steady at 68 degrees, perfect for keeping both bait and bass active through the night hours.

The timing's critical. Best action starts about an hour after sunset and runs through the first two hours of the outgoing tide. Miss that window and you're looking at scattered fish instead of the steady pick that's been happening. I've been watching the inlet drain at 9 PM with the new moon cycle, and that's when the water really starts moving with purpose.

Cupsogue Beach has been producing during daylight hours for anglers working the surf. The sandbar structure about 150 yards off the beach is holding fluke in the 3- to 5-pound range, with enough keepers mixed in to make the walk worthwhile. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are getting it done in that 12- to 15-foot trough behind the bar. The key is working the edges where the current creates that slight color change — that's where the fluke are positioned to intercept bait getting swept along.

Back in the bay proper, the shallow-water fluke bite has been surprisingly consistent for this time of year. The 6- to 8-foot flats between Tuthill Point and the marina are holding good numbers of fish, including some legitimate doormat candidates. I'm seeing reports of fish pushing 7 pounds coming from that skinny water, which tells me the bait concentration is exceptional right now. Tiny jigs — we're talking 1/4-ounce heads with 3-inch Gulp — worked slow on the drift are producing the quality fish.

Weakfish have started showing in the deeper holes around Smith Point, though it's still early in their summer pattern. The fish are small — mostly 14- to 16-inchers — but they're a good sign that the ecosystem's cycling properly. Look for this bite to improve as we move into July and the water temperature climbs another few degrees.

Looking ahead, this weekend's full moon is going to supercharge the tidal flow through the inlet. That means bigger volumes of water moving faster, which should concentrate even more bait in the feeding zones. I'm expecting the bass bite to peak Friday and Saturday nights, especially if we get those light southwest winds that have been setting up the perfect drift conditions. The inlet's primed for what could be the best week of striper fishing we've seen this season.
