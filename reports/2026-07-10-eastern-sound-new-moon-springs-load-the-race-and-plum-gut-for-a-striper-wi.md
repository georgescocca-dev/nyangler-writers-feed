# New moon springs load the Race and Plum Gut for a striper window through Monday
*Current's ripping again, fluke are pushing out of the bays into the guts, and the first bonito signs are creeping toward Fishers Island.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 10** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Last week was a build-up week — nothing explosive, just the Sound loading up for what's coming. Wind sat out of the southwest most days, laid a warm skin on the surface, and the current through The Race and Plum Gut kept grinding on the standard cycle without much push behind it. That's neap-adjacent water — current's there, but it hadn't sharpened yet. Fluke that had been sitting comfortable in the back bays started sliding toward the channels as bay water crept into the high 70s, which is exactly what you'd expect. Warm, soft water pushes flatfish toward better-oxygenated structure, and the inlets and guts are where that structure lives.

This week the picture changes fast. We're running into new moon springs, peak alignment landing Friday through Monday, and that means the current through Plum Gut and The Race is about to load up hard — 4 to 5 knots on the stronger stages, maybe more at the Race depending on wind. Bigger tides mean bigger eddies, which means bass stack harder on the back sides of the rips instead of getting strung out in open current. The piece that's got my attention is the PM flood lining up with sunset — first time that's happened since the June full moon. When high stand and last light converge, bass move up out of the depths to feed the moment the light drops, and that window is short, brutal, and worth being in position for an hour before it opens.

Striper fishing right now is best read as good-not-great — solid if you're in the right piece of structure on the right stage of tide, quiet everywhere else. I've been working the eddy line off the Orient Point rocks on the last two hours of the ebb, throwing 2-ounce Hab's Devils and glide-style pencils into the seam where the fast water meets the slick behind the boulders. Fish have been in the 28 to 34-inch range, a few pushing 20 pounds mixed in, but you have to be patient — two, three casts of nothing, then a violent take right where the foam line collapses. Night bite off Fishers Island's west end has been better than the boat bite in daylight; live eels drifted on a fish-finder rig through the down-current side of the reef at the top of the tide have put a couple of better fish in the 30-pound class on the deck. With the springs building, I'd expect that current to get meaner and the eddies to stack heavier through the weekend — that's where I'm putting my time Friday and Saturday night.

Fluke are the more consistent story right now. As bay water keeps cooking, that migration toward the inlets and guts is only going to get stronger, and the upwelled ocean water pushing into Plum Gut on the outgoing — running closer to 71 degrees against warmer water inshore — is prime fluke water. Best drifts have been 25 to 35 feet on the outgoing tide right at the mouth of the Gut, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow with just enough sweep to keep contact with bottom. Fish to 5 pounds, nothing that's going to make the wall yet, but a solid limit is realistic if you put in the drift time and don't leave once you find the right depth break.

Bluefish have been riding shotgun with the bait pushed through the rips — snapper-sized fish mostly, but there are pockets of 3 to 6-pounders showing up wherever bunker or peanut schools get squeezed by the current at Plum Gut and the eastern edge of The Race. Top-water poppers worked fast through the foam at first light have been drawing violent strikes; if you're throwing plugs for bass at night, don't be surprised when a blue cuts the leader instead.

Porgies are exactly where they should be for mid-July — Fishers Island reef structure and the rock piles off Orient are holding good numbers, bottom rigs with squid strips or clam, 15 to 25 feet on the slower stages of the tide when the current's not ripping too hard to hold bottom. Easy, reliable action if the bass window isn't lining up with your schedule. Blackfish season is closed through early August, so that's off the table until then. Weakfish — I'll be honest, there's nothing to report. Water's warm enough on paper, but the reports just aren't there, and I'm not going to send anyone chasing a fish that isn't showing up.

The piece I'm watching hardest for the back half of the week is what's happening east of us. The cold wall that's normally held between Block Island Sound and Rhode Island Sound has broken down, and that's opening a corridor for bonito to spread west of their usual grounds. If that warm water keeps pushing, Fishers Island's eastern rips could see the first bonito signs of the season before month's end — small, fast, and worth having a bag of Deadly Dicks rigged if you're already out there for bass. Best case this week: the Friday-through-Monday flood-sunset window delivers on the bigger bass while the current's loaded, and the fluke keep stacking in Plum Gut on the outgoing. If the bass window underperforms — and springs this strong can sometimes blow the current out too hard to fish clean — I've got the fluke drift as Plan B, and porgies as the fallback that never really lets you down.
