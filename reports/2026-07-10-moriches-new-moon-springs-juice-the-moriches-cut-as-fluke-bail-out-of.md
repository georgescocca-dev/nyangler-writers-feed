# New moon springs juice the Moriches cut as fluke bail out of a hot bay
*Great South Bay-style warm-up is pushing fluke and bait through the inlet just as bigger tides start scouring the throat — here's where to put the boat and the waders this week.*

**MORICHES INLET, NY — July 10** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what shallow water does in July — it cooked. Surface temps in the back bay were pushing into the high 70s, and while I don't have a hard number off Tuthill Point, the behavior told the story: fluke that were scattered over the flats and eelgrass two weeks ago have been funneling toward the channels, the inlet throat, and the ocean side where upwelled water is running cooler and better oxygenated. That's not a guess — it's the same pattern Great South Bay is showing right now, and Moriches Bay is built the same way: skinny, sun-baked, and quick to push its fish toward moving water when the flats get uncomfortable. If you were still working the marsh edges last week and getting picked at, that's why.

This week the big variable is the moon. We're building toward new-moon spring tides, with the peak alignment landing Friday through Monday (July 11-13). That means more water moving through the inlet on both ends of the cycle — bigger ebb, bigger flood, more current velocity through the throat between Cupsogue and the west jetty. For an inlet system that's still settling into its post-storm geometry, that's meaningful. More current means more scour on the bar, more bait getting pulled through the cut on the outgoing, and — critically — more current lane for bass and blues to sit in and let the buffet come to them. The flip side is safety: spring tides through a shifting inlet like this move fast and standing waves build quick on the ebb against any east wind. Respect it.

Fluke are the story right now, and the pattern is inlet and ocean, not bay flats. The best drifts have been the main channel off Cupsogue Coast Guard station and down through the throat toward Pikes Beach, working 12 to 18 feet of water on the last two hours of the outgoing. Bucktails in the 3/4-ounce range, white or chartreuse, teased with a 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet, dragged slow and bounced off bottom — that's put keepers in the box, though it's not a rip-your-arms-off bite. More like two or three quality fish per drift session if you're covering ground and staying tight to the deeper cuts where the current stacks bait against the drop. Ocean side, working just outside the bar in that upwelled 70-71 degree water, has been the more consistent producer for size — a few fish pushing 4 and 5 pounds coming from anglers working the deeper scour holes right off the jetty rocks on the outgoing push.

Striped bass are still holding at the inlet jetties, and with the spring tide building this weekend, I'd expect the outgoing to get more aggressive about pulling bait past the rocks at dawn and dusk. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig tight to the base of the east jetty has worked for the guys willing to sit through a tide change, and swimming plugs — the SP Minnow and similar — worked the seam where the jetty current meets the open bar have picked up fish in that low-light window. Nothing epic, but a consistent evening program if you're patient and pick your tide.

Bluefish have been showing in pods off Cupsogue surf, chasing bait pushed out by the bay's outgoing water meeting the ocean. Poppers and diamond jigs worked the wash line during the first two hours of outgoing have connected, though it's spotty — some mornings the blues are stacked up thick enough to keep you busy for an hour, other mornings you walk the beach and see nothing but bait skittering with nothing behind it. Worth checking at first light before the pressure builds.

Here's something newer worth watching closely: this is the tail end of the seasonal window for bonito and Spanish mackerel showing up on the ocean side of Moriches and Shinnecock, and the early signs have been there — small, fast schools working clean incoming water outside the bar. If you're a surfcaster with small metal in the bag — Deadly Dicks, half-ounce Kastmasters — this is the week to bring them along on a Cupsogue session. That window's closing fast, so I wouldn't wait past this weekend to check it.

Weakfish remain the disappointment of the summer. Water's been warm enough for a while now, and the fish just haven't shown in the numbers the temperature would suggest they should. I'm not writing them off, but I'm also not sending anyone out with weakfish as the primary target. If you pick one up, it'll be a bonus — likely a night bite around the channel markers on bucktail and a strip of squid or sandworm, not a program to build a trip around.

Porgies are steady and unspectacular in the bay proper, around Tuthill Point and the deeper holes off East Moriches, standard high-low rigs with clam. Blackfish season's closed this time of year in these waters, so that's a fall conversation.

Looking ahead: I'd fish the Friday-Saturday max ebb at the inlet throat for fluke, work the last two hours of outgoing before the tide goes fully slack, and keep an eye on wind direction — if it swings onshore against that spring ebb, the bar will stand up and you'll want to fish the inside of the jetty instead of fighting the cut. If the bonito window holds even one more week, Sunday morning at Cupsogue on clean incoming water is where I'd put my money. Plan B if that doesn't pan out: back to the fluke drift, because that pattern's proven itself two weeks running now.
