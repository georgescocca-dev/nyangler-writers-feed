# Aviation Road Launch: Blues Crash Bunker Under the Marine Parkway Bridge
*With the ocean stacked up and messy, the smart play this week is inside water — and the bay is holding up its end of the bargain.*

**FLOYD BENNETT FIELD, NY — August 7** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The ocean's been throwing a fit all week — 3.6-foot swell at 6 seconds isn't dangerous, but it's the kind of lumpy, disorganized water that turns Rockaway Beach and the Breezy Point surf into a workout instead of a fishery. That's the read that should decide your access point this week, and it points inside. If you've got a kayak and a permit, Aviation Road at Floyd Bennett Field is the launch — it drops you into protected water with a straight run toward the Marine Parkway Bridge and the Rockaway Inlet channel edges without ever having to fight ocean chop. If you don't have the Fishing Access Pass — and a lot of folks don't, since 2026 sold out on the NPS lots months ago — Canarsie Pier is the free alternative, no permit, and it still puts you on real bay structure with a rod's reach of the channel.

The bunker have been thick in the bay all summer, and this week that's paying off exactly the way it should. We flagged this pattern a few weeks back — that wherever bunker pods hold up, the blues would turn violent on top — and that's precisely what's showing in the Rockaway Inlet and around the bridge shadows on the last two hours of the outgoing. Cocktail blues in the 2-4 pound range are busting bunker schools hard enough to see it from a hundred yards out, and when that happens, don't overthink the presentation. A bucktail treble snagged through a bunker and free-lined into the chaos, or a simple Hopkins/diamond jig worked erratically through the melee, will get bit fast. If the surface action shuts off, drop a chunk on a fish-finder rig to the bottom — there are usually blues holding under the bait even when the top window closes.

The striped bass are doing what they do at that bridge on the outgoing — staging in the current seams off the pilings and the rip that forms as the tide dumps out through the inlet. Depths there run 15 to 25 feet depending on where you sit relative to the main channel, and the bass are working the edges where the fast water meets the slow, not the middle of the rip itself. Bucktails in the 3/4 to 1-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked with a slow lift-and-drop right along bottom, have been the more consistent producer than straight bait soak this week. Live bunker on a fish-finder rig still gets it done if you can net or snag your own — the bass haven't turned that down yet.

Fluke and weakfish are the other half of the story, and this is another one we called early — the moving-tide-around-structure pattern in the bay has held steady through the stable weather window, and it's still the reliable script. Grassy Bay and the flats off Ruffle Bar are giving up fluke in the 16 to 20-inch range on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with a strip of squid, drifted slow on 3/4-ounce bucktails as the tide swings. Weakfish are mixing in around the same structure, particularly tight to the channel edges near the bridge on the last of the incoming — nothing to build a trip around exclusively, but a welcome bonus when they show, and a fish worth handling gently given how thin that population has been running the last several seasons.

Porgies are steady but unspectacular over the skinny flats and channel bottoms inside the bay — sandworms or clam on a hi-lo rig, small hooks, patience. Not a headline fish this week, but a solid backup when the blues go quiet or the wind picks up enough to make bridge fishing uncomfortable.

Honest assessment: this isn't an explosive week, it's a solid one. The bay is doing the heavy lifting while the ocean sorts itself out, and anglers willing to work the inside water are getting rewarded for it. The mistake would be fighting the surf at Fort Tilden or Breezy Point just because that's where the legend lives — right now the fish and the comfortable water are both inside.

Looking ahead, keep an eye on that ocean swell. If it lays down toward the back half of next week, the surf at Fort Tilden and Breezy Point should come back into play, especially on the outgoing as the groins start concentrating bait again. Until then, the bridge, the inlet channel edges, and the bay flats are where I'd put my time. The bunker aren't going anywhere yet, which means the blues won't either, and as water temps hold in the mid-to-upper 70s, the fluke and weakfish bite on the moving tide should stay dependable right through the stable weather window. September's fall push is still a few weeks out, but this bay pattern is a solid bridge to get there.
