# Western Sound settles into early-June slot pattern as bunker stack the mid-Sound rockpiles
*With the analyst's live buoy feed dark today, June 3 SST imagery and bait-class signals point to a structure-and-tide game from Execution Rocks to Matinecock.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — June 5** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The primary oceanographic feed came in thin this morning — the analyst flagged no live tide-station or buoy readings in today's package, and the most recent published SST imagery in the catalog is dated June 3. That's a two-day gap on the satellite side and a full blackout on Kings Point real-time numbers, so the read on the Western Sound has to lean on the trajectory the data was already on: a Sound that crossed the 60-degree threshold in the second half of May, surface temperatures climbing into the low-to-mid 60s on the June 3 chart inside the Throgs Neck-to-Execution line, and a soft thermal gradient between the warmer harbor water of Little Neck, Manhasset and Hempstead and the cooler, better-flushed mid-Sound. Wind has been manageable, seas inside Execution are running tight, and there is no evidence of a fresh upwelling event to scramble the pattern.

That thermal setup is exactly where the Western Sound wants to be in early June. Bait is the controlling variable now, not temperature — adult bunker have set up on the mid-Sound rockpiles and along the deeper edges off Matinecock Point, and the resident slot-class bass have stopped chasing and started camping. The fishery this week is a structure game on moving water: Execution Rocks on the back half of the outgoing, the Stepping Stones light on the first two hours of the flood, and the deep edge off Matinecock whenever the tide is actually doing something. Overslot fish are around but they are the exception; the meat of the class is 28 to 33 inches, and that is what trolled mojos, three-way bunker spoons, and snag-and-drop livies are connecting with.

The on-the-water intelligence over the past ten days reinforces it. The trolling crowd working the mid-Sound has been into slot fish on mojos and spoons with almost no shorts mixed in, which tells you the year-class structure here is mature and the bait is large — peanut-sized profiles showing in the bays back that up, and it is unusual to see that size class this early. Jigging marked fish near the harbor mouths and lighthouses has produced as well, including egg-laden females still holding roe, which means the spawning push out of the western tributaries is not fully unwound yet and you still have post-spawn fish refueling hard on whatever they can corner against structure.

The fluke side of the beat is honest but not generous. The Oyster Bay-to-Lloyd Neck drift and the ground off the CSH light have been producing shorts with the occasional measurable fish, and the lack of dense bait pods on the sand has kept the bite from turning on. Drifts have been short and the better fish have come on jigs tipped with Gulp rather than on classic bucktail-and-strip presentations — a sign the fish are sitting tighter to structure than to open sand, which is consistent with water that is still organizing thermally. Porgies are setting up on the harder bottom from Hart Island east and the blackfish window is closed, so the bottom rotation right now is fluke on the edges and scup on the rocks.

Looking three to five days out, the move to watch is the next stretch of southwest wind. A sustained southwest will push warmer surface water up against the North Shore, tighten the bunker schools against Matinecock and the Bayville shoreline, and concentrate the bass on the upcurrent side of every prominent rockpile. If the analyst pipeline catches up and the next SST package shows the inside water pressing into the upper 60s while the mid-Sound stays in the low 60s, expect the dawn topwater window at Stepping Stones and along the Hart Island edge to sharpen considerably. A hard northeast would do the opposite — knock the surface temp back, scatter the bunker, and force a return to deeper presentations on the troll.

For now the playbook is straightforward. Fish moving water on hard structure, match the adult-bunker profile, and accept that the class of fish in front of you is slot and low-overslot rather than trophy. The Western Sound is doing in early June exactly what a healthy Western Sound is supposed to do, and the next satellite pass and a live Kings Point reading will tell us whether the next leg of the run is already staged behind it.
