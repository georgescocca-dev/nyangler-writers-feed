# Jamaica Bay fluke slide out to the inlet as bay water tops 78
*New moon springs build through the weekend, and the flush through Rockaway Inlet is setting up the best bite of July so far.*

**BROAD CHANNEL, NY — July 8** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay got warm — warmer than I like to see it in early July, honestly. We were sitting in the high 70s in the back channels off Broad Channel and up toward Grassy Bay, and that kind of heat pushes fluke off the flats fast. When the shallow water gets soupy like that, doormats don't stick around to get lazy in it. They slide toward moving water — the inlet, the channel edges, anywhere the tide keeps things flushed and oxygenated. That's exactly what happened, and it's why the bite this week has a different address than it did in June.

This week the moon is sliding toward new — full new moon lands the 13th — which means we're building into spring tides right now, bigger swings each day through the weekend. Bigger tide means more water moving through Rockaway Inlet on the exchange, which means bait gets pushed and pulled harder, which means gamefish know exactly where to sit and wait. It also means Marine Parkway Bridge, my favorite piece of moving water in this whole system, is about to get a lot more interesting on the outgoing. When the current really rips through there this weekend, that's when the bass stack up on the down-current side of the pilings and let the tide do their hunting for them.

So here's what's actually happening on the water, not just what should be happening. Fluke: the pattern has flipped from bay flats to hard structure and moving water. Guys drifting Gulp Swimming Mullets in white and chartreuse — 4 and 5 inch, on 3/4 to 1 ounce bucktails — through the Rockaway Inlet channel and the deeper cuts by the Ruffle Bar and Ambrose Channel edges are doing better than the flats guys right now. Depths in the 15 to 25 foot range on the last two hours of the outgoing has been the sweet spot. I fished it myself Sunday and had three keepers to 4 pounds in about ninety minutes of drifting, lost two more to short strikes I never connected on — that tells me there's fish there but they're not exactly falling over themselves for it. You've got to keep the bucktail ticking bottom, not just drifting through the water column, or you'll miss the bite entirely in that current.

Stripers at Marine Parkway are doing what they always do this time of year — staging on the outgoing, working the rip lines off the bridge structure. Live eels fished on the bottom, or bunker chunks if you can get fresh bait, have been the better call over the past week compared to plugs, though I did hear of a few fish taken on white bucktails worked slow along the bridge shadow line right at dusk. Nothing giant — mostly schoolies and a few fish pushing the mid-20s — but consistent enough that it's worth the trip if you're fishing the two hours around the outgoing. Bring a heavier leader than you think you need; those bridge pilings have teeth.

Bluefish have started showing in decent numbers off Rockaway Beach, chasing bunker pods that are working the surf line from about Beach 90th down toward Jacob Riis. Nothing like a fall blitz yet — don't get that confused — but if you see the bunker balling up and the birds start diving, that's your window. Diamond jigs or metal-lip swimmers thrown into the melee will get bit fast, but it doesn't last long, maybe twenty, thirty minutes before the blues push the bait somewhere else. Worth keeping a rod rigged in the truck if you're beach walking this week.

Porgies are steady but unspectacular around the rocks by Breezy Point and along the Cross Bay Bridge pilings — small stuff mostly, bloodworms or clam on a hi-lo rig fished right on bottom, good action if you've got kids along or just want a bend in the rod, but nothing that's going to fill a cooler with dinner-plate porgies yet. Blackfish season's closed this time of year in our waters, so that's off the table until fall.

On weakfish — I want to be straight with you, because I know the water temp reports have some people excited. The bay's been warm enough for a while now, but I haven't heard of a single weakfish caught in this system in weeks. Warm water gets them here, but warm water alone doesn't make them bite. Until somebody actually puts one in the boat, I'm not sending anybody chasing a ghost.

Looking ahead: the new moon springs peak around the 11th through the 13th, and that's when I'd expect the Marine Parkway outgoing to really turn on, especially right at sunset when the tide and the light change over together — that's a dependable combination in this bay. If the bigger tidal push also flushes more bait out through the inlet, the fluke bite on the channel edges should hold, maybe improve, through the weekend. I'd fish the outgoing at the bridge Friday evening if the wind stays reasonable, and I'd have a backup plan to drift the inlet channel for fluke if the bass don't show — that's how I'm planning my own week. Canarsie Pier is still a solid bet for bottom fishing if you want an easy access point without a boat, and Breezy Point surf is worth a look early mornings for anyone chasing that first real bluefish run of the summer. Keep an eye on the bait — where the bunker go, everything else follows.
