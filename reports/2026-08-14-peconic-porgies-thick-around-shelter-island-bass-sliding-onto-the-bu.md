# Porgies thick around Shelter Island, bass sliding onto the bunker as the new moon tide builds
*The Peconic's holding its August heat, the porgy bite is as good as it gets, and stripers are starting to lean on the bait pushed into the bay.*

**GREENPORT, NY — August 14** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

By mid-August the Peconic has settled into the pattern it always finds this time of year — warmer than the Sound, warmer than the ocean side, and holding that heat like a bathtub that never quite drains. The bay warmed early back in April and it's been running its own clock ever since, and right now that clock says porgy time. If you've fished these waters more than a season or two, you know what that means: it's not a maybe, it's a certainty, and this week backed it up.

Shelter Island is doing what Shelter Island does in August. The porgies are stacked thick on the humps and drop-offs ringing the island — Cornelius Point, the flat off Jennys Creek, the drop along Ram Island Reef, and out toward Southold Shoal on the north side. Fifteen to twenty-five feet of water, sand and shell bottom, and it's about as close to guaranteed as bay fishing gets. High-low rigs with size 4 or 6 hooks, sandworm or a chunk of clam, fished on the bottom on the last two hours of the incoming tide, and you can fill a cooler without much drama. These aren't giant porgies — mostly hand-sized, some pushing a pound and a half — but the numbers are there, and it's the kind of fishing you can bring a kid or a first-timer on and know they'll go home happy.

Fluke are the more honest story. I wouldn't call it hot — modest is the fairer word, same as it's been all August. The channels are still holding fish: the Noyack Bay channel edge, the drop off Jessup's Neck, and the cut along Robins Island where the current runs hardest on the ebb. Twenty to thirty-five feet, drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid if the current's soft. You're picking through shorts to find keepers, and some drifts you strike out entirely — but a few boats out of Southold and Greenport have been putting three or four keeper fluke in the box on a good outgoing tide, which for mid-August in this protected water is about right. Don't expect doormats in here; that's an ocean-side story. This is meat-and-potatoes fluking, and right now it's meeting expectations, not exceeding them.

The bigger development is the striper pattern building on bait. We flagged this last week — said building tidal flow into the August 12th new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what's happened. Peanut bunker have been pushing into the estuary system in real numbers, and the schoolie bass are leaning on them hard. Cedar Beach on Shelter Island's north side, the rip off Jessup's Neck, and the mouth of Orient Harbor have all had stretches of visible surface activity — bunker getting pushed up, bass slashing through, gulls diving. Live-lined bunker on a fishfinder rig is the simplest way to connect, or if you'd rather cast, a bone or bunker-colored Bomber worked through the melee will draw strikes. Most of what's coming over the rail is schoolie-class, 18 to 24 inches, with the occasional slot fish mixed in — nothing to write home about size-wise, but consistent, and consistent counts for something in a summer where a lot of guys have been grinding for a handful of fish.

Plum Gut is starting to wake up too, though I'd call it early rather than arrived. The current there runs hard enough to hold bait even in summer, and with the new moon behind us and tidal flow building toward the next cycle, I've had reports of bass showing on the last of the ebb, working the rip lines where the Gut spits into Gardiners Bay. It's not the fall run yet — that's still weeks off — but it's the first hint of the staging that leads into it, and I'd keep an eye on that stretch of water heading into September.

Bluefish are riding the same bunker wave, and it's mostly a harbor story right now. Snapper blues are thick in Sag Harbor and Three Mile Harbor, which every bay rat knows is the tell that adult-class blues will keep building through the back half of August. If you've got kids with light spinning gear and a bag of small Gotcha plugs, the harbors are the place to burn an afternoon.

Weakfish remain the ghost in this report — scarce, as they've been for years now in the Peconic, though a few have turned up mixed in with the bass around Orient. If you catch one, handle it gently and let it go; that population needs every fish it can get.

Blackfish season's still off in the distance, but worth noting for planning purposes — the wrecks and rock piles around Plum Gut and off Orient will be where you want to be once that opens, and the porgy grounds we're fishing now are a good preview of what that structure holds.

Looking ahead, I don't expect much to change through the coming week. The bay's holding its warmth, tidal flow is still building off the new moon, and that combination should keep the porgy bite steady around Shelter Island and the bass leaning on bait wherever the current concentrates it. If I had one day to fish, I'd take the last two hours of an incoming tide at Cornelius Point for porgies in the morning, then swing over to Jessup's Neck or Cedar Beach on the evening ebb to see if the bass are pushing bait again. That's the Peconic in mid-August — reliable in some corners, still waiting to fully turn in others, and worth the trip either way.
