# Giant bluefin crash the Hudson as 68-degree water sets up the canyon edge
*Jigs and stickbaits producing trophy fish as thermocline stacks bait at 75 feet.*

**HUDSON CANYON, NY — June 24** — _by Capt. Tony Rosetti, Zone Writer_

The Hudson Canyon is firing on all cylinders right now, and the water temperature readings tell the whole story. We're seeing 68.2 degrees at the 44025 buoy — that's the sweet spot that pulls bluefin up from the depths and concentrates them along the thermocline.

The action has been nothing short of spectacular. Giant bluefin are stacked at 75 feet, right where the temperature break sets up, and they're absolutely crushing jigs and stickbaits. I've been working the 100-fathom line hard, and the fish are responding to vertical presentations better than anything else. A 280-gram jig with a beefed-up hook has been money — bounce it off the bottom and work it slow through the water column. When they're tight to structure like this, that's where the heavy metal shines.

The stickbait bite has been equally impressive. When you see bunker getting pushed up by the current along the canyon wall, throw the biggest stickbait you've got. These fish are wanting it worked down and dirty — no finesse required. The UVT stickbaits have been producing fish that are damn near backflipping on the hookset.

What's really interesting is how the fish are positioning themselves. They're not showing on top like we sometimes see in the summer blitzes. Instead, they're hugging that thermocline at 75 feet, maybe 5 feet off the bottom in some spots. The 68-degree surface water is creating a perfect temperature gradient that's concentrating bait, and the bluefin are taking full advantage.

The trolling fleet has been getting their shots too, but the jig and stickbait guys are really cleaning up. There's something about working a lure vertically through that temperature break that these fish can't resist. I've been running 35 pounds of drag on the reel — these are serious fish that require serious pressure.

The Claw has been particularly productive, especially on the tide changes. That structure creates an upwelling that pushes bait right into the strike zone, and the bluefin are positioned perfectly to ambush anything that comes through. The current has been running clean at 2-3 knots — perfect for maintaining bottom contact with your jigs while still getting good action.

Looking at the broader picture, we've got light winds out of the northwest at 6 knots, and the sea state is nearly flat at 1 foot. These are ideal conditions for precision fishing, and it's showing in the catch rates. The barometric pressure is holding steady at 1012 millibars — no major weather systems to disrupt the bite.

The key has been reading the electronics and putting your lure right where the fish are marking. When you see those thick marks at 75 feet, that's your target zone. Don't waste time fishing water — get your jig down to where the fish are living and work it through their wheelhouse.

For the week ahead, I'm watching the moon phase closely. We're approaching a new moon, which typically means weaker tides but more stable conditions. The fish should continue to hold on that thermocline as long as the water temperature stays in this 68-degree range. If we see any significant warming, expect the fish to slide deeper, but right now they're positioned perfectly for the vertical bite.

The Texas Tower area has also been producing, particularly on the eastern edge where the current creates a natural feeding lane. The fish are using that structure to ambush bait, and a well-placed jig or stickbait in that zone has been getting crushed.

This is exactly the kind of fishing that makes the Hudson Canyon legendary. When conditions align like this — perfect water temperature, clean current, and concentrated bait — the fishing can be absolutely phenomenal. Get out there and get your shots while this bite lasts.
