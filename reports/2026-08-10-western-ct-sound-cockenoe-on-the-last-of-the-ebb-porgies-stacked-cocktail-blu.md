# Cockenoe on the last of the ebb: porgies stacked, cocktail blues finally show
*The full moon current sharpened every window this week — fish the right two hours and the Western Sound still delivers.*

**NORWALK, CT — August 10** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Every Tuesday morning I sit with the tide chart and decide which piece of structure gets my first two hours of daylight, and this week the decision was Cockenoe over Penfield — not because Penfield's dead, but because the full moon push made current the deciding factor, and Cockenoe's rockpile holds bait tighter against hard-running water than Penfield's open reef does. That's the whole game out here. You're not picking a spot, you're picking a spot on a tide, and right now with the moon just off full and the current running harder than it has in a month, the reefs that concentrate bait in tight seams are outfishing the broader structure.

Cockenoe paid off. Porgies stacked on the northeast corner in 14 to 18 feet, thick enough that two guys anchored there Thursday afternoon on the last two hours of the ebb had their five-gallon bucket full of 12-to-14-inch scup before the tide went dead. Sandworm and clam on high-low rigs, size 6 or 8 hooks, just enough weight to hold bottom in that current — nothing fancy, porgies don't need fancy, they need you in the right depth on the right push. The Norwalk Islands rockpiles are doing the same thing right now, and honestly this is the mainstay fishery on the Western Sound in August. If porgies are your target, don't overthink it — find the hard bottom, fish the last two hours of either tide when the current's still moving but not ripping your line sideways, and stay off the top of the reef on the peak of the flood when the boat traffic parks right on top of the good water.

Fluke are still the more consistent grade-A fishery if you're willing to drift instead of anchor. The channel edge off Sheffield Island and the drop-off on the west side of the Norwalk Islands have both been giving up keeper fluke to 21 inches on white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow through 20 to 28 feet on the outgoing tide. Kings Point is reading 72.5 degrees, which is warm enough that the fluke are sliding toward the cooler edges where current sweeps the bottom clean — that's exactly where the drop-offs earn their keep. Incoming tide has been more hit-or-miss; I'd plan your fluke trips around the two hours either side of high water slack into the first hour of ebb.

Striped bass are in that classic mid-August low-light pattern — daytime is mostly dead water unless you're sitting on moving current, so I've shifted my bass trips to first light and last light around Greenwich Point and the lit pilings off the Cows. Live bunker on a fishfinder rig, fished tight to structure right at dusk, has produced schoolie-to-mid-20s bass consistently, with the occasional better fish mixed in on nights when the bunker schools are thick. Nothing blitz-worthy, and I want to be honest about that — this isn't June. You're working for individual fish in tight windows, not covering water hoping to run into a wolf pack. If you can only fish one window this week, make it the hour around sunset with bait actively moving on the tide.

We flagged the bluefish pattern last week — said the cocktail blues would follow the peanut bunker spread and show up within two weeks — and that's exactly what happened. Snapper blues have been thick in the back bays off Norwalk and Westport, and now we're seeing legitimate cocktail-class blues, 2 to 4 pounds, mixing into the bunker pods off Sheffield Island and around the outer Norwalk Islands on the last of the incoming tide. Small metal — half-ounce Deadly Dicks or Kastmasters — worked fast through the peanut schools has been the ticket. Nothing trophy-sized yet, but the size class is climbing week to week, which is the pattern you want to see this time of year.

Black sea bass are steady out on the Cows and the deeper structure off Penfield, holding in 25 to 35 feet where the water stays a few degrees cooler. Squid strips and Gulp on a two-hook bottom rig, fished right on the rockpile itself, not off to the side, has been the difference between a limit and a handful. I'll skip blackfish entirely this week — tautog season's closed through the summer here, and there's nothing to report until the fall opener puts them back on the table.

Boat traffic is the other half of this chess match right now. Weekends on Cockenoe and Penfield are a parking lot by 10 a.m., so I'm planning every trip around either the sunrise window or the last two hours of daylight, when the recreational fleet has cleared out and the reef gets quiet enough to actually fish it properly. Weekday mornings are still your best bet for uncrowded water on any of these pieces.

Looking ahead, the moon's sliding past full now, so expect the exaggerated current we've had this week to ease back toward a more standard push by the weekend — that usually softens the bite windows slightly but makes them more predictable, which I actually prefer for planning charters. If the afternoon sea breeze builds like it's been forecast, I'd get my fluke drifts done in the morning before the wind kicks the surface up over the shallower reef structure. Watch the tropics too — nothing on our doorstep yet, but late August is when a stray system can send swell up into the Sound and change everything for a few days.
