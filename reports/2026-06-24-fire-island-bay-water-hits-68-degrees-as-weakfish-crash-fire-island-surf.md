# Bay water hits 68 degrees as weakfish crash Fire Island surf and fluke stack deep
*Seven-pound weakfish from the inlet surf signals the return of the ghost fish to South Shore waters.*

**CAPTREE, NY — June 24** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The water's telling a story this week, and it's one I've been waiting all season to hear. Bay temps finally cracked 68 degrees — a solid four degrees warmer than the ocean side of the inlet — and that thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do. It's stacking bait on the flood, holding bass on the ebb, and bringing back a fish we haven't seen in real numbers for years.

A 26-inch, 7.3-pound weakfish came out of the Fire Island Inlet surf this week on a 9-inch Gravity Tackle Sand Eel. That's not just a nice fish — that's a statement fish. Weakfish don't show up by accident. They follow the bait, and when you see one this size in the surf, you know the ecosystem is starting to hum the way it should.

The fluke bite has been absolutely ridiculous. The Laura Lee boats out of Captree are putting keeper fluke in the box every trip, with fish to six pounds coming off the Robert Moses bridge drift in 30 feet of water. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are the ticket — drag them slow on the outgoing tide when the current pushes bait against the structure. The key is staying patient with your drift speed. Too fast and you're pulling over their heads; too slow and you're not triggering strikes.

What's really got me excited is the sand eel bite. These little silver bullets are everywhere right now, and they're the reason everything else is working. The weakfish are on them, the fluke are gorging on them, and the bass are using them to stage for their summer patterns. When I see sand eels this thick in late June, I know we're in for a summer to remember.

The inlet itself has been fishing like a textbook. Outgoing water at sunset is producing bass to the mid-30s on everything from bottle plugs to soft plastics. The new moon spring tides we just had flushed a ton of bait out of the bay, and the bass are lined up in the channel waiting for the next push. Fish tight to the pilings on the ebb — that's where the current creates the ambush points they love.

Sea bass action has been lights-out on the party boats. The Captree Pride and Princess are coming back with boat limits regularly, and the fish are quality — plenty of keepers mixed with the shorts. Diamond jigs in gold are working, but don't sleep on the sabiki rigs when the bite gets finicky. Sometimes you need to match the bait size exactly, and those small hooks on the sabiki do the trick.

The ocean side has been producing mixed bags that would make any angler smile. Porgies, blackfish, ling, and even some early bonito are showing up on the deeper drifts. The water's clean, the bait's thick, and the fish are feeding aggressively. It's that perfect mid-summer window when everything comes together.

Looking ahead, we've got a full moon Friday, which means big spring tides and serious water movement. I'm watching the inlet drain at sunset for what could be the first real push of weakfish we've seen in years. That 68-degree bay water is going to meet the cooler ocean water, create some serious current breaks, and stack bait like a dinner bell. If you've been waiting for the right time to get out there, this is it.
