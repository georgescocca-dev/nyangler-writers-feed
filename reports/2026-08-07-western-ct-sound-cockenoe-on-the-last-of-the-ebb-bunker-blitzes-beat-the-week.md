# Cockenoe on the Last of the Ebb: Bunker Blitzes Beat the Weekend Fleet to Penfield
*The neap tide we flagged is easing current speed right on schedule, and that's opening a clean two-hour window on the reefs before the boat traffic shows up.*

**NORWALK, CT — August 7** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Every week this time of year comes down to the same decision: fish the reef when the current's working for you, or fish it when nobody else is on it. This week I'm picking Cockenoe Reef over Penfield Reef, and it's not about the fish — it's about the clock. Cockenoe sits far enough off the Norwalk Islands that the Saturday armada doesn't find it until mid-morning, and with the neap tide softening the current the way we said it would back on August 2nd, the last two hours of the ebb are fishable without fighting a runaway drift. That's the window. Show up before 8 AM, work the northeast corner of the reef in 18 to 24 feet, and you've got the structure to yourself before the center console traffic starts running lanes through the Norwalk Islands channel.

The thermal picture explains why that corner of the Sound is holding fish better than the water off Stamford right now. Kings Point is reading 74.8 degrees, and the gradient climbs as you move east toward New London at 77.4. That means the western end of our beat — Greenwich Point out to Cockenoe — is running noticeably cooler than the water off the Cows and Penfield, and cooler water in August means more comfortable bait, which means more comfortable predators. Porgies have been stacking tight to structure on Cockenoe and the rockpile off Sheffield Island, and they're not shy about it — bring green crab and squid strips on a high-low rig, size 6 hooks, and fish straight bottom on the last of the ebb into the start of the flood. Fish in the 12 to 14 inch class have been coming over the rail steadily, with the occasional jumbo mixed in for anyone patient enough to hold a spot through a tide change.

Black sea bass are riding the same structure but a little deeper — 25 to 30 feet on the outside edge of Cockenoe and along the drop past Sheffield Island. Small strips of squid on a bucktail teaser, bounced slow off the bottom, is still the most efficient way to sort through the shorts and find the 14-inch-plus fish worth keeping. That bite has been consistent rather than explosive — steady beats spectacular most days out there right now, and I'd rather report that honestly than promise a slaughter that isn't happening.

The more electric story this week is the bluefish, and it's exactly the pattern we called on August 2nd — wherever the bunker pods concentrate, the blues have been detonating on top. Those pods have been showing off Greenwich Point and drifting east along the beachfront toward the Cows, and when a school gets pinned against the shallows on an incoming tide, the surface explodes. Poppers and metal-lip swimmers thrown right into the boil are producing fish in the 4 to 8 pound range, and the window is short — fifteen, twenty minutes before the pod scatters and regroups somewhere else along the beach. This is a run-and-gun game, not an anchor-and-wait game, and it rewards anyone willing to keep the throttle down and watch the birds instead of committing to one piece of water.

Striped bass have been mixed in with those blitzes but not dominating them — a slot fish here, an undersized fish there, mostly holding on the edges rather than in the middle of the feed. The better bass action has come off Penfield Reef itself, worked on the last two hours of the flood when current pushes bait up against the reef's western face. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5 ounce range, white or chartreuse, cast up-current and swum back with the tide, have accounted for fish in the high 20s to low 30-inch class. That reef gets hammered by weekend boat traffic by mid-morning, so the early flood — first light through about 9 AM on a Saturday or Sunday — is the only realistic window to fish it without dodging wakes and anchor lines all day.

Fluke have been the quiet disappointment of the last couple weeks. Drifting the sand between the Norwalk Islands and Sheffield Island in 15 to 20 feet has produced fish, but they're running small and scattered — you're covering a lot of bottom with white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails to put two or three keepers in the cooler. Nobody's filling a limit out there right now, and I wouldn't plan a trip around fluke alone until the pattern tightens up.

Looking ahead, the softening current from this neap should hold through the middle of the week, which keeps that early Cockenoe window workable and keeps Penfield's flood manageable for bass. The bigger question is the bunker — as long as those pods stay thick and the high-pressure pattern holds, the topwater blitz along Greenwich Point and toward the Cows should keep firing on the incoming tide. If the bunker push offshore or scatter, that bite could shut off fast, so I'd get out there sooner than later if the blitz is the target. Blackfish stay off the table until the fall reopener, but with porgies and sea bass holding steady on the structure, there's still a repeatable pattern out there for anyone willing to work the tide windows instead of fighting the crowds for them.
