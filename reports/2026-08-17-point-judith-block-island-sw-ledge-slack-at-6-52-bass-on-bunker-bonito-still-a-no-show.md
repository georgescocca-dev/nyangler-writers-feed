# SW Ledge slack at 6:52: bass on bunker, bonito still a no-show off the Block
*Daylight blitzes are stacking bass on peanut bunker from the Ledge to Sandy Point, but the first bonito flip hasn't happened yet — here's why, and what's next.*

**GALILEE, RI — August 17** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

SW Ledge slack hit at 6:52 this morning, and for about twenty minutes on either side of it we had bass stacked on the down-tide edge in 36 to 40 feet, working through a peanut bunker school like they had a schedule to keep. That's the tell right now — the fish aren't waiting for low light. There's so much bunker piled up between the Ledge and Sandy Point that the bass have stopped caring about the sun. We had three keepers and a dozen releases by 7:30, all on white bucktails in the 1.5-ounce range tipped with a 4-inch white Gulp, worked slow along the current seam where the ledge drops from 30 into the deeper water.

This is the same pattern that's been running from Narragansett Bay north — dense bait overriding the normal dawn/dusk rulebook. We're seeing it play out right in our own water. The rip line off Sandy Point has had the most consistent daylight activity all week, particularly on the last two hours of the ebb when the bunker get funneled tight against the beach and the bass pin them against the drop-off. If you're running out of Galilee, don't sleep past 8 a.m. thinking you missed it — some of the better blitzes we've seen have come at 10 and 11 in the morning, right in bright sun, with birds working tight over showering bait.

We flagged the bluefish angle a few days back — said the snapper blue explosion in the back channels was a sign adult-class fish would keep building through late August, and that's exactly what's happening. The blues mixing into these bunker blitzes now are pushing 3 to 6 pounds, not the little choppers we had in July, and they're plenty willing to eat the same bucktail the bass are hitting. If you want to separate the two, a wire leader ahead of a Deadly Dick or a bunker spoon will keep you in blues without shredding your bass gear every other cast.

Fluke fishing has been a grind-it-out proposition rather than a sure thing. We're finding keepers on the drift off SW Ledge and along the flats south of the island in 25 to 45 feet, but you're picking through a lot of shorts to find a 20-incher. Bigger baits are the move — a 5-inch white or chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail, fished slow and dragged rather than hopped, has been outproducing the smaller stuff. Best window has been the last two hours of the outgoing, same as the bass, when the current tightens up the bait and the fluke stop being lazy. Black sea bass have filled in nicely on the same structure — the humps and rock piles southwest of the island are holding decent numbers on squid strips and clam, a dependable backup when the fluke bite is spotty, which it has been more often than not this week.

Now for the disappointment of the week — no bonito, no albies off the Block yet. I know the chatter out of Nantucket Sound and around the Cape has bonito showing and even a sailfish sighting off Chatham, which tells you the water up that way has gone unusually warm. We haven't seen that heat signature push into our rips yet. North Rip and the water off North Light and Clay Head are still fishing like mid-August striper and bluefish water, not albie water. My read is we're maybe two to three weeks out from the first bonito flip showing off Southeast Light, and that's contingent on this warm pattern holding and sliding our way rather than staying bottled up around the Cape. I wouldn't burn a trip specifically hunting bonito at the Block yet — but I'd keep a small metal rigged and ready in the boat, because when they show, they show fast.

Offshore, the tuna bite out toward Block Canyon and the East Canyons remains the more interesting long-range option for Galilee boats with the range to run. That warm eddy water pushing up from the canyons is still in position, and bluefin and some yellowfin opportunity is there for boats willing to make the trip — better than the dead water further south right now.

Tide-wise, we're five days off the new moon and building back toward the August 28 full moon, which should sharpen the current at every rip in this zone — SW Ledge, the Sandy Point rip, the water off North Light. If that bunker stays put through the build, expect these daylight blitzes to hold steady rather than fade, though the wind will have the final say — a stiff southwest blow will flatten the bite fast by pushing the bait off the structure. My plan for the coming week is early starts at the Ledge on the last of the ebb, then working east toward Sandy Point as the sun climbs, keeping one rod rigged with metal in case something with a longer tail shows up sooner than expected.
