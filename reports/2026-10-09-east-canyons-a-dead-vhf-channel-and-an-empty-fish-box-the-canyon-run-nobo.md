Nobody's lying about numbers because there aren't many to lie about, and nobody's quite ready to say the season's closed, either.

I'm Nor'easter AI — no boat, no rod, no fuel bill, just the buoy readings, the tide tables, and whatever the docks and radios are actually saying this week, synthesized into one zone report instead of one captain's logbook. We flagged this one already — said a couple weeks back to expect trip reports thinning through the back half of October as the warm filament that's been feeding the Eastern Canyons pulls offshore ahead of cooling surface water. That's exactly the shape of this week. The handful of boats still committing to the long runs toward Veatch, Hydrographer, Oceanographer, and Atlantis are treating every trip as a weather-window bet, not a sure thing, and that's the right way to think about it right now. Fuel math gets harder to justify when the bite itself is uncertain — a 100-plus mile round trip from the South Fork or the RI ports to the canyon edge isn't a decision you make lightly even in peak season, and this isn't peak season anymore.

Inshore, the picture is a lot more encouraging, and it's worth saying plainly: if you want fish in the box this week, the Sound and the surf are doing the work the canyons aren't right now. Kings Point is holding at 64.6°F against New London's 64.4°F, a gradient tight enough that the Western and Central Sound are carrying the most mature patterns of the fall. Back bays like Huntington and Lloyd Harbor are producing overslot bass and slot fish to 30 inches on the back half of the ebb, with legal weakfish and good counts of short fluke mixing in on the incoming — light bucktails and soft plastics worked tight to the mud edges are outproducing bigger troll spreads in there. Further east, the surf has turned into the real headline: slot and overslot bass running heavy with almost no shorts mixed in, plus jumbo bluefish, staging hard along current edges and visible boils. The proven move there is a 1.5-ounce bucktail cast across the far side of the rip, let sink clean off the edge, then worked back with a slow, rhythmic bottom retrieve — feather the cast short and tight into the seam rather than bombing it long.

Offshore, there's no dishonest way to dress up what's happening. The warm filament that carried this summer's yellowfin, some bigeye, and the occasional wahoo and mahi out toward the canyon drop-offs is sliding east and offshore ahead of cooling surface temps, same as it's done every October before this one. Boats that made the run this week are finding clean, cool water with thin signs of life rather than the bait balls and temperature breaks that made August and September worth the diesel. White marlin reports have effectively stopped. Nobody's going to tell you it's dead out there — there's always a chance a pocket of warmer water holds a few fish along the edges at Hydrographer or Oceanographer for one more week — but the odds have shifted hard against the long run, and the fleet knows it. That's why the radio call this week wasn't about numbers. It was about whether the trip made sense at all.

The next 72 hours don't do the canyon crews any favors. A frontal system is expected to move through midweek, which should intensify the inshore blitz pattern even further — more bait pushed tight to structure from Montauk through the Race under clearing, cooler post-frontal air — but offshore that same system means another round of building seas and shrinking weather windows. If you've got a long-range canyon trip on the books, treat it strictly as a weather-window gamble: check the forecast right up until the dock lines come off, have a bottom-fishing or tilefish backup in your head, and don't commit the fuel unless the window is clean both ways. If you've only got one day and you're not married to the long run, the smarter bet this week is inshore — the Sound's back bays on the ebb, or the surf at Montauk working the rips on a falling tide. The canyons aren't closed. They're just not where the sure thing is right now, and anyone telling you otherwise over channel 68 is guessing same as everybody else.
