# Bay water pushes past 78°F — fluke and bass slide to the inlet as new moon tides build
*Hot flats, a big spring tide on the way, and the first real shot at inlet bonito make this a week to fish the moving water, not the mud.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — July 8** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what it does every July when the sun sits on it for days without a good blow to stir things up — it cooked. Surface temps on the flats pushed up into the upper 70s, and out toward the Grassy Bay and North Channel flats I was reading water close to 79 on the graph by midafternoon. That's too warm for fluke to sit comfortably on the sand, and it showed. The bite on the shallow flats that was solid three weeks ago went quiet, and the fish did exactly what they always do — they slid off into the channels, the bridge rips, and out toward the inlet where there's current and cooler water pushing in off the ocean. Mornings were calm glass, afternoons brought the usual sou'west sea breeze kicking up a light chop by 1 or 2 o'clock, nothing that shut the bite down, just enough to make the afternoon drift a little sloppier than the morning one.

This week the moon's the story. We're building toward new moon, which means bigger spring tides stacking up through the weekend — more water moving, faster current at the pinch points, and that's exactly the medicine this bay needs after a week of warm, lazy water. Rockaway Inlet is going to rip harder on the outgoing than it has in weeks, which means more bait getting flushed out of the back bay through Beach Channel and past the Marine Parkway Bridge. That's good news for bass and fluke both — bigger tidal exchange pulls cooler, cleaner ocean water in on the flood and shoves bay water and bait out on the drop. I'd plan around the outgoing all week, especially the last two hours into low, because that's when the channels concentrate everything that's been sitting in that warm water and funnels it toward structure.

Fluke are still catchable, they've just relocated. Forget the open flats for now — I'm working the edges of North Channel and the drop-off along Ruffle Bar, 15 to 25 feet, dragging 3/4 to 1-ounce bucktails dressed with white Gulp Swimming Mullets, teased with a strip of fluke belly for scent. Slow drift, bounce it right on bottom, and don't be afraid to let it sit a beat at the bottom of the drop where the current stalls — that's where they're stacking. A few boats working the Beach Channel side of the inlet have had decent counts of keeper-class fish in that 18 to 22-inch range, with the occasional bigger fish mixed in. Jersey side, they weighed a 12-plus pound doormat off a tournament boat a couple weeks back, and that tells you the big females are around in these warmer inlet and ocean-adjacent waters this summer — worth remembering when you're picking through short fish and thinking about running one more drift.

Stripers are stacking at the Marine Parkway Bridge again, which is the pattern I expect every summer once the water in the open bay gets uncomfortable for them. They're holding tight to the pilings and current breaks, ambushing bait as it gets swept through on the outgoing. Dusk and dawn have been the windows — throw bunker spoons or swim a white paddletail through the seams, and if you can get fresh bunker for a fishfinder rig, drift it right along the pilings on the drop. Sizes have been mixed, mostly schoolies in the 20-26 inch range with the occasional better fish for guys fishing the darker hours. With the new moon building bigger current through the weekend, I'd expect that bite at the bridge to sharpen up — more water moving means more bait getting pushed past those pilings, and the bass know it.

Bluefish have shown up in real numbers off Rockaway Beach and out toward Breezy Point, chopping up bunker pods right in the wash. If you see the birds working close to the beach, get a metal — an Ava jig or a Deadly Dick — and fan-cast into the melee. It's been good action, not necessarily big fish, mostly 2 to 4 pounds, but steady enough that a morning session with a bucket of poppers will keep you busy. Porgies are still solid around the Cross Bay Bridge pilings and off Canarsie Pier — bloodworms or clam on a simple hi-lo rig, fishing bottom on the last of the outgoing, and you'll fill a bucket with panfish-sized fish, a few pushing toward a pound and a half. Weakfish, though — I've got nothing to report there. Water's been warm enough for a while now, but I haven't heard of a single weak taken in the bay this season, and that tracks with what's been happening region-wide. Warm water alone isn't bringing them back this year, at least not yet.

Looking ahead, the new moon spring tides peak this weekend, and that's when I want to be fishing the inlet on the outgoing — both for the bass and fluke bite it should sharpen, and because there's a real chance for something extra. The window for the first bonito and Spanish mackerel of the year along the South Shore is closing out over the next few days, and if that warm plume keeps pushing, Rockaway Inlet's mouth on a clean incoming tide is worth a speculative hour with small metal or a Deadly Dorado worked fast. It's a long shot from the bay side, but with the pattern building the way it is, I wouldn't rule it out. Either way, fish the moving water this week — the fish have told us where they want to be, and it's not the mud.
