# Porgies pay the bills while the bass wait for dark
*Daytime stripers are ghosts on the Sound this week, but scup on the reefs and fluke on the edges are keeping rods bent.*

**ORIENT, NY — August 10** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Had a fella pull up to the launch at Mattituck the other morning, still had the tags on his rod, asked me straight out where the bass were biting. I told him the truth, which nobody wants to hear in August: they're biting where the sun isn't. He looked at me like I'd told him to go fish on the moon. But that's the Sound in the second week of August — it doesn't care what you want, it cares what the water's doing, and right now the water's doing what warm water does. It sits on top, it gets soft, and the stripers go find somewhere with shade or current or both.

I've got numbers off the buoys that back up what I'm feeling with my hand off the transom. Kings Point down west is sitting around 72, 73 degrees. New London out east is closer to 70. That gradient runs right through my water, and it means the far end of my beat — Rocky Point on out to the Gut approach — stays a couple degrees cooler and a whole lot more comfortable for a striper that doesn't want to work too hard to breathe. So that's where I've been putting my time, and that's where the dawn bite has held up. Bunker are still moving through the Gut approach, not in the thick schools you get in October, but enough that the blues and the occasional bass are cutting through them at first light. Anchor up on the last of the ebb, live-line a bunker off a fish-finder rig, and don't be surprised if it's a bluefish that eats it — the adult blues have been running tighter to that bait than the stripers have, which tells you something about who's willing to feed in bright water and who isn't.

After sunup the bass go quiet and stay quiet till the light drops again. That's not me being cranky about it — that's just where we are. If you want a rod bent at nine in the morning, go sit on the reef off Rocky Point or work the rock piles out past Horton Point light with a bank sinker and half a green crab. Porgy fishing has been the most honest thing going on this beat all week — steady, dependable, no drama, fish stacked tight to structure regardless of how hot that surface water gets. I had a morning last week where the scup came two at a time on a high-low rig, size 6 hooks, clam strips working just as well as crab. Nothing glamorous about a porgy trip, but you go home with dinner, which counts for something.

Fluke have been the other honest fish. They've slid off the flats and settled into the deeper cuts and channel edges — Mattituck Inlet's outer channel, the drop past the jetty, anywhere the bottom breaks and holds a few degrees cooler water than the sand around it. Full moon's swinging the tides harder this week, and that extra push has sharpened the drift bite right at the turns. White Gulp Swimming Mullet on a bucktail, three-quarter ounce, dragged slow along the edge where the sand gives way to mussel bottom — that's produced keepers, nothing huge, but enough to make a trip worthwhile if you're patient with the drift and don't rush it.

Now — we said a couple weeks back that these August springs, the ones building through the 11th and into the 13th, would put some new life back in this whole stretch, and they're sitting right on the doorstep now. When that tide starts hauling harder through the Gut, I expect the bass that have been laying low to get pushed up onto the seams a little more, especially where that cooler water off Rocky Point meets the harder current. Worth being out there at first light on the building tide once those springs kick in — not because it's a sure thing, but because that's exactly the kind of current change that's put fish on the end of my line more Augusts than I can count.

Blackfish are still off the table — season's closed till fall, and truthfully the tog don't want much to do with this warm water anyway, they're tucked deep in the rock and staying there. Weakfish — I'll be honest, I haven't heard a real weakfish report worth passing along this month. A few years back you could count on them mixing with the porgies on the reef, but that's thinned out bad, and I'm not going to tell you they're around when I haven't seen it myself.

Look, this isn't a week to expect miracles. It's a week to fish smart — dawn and dusk for the bass and blues around bait, daytime for porgy and fluke on structure, and keep an eye on that moon tide building through midweek. If I had one morning to give you, I'd put you at the Gut approach an hour before first light with a live bunker out, and I'd have a bucktail rigged and ready in case the fluke bite turns on once the sun's up over the drop-off. Cover your bases. The Sound's not handing anything out for free right now, but it's not skunking anybody who shows up with a plan either.
