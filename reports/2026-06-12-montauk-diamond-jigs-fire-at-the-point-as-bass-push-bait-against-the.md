# Diamond jigs fire at the Point as bass push bait against the boulder fields
*Keeper stripers to 26 pounds hitting metals on the south side while fluke action stays steady in 30 feet.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 12** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is doing what it does best right now — concentrating bass against structure and giving diamond jig fishermen the kind of action that makes this place legendary. The south side boulder fields have been producing keeper stripers consistently, with fish pushing into the 20-pound class and beyond. One charter this week brought 15 bass to the boat with five going over 18 pounds, topped by a 26-pounder that hit a diamond jig in the classic Montauk fashion.

The key has been working the jigs vertically along the boulder edges where the current sweeps bait against the rocks. These aren't your typical schoolie bass — most of the fish coming up are clear keepers, with plenty in the 31-inch-plus range. The diamond jigs are outproducing everything else because they get down fast in the current and trigger that aggressive response when bass are feeding on sand eels and small bunker pushed tight to structure.

Fluke fishing has been the steady producer for those looking to fill the cooler. The south side drops in 25 to 35 feet have been holding good numbers, with keepers mixed in among the shorts. White Gulp swimming mullets on bucktails continue to be the go-to rig, though you need to work them slow on the drift. The better fish — including some pushing 5 pounds — are coming on the longer drifts where you can keep the bait in the strike zone.

Sea bass are showing up as a bonus on the fluke grounds, with some nice 17-inch fish coming over the rail. The structure south of the lighthouse has been particularly productive, especially on the incoming tide when bait gets pushed up against the rocks.

Water conditions have been prime for this kind of fishing. The south side has been fishable most days, with that gentle 2-foot swell that lets you work structure properly without getting beat up. The key is timing your trips around the tide — the last hour of the ebb and first two hours of the flood have been the most productive windows.

The bait situation is what's driving this action. Sand eels are thick around the Point, and when the current pushes them against the boulder fields, the bass stack up to ambush them. That's why the diamond jigs are so effective — they mimic those sand eels perfectly when worked vertically along the structure.

Looking ahead, we're moving into the heart of summer fishing at Montauk. The full moon this weekend should fire up the tides even more, which typically means better current flow around the Point and more aggressive feeding. I'll be watching for the classic Montauk pattern — bass feeding hard on the moving water, especially during those prime tide changes at dawn and dusk. The south side should remain the focus, but don't sleep on the north side if the wind allows. When conditions line up right at the Point, there's no place like it on the East Coast.
