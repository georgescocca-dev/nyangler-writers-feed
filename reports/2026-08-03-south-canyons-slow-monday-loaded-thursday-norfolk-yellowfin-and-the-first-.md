# Slow Monday, loaded Thursday: Norfolk yellowfin and the first real white marlin flurry of August
*A rough start off Poor Man's turned into a three-canyon run that finally put marlin flags up at Norfolk.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 3** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Monday was a grind and Thursday made up for it — that's the report card for this week, and if you fish four canyons out of two inlets like I do, you learn to live with that split. We started the week at Poor Man's, around 38°32'N, 73°57'W, chasing leftover push from weekend wind, and the water there was still working through a green-to-blue transition on the edge. Marked bait, marked structure, couldn't buy a strike before 10 a.m. By Tuesday I'd had enough of that and ran north to the Spencer/Lindenkohl string instead, and that's where the week turned.

The warm filament off the Gulf Stream has been sitting stubborn against the wall between Spencer (38°44'N, 73°58'W) and Lindenkohl (38°50'N, 73°50'W) for going on two weeks now, and it hasn't moved much. We flagged this exact pattern last week — said the warm push would hold the canyon wall and the tuna and mahi bite would keep producing while white marlin activity built underneath it. That's exactly what showed up. Wednesday morning we set up a chunk line on the 600-fathom break at Lindenkohl, butterfish and bunker chunks fished on 6/0 circle hooks, and had a steady pick of yellowfin in the 25-40 pound class from first light until the sun got high and the fish slid deeper. Nothing frantic, but a real bite — six boats working that same stretch of edge all morning and everybody I talked to on the radio was getting bit.

Thursday is where it got interesting. We ran south to Norfolk, out past 37°44'N, 74°30'W, on intel that the temp break down there had tightened up and cleaned out overnight. Trolled a mixed spread — Ilander/ballyhoo combos in the shotgun, a spreader bar down the middle, naked ballyhoo skipping off the flat lines — and inside of two hours we had our first white marlin flag of the season up, then a second an hour later. Neither fish came tight, we went 0-for-2 on the release, but two legit bites in a morning after a season of scattered singles tells you the marlin are starting to stage the way they're supposed to this time of August. Bigeye showed up that same night on the drop-back, one fish right around 140 pounds taken on a deep chunk after dark, six hours into a soak with lights down and butterfish strips fished off a fluorocarbon leader in 700 feet of water.

Mahi have been the wildcard piece all week. Anywhere we crossed a weed line or a piece of floating structure — pallets, a lobster ball, a stray car tire once, don't ask — there were schoolies stacked underneath, 3 to 8 pounds, and a couple of gaffers pushing 15 mixed in on the better pieces. Small skirted jigs and a handful of Sabiki-caught bait tossed live off the stern will keep a school fired up around the boat for twenty minutes if you work it right, and that's free fish while you're running between canyon edges anyway.

Wahoo have been quieter than I'd like for early August. We picked up two on high-speed gear — Braid Marauders and a black/purple Ilander combo run at 16-18 knots off the wire line — both at Norfolk on the same stretch of clean blue water where the marlin showed. That's usually the tell: when the wahoo start showing on the same water as the white marlin, the whole southern string is getting into its rhythm for the month.

What didn't work: sitting still and hoping. Poor Man's stayed off most of the week, still working through some leftover chop and discolored water from the weekend blow, and I wouldn't burn fuel there again until it cleans up. If you've only got one day and you're deciding between canyons, I'd lean toward Spencer or Lindenkohl right now for the more consistent yellowfin chunk bite, with a look south toward Norfolk if you've got the range and want a shot at that building white marlin and bigeye combo after dark.

Looking ahead, if that warm filament holds its position on the wall — and there's no sign yet that it's sliding — I expect the yellowfin chunk bite at Spencer and Lindenkohl to stay steady through the coming week, the mahi to keep stacking under any structure you cross, and the white marlin numbers at Norfolk to keep building rather than fade, which is the normal pattern for this point in August. The one thing I'm watching is whether that edge tightens up enough to concentrate the bigeye bite into a shorter window after dark — if it does, that's where the bigger fish are going to come from. I'd have the chunk gear and the deep-drop rig both rigged and ready before you leave the dock, because right now you genuinely don't know which one you're going to need.
