# Race and Gut wait on data as June bass class settles into slot-fish pattern
*Buoy and tide feeds offline for the run, but seasonal thermal range and bait stage point to a classic early-June rip bite on the ebb.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — June 5, 2026** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The oceanographic desk came up thin this morning. The Dr. Fish analyst run published no live conditions, no current buoy readings, and no tide station numbers — Kings Point, Montauk, Bridgeport and New London are all dark in today's package. The most recent published SST imagery in the catalog is dated June 3, two days stale, and no June 4 or June 5 thermal frames have cleared the pipeline. That means there is no verified water-temperature figure to anchor this report against, and the eddy and front structure off the east end has to be inferred from the early-June climatology rather than read off a chart. What we can say with confidence: the Sound is in its early-June warming ramp, which in this zone historically means surface temps in the upper 50s to low 60s through the Race and Gut, with the outside water off Fishers and Plum running a tick cooler than the inside Gardiners and Peconic system. Bunker have been around the east end for weeks, sand eels are stacked on the harder bottom out toward Valiant Rock, and squid are still in the mix on the moon tides.

For the beat, that thermal window is the sweet spot. Bass are fully transitioned out of the spring staging mode and into rip-feeding posture. The Race and Plum Gut are the two engines driving the zone right now, and both fish the ebb harder than the flood at this stage of June — the ebb pulls colder, bait-loaded water out of Gardiners Bay through the Gut and dumps it into the temperature break at the west end of the Race, which is where the predator class sets up. Expect a slot-heavy box with a real shot at overs on the bigger moon tides, and bluefish moving in and shredding tails the second the bass window closes. Fluke are on the sand edges off Orient and the north side of Gardiners, and porgies are thick on any hard bottom from Mattituck east.

The Gut bite has been running a classic pattern — three-way rigs with bucktails down deep on the heavy current, then switching to surface presentations when the birds start working and fish push bait to the top. A six-foot casting rod with a small pencil popper has been a real weapon at the back end of the ebb when the rip lays down and fish come up on scattered bait. Drift discipline matters more than lure choice. The boats putting fish on ice are the ones managing their drift line on the down-current side of the rip and not getting swept into the standing water. Power-drifting through the slack into the early ebb has produced quality fish for guys willing to bump in and out of gear instead of just setting up for the screaming current.

Out at the Race, the boat traffic is going to be the limiting factor on weekends. Setting a clean drift through a fleet of 30 boats is harder than catching the fish. The bigger biomass is on the CT side near Valiant, and the bluefish presence is heavy enough that bucktail attrition is real — bring twice what you think you need. Soft plastics on a heavy jighead have been outproducing bucktails for guys who can keep them in the strike zone, particularly when the bass are keyed on sand eels rather than squid. Fishers Island rock structure is starting to fish for the wader crowd on the night tides, and the surf line from Orient State Park east is worth a 3 a.m. shot on a moving tide with a big swimmer.

Looking ahead three to five days, the controlling variable is going to be wind direction relative to tide. A sustained southwest will pile bait against the north-facing shorelines and prop up the daytime bite at the Gut. A northeast blow shuts the Race down for small boats and pushes the action inside to Plum Gut and the bay drops. The next significant moon push is the driver — when current velocity ramps, the overslot class moves in on the rips and the night bite on the Fishers rocks gets serious. I want updated SST imagery and a live read on the New London buoy before I commit to calling a thermal break location, but the seasonal mechanics are lined up.

Fish the ebb, manage your drift, and do not waste a clean weather window waiting for perfect intel. The water is ready.
