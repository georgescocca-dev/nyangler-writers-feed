# Bluefin blitz hits Poor Man's as 68-degree water stacks bait on the shelf
*Canyon edges firing with yellowfin and mahi as thermal breaks set up perfect feeding zones.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — June 24** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

The southern canyons are absolutely on fire right now, and it's all about that 68-degree water sitting on the shelf. Poor Man's Canyon has been the star of the show this week, with bluefin tuna crashing bait balls from the 100-fathom curve all the way up to 30 fathoms. These aren't the giants we see in the fall — we're talking 40 to 80-pound fish that are absolutely wired on the abundant bunker and squid that's been pushed up from the depths.

The key has been working that temperature break where the 68-degree inshore water meets the cooler 64-degree canyon water. That 4-degree differential is creating a perfect feeding zone, and the bluefin are taking advantage. Butterfly jigs in the 150 to 200-gram range have been deadly when you find the schools, but don't overlook the chunk bite. Fresh bunker on a circle hook, fished on a sliding sinker rig about 20 feet down, has been producing consistent hookups during the early morning and late afternoon feeds.

Spencer Canyon has been giving up some beautiful yellowfin in the 30 to 60-pound class, particularly along the northern wall where the current sweeps around the 50-fathom line. The water there is running a solid 70 degrees, and the yellowfin are absolutely hammering cedar plugs and small feathers trolled at 7 to 8 knots. Pink and white has been the hot color combination, especially when you're working the edges where the clean blue water meets that slightly green inshore stuff.

Lindenkohl has been the mahi spot this week. The weed lines have been incredible — thick mats of sargassum holding everything from small schoolies to some legitimate gaffers pushing 20 pounds. The trick has been working the edges of the weed with live ballyhoo on a circle hook, but don't be afraid to throw some artificials. Small poppers and swimming plugs in blue and white have been getting crushed when you find active fish.

Norfolk Canyon has been a bit more hit-or-miss, but when you connect, it's been worth the ride. The white marlin bite has been picking up along the 100-fathom curve, especially where you see those temperature fingers pushing up from the deep water. Rigged ballyhoo on a 7/0 circle hook, pulled behind a small planer at about 6 knots, has been the go-to presentation. The key is staying in that 72 to 74-degree water — any cooler and the marlin seem to disappear.

What's really got me excited is the bait situation. The bunker schools have been massive, and they're holding in that perfect 30 to 50-fathom zone where all these species can get at them. The squid have been thick too, especially at night, and that's when some of the better bluefin action has been happening. If you're making the run, don't forget to bring some squid bars — the tuna have been absolutely crushing them on the troll.

The weather pattern has been cooperating beautifully. Light northwest winds have kept the seas manageable, and that high pressure system has been locking in these stable conditions. The water clarity has been exceptional — that deep blue you dream about when you're sitting at the dock.

Looking ahead, I'm watching this weekend's moon phase closely. We're coming up on a new moon, which should fire up the night bite even more. Those spring tides are going to move a lot of water, and that usually means the bait gets concentrated even tighter along the canyon edges. I'm expecting the yellowfin bite at Spencer to really turn on, and Poor Man's should continue to produce bluefin as long as that temperature break holds.

The only thing to watch is a potential weather system moving up the coast early next week. If it tracks offshore like the models suggest, we should be fine, but keep an eye on the forecast. These canyon conditions don't last forever, so if you've been thinking about making the run, this is your window. The fish are there, the bait is there, and the water is perfect. Time to go fishing.
