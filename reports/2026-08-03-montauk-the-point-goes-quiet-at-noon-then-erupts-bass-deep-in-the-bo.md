# The Point Goes Quiet at Noon, Then Erupts: Bass Deep in the Boulders, Bunker Everywhere You Look
*Dog-day stripers are holding tight to structure through the heat, but the bunker schools and a softening tide are setting up the next move.*

**MONTAUK, NY — August 3** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

There's a rhythm to the Point in early August that anybody who's fished it for more than a season learns to respect. The big bass don't disappear this time of year — they just go underground, tucking into the boulder fields off the Lighthouse and North Bar where the water stays cool and the current never stops moving. You have to go find them, and you have to fish the clock, not the calendar. That's exactly what's happening right now, and it's about as honest a read as I can give you.

Water at the Point has settled around 70 degrees, which sounds cool until you remember what the surface has been doing on a flat, sunny afternoon — pushing warmer than that skin-deep, enough to shove the bass down onto structure during daylight and hold them there until the light starts to fail. I've had the best luck jigging bucktails and diamond jigs — 4 to 6 ounces depending on the drift — down into 50 to 80 feet along the boulder field between the Light and North Bar, working them slow and vertical right on bottom where the rock breaks the current. Get the jig moving too fast and you're just tiring your arm out. Let it fall, twitch it, let it fall again. That's when the thump comes.

Dawn and dusk are still the money windows, and this week they've been worth setting an alarm for. Bunker schools have been thick off Shagwong and drifting past Turtle Cove, and where you find the bunker balled up and nervous, the bass and blues are usually working underneath or right through them. I watched a school get bulldozed off the Elbow at first light Tuesday — birds diving, bunker skying, then that unmistakable boil of bass coming up through the middle of it. Poppers and big Danny-style swimmers worked the edges of the bait got hit hard, and a few guys snapping bucktails through the chaos on 60-pound fluoro leader put nice fish in the boat, mostly 20 to low-30-inch fish with a couple of overslots mixed in.

The bluefish have been the more reliable of the two right now, and it's exactly the pattern we flagged last week — this is a bait-driven fishery, plain and simple, and wherever the bunker have re-formed after getting pushed around, the blues show up within hours. Poppers and darters off Turtle Cove and along the south side rips have been getting hit hard enough to test your drag, some fish pushing into the 8 to 10-pound class. If you want a workout with topwater gear, this is your week.

Fluke fishing has been the steadiest thing going, and honestly the least dramatic to write about because it's just been solid, day in and day out. Drifting the Lake Montauk basin and the harbor entrance near Star Island on the outgoing tide, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails tipped with squid strips have been putting keeper fish in the cooler consistently, with a few doormats pushing 6 pounds coming off the deeper edges of the channel near the jetty. On the ocean side, the structure off the south side beaches has been holding fish too — smaller boats working the sod banks and rock piles in 20 to 35 feet have found good numbers, though you have to cull through some shorts to get there.

The bottom bite hasn't skipped a beat, and that's no surprise — we said the porgy and sea bass fishery out here is weatherproof, and it's picked up exactly where it left off. Shagwong Reef and the humps off Frisbie's have been giving up porgies all the way to 2 pounds on top-and-bottom rigs with clam and squid, plus a mix of sea bass for anybody dropping bucktails or diamond jigs tipped with squid down into 40 to 60 feet. It's not glamorous, but it's the kind of fishing you can set your watch by out here, and right now it's about the most dependable thing on the chart.

Offshore, the bluefin bite has stayed alive on the recreational slot fishery since the trophy closure, with boats working the grounds south and east of the Point under a stable weather pattern. That's a long run from the harbor, but the reports have been consistent enough that it's worth the gas if you've got the boat and the weather window.

Here's what I'm watching hardest this week: the tide is easing toward neap around August 5th and 6th, and when that current softens up, the bass at the Point and around Shagwong tend to compress into the strongest remaining current hours and slide into the deepest, coolest structure they can find. That means the daytime jigging window gets tighter, and the play becomes night eels — live eels drifted slow through the rip at the Gut and around the bridges after dark, when the bass climb up to feed under cover. If you can only fish one tide this week, fish the last two hours of the outgoing at night.

The other thing I keep half an eye on every August is the first albie or bonito scout, and the ingredients are lining up — squid pushing into Rhode Island and Nantucket Sound waters, temperature breaks sharpening along the south side rips. Nothing confirmed yet out here, but if you're fishing the rips off the Point and you see nervous water and small, fast slashes instead of the big rolling boils of a bass blitz, don't assume it's just bluefish. Keep a small epoxy jig or a Deadly Dick tied on just in case. That first fish of the season always shows up when you least expect it, and out here, it's usually right around now.
