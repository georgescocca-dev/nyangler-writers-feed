# Quick's Hole peanut bunker stacking up — bonito flashing, albies on the clock
*Warm water and building current have bonito showing early in the Sound, and the bait in the Elizabeth Islands rips says the albies aren't far behind.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — August 17** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Peanut bunker are balling up thick in the rip off Quick's Hole right now, tight little pods getting pushed and turned by the current like they know something's coming for them. I sat on that rip Tuesday afternoon on the last of the ebb and watched three separate bait balls get worked over from below — no visible fish, just that telltale nervous water, bait skittering sideways, then going quiet. That's not bluefish behavior. That's something faster underneath, testing the pack before it commits. I don't have a hookup to prove it yet, but I've fished that hole long enough to know what that looks like, and it looks like the first bonito are already in the neighborhood.

We flagged this a couple weeks back — said the building tide into the August 12 new moon would push bait hard enough through the rips to bring bonito and albies into range around Buzzards Bay and the Vineyard, and that's exactly the pattern setting up now. The Sound's running warm, warmer than it has any business being in mid-August, and that Chatham sailfish sighting a few weeks back wasn't a fluke — the whole southern New England coast is sitting in an unusually warm pool of water that's dragging exotic fish north with it. Nantucket Sound already has anglers connecting on bonito. Buzzards Bay is next, and Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole are where I'd be parked waiting for it.

Here's the honest read: it's not blitz-on-top yet. I talked to two guys at the Cuttyhunk town dock who worked Canapitsit Channel and Robinson's Hole both tides Wednesday and came up with bluefish and one dropped bonito between them — no netted fish, but real signs of life. That's where we are right now: transitional, promising, not a sure thing. If you're heading out this week, fish the last two hours of the ebb through Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole, small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dick-style metals in the half-ounce to one-ounce range, worked fast on top of the peanut bunker schools. Don't overthink the retrieve — bonito want speed, and if albies are mixed in they'll want it even faster.

Striped bass are still in their late-summer holding pattern, and that's not changing until the water eases off in September. Daytime bass have gone quiet in the warm shallows — I'm not finding much inside Buzzards Bay proper between 10 and 4. But the Cape Cod Canal is a different animal entirely. The new moon tide dumped a serious surge of current through there, and slot and over-slot bass have been eating topwater plugs — Doc's or Guppy-style poppers — off the herring run current lines through the middle of the week. Fish that current seam hard on the last two hours of either tide, dawn or dusk, and you'll find fish stacked where the flow narrows near the railroad bridge.

Bluefish are thick and getting thicker. Snapper blues are packed into the back bays around Menemsha and up into the Cuttyhunk pond — that's the bait pipeline for everything else happening out there, and it's a strong tell that bigger adult blues will keep building through the rest of August into September. If you want a guaranteed bend in the rod while you wait on the albies, drop small Kastmasters or bucktail teasers around the Menemsha jetty on the incoming and you won't wait long.

On the bottom, Mashnee flats are still the most consistent thing going in the bay for fluke — 3 to 5 pound fish coming on Gulp Swimming Mullets in white and bubblegum, 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow across 15 to 20 feet on the outgoing. The channel side of Mashnee has been better for scup and the occasional sea bass, small strips of squid on a two-hook bottom rig doing the damage in 25 to 30 feet. Structure east of Cleveland Ledge is worth a stop too — anglers running spot-lock trolling motors have quietly turned into the smartest guys on the water this month, holding dead-still over marks along Hog Island Channel and the Stony Point Dike drop-offs and jigging bucktails straight down without the hassle of anchoring twice a tide. If you've got the technology, use it — precision over structure is what's separating full coolers from empty ones right now.

Looking ahead, the tide is building back toward the August 28 full moon, and that's the thing I'm watching closest. More current through Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole means more bait getting pushed and compressed, which is exactly the recipe that turns a few nervous bait balls into a real blitz. If the warm pool holds its position — and there's no sign yet that it's breaking down — I'd bet on bonito showing in numbers around the Elizabeth Islands within the next week to ten days, with false albacore stacking in right behind them, same as they do every fall once the current gets some teeth in it. I wouldn't skip a trip to Robinson's Hole this weekend. Bring metals, bring patience, and watch that water — the bait's already telling you what's coming.
