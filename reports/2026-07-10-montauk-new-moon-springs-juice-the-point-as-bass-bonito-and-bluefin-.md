# New moon springs juice the Point as bass, bonito, and bluefin all show at once
*Big water at the boulder field, the first bonito of the year in the wash, and schoolie bluefin running practically in sight of the Lighthouse.*

**MONTAUK, NY — July 10** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

We're sliding into new moon on the 13th, and if you've fished this rock long enough you know what that means before you even check a tide chart: bigger swings, harder current, and a Point that comes alive in ways it just can't on the neaps. Last week the tides were still building off the last quarter, current lazy through the rip, and honestly the bite reflected it — some bass, some blues, nothing that made you skip breakfast. That's changing fast. By Friday through Monday we're into peak spring tide alignment, and the moon's going dark right alongside it, which is the double-whammy I wait for all summer: strong current stacking bait against the boulder field, and no moonlight up top to spook fish off the structure at night. Water on the ocean side has stayed remarkably clean and cooler than the bays — we're seeing upwelled water in the low 70s pushing onto the reefs while Fort Pond Bay and the harbor are sitting warm, closer to the high 70s. That split matters. It's exactly why the fluke have abandoned the bay skinny water and slid out to the inlet edges and the ocean structure, where the temperature break and the current are doing their job.

The striper fishing at the Point itself has been the real story. Guys are still connecting with fish to 50 pounds working the boulder field on the incoming, and I don't say that lightly — that's not a number I throw around for hype, that's what's actually coming over the rail. The pattern hasn't changed in the twenty years I've fished it: on the building incoming, position yourself so you're casting bucktails and topwater up-current into the boulders off the Lighthouse, letting the sweep carry your presentation across the rock piles rather than fighting it. White or black 2-ounce bucktails tipped with pork rind or a swim shad have been the money bait in the low light, and once the sun's up, switch to a Gibbs Danny or a needlefish worked slow through the wash. Fly guys throwing big Deceivers and Half-and-Halfs on 10-weights have been sticking fish too, particularly right at first light before the boat traffic stacks up. North Bar has been holding fish on the same tide stage — same idea, work the seam where the bar drops into deeper water, watch your fishfinder for that hard edge, and hold there through the push. Shagwong Reef is more of an outgoing spot for me; the current there sets up cleaner on the ebb, and that's when I've seen the better blues mixing in with bass, 8 to 12 pounds, hammering diamond jigs and Ava 27s bounced off bottom in 20 to 30 feet.

Here's the honest part: it's not been a wire-to-wire bite. Some days the boulder field goes off for two hours around the tide change and then shuts down cold. I fished it three mornings last week, connected solidly on two of them — the third the current never quite got going the way I wanted and we scratched out a couple schoolies and went home. That's the nature of springs building versus springs peaked; once we're fully into the new moon tides this weekend, I expect that window to widen out.

Fluke has genuinely shifted. With the bay running warm, the better drifts have been ocean side and around the harbor inlet on the outgoing, working 25 to 40 feet with white bucktails and Gulp Swimming Mullets, chartreuse and pink both producing. Turtle Cove has had some quietly solid days for shorts mixed with a few keepers to 4 pounds — not a program to build a whole trip around yet, but worth a drift if you're already out there working the structure for bass.

Now the fun stuff. The first bonito and Spanish mackerel of the season are showing at the eastern end — this is right on schedule, and with the cold wall between Block Island Sound and Rhode Island Sound breaking down, that corridor is spreading wider than just the island itself. I'd be working the rips off the Lighthouse and out toward Frisbee's Bank on clean incoming water with size 1 Deadly Dicks and small epoxy jigs, high-speed retrieve, watching for diving terns rather than committing blind. It's early — one or two fish a trip right now, not a blitz — but the signal is there and it tends to build fast once it starts.

And south of the Point, it's been a legitimate scramble. Schoolie bluefin have pushed in close enough that small boats without tuna towers are getting their first fish of the year practically within sight of the Lighthouse. The bigger meat is out on the 20-fathom breaks — the upwelling this week has sharpened that edge, and boats working ballast tanks of squid at night under lights have been finding bluefin stacked there consistently. If you've got the boat and the stomach for an overnight, that's the play on this dark moon; squid-jig at anchor, let the current do the work, and be ready when the marks show up mid-water.

Looking ahead: if the springs build the way they're forecast to through the weekend, I'd fish the incoming at the boulder field Friday and Saturday morning before the tourist traffic thickens the water, then pivot to bonito scouting on the afternoon outgoing once the sun's high and the terns start working. Plan B if the bass go quiet is always the same out here — there's a fluke drift or a blitz of bluefish somewhere off Shagwong. This rock rarely leaves you with nothing.
