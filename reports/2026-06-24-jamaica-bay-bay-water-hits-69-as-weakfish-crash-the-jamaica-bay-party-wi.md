# Bay water hits 69 as weakfish crash the Jamaica Bay party with keeper fluke
*Trifecta fishing fires up as stripers move out and summer species settle in.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — June 24** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The bay hit 69 degrees this week and everything changed. I'm talking about a real shift — the kind that happens when water temps cross that magic threshold and the whole ecosystem flips the switch. The stripers that have been our bread and butter since April are starting to thin out, but don't cry about it. What's moving in is even better.

Weakfish are back in Jamaica Bay in numbers I haven't seen in years. Real weakfish, not the scattered singles we've been picking at. I'm talking keeper fish to 19 inches mixed with throwbacks, and they're hitting everything from bucktails to soft plastics to cut bait. The Marine Parkway Bridge area has been the hot zone, especially on the incoming tide when the cooler ocean water pushes bait up against the structure.

The fluke bite has absolutely exploded. Twenty-five-pound doormat? That happened this week right here in our backyard. The fish are staging in 8 to 15 feet of water along the channel edges, and they're not picky about presentation. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are deadly when you drag them slow on the drift. But here's the thing — don't sleep on the hi-lo rig with lugworms. The old-school approach is putting fish in the boat when the fancy stuff fails.

What's really got me excited is the mixed bag action. You drop a line and you honestly don't know what's coming up. Keeper fluke, weakfish, sea robins the size of small dogs, and even some late-season stripers in the 24 to 28-inch range. That's the Jamaica Bay special right there — the kind of fishing that keeps you guessing and keeps it fun.

The bait situation tells the whole story. Bunker are still around but they're thinning out and spreading deeper into the bay system. The spearing are thick along the shorelines, especially around the Cross Bay Bridge pilings where the current creates eddies. Sand fleas are everywhere on the flats, which explains why the fluke bite has been so consistent.

Tide timing matters more now than it did a month ago. The outgoing has been producing the best weakfish action, especially that last two hours before low water when the bait gets funneled through the deeper cuts. For fluke, I'm seeing the best action on the incoming, particularly around the slack high when they settle on structure and ambush whatever drifts by.

The squid bite is absolutely insane right now. Sheepshead Bay has been the epicenter, but they're showing up in Jamaica Bay too, especially after dark around any structure with lights. Simple squid jigs worked vertically are all you need, and you can fill a cooler in an hour when you find them.

Water clarity has been decent despite some algae blooms earlier in the month. The 69-degree reading is about four degrees warmer than the ocean side of Rockaway Inlet, and that temperature gradient is creating a perfect setup. Bait gets pushed in on the flood, predators follow, and we get to intercept them.

Looking ahead, this full moon cycle should really fire things up. Spring tides mean stronger currents, which means better bait movement and more aggressive feeding. I'm watching for the first real push of porgies, which should happen any day now as the water continues to warm. The blackfish bite should also start to pick up around the deeper structure as we head into July.

If you haven't been out to Jamaica Bay lately, this is your week. The fishing is as good as it gets for this time of year, and with multiple species cooperating, you're almost guaranteed to bend a rod. Just remember — the bay is alive right now, so fish it like it is.
