# Cape May Rips Turn Picky as the Bunker Start Pouring Out of the Bay
*Early stripers and weakfish are showing on the bait exodus, but the rips haven't found their rhythm yet — and the drum run is long over.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 14** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Report card on the Rips this week: C-plus, trending up. Not a bad grade for mid-September, but nobody's calling it steady yet. The bite is there in flashes — a good drift here, a dead one right behind it — and that inconsistency is exactly what you'd expect when the season is turning over. Peanut bunker and spot are staging thick at the bay mouth right now, and that's the tell. We flagged this back on September 7th — said watch for stripers to start staging at the bay mouth as the first cooler nights pushed bait out — and here it is, right on schedule. The fish are following the forage out of the back bays and stacking on the edges where the bay water meets the ocean push, which means the Cape May Canyon entrance and the outer edge of the Rips are holding early-run stripers and a scattering of weakfish working the same bait.

The problem for boats working the Rips proper has been timing more than location. The window is tight — first two hours of the ebb out of Cape May Point, when the bay is dumping bait and current against the shoal edges — and if you miss it, you're fishing dead water with a lot of boat traffic on top of it. Slack tide has been a killer this week. Multiple captains working the rips report the same thing: good marks on the sounder, fish not committing until the tide really gets moving. That lines up with what we're seeing on the moon — tide range is building back up heading into the next phase, and that increased current should tighten up the bite windows and make them more predictable rather than more lax. If you can only fish one tide stage right now, make it the first hour of outgoing at the Rips, working bucktails and swim shads down through the color change where the green bay water meets the clean ocean water.

Delaware Bay has actually been the more honest bite this week. We called this one too — said on the 11th that the fall croaker and weakfish run was building in the back bay channels as the new-moon tide pushed bait, and that's exactly what's playing out from the Fortescue channels down toward the mouth. Weakfish in the 16 to 20-inch class are showing on peeler crab and Fishbites fished on high-low rigs in six to ten feet of water, best on the last two hours of the incoming when the current slows enough for the bite to key in. Croaker are mixed in with them, thick some days and absent the next — that's typical for this stretch of the run, and it should keep building as water temps ease down through the 60s over the next couple weeks. Nobody's finding black drum. That run's done — May and into early June is the window, and anybody still soaking crabs for drum in the bay right now is wasting bait. Save the crab for the weakfish.

Fluke are still worth the trip if you're not locked into the Rips. The bay-side grounds and the ocean structure off Hereford Inlet are holding legal fish on bucktail and Gulp combinations — white 4-inch swimming mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the channel edges on the drift, still catching keepers to 5 pounds. Live spot and killies are producing too where boats can get them, particularly around the deeper holes off Brandywine Shoal where bait's been holding tight to structure. This bite has another couple weeks in it before the fluke start their move toward deeper water, and right now it's more consistent than the striper bite, which says something about where the effort should go if the Rips stay picky.

Bluefish are the wildcard building underneath all of this. The bunker schools pushing out of the back bays aren't just pulling stripers — blues are shadowing the same bait, and the size class coming through the region has been notably better than a typical September push. Expect that to show up here as bigger cocktail blues and the occasional gorilla mixed into the bunker schools working the mouth of the bay and the nearshore lumps off Cape May Point, especially as tidal range builds this weekend and pushes more bait through the inlets. Silversides are thick on the nearshore reefs too, and that's drawing a few bonito into the mix on the troll and cast — small feeders, but worth a look if you're running spreads outside the Rips.

Looking at the next few days: if the tide range keeps building like it should heading into the new moon phase, the Rips should tighten up and get more reliable, not less — more current means the bait gets concentrated against the shoal edges instead of spreading thin. That's when this bite typically clicks. Best case, the ebb tide at the Rips starts holding fish through the whole window instead of just the first hour, and the bay-side weakfish bite keeps building as more bait gets pushed out of the marsh. Worst case, we get a stretch of light wind and slack water that lets the bait scatter again, and everybody's back to picking through short stops. Either way, the direction is right — the fall bait exodus is underway, and that's the engine that drives everything else for the next six weeks.
