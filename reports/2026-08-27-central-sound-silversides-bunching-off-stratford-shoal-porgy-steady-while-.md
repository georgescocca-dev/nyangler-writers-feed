# Silversides Bunching Off Stratford Shoal — Porgy Steady While Bass Wait for Dark
*Bait is stacking in the rip and along the reefs, but the stripers won't commit until the sun goes down and the moon keeps building.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — August 27** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

The bait tells the story before the boats do, and this week the story is silversides and peanut bunker pushing into tight, nervous pods along the edges of Stratford Shoal and down through the Norwalk Islands. Terns have been working short, choppy dives over these pods rather than the sustained blitzes you get when predators are underneath pushing bait to the surface — a sign the bass and blues know the bait is there but aren't committing hard in daylight. That matches what the thermometer is telling us. Kings Point is reading 70.9 degrees, New London down around 69.4, and that west-to-east gradient is exactly the kind of setup that keeps bait comfortable and spread out across the middle Sound rather than pinned into one obvious ambush point.

We flagged this pattern last week — said bass would hold in a summer pattern rather than break into fall migration until nighttime temps really start dropping, and that's precisely what the fleet is seeing. Daytime surface water is still too warm for stripers to feed with any consistency in open water, so the program has shifted almost entirely to structure and darkness. The better reports are coming after sundown on eels fished tight to current — around the bridge abutments and points from Oyster Bay through Port Jeff, and along the rock piles off Charles Island on the Connecticut side. Rigged eels or 6-inch swimming plastics worked slow on the drop, right on the current seam, are picking up fish in the 24 to 34-inch range on the front half of the incoming tide, when the water is still cool from the overnight low. Daylight hours have been mostly a stretch-and-wait game unless you're working deep structure — the Stratford Shoal rip itself, worked slow with a bucktail bounced off bottom on the tail of the ebb, has given up a few daytime fish, but it's not the kind of bite you can set your watch to right now.

Porgy and scup remain the most dependable meat-in-the-cooler fishery in the zone, and that consistency is holding exactly as expected. Party boats working the reefs off Middle Ground Light and the rock piles scattered through the Norwalk Islands corridor are still icing solid catches on standard high-low rigs with squid strips and sandworms, fished in 20 to 40 feet of water on the last two hours of the incoming. This is one of the more reliable programs in the region right now, and there's no sign of it slowing down heading into September.

Bluefish are filling in the gaps everywhere else. Snapper-sized fish are thick in the back bays and harbor mouths — Oyster Bay, Port Jeff Harbor, the Norwalk River mouth — giving kids and light-tackle guys steady action on small spoons and Kastmasters worked fast near the surface. Out on the drift over structure, cocktail blues in the 3 to 6-pound range are mixing into the fluke and bass catches, especially anywhere bait is bunching along a current edge. If you're marking bait on the sounder and getting nothing but blues, don't fight it — that's usually the sign to move rather than grind it out.

Fluke are the soft spot this week, and it's not for lack of effort. With water this warm spread across the flats, fluke have scattered rather than held tight to structure, which is softening catch rates across the board except in the places that still carry a cooler bottom layer — channel edges, deeper holes, and current-swept structure like the drop-offs around the Norwalk Islands and the deeper stretches off Middle Ground. Four-inch white Gulp paired with a 3/4-ounce bucktail, dragged slow right on bottom through those deeper cuts, is still the most consistent producer, but the days of picking fish up anywhere shallow and sandy are on pause until the water cools.

The moon is building toward full, and that's already sharpening the tidal swings — we called this too, expecting the bigger current push to improve current-driven bites, and the last few days of stronger sets through the Stratford Shoal rip and around Charles Island back that up. Over the next 48 to 72 hours, expect that trend to continue: bigger tides, tighter bite windows, better current definition at the structure spots. Watch the bait closely through this stretch — if those peanut bunker and silverside pods start getting nervous and balling tight along the beaches near Port Jeff or the Norwalk Islands, that's often the first tell that bonito or false albacore have slipped into the middle Sound, and nobody will see it on a boat before the birds and the bait give it away first. Nothing confirmed yet this week, but the timing lines up and it's worth keeping an eye on.

Looking further out, the overnight air is starting to carry a slightly cooler edge, and if that holds through the coming week it should begin nudging surface temps down enough to stretch the striper bite later into the morning and earlier into the evening. That's usually the first real crack in the summer pattern before the fall push gets serious. For now, if you've only got one trip in you, fish the dark side of the tide on eels around structure for bass, hit the reefs on the incoming for a guaranteed box of porgy, and keep the bucktails ready in case those blues — or something faster — show up on the drift.
