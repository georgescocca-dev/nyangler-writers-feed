# Moriches Inlet's West Shoulder Is Cutting Deeper — Bass and Blues Found the New Seam
*A shifting sandbar off Cupsogue has redrawn the drift line, and the fish followed the new edge before the anglers did.*

**MORICHES BAY, NY — August 7** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

_AI fishing-intelligence report. It was prepared from 0 supplied evidence records and 17 supplied analysis inputs; it does not claim firsthand fishing._

The west shoulder of Moriches Inlet has been quietly deepening through July, and this past week the current signature at the throat finally showed it. Where the ebb used to fan out broad and shallow off the Cupsogue groin field, it's now cutting a tighter, deeper channel closer to the west jetty rocks — the kind of storm-cut adjustment this inlet makes every couple of seasons when a summer swell train reworks the bar. With 3.6-foot southerly groundswell running at a 6-second period offshore, the ocean side hasn't been comfortable water to stand in, and that discomfort has pushed bait and predators alike off the open beach front and into the structure where the bay drains out. That's exactly where this week's action has concentrated.

Offshore water is reading warm — 75.9°F on the open buoy, with Montauk holding a similar 74.5°F — and that warmth is backing up into the inlet mouth on the last of the incoming tide, then getting squeezed out hard on the ebb through the new, narrower cut. The result is a sharper thermal and current edge right where bay water meets the ocean push, and stripers have been stacking on it. The pattern matches what's showing up further west along the south shore corridor: fish relating tightly to current seams and visible boils rather than open water, working bucktails cast across the seam and swept down with the drop, then retrieved with a slow bottom bump through the deepest part of the cut. A 1.5-ounce bucktail tipped with a strip of squid or a white Gulp swimming mullet, fished on the outgoing tide as the current rips through the new channel, has been the most consistent producer — slot fish in the 28- to 32-inch range, with the occasional overslot working the deeper pocket on the inside edge of the west jetty.

Bluefish have been the more reliable story. We flagged this two weeks back — said blitz activity was likely given bait movement along the south shore — and bunker pods pushing through the inlet mouth on the tide changes have delivered exactly that. Early morning has been the window: birds working tight over bait balls just outside the throat, blues busting bunker on top hard enough to see the wakes from the beach. Bunker spoons and Hopkins No-Eql in chrome or bunker patterns, cast into the melee and ripped back fast, have been drawing quick strikes. These fish aren't picky and they aren't shy — the trouble is timing it, because the blitzes have been short and mobile, following the bait rather than holding station. If you're set up at Cupsogue or the eastern side near Tuthill Point at first light, keep the rod rigged and ready; the window can close in twenty minutes.

Fluke fishing inside the bay has been steady but unspectacular — which is an honest way to put it. The deeper cuts adjacent to the inlet, particularly the channel edges running past Pattersquash Island and the flats off Senix Creek, are holding fish in that 8- to 14-foot range over clean sand and shell. White and chartreuse Gulp on 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow with the tide rather than power-drifted, have taken keepers to 20 inches, but the ratio of shorts to keepers has been high enough that this isn't a fast box-filler right now. Work the drop-offs where the bay bottom breaks toward the inlet channel — that's where the bigger fish have been sitting, using the depth change to ambush bait getting pulled through on the current.

Porgies remain a dependable backup at the Cupsogue jetty rocks, especially on the last two hours of the incoming when the water's had time to warm and clear. Bloodworms or clam on a simple hi-lo rig, fished tight to the rock base in 6 to 10 feet, will keep a rod bent even when the bigger species go quiet. Weakfish have been thin — a few reports of fish working the deeper bay flats at dusk near Tuthill, but nothing to build a trip around this week. Blackfish season is closed inshore through the summer months, so that's off the table until fall regulations open it back up.

Looking ahead, the ocean swell is the variable to watch. If it lays down through the middle of the week, the open beach front at Cupsogue and Smith Point should start producing again as bait redistributes along the surf line instead of funneling exclusively through the inlet. If the swell holds in the 3-to-4-foot range, I'd keep working the inlet structure — the new cut on the west shoulder isn't going anywhere fast, and it's proven itself as the most consistent current edge in the system right now. Best bet for the coming days: fish the last two hours of the outgoing at the throat, bucktails on the bottom, bunker spoons ready for the surface show, and don't wander far from the jetty rocks until the swell tells you it's safe to.
