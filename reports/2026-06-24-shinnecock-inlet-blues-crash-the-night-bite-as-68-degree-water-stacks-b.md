# Inlet blues crash the night bite as 68-degree water stacks bait on the flood
*Outgoing tides at Shinnecock producing slot bass to 28 inches while big bluefish hunt the rips.*

**SHINNECOCK, NY — June 24** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The inlet's been electric after dark this week, and it's all about that 68-degree water pushing through on the flood tide. When bay water runs four degrees warmer than the ocean side, you get that thermal gradient that stacks bait against the jetties — and the predators follow.

Best action's been on the outgoing after midnight. I've been working the north jetty with 5-inch Yozuri Mag Darters in the traditional white-and-blue, letting the flat head wiggle in the current on a dead drift. That D-wood action is deadly when the water's moving — even on the pause, the current keeps the bait alive. Slot bass to 28 inches have been crushing it in the deep water off the tip, especially when the tide's really ripping.

The bluefish invasion started three nights ago. Big choppers — some pushing 15 pounds — are hunting the rips like wolves. They're scattered throughout the water column, so you need to adjust. When they're blitzing on top, I swing a 3/4-ounce bucktail with a white Zoom fork tail underneath the chaos. Pop-pop-pause retrieve gets the bass while the blues tear up the surface.

Crystal-clear water's made presentation critical. These fish can see everything in this gin-clear blue-green water we've got right now. I'm running 30-pound fluoro leaders even on my light tackle setup — that PC Fun Carbon rod handles it fine, and you need the insurance against those blue teeth. Lost too many Mag Darters early in the week before I wised up.

Bay fishing's been solid for mixed bags. The Ponquogue Bridge has been holding weakfish on the incoming tide — first real push I've seen this season. Small Deadly Dicks in silver work best, cast tight to the pilings and worked slow. Fluke are scattered through the deeper cuts, with the best action in 15 to 20 feet on the flood. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 1/2-ounce bucktails are producing keepers to 4 pounds.

What's driving this bite is simple physics. Ocean temps are sitting at 64 degrees while the bay's running 68 — that's a massive gradient for June. Add in these light northwest winds that have been holding for three days, and you get perfect conditions. The thermocline's setting up shallow, concentrating bait in the top 20 feet of water.

The new moon spring tides are flushing everything out of the back bay. Peanut bunker, spearing, even some sand eels are getting swept through the inlet on the ebb. I'm seeing bait balls stacked against the south jetty around 2 AM when the current's really screaming.

One thing I've noticed — boat traffic during prime time is killing schools. These big center consoles blasting through the inlet at sunset are scattering fish that took hours to set up. If you're serious about the night bite, wait until after 11 PM when the weekend warriors head home.

Looking ahead, this pattern should hold through the weekend. We're getting a full moon Friday, which means even bigger tides. That's when I expect the weakfish bite to really turn on — those big spring tides will flush more bait out of the Peconic system through the canal. I'll be watching the inlet drain at sunset for the first serious run of tiderunners.

Water temps should climb another degree or two this week if these light winds hold. Once bay water hits 70, the fluke bite in the deeper holes should explode. Until then, it's all about reading the current and being there when the bait moves.
