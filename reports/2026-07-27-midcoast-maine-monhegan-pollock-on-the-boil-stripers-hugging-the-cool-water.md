# Monhegan pollock on the boil, stripers hugging the cool water at the river mouths
*Groundfish still hold in the Bay's deep water while the bass push into every spring-fed mouth from the Kennebec to the Medomak.*

**BOOTHBAY HARBOR, ME — July 27** — _by Rusty Pelletier, Zone Writer_

Had a fellow stop me at the Co-op dock Tuesday morning, hauling gear off the *Sea Hag*, and he says to me, "Rusty, water's warm enough to swim in off Ocean Point." I told him that's exactly the problem. Forty years watching this coast and I can tell you the surface heat does two things every July — it pushes the stripers into the mouths where the rivers keep dumping cold water, and it makes the groundfish sulk deeper in the Bay than most sport boats bother to go. Neither one is news to anybody who's fished here longer than a season, but it bears repeating because half the boats I see anchored up on open water this week are fishing water that's ten degrees too warm for what they're after.

The bass have been thick and predictable where the fresh water hits the salt. Kennebec mouth off Popham, the Sheepscot around the Cross River ledges, and the Damariscotta down by Fort Island — all three have been holding fish on the last of the ebb and the first two hours of the flood, when the current stacks bait against the ledge rock and the cooler outflow keeps the water livable through the heat of the day. Live mackerel under a slip float has been the best producer, but guys drifting chunk mackerel on fish-finder rigs in 15 to 25 feet are doing just as well without the hassle of keeping bait alive. After dark, eels on the swim have taken some good fish off the Damariscotta ledges — nothing enormous, mostly 26 to 32 inches, but steady, and that's the pattern we flagged last week when we said this warm, stable stretch would keep the bass locked into that river-mouth program. It has, right down to the tide stage.

Monhegan's been the bright spot for anybody willing to make the run. Pollock have been busting bait right on the surface off White Head and around the backside ledges at first light — schoolie-sized mostly, two to five pounds, but they'll hit anything silver thrown fast, and it's a fine way to fill an hour before the bigger fish wake up. There have been bass working those same ledges on the tail of the tide, sliding up onto the rock as the current slackens, and a few boats have picked off stripers to 34 inches on topwater plugs worked right in the wash. Eastern Egg Rock has been similar — the puffins are still working the bait balls out there, and where the puffins are diving you'll usually find bass or mackerel underneath doing the same thing from below. Diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid have been drawing mackerel by the bucketful around the Rock, which is as good a livewell fill-up as you'll find this time of year.

Out in Penobscot Bay proper, the groundfish story hasn't changed and isn't likely to. Cod and haddock are holding in the deep water — the channel east of Vinalhaven, the deep hole off Isle au Haut, and the drop-off between Matinicus and the Bay islands are all still stacking fish in 150 to 220 feet. You need to jig it heavy, and you need to fish it on the slower stages of the tide, because that current runs hard enough through those channels to sweep a light jig sideways before it ever touches bottom. Herring strips or a diamond jig tipped with squid, dropped straight down and worked with short hops off the bottom, has been the ticket. It's not a numbers game — three or four keepers for a morning's work is a solid trip right now — but the fish are there if you're willing to fish deep and slow instead of chasing surface activity that isn't happening in that colder water.

Bluefish are still mostly a rumor up here, though I'd keep half an eye open. The bunker-driven blitzes that have been tearing up beaches to the south have been pushing steadily northward all month, and there's been enough warm water sitting in the outer bay that I wouldn't be shocked to see scattered blues show up around Boothbay or Pemaquid before August gets old. Nothing confirmed yet — just something to watch if you're fishing the open water outside the river mouths.

The moon's building toward full over the next week, which should sharpen the current through the Bay's channels and the Thorofares, and that's good news for the groundfish game — stronger tide means better drift speed for the jig and more predictable bite windows around the turns. For the stripers, as long as this warm, settled weather holds, I'd expect the same program to keep running: fish the cool water at the river mouths on the moving tide, save the ledges at Monhegan and Egg Rock for early and late light, and don't waste a morning fishing open bay water for bass that have already moved somewhere colder. If a front comes through and mixes things up, that could change fast — but for now, this is a coast that's telling you plainly where to be, if you're willing to listen to the water instead of the calendar.
