# Weakfish surge hits Jones Inlet as bay water climbs to 68 degrees
*Trophy weaks return to the inlet while fluke fishing stays steady on the drift.*

**JONES INLET, NY — June 24** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The water's telling a story this week, and it's one I haven't heard in years. Bay temperatures hit 68 degrees — a solid four degrees warmer than the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do. It's stacking bait on the flood tide and holding weakfish on the ebb, just like the old days.

I've been watching this setup develop all month. The inlet's been flushing clean with these new moon spring tides, and now we're seeing the payoff. Weakfish to 26 inches are showing in the surf at night, and I'm hearing about fish in the 6-pound class coming from the inlet mouth on the outgoing water. These aren't the schoolie weaks we've been getting used to — these are the real deal, the fish that made this inlet famous back when my father was teaching me to read the current.

The pattern's been consistent: hit the inlet on the start of the ebb with fresh bunker chunks or live peanuts on a fishfinder rig. The fish are holding in the deeper cuts where the current sweeps bait out of the bay. I've been working the north side of the channel, tight to the jetty rocks, and the takes are solid — none of that tentative pecking you get from schoolies.

Fluke fishing's been steady but not spectacular. The Meadowbrook drift is producing keeper-sized fish to 20 inches, but you're working for them. White bucktails with squid strips on the bottom, chartreuse teaser up top, and you need to stay mobile. The fish are scattered, not packed up like they'll be in July when the water really heats up.

What's interesting is the bait situation. The inlet's loaded with sand eels right now — thick clouds of them moving on every tide change. That's what's drawing the weakfish in from deeper water, and it's also why the striper bite has been hit-or-miss. When bass are keyed on sand eels, they get selective. Diamond jigs in the 2-ounce range with green tails are taking fish at sunset, but you need to work past the break and into the wash where the eels are getting tumbled.

The Wantagh Bridge area has been producing short fluke consistently, but most are running 15 to 17 inches. Still, it's good action for kids and light tackle. Smelt has been outfishing squid there — hook it behind the head and let it flutter on the drift.

Looking at the water conditions, we're sitting pretty for the next week. Ocean temps are holding in the mid-60s while the bay stays warm, and that temperature break is critical for keeping bait concentrated in the inlet. The southwest winds we've been getting are actually helping — they're pushing warmer surface water into the bay and creating that thermal layering that holds fish.

Full moon's coming Friday, which means big tides and serious water movement. I'm expecting the weakfish bite to really turn on during the evening ebb tides. The inlet will be draining hard, concentrating bait in the channel, and those big weaks will be there to ambush them. This could be the week we've been waiting for — the first real run of quality weakfish in years.

For the weekend warriors, focus on the inlet mouth from sunset through the first two hours of outgoing tide. Fresh bunker chunks on circle hooks, fished on the bottom in 15 to 25 feet. Don't overthink it — these fish want a big meal in moving water. And if you hook into something that feels heavy and fights deep, don't horse it. These weakfish have soft mouths, and we need every one of them to make it back to spawn again.
