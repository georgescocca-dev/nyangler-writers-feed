# New moon springs load up The Race — sunset flood window back on for eastern Sound bass
*A week of neap-tide sluggishness gives way to building current as the new moon approaches, and the first real evening bass bite since June's full moon is lining up for the weekend.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 8** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Last week was a grind, and I'll say that up front. We were sitting in that dead zone after the June full moon — neap tides, current dying out early in The Race and Plum Gut, and a stretch of southwest wind that let the bay water climb into the upper 70s. That warm, soft water pushed the fluke out of Gardiners Bay and Orient Harbor and stacked them into the channels and inlet mouths where the outgoing tide still moves clean, cooler water off the bottom. Anybody still working the flats got picked apart. Anybody who moved to the drop-offs did fine.

This week the moon changes everything. We're building toward new moon on the 12th-13th, and that means spring tides are loading up fast — bigger exchange, harder current through The Race and Plum Gut, and critically, the PM flood is now lining up with sunset. That's the window I've been waiting on since the last full moon. Friday through Monday, the high stand at Kings Point converges right at dusk, which means the bait gets pushed hard against structure exactly when the light drops and the bass turn predatory. If you've only got one evening this month to fish eastern Sound structure, this is it.

The Race has been the most consistent piece of water for stripers, but you have to work the stage of tide correctly. On the last two hours of the ebb, I'm anchoring up-current of the eddies on the north side — the humps around 80 to 100 feet where the current splits off the reef structure — and drifting big parachute jigs and bunker-imitation umbrella rigs down into the seam. Fish have been holding low, right on the structure, not up in the water column, so weight matters. I've been running 12- to 16-ounce jig heads just to hold bottom in that 4- to 5-knot water. Once the tide starts to slack, that's when the eels come out — 12-inch rigged eels drifted naturally through the slower eddies produce the better fish, mid-20s to low-30s, with a few reports of fish pushing 40 mixed in from boats working the deeper holes off Fishers Island. Nothing guaranteed there, but the size class has been real.

Plum Gut has been more of a bluefish show the last ten days. Diamond jigs — the 4- to 6-ounce Point Jude / Deadly Dick style — worked with a fast retrieve through the rip on the last of the incoming has been drawing blues in the 5- to 10-pound class, sometimes breaking the surface hard enough that you don't even need to jig, just cast into the boil. If you want bass instead of blues in that same water, slow down and go subsurface — bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, bounced along the bottom edge where the Gut narrows down to about 40 to 50 feet, has been the better bet for stripers in that pressure-cooker current.

Fluke are the story worth paying attention to right now because of where they've relocated. With the bay running warm, the better fish have slid out to the mouth of Plum Gut and the deeper flats off Orient Point Reef, where the upwelled Sound water sitting around the low 70s is holding bait. Drifting 4- to 5-inch white or chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾- to 1-ounce bucktails, right on the outgoing tide when that current runs clean out of the harbor mouths, has produced the most consistent keeper-class fish — nothing giant, mostly 18 to 22 inches, but steady enough that a few drifts will fill a limit if you're patient about staying on the edge of the channel rather than working the middle.

Porgies have been reliable filler fish on the structure around Fishers Island and the reef ground off Orient — small green crab or sandworm on a high-low rig, fished tight to the rocks in 20 to 35 feet on the slower parts of the tide, will keep a rod bent between striper drifts. Blackfish are still a ways off from being worth targeting hard this stretch, so I wouldn't burn a trip on tautog just yet — that bite firms up later in the season once water starts to cool.

Looking ahead, the alignment I'm watching hardest is Saturday and Sunday evening. New moon springs plus a PM flood hitting right at last light in The Race and Plum Gut — that's the kind of setup that produces the biggest fish of the summer in this zone, because the current volume moving through those cuts on a spring tide is enormous, and it concentrates bait against structure in a way neap tides simply can't. If the wind stays out of the south and doesn't blow the rips into chop, I'd be anchored on the north side eddies of The Race an hour before sunset with an eel in the water, working through the tide change into full dark. If the wind kicks up and the surface goes ugly, Plum Gut is more protected and will still fish well on bucktails through the same window. Either way, this is the best tide-and-light setup we've had since June, and after a slow stretch, that's worth building a trip around.
