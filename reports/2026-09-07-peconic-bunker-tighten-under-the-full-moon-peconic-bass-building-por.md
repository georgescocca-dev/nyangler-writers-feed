# Bunker Tighten Under the Full Moon — Peconic Bass Building, Porgies Thick at Shelter Island
*The bay's holding onto summer warmth while the bait balls up and the first real fall bass pattern starts to take shape.*

**GREENPORT, NY — September 7** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic runs on its own clock, and right now that clock says late summer even though the calendar says early fall. This bay warms faster than the Sound every spring and it holds its heat longer every September — shallow, dark-bottomed, and protected from the ocean swell that's been hammering the south shore all week. That heat retention is exactly why the porgy fishing has stayed textbook and why the bass pattern building right now is worth watching closely over the next several days.

The big story this week is the bunker. Schools that had been loosely scattered through Gardiners Bay and up into Orient are tightening up under the full moon that just passed on the 6th and 7th, and the stripers know it. We flagged this last week — said the building moon toward full would tighten these bait schools and that the first legitimate blitz activity of the fall was likely within the week to ten days as nights started cooling, and that's exactly the shape things are taking now. Boats working the mouth of Orient Harbor and out toward the Gut are seeing bass showing real interest in those bunker pods rather than just picking at the edges the way they were in August. It's not full blitz mode yet — the fish are staging, testing the schools, occasionally busting on top for a few minutes before going quiet again — but the ingredients are there. Nights have started dropping into the 50s, and that's the trigger this bay needs.

Plum Gut remains the spot to watch as that pattern develops. The current there rips hard enough to hold bait against the structure regardless of tide stage, and once those bunker schools get squeezed into the Gut on a strong ebb, the bass will find them fast. Live bunker under a balloon or on a fish-finder rig fished tight to the rip edge is the go-to right now, and a few boats are already picking off fish in the 20s to low 30s on bucktails worked along bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing. If the moon swings toward new phase by midweek and the bait keeps compressing, expect the first real topwater activity to show up in the Gut and along the Orient Point rips — that's usually how it starts out here, small and localized before it spreads down through Gardiners Bay.

The more consistent action all week has been the porgies, and that part of the report needs no hedging. Shelter Island continues to be the place — the reefs and rocky edges around Cedar Point, the drop-offs off Coecles Harbor, and the deeper holes along the North Ferry side are holding heavy numbers of scup in the 15- to 25-foot range. Sandworm and clam on a standard high-low rig, fished on the bottom during the last two hours of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing when the current slows enough to hold bottom, has been the producing combination. This is about as good as porgy fishing gets on the East End, and it should hold through the month as water temps stay favorable in that protected water.

Fluke are the mixed bag of the week. The bite is winding down from its summer peak, which is normal for this time of year, but there's still fish to be had in the channels — particularly around Jessup's Neck and the deeper cuts off Robins Island, 20 to 30 feet down, on white Gulp paired with bucktails or live killies fished slow on the drift. Sizes have been decent, a few fish pushing 4 to 5 pounds mixed in with the shorts, but the overall pace has slowed noticeably compared to July and early August. Expect that taper to continue as water cools and fluke start their move toward the ocean side over the coming weeks — if fluke is the target, the next two weeks are the window, not the month after.

Weakfish have been showing intermittently on the flats in Noyack Bay and off Three Mile Harbor, mostly at first light and again at dusk. It's not a reliable pattern yet — more of a bonus fish for anglers working soft plastics, chartreuse or white paddletails on light jigheads, than something to plan a whole trip around.

The other story worth watching closely is the approaching bay scallop season opener. Once that date is locked in, expect boat traffic and local attention to shift hard toward the scallop grounds, which will thin out some of the pressure on the porgy and fluke spots and change the rhythm of the whole bay for a few weeks. Worth keeping an eye on the announcement if scalloping is part of the plan.

Looking at the next few days, the bay stays warm enough that the porgy bite shouldn't slow down regardless of what the moon does. The bass situation is the one to track — if the bunker keep tightening in the Gut and Orient rips through the week and nighttime temps keep sliding, the first real blitz window of the fall should open up within the next several days. That's where the time is best spent right now: live bunker or bucktails on the ebb at Plum Gut, with an eye toward the rips lighting up on top once that bait gets squeezed hard enough.
