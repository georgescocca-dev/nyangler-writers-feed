# Cockenoe on the Neap: Fluke and Porgies Deep, Bass Punching In After Dark
*A softening tide and thinning weekend traffic put the reef bite back in reach — but the stripers want you out there when the moorings go quiet.*

**NORWALK, CT — July 21** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday to skip Penfield and run Cockenoe Reef on the back half of the ebb, and it was the right chess move. Here's the thinking: we're sliding into the first-quarter neap, current speed easing off across the whole western basin, and on a soft tide Cockenoe's structure holds bait tighter than Penfield's broader bar. Less current means less scatter — the porgies and fluke stack right on the rockpile instead of getting strung out across open sand. Add in a Tuesday morning with no regatta traffic out of Norwalk Cove, and I had that reef mostly to myself for three hours. That's the kind of window you plan a week around.

The fluke bite has been the most honest thing going in this zone. Cockenoe in 14 to 18 feet, drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, produced four keepers and better than a dozen shorts on that ebb. The channel edge between Sheffield Island and the mainland is doing the same thing — moving water pushing bait off the flats into that deeper cut, and the fluke are sitting right on the seam waiting for it. Best window is the last two hours of either tide, when there's still enough push to make the bucktail work but not so much you're bouncing bottom every other cast. Slack water on Cockenoe is dead — I've proven that to myself twice this month, no sense doing it a third time.

Porgies are thick on the Cows off Stamford, and I mean genuinely thick — top-and-bottom rigs with sandworm or clam in 20 to 25 feet putting anglers into steady action, fish running 10 to 12 inches with enough jumbos mixed in to make it worth the trip. That bite doesn't care much about tide stage, which makes the Cows my go-to when I want a sure thing instead of a tactical gamble. Same water is starting to produce black sea bass in better numbers — 20 to 30 feet of structure holding fish now, and that's exactly the pattern I flagged last week: sea bass fishing improving as we move deeper into summer, and it's playing out right on schedule. Squid strips or clam on a high-low rig, bounced tight to the rockpile, is all it takes. Expect that bite to keep building through August as water stays warm and those fish settle deeper into the structure.

Blackfish are off the table right now — Connecticut's summer closure has tautog shut down until September, so if you're marking fish on the Cows or Greenwich Point structure, note the spot and come back for the fall reopener. Plenty of guys are doing exactly that, dropping waypoints now for when the season flips back on.

Striped bass have gone almost entirely nocturnal, and that's not a surprise this deep into July with Kings Point reading 70.9 and the shallow basin holding heat all day. Daytime bass fishing in this zone right now is mostly a lost cause — I've made peace with that. The move is Greenwich Point and the rockpiles off Great Captain Island from last light through the first couple hours of dark, fishing bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig or swimming a live eel along the current edge. The bass are pushing up on bunker pockets working through the Sound after dark, and there've been enough marks and a few solid fish taken on those chunk baits to say the pattern's real, even if it's not a blitz. If you want blues instead, daylight bunker schools moving past the Norwalk Islands have triggered a couple of surface flurries this week — nothing sustained, but enough slashing bait to make it worth keeping a bucktail rigged and ready if you see birds working tight over bait off Sprite Island or Copps Island.

Weekend traffic is still the other variable you have to fish around out here. Saturday and Sunday, the mooring fields off Norwalk and the anchor fleet on Cockenoe get stacked by 9 AM, and that pressure pushes fish off the shallower structure. My move stays the same — first light or last two hours of daylight, before or after the recreational fleet shows up, especially on the reefs closest to the harbors.

Looking ahead, the neap should keep softening current through midweek, which widens the productive window on Cockenoe and the Sheffield channel beyond just peak tide — good news if you can only get out midday. I'd keep leaning on fluke and porgies as the bankable bite, treat sea bass on the Cows as the building story worth checking weekly, and save your best effort for the bass after dark. If the bunker schools hold their current push through the islands, I wouldn't be shocked to see a real daylight blitz develop off Norwalk before the week's out — but until birds are working and bait's exploding on the surface, I'm not betting the trip on it.
