# Peanuts Ball Up at Robinson's Hole — The Albies Aren't Far Behind
*Bait's stacking in the rips from Quick's Hole to Menemsha, the water's finally right, and the first false albacore of the season could show any tide now.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — August 3** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Stood on the rocks at Robinson's Hole Thursday evening and watched peanut bunker ball up so tight against the current line you'd have thought someone poured a bucket of mercury into the water. Terns were diving on them, a couple of bluefish were slashing through from underneath, and out past the second rip line I saw a nervous flicker of bait get pushed sideways hard enough to make my stomach drop — that quick, violent push that isn't a bluefish. Couldn't confirm it. Didn't see the fish itself. But that's exactly the kind of push that means something toothy and fast just came through underneath.

We flagged this zone two weeks back as one of the better bets in New England for the season's first albies and bonito, and everything happening in the water right now backs that call up. The temperature breaks are set up textbook — warm water pooling in Vineyard Sound, colder push still coming through Quick's Hole and Robinson's Hole on the bigger tides, and bait getting squeezed right into the seam where those two temperatures collide. That seam is where I'm putting my time over the next week and a half. Nobody's boated a confirmed false albacore in our waters yet this year — I want to be straight about that, no albies in the box, no bonito on the leader — but the bait behavior, the birds, and the water temp gradient are all lining up the way they do right before the first scout fish show. Historically that's late here, but this feels early-normal, not late.

While we wait on that, the fishing that's actually happening has been solid, if you know where to put the anchor. Striped bass have gone exactly where you'd expect with surface temps sitting in the mid-to-upper 70s — down deep on structure through the daylight hours, then sliding up to feed hard in the low-light windows. Sow and Pigs Reef off Cuttyhunk has been giving up bass to guys jigging bucktails and diamond jigs tight to the rocks on the last two hours of the ebb, fish mostly in the 26 to 32-inch range with a few better ones mixed in after dark. The Woods Hole ledges are holding fish too — that's a night bite almost exclusively now, eels drifted on the current seams by the Coast Guard station producing better than anything thrown in daylight. If you want a day fish, you're going deep — 25 to 40 feet over structure, heavier bucktails, slow bounces, patience.

Fluke are having their best stretch of the summer right now and I'd be fishing them hard if the albie itch weren't so strong. Menemsha Bight has been quietly excellent — 4 to 6-pound fish coming on white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow through 18 to 25 feet of water on the last of the incoming tide. The Woods Hole channel edges near the drawbridge have produced too, though you're fighting current there that can rip through at 3-plus knots on a big tide — light tackle guys, bring heavier jig heads than you think you need or you'll never feel bottom. Scup fishing has been the easy money all week — structure around Tarpaulin Cove and the rocks off Naushon are stacked with them, squid strips or sandworms on a simple high-low rig putting a fast dozen in the cooler for anybody who wants dinner without much drama.

Bluefish have been scattered but present wherever bait's been thick — a few blitzes reported off Oak Bluffs on schools of peanut bunker pushed close to the beach, topwater plugs and tins getting bit hard for short windows before the fish disappear as fast as they showed. That's a bait-driven fishery completely — find the bunker, find the blues, and when the bunker move off the blues go with them within hours.

Tide-wise, we're sliding into the neap around August 5th and 6th, and that's going to soften the current through the rips noticeably — Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole both run harder on the bigger tides, so expect the water to lay down some mid-week before building back up toward the next new moon push. Softer current means lighter jigs, slower drifts, and honestly it's not the worst thing for albie scouting — those fish will sit and pick at bait in a gentler rip just as happily as a screaming one, sometimes more comfortably.

If I had one day this week I'd fish the last two hours of daylight at Robinson's Hole on the building tide after the neap, watching for that nervous bait push and a fin that isn't a bluefish. Bring both an albie rod rigged with a small Deadly Dick or an epoxy jig and your bass gear, because right now you genuinely don't know which one's going to show first. That uncertainty is the whole fun of the first two weeks of August out here — everybody's watching the same rips, waiting for the same one boil that changes the whole conversation for the rest of the fall.
