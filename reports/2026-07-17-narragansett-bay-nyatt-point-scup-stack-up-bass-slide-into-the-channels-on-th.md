# Nyatt Point Scup Stack Up, Bass Slide Into the Channels on the Bunker
*The herring run is a memory now, but Narragansett Bay's summer pattern is settling in with scup thick on the ledges and stripers working bait pods off Prudence.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — July 17** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Ran into Sal down at the Fields Point pier Tuesday morning, coffee in one hand, scup rod in the other, telling me his kid brought home eleven keepers off the rocks the day before and didn't even have to move twenty feet. That's July on this bay in a nutshell — the herring's long gone up Buckeye Brook, the run wound down back in May like it always does, and now the whole system's shifted into that thick, buggy, mid-summer rhythm where the porgies own the ledges and the stripers have gone quiet and structure-bound.

Scup are doing exactly what they do this time of year — piling onto every rock pile and ledge from Nyatt Point down through Rocky Point and out toward Conimicut. I fished the rip off Rocky Point Wednesday on the last two hours of the incoming and it was about as steady as scup fishing gets — squid strips and bloodworms on high-low rigs, size 6 to 4 hooks, fishing tight to bottom in 12 to 18 feet. You don't need much weight there, just enough to hold in that current, two, maybe three ounces depending on how hard she's pushing. Guys are limiting out most mornings on the early flood before the pleasure boat traffic picks up and pushes the fish down off the structure. Same story at Nyatt — that rocky shelf drops off quick, and once the tide gets moving good you can feel those porgies stacked right on the edge where the mud meets rock.

Stripers are a tougher conversation right now, and I'm not going to sell anybody a bill of goods on it. The bay's warmed up enough that the bass have pushed off the flats and out of the upper river, and they're holding tighter to current-swept structure — the Mount Hope Bridge piers, the drop-off edges around Prudence Island, and the deeper holes off Conimicut Point where the Providence River current squeezes down. Best bite I've heard of and had myself came right at the top of the tide change, working live eels and cut bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs in 20 to 30 feet off the east side of Prudence, right around first light before the sun gets up over the water. Bunker schools have been showing up thick off Greenwich Bay and around Warwick Neck, and where you find those pods tight to the surface, especially on a dropping tide with some current pulling them off the flats, that's where the bigger fish have been sitting underneath, picking off stragglers. Nothing blitzing, but a few nice 28- to 34-inch fish coming out of that pattern on bunker chunks fished on the bottom, and on topwater plugs worked over the pods early before the boat traffic spooks everything.

Fluke are doing what fluke do in mid-July — pushing further up into the bay behind the warming water and the bait that's riding in with it. Greenwich Bay's holding a decent population right now, drifting the mud-to-sand transition zones in 15 to 25 feet with white Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid. The fish aren't huge — mostly 16 to 19 inches with the occasional keeper mixed in — but there's enough of them stacked in there that a slow drift on the outgoing tide will put bends in the rod. I'd also check the flats off Nyatt Point and down toward the mouth of the Providence River itself, where the current funnels bait through that channel edge — that's classic fluke water this time of year, and a few guys have been picking off solid fish there working bucktail and Gulp combos tight along the drop.

Bluefish have been scattered but present — a few schools of choppers working bunker off Rocky Point and out toward the lower bay near Jamestown, mostly in the mornings before the wind builds. If you see the birds working and the water nervous, that's your window — poppers and metal lips will get bit fast, but those pods move quick and don't sit still for long. Black sea bass are holding around the rockier structure off Prudence and out toward the lower bay ledges, decent numbers on squid strips fished tight to bottom in 25 to 35 feet, though the size has been modest — plenty of shorts mixed with a few keepers.

Weakfish, I'll be honest, have been scarce. A few reports of fish showing on the edges of the Providence River channel on quiet evenings, but nothing consistent enough to plan a trip around.

Looking ahead, the tides are building toward the next moon and that usually means better current, which is exactly what this bay needs to push bait around and get the bigger bass moving again. If the wind lays down and stays out of the southwest through the weekend, I'd focus early mornings on those bunker pods off Prudence and Greenwich Bay before the boat traffic churns things up, and I'd hit the scup ledges on the incoming tide because that bite's been the most dependable thing going right now. Dog days are here — fish the edges of the day, work the current, and don't expect the bass to make it easy on you.
