# Bass blitz fires the Point as 68-degree water stacks bait on the rips
*Eels after dark producing 40-pound class stripers while diamond jigs work the daylight hours.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 24** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is absolutely on fire right now, and it's not just the summer crowds I'm talking about. Water temps hit 68 degrees this week — that magic number that flips the switch on everything from the lighthouse rocks to the deep rips off Shagwong. When the thermometer climbs into the upper 60s out here, the bait gets nervous and the bass get aggressive.

The night bite has been nothing short of spectacular. Live eels fished tight to the boulder fields are producing bass in the 30 to 40-pound class, with fish hitting hard in that golden hour right after sunset. The key is getting your eel down into the rocks where these big girls are ambushing sand eels and squid. I'm running a simple fishfinder rig with a 3/0 circle hook and just enough weight to tick bottom — usually 2 to 3 ounces depending on the current. The new moon we just passed stirred things up good, and now with the tides building back toward springs, the current is moving bait through the rips like a conveyor belt.

Daylight hours are producing too, but you need to work for it. Diamond jigs in the 3 to 4-ounce range are the ticket when the current is ripping. White and chartreuse are getting hit, but don't overlook the classic silver — sometimes simple is better when the water's clean like it's been. I'm seeing guys boat a couple dozen bass and blues mixed together, with the blues running thick in that 8 to 12-pound range. Those chunky cocktail blues are a great sign — means the bait chain is healthy and the big bass aren't far behind.

The south side has been the consistent producer, especially that drop-off in 30 to 40 feet where the bottom transitions from sand to rock. The water clarity has been exceptional, which means you can see the fish working, but it also means they can see you. Light leaders are critical — I'm dropping down to 30-pound fluorocarbon when the water's this clear, even though it means losing a few rigs to the rocks.

Fluke fishing has picked up considerably as the water warmed. The Viking Fleet and other party boats are seeing steady action with keepers mixed in among the usual shorts. White Gulp on bucktails is still the go-to, but I'm hearing good things about chartreuse when the current is really moving. The key spots are the edges where sand meets structure — those transition zones where fluke set up to ambush bait getting swept along by the tide.

Porgies are showing in decent numbers, though they're schooled up tight rather than spread out everywhere. When you find them, you find them good. Small hooks and small baits — clam bellies and sandworms on size 6 hooks are getting it done. The deeper water off the Point has been holding the better-grade fish.

What's really got me excited is the bait situation. Sand eels are thick, squid made a strong push through the area, and I'm seeing butterfish, shad, and mackerel all mixed in. That diversity tells me the ecosystem is healthy and the fishing should stay strong through the summer. The bunker are still scarce, which is actually a good thing — when there's no easy meal, the bass have to work the structure and that's where we can get at them.

Looking ahead, we're building toward full moon Friday, which means big tides and serious current. That's when the Point really comes alive. The spring tides will flush even more bait out of the back bays and estuaries, and with water temps climbing toward 70, we should see the summer pattern lock in solid. I'll be watching for the first real push of weakfish — they love these big moon tides and warm water. If you're planning a trip, focus on the tide changes, especially that last hour of outgoing when everything gets pushed through the rips. That's when the magic happens at Montauk Point.
