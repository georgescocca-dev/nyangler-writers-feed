# Jones Inlet sits in a data fog as early-June bait stacks build inside the bay
*With buoy and SST feeds running thin, the read is on bait position and tide stage — bunker pods inside the inlet are the controlling variable.*

**FREEPORT, NY — June 5** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The analyst desk is running with a partial deck this morning. No live buoy temperatures came through the Dr. Fish pipeline overnight, the most recent published SST package is two days stale out of June 3, and the tide station feeds that normally anchor a Jones Inlet read — Sandy Hook to the west, Montauk to the east — are not refreshed in today's brief. That means the report has to lean on what the water was doing 48 to 72 hours ago and on what early June around Jones Inlet typically delivers when the bay has had a stretch of settled weather behind it. The June 3 SST imagery, before the gap, showed the South Shore inshore band running warmer than the open Atlantic by a meaningful margin — the classic estuary-thermal setup where Hempstead Bay acts as a shallow solar panel and the inlet throat becomes the exchange valve twice a day. Bunker, both adult and peanut class, have been visible from the Wantagh Bridge south to the inlet mouth for the past two weeks, and that bait position is the single most useful piece of information on the board today.

What that means for the beat is straightforward. When the inside water is several degrees warmer than the ocean and bunker are stacked in the bay, the bass behavior splits cleanly by tide. On the outgoing, the predators set up on the ocean side of the inlet — the west bar off Jones Beach, the rip line off Point Lookout, and the deeper slot just outside the green can — and ambush bait getting swept out. On the incoming, the bite migrates inside, with fish pushing up the channel edges past the Coast Guard station and into the deeper holes behind Short Beach. Early June in this water column is a slot-and-overslot story, not a schoolie story; the fish working bunker right now are the post-spawn migrants from the Hudson and Raritan, and they are hungry, lean, and aggressive on big profiles.

The on-the-ground intelligence supports that picture. Bunker schools have been holding under birds inside the inlet on the morning incoming, and the reports of bass on big paddle-tail soft plastics in white — the bigger the better — match what the water column is asking for. Diamond jigging has been notably soft compared to plastics and big swimmers, which tells you the fish are keyed on full-bodied bait, not sand eels. Snag-and-drop with live bunker, when you can find a tight pod, is the high-percentage play at first light around the inlet markers. The fluke fleet has started to settle into the Meadowbrook Bridge southeast corner and the channel edges between the second and third Wantagh, with most of the action still on the short side of the slot — squid and smelt combos on white bucktails are doing the work, and a teaser dressed small is outproducing a bare hook.

Weakfish chatter is quiet but the water is right for them. The deeper holes in the Sloop Channel and the cuts north of the Loop Parkway bridge are worth a slow drift with pink or chartreuse plastics on a light jig as the sun drops. Bluefish are scattered — the snapper class is still building in the back, but legitimate cocktail blues to three or four pounds have been mixed in with the bass on the bunker schools, and any wire leader work near a visible pod will tell you quickly which predator is in charge that morning.

The next three to five days hinge on wind. If the forecast holds light southwest through the weekend, the inlet water will continue to warm and stratify, the bunker will stay put, and the morning incoming will be the window. A hard northeast or a frontal passage flips that — it pushes bait out of the bay, dirties the inlet, and shifts the bite to the ocean side of Jones Beach for a day or two before the system resets. With the SST pipeline behind schedule, I would not commit to a long offshore run on speculation; the inshore engine is firing and the bait is in front of you. Watch the bird work at the inlet mouth on the last two hours of the flood, and watch what the bunker pods do when the tide turns — that is the tell.

The inlet is fishing the way early June is supposed to fish when the bay warms before the ocean does. Big plastics, live bunker when you can get them, and a willingness to move with the bait rather than camp on a number. The data feed will catch up. The fish will not wait for it.
