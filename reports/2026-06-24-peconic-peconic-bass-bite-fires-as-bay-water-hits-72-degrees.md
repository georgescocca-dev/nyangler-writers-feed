# Peconic bass bite fires as bay water hits 72 degrees
*Slot stripers stack up on bucktails while porgies pack the deeper holes around Shelter Island.*

**SHELTER ISLAND, NY — June 24** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic finally hit that magic 72-degree mark this week, and the bass responded like someone flipped a switch. Water temps jumped nearly 4 degrees in five days, creating the kind of thermal gradient that pulls stripers out of the Sound and into our protected waters. I've been watching this pattern for twenty years — when bay water runs 4-5 degrees warmer than the ocean side, the bass follow that heat like a highway.

The slot fish are stacked up on the flood tide around the deeper channels between Shelter Island and Greenport. White bucktails with 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets are absolutely crushing them in 25-30 feet of water. The key is working the transition zones where the channel edges meet the flats — that's where the bait gets pinned on the incoming water. I'm seeing consistent action from first light through the first two hours of flood, then it shuts down like a faucet.

Best action has been on 3/4-ounce heads, bounced slow along the bottom with just enough lift to keep contact. The bass are sitting tight to structure, not chasing anything flashy. Natural presentations are winning — that white Gulp mimics the spearing and small bunker that are thick in the water column right now.

The porgy bite around Shelter Island continues to be world-class, though you need to work for the jumbos. The 16-18 inch fish are holding in 35-40 feet around the deeper humps, especially on the south side where the current sweeps clean water across the structure. Clam bellies on high-low rigs are producing steady action, but the real trick is getting your chum line established before the tide changes. I'm seeing boats struggle when they don't give the scup time to find the slick.

Weakfish are making a stronger showing than we've seen in years. The 18-20 inch fish are scattered throughout the bay, but the most consistent action is coming from the edges of the deeper holes in 20-25 feet. Small bucktails tipped with squid strips, worked on a slow retrieve just off the bottom, are accounting for most of the keepers. The bite seems strongest on the last hour of outgoing tide when the bait gets concentrated in the channels.

Water clarity has been exceptional despite the recent southwest winds. The thermal stratification is setting up nicely — surface temps running 72-74 degrees while the bottom stays around 68-70. That temperature break is holding bait at mid-depth, which explains why the bass are feeding so aggressively on bucktails worked in that zone.

The new moon spring tides this weekend should really fire things up. Those big tidal swings will flush fresh bait out of the back bays and concentrate everything in the main channels. I'm expecting the bass bite to peak during the last two hours of outgoing tide, especially around the deeper structure between Shelter Island and Sag Harbor.

Looking ahead, this heat wave should push water temps into the mid-70s by week's end. That's when the summer pattern really locks in — early morning bass on bucktails, midday porgies on the deeper structure, and evening weakfish on the channel edges. The Peconic is fishing like mid-July in late June, and I'm not complaining.
