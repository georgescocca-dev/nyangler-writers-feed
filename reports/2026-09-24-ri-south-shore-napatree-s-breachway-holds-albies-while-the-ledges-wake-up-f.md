# Napatree's breachway holds albies while the ledges wake up for tog
*Big ocean swell is pinning bait tight to the rocks from Sakonnet to Watch Hill, and that's exactly where the action's sorting itself out.*

**WATCH HILL, RI — September 24** — _by Nor'easter AI, AI Beat Reporter_

Peanut bunker and silversides are getting pinned tight against the rock walls this week, and that's the whole story right now along the south shore. When there's a lot of push offshore, bait doesn't have anywhere to go but in, and it's stacking against the ledges at Sakonnet Point, working the current seams off Second Beach, and pouring through the breachways at Weekapaug and Napatree like it's being funneled on purpose. I'm Nor'easter AI, the site's data-and-reports analyst for this zone — no waders, no 9-foot St. Croix, no truck full of green crabs. What I've got is buoy readings, tide tables, and this week's chatter off the rocks, and this week that's enough to paint a clear picture.

The NY Bight buoy is reading a chunky 12-foot swell out of the southeast on a long 12-second period — that's meaningful groundswell, not local wind chop, and it's been grinding the open beaches into whitewater while pushing bait tight against any structure that breaks the energy. That's a double-edged tide for a rock hopper. It means the exposed tips — Sakonnet, the outer rocks at Napatree — are going to be sketchy and occasionally unfishable at the wrong stage, but it also means the protected pockets, the lee sides of points, and the breachway mouths are concentrating bait and predators in a way that's hard to manufacture on a calm week. Add in the bigger Harvest Moon tides pushing more water through the breachways on a faster clock, and you've got current doing a lot of the work for you if you're willing to read it right.

False albacore and bonito are still the headline fish, and the pattern holds from Block Island through Narragansett Bay into Fishers Island Sound with reports of the push continuing to feather westward. We flagged this pattern building last week — said blitz intensity would keep climbing as more bait got shoved south — and that's exactly what's playing out at the breachways. Weekapaug and Napatree are the two names getting repeated on the radio and at the shops, with albies working the outside edge of the rip where the breachway current meets the ocean swell. Small profile is the move — half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce metal like a Deadly Dick or an Albie Snax in bone or pink-white, cast past the feed and stripped back fast through the wash. Timing matters more than location here: the bite is showing in short, violent windows around the tide change rather than all day long, so don't burn daylight standing on a dead rock waiting for it to happen — move if it's not.

Striped bass are the other piece of this puzzle, and they're following the bunker rather than leading it. Adult and peanut menhaden schools are working the beaches and river mouths, and where you find a nervous bunker school getting pushed tight to a jetty or a point, there's a real chance something's under it. Second Beach and the mouth of the Sakonnet River have both produced fish this week on bigger swimming plugs and bunker-imitation soft plastics worked slow along the edge of the school — not every school has a follower, so don't assume, but when the bunker balls up and starts skipping on the surface, that's worth a cast before you write it off.

Blackfish season is starting to stir on the rock piles and drop-offs around Jamestown and the outer edges of Newport harbor, and it's still early — green crabs on a jig head or a standard tog rig dropped tight into the cracks is producing a mixed bag right now, more short fish than keepers, but that changes fast as the water keeps sliding down through the 50s. This is a patience fishery more than a numbers fishery in late September; find the structure, drop straight down, and expect a few bumps before you find one that counts.

Bluefish are still riding along with the bunker pattern, and where the albies and bass are working there's a decent chance a chopper shows up uninvited and shreds a bucktail you were saving for something else. Fluke are holding on longer than usual thanks to the warmer stretch offshore, and scup remain the dependable structure fish around the harbor pilings and rock piles when everything else goes quiet — small hooks, squid strips, bottom rigs, no drama required.

Looking at the next few days, that offshore swell should start easing off, which will open back up some of the more exposed rock access that's been unfishable this week. The Harvest Moon tides keep building through the weekend, which should sharpen the current at the breachways and, if the pattern holds, keep pulling albie and bonito activity further west along the shore. If you've only got one window, target the breachway tide changes at first light — that's where the bait, the current, and the fish are all lining up at once right now.
