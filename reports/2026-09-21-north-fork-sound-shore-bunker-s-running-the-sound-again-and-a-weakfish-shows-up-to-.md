# Bunker's Running the Sound Again, and a Weakfish Shows Up to Prove It
*Harvest Moon tides push bait through Plum Gut as stripers start shadowing menhaden from Mattituck to Orient, and a weakfish turns up where nobody expected one.*

**ORIENT, NY — September 21** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

A fella came into the shop Tuesday with a fish in a cooler he couldn't name. Silvery, purple sheen along the back, spots running down the side like somebody flicked a paintbrush at it. Weakfish. Took him a minute to believe it when I told him, on account of it's been the better part of a decade since anybody around here pulled one worth talking about. He'd caught it on a bucktail meant for bass, working the drop-off east of Rocky Point on the last of the outgoing. That's the kind of week it's been — not a blowup bite, but one where the Sound is showing you things it hasn't in a while.

The short version of why: bunker. Peanut bunker and grown menhaden both are moving through on these bigger Harvest Moon tides, and everything that eats bunker is falling in line behind them. Water's still carrying decent warmth for this late in September — reads out toward Kings Point and up around New London both sit in the high 60s to low 70s, and that gradient runs the same way it always does out here, warmer toward the west end of the Sound, cooler as you push toward the Race and Plum Gut. That's kept the bait comfortable and spread it out along the whole stretch from Mattituck to Orient instead of bunching it in one spot, which is part of why the reports have been good but scattered rather than one obvious hotspot.

We flagged two weeks back that the bass would start shadowing bait tight to structure and rips as the water cooled and more forage got pushed into the system, and that's exactly what's setting up now. Fish aren't stacked yet the way they'll be in another few weeks, but they're committing — working the bunker schools instead of just cruising past them. Bluefish are riding along same as always, and they've picked up some size and temper the last several days as the bigger bunker schools funnel past the points. That's no surprise either — we said this pattern would build as more bait got flushed south and east, and it has.

Horton Point has been the most honest read on the week. Bass to the high 20s and a few pushing 30 inches have come on bunker chunks fished on the bottom during the last two hours of the ebb, with the current running hard enough to hold fish tight against the rock. Live-lining a snagged peanut bunker off the point at first light has also produced, though you need to be quick about it — the blues will find that bait before the bass do more often than not. Plugging has been spottier. A few school bass came on white bucktails worked slow along the drop, but topwater in the middle of the day has been mostly quiet, which tells you the fish are feeding on the tide change and sulking the rest of the time.

Mattituck Inlet has porgy holding steady, same as it does every fall, and that's not a bad thing to lean on when the bigger fish are being fussy. Sandworms and clam on the bottom in fifteen to twenty-five feet just off the jetty rocks are producing a full bucket most mornings, size running average to a little better than average. Fluke are still around in the deeper water off the inlet mouth, which is later than usual — the warm water's kept them from clearing out the way they normally would by this point in September. Gulp on a light bucktail, drifted slow through the deeper cuts, is still worth the time, though don't expect the numbers you'd have found in July.

Out toward Orient and the Plum Gut approach, the story is bunker and bass working the rip on the tide changes, with blues mixed in thick enough that you'll go through a few bucktails if you're not careful about wire or heavier leader. The current there does what it always does — pinches the bait and holds it, and anything hunting bunker knows to sit on that seam and wait. A few boats working live bunker on circle hooks through the Gut have had the better bass of the week, upper 20s to low 30s, fished right on the edge where the fast water meets the slower stuff.

As for blackfish, it's early yet. We said a couple weeks back that numbers would build steadily through late September, and there's been a little more chatter about fish showing on the rockier structure off Rocky Point and out past Orient, but it's not a real bite yet — more a sign of what's coming than something worth planning a trip around today.

Looking ahead, the next few days should keep this pattern going as the moon tides stay big and the bait keeps moving. If the water holds where it is, the best window is still the last two hours of the ebb around the points, especially Horton and Rocky Point, where the current concentrates the bunker against structure. Keep an eye on the weather late in the week — any real cooling push would tighten the fluke bite fast and start nudging the bigger bass migration along quicker than the calendar usually calls for. Until then, work the bunker schools where you find them, fish the tide change hard, and don't be shocked if something unusual — like that weakfish — turns up in the mix.
