# Twotree fluke steady, Black Point sea bass thick — the dock's watching for the first albie
*Niantic and Waterford boats are grinding out solid mixed bags while the whole fleet keeps one eye on the Race for the season's first false albacore.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 8** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Stood on the dock at the Niantic launch Tuesday morning watching the *Blackhawk* unload, and I swear every other sentence out of the mate's mouth was "albies." Not because anybody's caught one yet — nobody has, not here — but because the chatter's been building since last week when I told you this zone, sitting right off the Race with all that current running past Twotree and Black Point, was worth watching as the new moon loaded up. We're three days out from that August 12 new moon now, tidal range building every day, and the fleet's primed. Nothing's shown up on the tuna sticks yet. But give it a few more days of stronger current pulling that Rhode Island warm water through Watch Hill and into the eastern Sound, and I wouldn't be shocked if the first one gets stuck somewhere between Hole-in-the-Wall and the Twotree can.

In the meantime, the bread and butter is still paying the bills. Fluke fishing out of Niantic has been steady, not spectacular — the kind of week where you keep moving and you keep a few fish, but you're not filling a cooler by 9 a.m. Twotree Island Channel has been the most consistent stop, 28 to 35 feet, working the last two hours of the outgoing into the start of the flood when the current slows enough to hold bottom without a half-pound of lead. Guys are doing well with a 3/4-ounce white bucktail tipped with a strip of squid, or a 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullet in pearl white on a light jig head, dragged nice and slow along the channel edge where the sand meets the mussel bottom. Hole-in-the-Wall has produced a scattered better grade — a few fish pushing 4 and 5 pounds mixed in with the shorts — but you're picking through a lot of fish under 16 inches to find them. Water's sitting around 69 to 70 degrees off New London, a touch cooler than the western Sound, and that's kept the fluke comfortable and spread out rather than balled up tight, which is part of why it's a pick, not a slaughter.

Black sea bass have been the more reliable rail-filler. Black Point's rock piles and the reef structure off McCook Point are holding good numbers in 25 to 40 feet, and on the right drift — light current, not screaming — the party boats have been putting decent bags together with squid strips and Berkley Gulp on standard sea bass rigs. Legal fish are running 14 to 17 inches with enough shorts mixed in to keep it interesting for the kids on board. Best window has been the top of the tide into the first hour of the drop, before the current gets too ripping to hold bottom cleanly.

Porgies are doing exactly what porgies do in August — keeping the rail busy and the little ones smiling. Niantic Bay's breakwaters and the shallower structure around the river mouth have been thick with scup in the 10 to 12 inch range, occasional jumbo mixed in. Small hooks, half a sandworm or a chunk of clam, bottom rig, nothing fancy. It's not glamorous fishing but it's the most dependable thing going on my beat right now, and it's a good call if you've got a slow afternoon and want guaranteed bend in the rod.

Striped bass have been the frustrating one. There are fish around the Race and working the rips off Black Point on the bigger tides, but the bite's been on-again, off-again — you need to be there right as the tide turns, and if you miss that window by twenty minutes you're fishing empty water. A few boats have connected on bunker schools pushing through Twotree Channel, live-lining or snap-jigging bunker spoons, with fish in the 20 to 30 pound class coming over the rail on the better days. But it's not an every-trip thing right now. I'd call it a tide-dependent bite, not a bite you can plan a whole day around.

Bluefish are the one thing actually trending up, and that tracks with what's been building west of us — the resurgence that started on the deep reefs in the western Sound has been sliding east all week. We've had a couple of legitimate blitzes reported off Hole-in-the-Wall in the last few days, gulls working bait right on the surface, and boats running metal — Kastmasters and half-ounce Ava jigs — into the middle of it have had no trouble getting bent. If you're bunker chunking for stripers and the blues show up and steal your bait, don't fight it, just switch over. They've been running 3 to 6 pounds with a few bigger ones mixed in.

Blackfish season's still a ways off here, but I'll tell you the dock talk has already turned to scouting. A few guys have been quietly checking the rock piles off Black Point and around McCook, not fishing them hard, just marking bottom and confirming structure holds fish before the fall run gets going.

Looking ahead: that new moon on the 12th is going to stretch the tidal range out and sharpen up every current-driven spot on this beat — the Race, Twotree, the rips off Black Point. If the warm water corridor keeps pushing west like it has been, I'd start swinging a small metal jig or an epoxy pattern off the stern when you're running between drops, just in case something with a torpedo body and no patience shows up early. That's the move I'd make this week — fish the fluke and sea bass like normal, but keep a rod rigged light and ready, because if the albies do show, they won't announce it.
