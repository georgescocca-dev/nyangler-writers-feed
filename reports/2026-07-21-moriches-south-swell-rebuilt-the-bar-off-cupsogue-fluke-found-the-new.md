# South Swell Rebuilt the Bar Off Cupsogue — Fluke Found the New Edge First
*A stretch of 4-foot groundswell reshaped the inlet throat this week, and the moving water over the new structure is producing better than the old lines did.*

**MORICHES INLET, NY — July 21** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

I ran the west end bar off Cupsogue on Tuesday's ebb and the depth finder told me something the charts hadn't caught up to yet. That stretch of south groundswell we've been eating all week — 3.9 feet at five seconds, dead out of 177 degrees according to the offshore buoy — has been stacking sand against the outer bar at the throat of the inlet. The old drop that ran roughly parallel to the training rocks has filled in a few feet, and a new scour channel has cut in tighter to the Cupsogue side. That's not a subtle shift. If you fished the old edge this week and marked nothing, that's why. The fish didn't leave, the bottom moved.

I found the new edge Wednesday on the last two hours of the outgoing, working a bucktail-and-Gulp combo — half-ounce white bucktail with a 4-inch pearl Gulp Swimming Mullet trailer — right where the new scour drops from six feet down to eleven off the flood shoal. Fluke were sitting right on that break, and it wasn't subtle fishing either. Six keepers in ninety minutes, biggest going 21 inches, with a handful of shorts mixed in that needed to go back. That's a real bite, not a rumor. We flagged this corridor two weeks back as the stretch to watch through the rest of July, and the water's backing that up — Moriches into Shinnecock is fishing as well as anywhere on the south side of the Island right now for fluke, and the reason is simple: moving water over structure, which is exactly the kind of thing that doesn't care how hot the surface temperature gets.

The timing helps too. We're sliding into the first-quarter neap, and that's softening the current through the cut noticeably day over day. Less velocity means the drift window on the fluke grounds is opening up wider than it was on the full-moon tides two weeks ago — instead of a tight ninety-minute window around the max ebb, I was still marking fish and getting bit an hour past what I'd normally call quitting time. If you're planning a trip this weekend, don't rush off the water the second the tide slacks. Stay another half hour and work the seam where the current is still trickling off the flats into the main channel.

Striped bass are a different animal right now, and anybody telling you otherwise is selling something. With the bay running warm through the shallows — that's typical for this point in July — the bass have gone almost entirely nocturnal. Daytime casting around Tuthill Point and the sod banks off the north side of the bay has been mostly dead unless you're right at first light. The better program has been live bunker fished on a fish-finder rig after dark, worked tight to the rocks on the ocean side of the inlet jetty where the current still runs cool over structure even when the flats have gone soupy. I had a report of two bass in the mid-30-inch range taken that way Thursday night on the incoming, right against the rocks where the tide pushes bait through the gut. If you want daytime action on bass, you're better off targeting the very edge of dawn — first hour of light, working a black Slug-Go or a bunker-imitation swimbait along the current seam where the inlet meets the ocean.

Bluefish have been the wildcard. Bunker schools are moving along the beach the way they always do this time of year, and where those pods show up off Cupsogue or Smith Point in the early morning, the blues have been slashing through them hard enough to see from the beach — birds diving, water churning white. It hasn't been a daily thing out here the way it's been reported further west along the Jersey coast, but when it happens it's fast and it's obvious. If you're surfcasting at dawn, keep a metal — a Kastmaster or an Ava jig — rigged and ready, because those windows close in ten minutes once the bait pushes through.

Weakfish have been quieter but not absent. The calm, warm evenings we've had this week are exactly the conditions that tend to rebuild that bite in the bay's deeper holes — I'm talking the channel bends off East Moriches and the deeper cuts inside Pattersquash. A bucktail tipped with a strip of squid, worked slow along the bottom on the last of the outgoing, has picked up a few keeper-class weakfish in the 16 to 18-inch range. It's not a blitz, it's a pick, but it's real and it's trending the right direction if the weather holds calm.

Looking ahead, the ocean's going to stay a factor. That south swell isn't laying down completely, which means Cupsogue's surf fishing will stay tough and the inlet bar will probably keep shifting through the weekend — I'll be back out mid-week to see if the new scour channel holds or fills back in. The neap tide should keep widening those fluke windows through the weekend, so that's where I'd put my time if I only had one trip. Bass fishing stays a dark-hours game until we get a real cool-down, and if the bunker keep pushing along the beach, don't be surprised if Smith Point sees a legitimate blitz morning before this week's out.
