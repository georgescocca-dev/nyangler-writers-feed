# Appledore Ledges on the Slack: Cod Steady, Stripers Stacking the Piscataqua Bridges
*Thirteen miles of coastline is plenty when you fish it like it's a hundred and thirty — the Shoals gave up cod on the drop, and the river's holding bass all week.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — July 20** — _by Cole Robinson, Zone Writer_

People hear thirteen miles of coastline and figure there's nothing to it. I've had guys at the launch ask why I bother trailering the Lund out past Rye Harbor every morning instead of running down to the Cape where everybody else fishes. Thing is, those thirteen miles include the Isles of Shoals, and the Isles of Shoals fish like they're a hundred and thirty miles offshore. Structure, current, cold water holding right through summer — I don't need more coastline, I need to know where the rockpiles are. I was on the water before 5 AM most days this week, and I was back at the dock with fish in the box before most folks had poured their second cup.

The cod bite at Appledore held up better than I expected for late July. I worked the ledges off the southwest corner in 65 to 80 feet, and the trick has been timing it to the slack — that window either side of the tide change when the rip between Appledore and Smuttynose lays down enough to actually hold bottom with a jig. Fight the current out there and you're not fishing, you're just paying out line. Diamond jigs, the heavier ones, tipped with a strip of clam, dropped straight down and worked in short lifts off the rock. Took cod to 8 pounds Tuesday morning, a mixed bag of keepers and shorts most other days. Haddock have been mixed in but thinner than I'd like — a few nice ones came up off White Island in 70 feet on the same drops, but it's not the kind of numbers game it can be come fall. If you're going out there, plan your trip around the tide table, not the clock. Miss the slack and you'll be jigging your arm off for nothing.

The river's been the more consistent producer, honestly. Stripers have parked themselves around the Memorial Bridge and down toward the Sarah Long Bridge pilings, working the current seams on the last of the ebb and first push of the flood. Cooler Gulf of Maine water is doing us a favor up here — while the water south of Boston has pushed bass into a strictly low-light, after-dark pattern, our stripers are still willing to eat in daylight because the Piscataqua and the water off the Shoals just haven't warmed up the way the Sound and the Bight have. I've been running live mackerel under a slip float through the bridge shadows on the tide change, and jigging soft plastics — 5-inch white Slug-Go's, weightless, worked with a slow twitch — right along the pier faces where the current breaks. Fish from 24 to 34 inches, with a few short ones mixed in that needed releasing quick.

Hampton Harbor's doing what it does on a good push tide — bass working into the inlet on the flood, holding along the Seabrook side channel edges and around the mouth of Meadow Pond as bait gets flushed through. Eels fished on a fish-finder rig after dark have been the most consistent producer down there, but during the day I've had success with a bucktail, half-ounce, white and chartreuse, bounced along the channel drop-off where it meets the flats. Tide's everything in that harbor — fish the last two hours of the flood into the top, and you're in business. Fish it on a dead low and you're walking the flats looking for herons.

Mackerel are still thick right off Rye Harbor and around Odiorne Point, which is keeping everything fed and keeping the bluefish interested. I've seen the first real blitzes of the summer this week — blues busting through mackerel schools right off the Rye breakwater and out toward White Island, visible from shore in the mornings before the wind picks up. Diamond jigs or small metal, cast right into the boil, and you'll connect. Nothing huge yet, mostly 2 to 4 pounders, but they hit hard and they're fun on light tackle, and where there's blues on mackerel there's usually stripers not far behind working the same bait.

We flagged a few days back that the summer striper pattern up here would hold into August, and that's exactly what's playing out — the Piscataqua's been the steadiest thing on my whole beat this week, more reliable than the ocean side has been for cod.

Looking ahead, we're heading into the first-quarter neap on the 21st, which should soften the rip between the Shoals enough to widen that cod window beyond just the dead slack — worth getting out there and testing the edges of the tide change rather than just the exact turn. Flounder should start showing better in the harbor mud as the neap tide settles things down, so I'll be soaking clam bait off Rye Harbor's inner basin if the cod bite off the Shoals gets picky. If the mackerel schools hold tight to shore like they have, I'd expect the bluefish blitzes to keep showing up in the mornings, and I wouldn't be surprised to see the first false albacore scouts nosing around the deeper water off the Shoals before the month's out. Worth a look if you've got a rod rigged light and ready.
