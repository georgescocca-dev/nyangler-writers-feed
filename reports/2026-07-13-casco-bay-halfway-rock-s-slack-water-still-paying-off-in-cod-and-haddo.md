# Halfway Rock's Slack Water Still Paying Off in Cod and Haddock
*Mid-July has the bass scattered but committed to structure, while the groundfish bite off the Rock stays steady on the turn.*

**PORTLAND, ME — July 13** — _by Pashmi Cooper, Zone Writer_

I was out past Jewell Island Tuesday morning, engine idling, watching the current build against the eastern ledge, when a school of pollock came up under a raft of gulls working bait that wasn't quite mackerel and wasn't quite pogy — young herring, most likely, pushed tight to the rocks by something bigger underneath. That's been the story of this bay for two weeks now: bait scattered, structure holding the answers, and the fish making you work a chart before you work a rod.

The mackerel run that the bay through late May and into June has thinned out considerably. I'm not seeing the tight, glass-ball schools we had off Peaks Island a month back. What's left has pushed deeper and farther out — I marked scattered pods on the sounder in 40 to 60 feet off the backside of Long Island, but you're jigging for them now rather than watching them boil the surface. If you want mackerel for bait or the pan, fish small, weighted jigs — the size 6 or 8 diamond jigs with no bait needed — worked slow through that depth range on the last hour of the incoming. Don't expect the manic action of June. It's a patient man's game now.

Striped bass have been the more honest story, and I'll be straight with you: it's not been easy fishing, but it's been productive fishing for those willing to move around. The ledges off Peaks and Diamond Island have held fish on the last two hours of the incoming tide, when current stacks bait against the rock faces and the bass set up in the eddies behind them. I've done well with 3/4-ounce bucktails in white or chartreuse, cast up-current and swum back with the tide rather than against it — that's the mistake I see visiting boats make, fighting the current instead of using it. Slot fish in the 26 to 32-inch range have been the norm, with the occasional better fish pulled off the north side of Bailey Island on live pogies fished on a fish-finder rig in 15 to 20 feet of water, dead-slack into the first push of the ebb.

Cape Small has been quieter than I'd like for mid-July. The bass are there — I know men who've taken them off the ledges near the Sisters — but they're not concentrated, and the bite windows have been short, tied tightly to the top of the tide and the first thirty minutes of the drop. If you're fishing that stretch, don't linger past the turn. Move rather than wait them out.

Halfway Rock remains the most dependable piece of water in the bay right now, and it's where I've spent most of my own time this week. The groundfish bite there has been steady rather than spectacular — cod running 3 to 8 pounds, with a scattering of haddock mixed in on the deeper drops to the southwest of the Rock, 90 to 110 feet of water. The pattern hasn't changed in thirty years and it hasn't changed this week: fish the slack, both top and bottom of the tide, when the current eases enough to hold bottom without a pound of lead dragging you sideways. Clam bait on a simple two-hook bottom rig still outfishes the jigging crowd out there, though I've seen guys do well jigging diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid on the up-stroke, worked slow off bottom in 15-foot lifts.

Bluefish have started showing in the outer bay, which is about on schedule for mid-July as the surface water warms. I had a report of choppers in the 3 to 5-pound class working bait off Two Lights over the weekend, and I wouldn't be surprised to see that push into the bay proper over the next ten days as water temps climb another degree or two in the shallows. Poppers and metal — anything that throws a wake — will draw strikes once they set up on a bait concentration. They're not subtle fish and they won't require subtle presentation.

Wind's been the usual July pattern: calm mornings, building southwest in the afternoon, which means if you're planning ledge work around Peaks or Diamond, get out early. That afternoon breeze pushes a chop into the eastern approaches that makes precise bucktail work difficult by early afternoon.

Looking ahead, I expect the bass pattern to hold roughly as is — scattered, structure-oriented, tide-dependent — through the coming week. If the bait pods I saw off Jewell consolidate rather than scatter further, I'd expect the bass to follow them tighter to the islands, which would make for better fishing than we've had. The groundfish at Halfway Rock should stay consistent regardless; that's deep, cold structure that doesn't care much what the surface is doing. If I had one morning to fish this week, I'd take the last of the incoming at Diamond Island for bass, then run out to the Rock and sit the slack for cod. That's given me the best mixed bag of the summer so far, and I don't see a reason it stops working just yet.
