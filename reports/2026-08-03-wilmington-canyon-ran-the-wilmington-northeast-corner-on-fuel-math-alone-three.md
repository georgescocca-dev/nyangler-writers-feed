# Ran the Wilmington Northeast Corner on Fuel-Math Alone — Three Yellowfin and a White Marlin Bite to Show for the Diesel
*A stable high and a softening tide made the call easy this week: troll early, chunk if the bite stalls, and don't burn extra miles chasing a break that hasn't moved much.*

**INDIAN RIVER, DE — August 3** — _by Capt. Dana Cooper, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday morning before we ever left the dock: troll first, chunk as backup, and don't run past the northeast corner looking for something better. That's not a glamorous decision, but it's the right one when fuel's pushing $4.60 at the marina and the trip's got to pay for itself. We'd flagged last week that the midweek blow was going to nudge the eddy edge some, and it did — but the northeast corner of Wilmington held as the high-percentage starting point, same as it's been for going on three weeks now. We just had to verify the break on the way out instead of assuming it, and that fifteen minutes of running the temp gauge saved us from wasting gas on the wrong water.

The run out of Indian River put us on the canyon around 38°49'N, 73°57'W, working the 50 to 100-fathom transition where the bottom falls off the shelf and the Gulf Stream filament has been laying a warm tongue against the cooler shelf water. That seam was sitting right around 75-76°F on the warm side, dropping to the low 70s on the inside edge — sharp enough to see bait balling up and birds working it from a mile out. We trolled a standard spread — naked and skirted ballyhoo, a couple of Ilanders in blue/white and green machine off the shotgun — starting around 7 AM and working the color change north to south along the break for about four hours before we connected. Three yellowfin, all in the 35-50 pound class, came off that same stretch of water within about ninety minutes of each other, which tells me the school was sitting on that edge rather than roaming. We also had a white marlin come up on the long bait and eat it half-heartedly — no hookup, but it's the first marlin interest I've had in two weeks, so worth mentioning if you're a troller with mixed-bag intentions.

Bluefin were mixed in but scattered — we marked fish on the sounder in that 60-80 foot range on the temp break but never got a dedicated bite going on them. That's consistent with what I'm hearing up and down the canyon string this week: bluefin are around, but they're not stacked tight enough yet to target specifically. If you want bluefin, you're more likely to pick them up incidentally while working the yellowfin spread than by hunting them directly right now.

No wahoo for us this trip, though we had the high-speed line out most of the day. Water clarity out there was decent, not gin-clear, and the wahoo bite tends to want that cobalt, ripping-clean water — we didn't quite have it Tuesday. If I run again this week I'd add a second high-speed rig with a bigger lead and fish it closer to the color change itself rather than the open blue, since that's where we've picked up wahoo in past Augusts.

Mahi were a bonus, not a target — we picked up two schoolies, maybe 6-8 pounds, off a mat of sargassum drifting right on the break. If you've got light spinning tackle rigged, keep it ready; those weed lines are holding fish and a quick pitch bait can save an otherwise slow stretch between trolling passes.

As for chunking — we didn't need to switch over Tuesday because the troll bite held, but I want to flag the timing here because it matters for anyone planning a trip this week. The tide range is narrowing into the coming neap around August 5-6, and that's going to soften the current out on the canyon edge. Softer current means your chunk slick holds shape longer and drifts slower, which is exactly the setup you want for a night bigeye bite on butterfish chunks. If the troll bite stalls out there in the next few days, that neap tide is your cue to switch tactics rather than keep burning fuel running the spread. I'd anchor up or drift-chunk right on that same northeast corner break, butterfish chunks with a few whole ones skipped out as teasers, starting a couple hours before dark and working through the first part of the night tide.

Conditions on the ride out were manageable — three-foot swell, seven-second period, nothing that beat up the boat, and that's held steady enough that I don't expect weather to be the limiting factor this week. The bigger variable is whether that eddy edge holds its position or slides again. Best case, it stays put through the weekend and the yellowfin keep sitting right on that same stretch of the break — in that scenario I'd run the same numbers again without hesitation. If it slides east like these filaments tend to do when the Stream pulses, I'd shift my starting point a few miles that direction rather than assume the fish moved with it exactly.

Bottom line for anybody planning a trip: the yellowfin bite on Wilmington's northeast corner is real but it's not a lock — three fish in six hours of trolling is a decent day, not a slaughter, and you earned every one of them working the break properly. If I had one day this week I'd go early, troll the temp change hard through the morning, and have chunk gear rigged as a fallback once that neap tide starts softening the current. That's the trip I'd book, and it's the one that makes sense on the fuel gauge.
