# New moon springs line up sunset flood for first real PM bass window since June
*Bunker still stacked off Matinecock, fluke have slid to the channel edges, and Friday through Monday brings the tide-and-light convergence I've been waiting on all month.*

**THROGS NECK, NY — July 10** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Last week the Sound did what it does in early July — it went quiet in the middle of the day and came alive at the edges. Water in the back bays climbed into the upper 70s, Little Neck and Manhasset both running warm and a little green with the algae bloom that shows up this time of year, and that pushed the bigger bass and the better fluke out toward the channels and rockpiles where there's still some cooler water moving. Wind was the typical July pattern — glassy at dawn, building out of the southwest by early afternoon, laying back down after sunset. Nothing that shut the bite down, but enough afternoon chop that if you were fishing 11 to 3 off Execution Rocks or the Stepping Stones you were probably wondering where everybody went.

Here's why this week matters: we're building into new moon, and that means spring tides — bigger swings, faster current, more water moving through the pinch points. The piece I'm watching closest is the PM flood lining up with sunset at Kings Point. That high-stand-meets-dusk convergence hasn't set up since the June full moon, and it's back Friday through Monday, peaking July 11 through 13. When the flood tide crests right as the light drops, bass that have been sulking on bottom all day in 78-degree water slide up onto the structure to feed in that last hour of shooting light and the first hour of dark. That's the window. Miss it and you're fishing memory for the rest of the day.

Stripers: this is still very much a low-light, moving-water game right now. Execution Rocks on the outgoing has been the most consistent read all week — fish stacking on the northeast side of the rockpile in 18 to 25 feet as the tide starts to drop, then sliding out toward the deeper edge as it really gets moving. Bucktails, 1 to 1.5 ounce, tipped with a strip of fresh bunker belly, worked slow along bottom, have out-produced everything else. At Stepping Stones, same idea but I want the last two hours of the incoming into that high-stand window — live bunker on a fish-finder rig, weight just heavy enough to hold in the current, fished tight to the light structure. Sizes have been mixed, mostly schoolies and mid-20-inch fish, with the occasional 30-incher mixed in for guys willing to sit through the slow stretches. Nobody's filling a cooler. But three or four fish in that magic hour and a half around sunset is a realistic night right now, and with the new moon springs building through the weekend, I like the odds better than I have in three weeks.

Bluefish have been the more reliable action, honestly. The bunker schools that stacked up off Matinecock Point in June never left — if anything they've thickened, and the blues have been slashing through them most mornings before the wind builds. Run-and-gun with 2-ounce Kastmasters or a Hopkins spoon when you see the bait nervous on the surface, or slow-troll a bunker spoon along the edge of the school if it's sitting deeper. Nothing subtle needed here — these fish are aggressive and they'll hit anything that moves fast through the bait.

Fluke are the story I want to be straight with you about. The bay temps pushed the better fish out of the shallow flats and into the channel edges and deeper water — think the mouth of Manhasset Bay, the drop-off along the Hempstead Harbor channel, and the deeper stretch running off Execution Rocks toward the shipping lane, 25 to 40 feet. Drifting bucktails tipped with white Gulp Swimming Mullet, or a straight Gulp/spearing combo on a bare hook rig, has been the play on the outgoing when the current's moving enough to keep the boat drifting clean. It's not a banner year for numbers in this zone — you're working for keepers, not filling a limit — but the fish that are coming over the rail have been quality, several in the 4-to-6-pound range out toward the Sound side of the harbor mouths.

Porgies are exactly where you'd expect them — Hart Island rubble and the rock piles tight to the Throgs Neck Bridge pilings, sandworms and clam on light tackle, high-low rigs with small hooks. Steady action, good for keeping the rod bent between the bigger-fish windows. Blackfish season's still a ways off for real numbers, but I've heard of a few incidental tog coming off the deeper Execution Rocks structure on crab baits meant for something else.

Weakfish — I'll be honest, despite water temps that should be right for them, I haven't heard a credible report out of this zone in weeks. The threshold's been crossed on paper but the fish aren't showing up to confirm it. I wouldn't plan a trip around them right now.

Looking ahead: if the wind stays out of the southwest and lays down like it has most evenings, I'd be at Stepping Stones or Execution Rocks Friday through Sunday for that flood-into-sunset window — that's where I'm putting my own time. Bring bucktails and a few bunker chunks, work the last hour of daylight into full dark, and don't be surprised if the best fish of the night comes on your last cast before you can't see the rod tip anymore. Plan B if the bass don't cooperate is the fluke drift along the Manhasset channel edge — that bite's been steady enough to save an otherwise quiet trip.
