# Rough start, strong finish: the warm filament keeps Spencer and Lindenkohl paying off
*A shaky opening week turned into a solid string of tuna and white marlin days once the edge settled back into place.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 11** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Grade for the week across the four canyons: a C-minus open and a B-plus finish. Monday and Tuesday were a scratch — leftover swell out of the weekend blow had the edges sloppy and the color pushed around, and boats running to Spencer and Poor Man's both came back with more diesel burned than fish in the box. By Wednesday the wind laid down, the swell dropped out of the low single digits, and the water sorted itself back out. That's when the week turned.

We flagged this last week — said the pelagic bite would hold as long as the warm water mass stayed put, and that's exactly what happened. The filament that's been sitting off the mid-Atlantic didn't slide or break down, it just held its ground, and that kept the yellowfin and white marlin biting steady right through the back half of the week.

Here's the mechanism, for anyone who wants the why and not just the where: you've got a sharp temperature edge running into a chlorophyll boundary — basically a color change stacked right on top of a temperature change. That's the setup that pins bait in place instead of letting it wander, and once bait sits still, everything with fins lines up behind it. Right now that overlap is about as tight as it's been all season, and it's one of the better structural reads in the canyon system this month.

Route-wise, the fleet's been chasing that edge more than any single canyon. Spencer got the early-week play when the filament was sitting closest to it, then by Thursday and Friday boats were sliding south and picking up Lindenkohl as the edge drifted that direction. Poor Man's saw a couple of quality days mid-week on the troll before going quiet — a reminder that this pattern rewards boats that check in with the fleet each morning instead of running the same numbers out of habit. Norfolk is the long run from Cape May or Ocean City, and it only made sense on the days the intel was strong enough to justify the fuel; a couple of crews made that call and didn't regret it, but it's not a default trip right now.

On the troll, the yellowfin have wanted a mixed spread — green-and-gold Ilanders paired with naked ballyhoo, a couple of cedar plugs run short to clean up the pattern, worked right along the color change where the water goes from that hazy green to true blue. Once the sun's up and the fish get finicky, boats have been switching over to the chunk, butterflied bunker fished on circle hooks back in a slow chum slick, with the better bites coming early and then again as the light starts to fade. Bigeye have shown on the deep drop in that same general stretch of real estate — squid strips and glow jigs worked well below the thermocline, patient work, not a numbers game but worth the soak for the size class.

White marlin have been mixing into the yellowfin spreads all week, mostly on naked ballyhoo and small green machines pulled just off the edge itself rather than out in the blue water. Wahoo have been a bonus rather than a target — a couple came on high-speed gear run with wireline and skirted lures when boats were making the run out and decided to pull a line, but nobody's dedicating a whole spread to them yet. Mahi have been scattered around any floating structure or weed line that's held together, small stuff mostly, though a few gaffer-class fish showed up around debris on the way out to Lindenkohl.

Looking at the next few days, the tide is still riding the push off this new moon, which means stronger current on the edges and, usually, a better bite window around the tide change out there just like it does inshore. If the filament keeps holding position — and there's no sign yet that it's breaking down — the smart play is to keep checking which canyon the edge is sitting closest to each morning rather than committing to one spot for the week. Spencer and Lindenkohl are the shorter runs and have been trading the better days between them; Poor Man's is worth a look if the color sets up right; Norfolk stays a save-it-for-when-the-intel-is-strong run given the distance involved.

The one thing to watch is exactly what the outlook flagged — if that warm-water edge slides or breaks apart, that's the story that changes everything else in this report. Right now it hasn't. Until it does, this is a week where checking in with the docks before you run pays off more than any single spot ever will.
