# Mixed Bag Monday, Money Wednesday: Spencer Chunk Bite Carries the Week
*A warm filament off the Stream kept yellowfin honest at Spencer while Norfolk's white marlin bite finally showed some teeth.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 31** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Grading this week a solid B. Monday and Tuesday were a grind — six-hour runs to Spencer with only a few marks on the meter and a couple of teaser bites that never turned into hookups. Wednesday changed everything. That's the day I want to talk about, because it tells you where to point the bow the next few days.

Here's the setup: we've got a Gulf Stream filament pushing warm water unusually far north for the end of July, and it's sitting right up against the canyon wall from Hudson down through Wilmington and into our water — Spencer, Lindenkohl, and on into Norfolk. High pressure's been parked over the mid-Atlantic for the better part of ten days now, winds out of the southwest and light, seas mostly 2 to 3 feet with the occasional 4-footer riding through on a longer period. That's a fishable ocean, and it's held the warm edge in place instead of letting it slide out or get chewed up by a front. We flagged this pattern last week — said the southern canyon grounds would keep producing tuna and mahi with white marlin building as the season point demands — and that's exactly the arc we're on.

Spencer's been our go-to. We're working the 100-fathom curve on the northeast side, roughly the color change where you go from that olive-green inshore water to clean blue, somewhere around 38°45'N by 73°50'W depending on the day the edge sets up. Wednesday we anchored up on a good temp break — you could see it in the water color and feel it in the chunk slick pulling bait — and put down butterfish chunks on 50-pound fluoro leader, 7/0 circle hooks, no weight, just let it flutter down naturally in the current. Yellowfin from 25 to 45 pounds, steady through the morning bite window and again for about ninety minutes right at last light. Nothing spectacular in size, but consistent — six to nine fish in the box most days this week when the chunk bite connected. A couple of bigger models pushing 60 came off the troll on green machine spreader bars pulled early morning before we set up to anchor.

Lindenkohl's been quieter, and I think it's simply positioning — the warm filament hasn't wrapped as tight around that canyon as it has Spencer, so the bait hasn't stacked up the same way. We ran up there Thursday, marked some scattered fish on the meter around 300 to 400 feet, but couldn't buy a solid chunk bite. If you're going to fish Lindenkohl right now, I'd troll it rather than anchor — ballyhoo and small Ilanders in blue/white and green/yellow combos, working the drop-off edges rather than sitting still.

Poor Man's has been the mahi spot all week, and that's typical for a canyon that close in — it doesn't need the same deep-water temp break to produce, it just needs floating structure. We found two decent weed lines Tuesday holding gaffer-class mahi, 8 to 15 pounds, and worked them with small skirted jigs and bucktails cast right into the shade line. If you're running a shorter day out of Ocean City and don't want to burn fuel to Spencer, Poor Man's with a mahi program is the smart call right now — quick bite, good eating fish, and you're back at the dock by early afternoon.

Norfolk is the story to watch. That's our southernmost run, and it's where the white marlin activity has actually started building the way we expected for this point in the season. We had two white marlin bites Wednesday trolling ballyhoo behind Sea Witches, converted one, and heard from another boat working the same edge who went two-for-three. That's not a red-hot bite, but it's a real uptick from where we were two weeks ago, and it lines up with the warm water continuing to push and hold along that canyon wall. There's bigeye potential down there too on the night chunk — we haven't connected yet, but the water column temps and bait presence say it's worth a soak after dark if you've got the fuel range and the patience.

Bottom line for the week ahead: as long as this high pressure holds and that warm filament stays pinned against the canyon structure — and both of those look stable through the next several days — I'd keep Spencer as the primary target for the chunk bite, run Poor Man's for a quicker mahi trip, and start committing more time to Norfolk if you're chasing white marlin or want a crack at bigeye after dark. The one thing that could change this fast is a frontal passage — early August tends to throw a quick thunderstorm system through, and if that happens the bait can scatter and you'll need a day to relocate the edge again. Watch the color change before you committing to an anchor spot, and don't be afraid to burn an extra twenty minutes running the edge before you set up. That's what separated our Wednesday from our Monday.
