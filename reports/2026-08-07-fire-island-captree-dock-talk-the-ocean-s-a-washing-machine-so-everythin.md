# Captree dock talk: the ocean's a washing machine, so everything's stacking in the inlet
*Rough surf and a warm ocean layer are pushing bass, fluke, and blues into Fire Island Inlet's current seams ahead of the August 12 new moon.*

**CAPTREE, NY — August 9** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Guy pulls his boat up next to mine at the Captree fuel dock Thursday morning, looks out past the barrier beach where the swell's still humping up green and white, and says, "You going out there today?" I told him no chance — not with 44025 reading almost four feet at five seconds and a mean direction dead out of the south. That kind of washing-machine water off the beach doesn't drown the fishing, though. It just moves it. And this week it moved everything into the inlet.

That's the story right now on the South Shore: the open ocean is a mess, running warm on the surface — low 70s out past the bar — but rough enough that bait and predators alike are ducking into the protected current seams instead of sitting exposed on the beachfront. Fire Island Inlet has been the address. Democrat Point on the outgoing has been holding stripers tight to the rip where the incoming tide from the ocean side collides with the bay water pouring out — that seam is doing exactly what it always does when the surf's up and dirty: it concentrates everything that wants to eat.

I worked that rip three mornings this week on the last two hours of the ebb, anchoring up-current and drifting bunker chunks back on fish-finder rigs, 3 ounces of egg sinker to hold bottom in maybe 12 to 18 feet. Picked up stripers to 32 inches, a couple of shorts mixed in, nothing that's going to make the cover of a magazine but a real, honest bite — three keepers one morning, a skunk the next when the wind swung and killed the current push. That's the truth of inlet fishing this time of year: when the tide's moving right, it's good. When it goes slack or the wind fights the current, it shuts off fast. I'd rather be honest about that than tell you it's a sure thing every trip.

Bluefish have made a real comeback the last week or so after a quiet July, and they're doing it right alongside the bass in that same inlet water. Snapper-sized fish have been thick off the docks in Patchogue and Bayshore for weeks, but now we're seeing real cocktail blues, 3 to 5 pounds, blitzing bunker schools that got pushed tight against the beach by the swell. If you see the birds working off Democrat Point or along the ocean side of the barrier near the Fire Island Lighthouse rip, throw a diamond jig with a green tube, or a bunker spoon if you've got the metal — you don't need finesse, they're eating anything that flashes.

Fluke are a mixed bag right now, and I'll level with you — the bite at the Robert Moses bridges has slowed from where it was in June. Water's warm, current's still solid on the outgoing under the spans, and we're still picking fish, just fewer of them and more shorts in the mix. Best drift has been on the south side of the Captree side channel in 25 to 30 feet, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow with the tide, tipped with a strip of squid. A couple of guys have had four-pound-plus fish doing that same drift right at the base of the pilings on the last hour of the ebb, but you're earning those, not stumbling into them.

Weakfish have shown flashes in the bay — a few reports of fish in the 18 to 20 inch range around the deeper holes off Nicoll Bay and near the Sayville flats on soft plastics fished slow on bucktails at dusk — but it's spotty enough that I wouldn't plan a trip around them specifically. Consider it a bonus if you're already out there working bass or fluke.

Porgies are doing what porgies do in August — they're everywhere in the bay, and they're an easy box-filler if the bigger stuff isn't cooperating. Sandworms or clam on a high-low rig over the mussel beds off Sexton Island or in the channel edges near West Sayville, 15 to 20 feet, and you'll load up in twenty minutes. Good for a kid's first trip or when you just want to feel something pulling back.

We flagged this last week — said the building tide into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure — and that's exactly what's playing out at the inlet right now. The rip at Democrat Point has gotten meaner and more productive every day as the tidal range builds, and that trend's got another two, three days to run before it peaks.

Looking ahead: if the swell holds through the weekend, I'd stay off the exposed beachfront and keep working the inlet seams and bay structure — that's where the fish are concentrated and that's where they'll stay as long as the ocean's kicking up. If the surf lays down by midweek and that south wind backs off, I'd expect some of that bass and blue activity to spread back out along the Fire Island beaches on the early morning tide, which is when I'll be walking the sand at first light with a bucktail rather than running the boat. Either way, watch that new moon tide on the 12th — bigger water movement usually means a better bite at the bridges and the inlet, provided the wind doesn't turn it into another washing machine.
