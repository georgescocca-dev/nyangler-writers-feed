# Bay Heat Pushes the Doormats Out — Sloop Channel and the Inlet Take Over
*Great South Bay ran hot enough to shove the fluke into the channels while bunker still stack the inlet mouth for blues and the occasional bass.*

**JONES BEACH, NY — July 8** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what the bay does in a heat spell — it cooked. Great South Bay pushed close to 79 degrees on the flats, and that's too warm for a fluke to sit comfortable on bottom all day. What that does is push fish. The doormats that were scattered around the grass flats off Wantagh and the mouth of the creeks packed up and moved into the channels and the inlet itself, where there's current, cooler bottom water, and bait getting flushed through on the tide. I've said it before and I'll say it again — when the bay gets soupy in July, Sloop Channel and the throat of Jones Inlet become the address. That's exactly what's happening right now, and the ocean side is helping the cause — there's upwelled water sitting around 71 degrees just outside the inlet, which is a nice cool seam for a fluke that just got pushed out of a warm bathtub.

This week the moon's the story. We're building toward a new moon on the 11th, which means bigger tides stacking in through the weekend — the spring tide push peaks right around the 11th through 13th. More water moving on both the flood and the ebb through Jones Inlet means harder current in the throat, which is good news and bad news. Good, because it flushes bait through the cut and gets predators keyed up on a tighter window. Bad, because if you're not there for that window, the fishing can go dead fast once the tide slacks. Wind's been the usual summer variable — when it's held out of the south or southwest, it's kept the ocean side a little riled, but the inlet itself has stayed fishable on the inside of the tide stages. Watch that forecast into the weekend because bigger springs plus any onshore wind can turn Jones Beach ocean side into soup in a hurry.

Fluke is the honest bread and butter right now. Best water has been 15 to 25 feet in Sloop Channel and along the edges of the West End channel where it dumps into the inlet — fishing the last two hours of the outgoing into dead low, then picking back up as it turns. Three-quarter ounce white bucktails with a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet trailer is what's putting fish in the cooler, dragged slow along bottom, short hops, don't work it too hard. A few guys are doing well with a simple high-low rig and squid strips fished dead-stick style off the channel edges when the current's really ripping on the big tides — sometimes a fluke just wants it sitting still in that kind of flow. Nothing epic size-wise, mostly 16 to 19 inches with a keeper mixed in here and there, but the numbers have picked up noticeably since the bay pushed those fish out.

Bluefish have been the more reliable show at the inlet mouth itself. There's still bunker stacked up around the Jones Beach jetty and out toward Short Beach, and the blues have been slashing through them at first light and again right at dusk — that's your window, don't sleep in. Kastmasters and Hopkins jigs worked fast through the melee, or if you want to match the hatch, a bunker spoon trolled along the edge of the school will get bit hard. Some of these blues are pushing 4 to 6 pounds, real fun on light tackle, and they'll keep you busy while you're waiting to see if anything bigger shows under the bait.

Striped bass, I'll be straight with you — it's a summer lull right now, and anybody telling you different is selling something. The daytime bite on the ocean side is mostly quiet, resident fish holding deep and not real interested until the sun's down. If you want a shot at a bass, it's a night program — live eels or bunker chunks fished tight to the rocks at Short Beach and along the Jones Inlet jetty on the incoming tide has produced some fish in the 20 to 30 pound class for the guys willing to put in dark hours. It's not a blitz fishery this time of year, it's a grind-it-out program, one or two quality bites a trip if you're in the right spot at the right stage of tide.

Weakfish — I know there's been chatter about a comeback, and I want to believe it as much as anybody who grew up fishing these waters when weakfish were a real player in this bay. But I'm not going to blow smoke. Water's been plenty warm enough for weakfish for over two weeks now in the back bay, and I still haven't heard of a single quality fish coming out of Freeport or the South Oyster Bay creeks. Warm water alone doesn't do it — there needs to be the right bait and the right structure lining up too, and right now that alignment hasn't happened. Keep it on your radar for a cooler snap later this summer, but don't build a trip around it yet.

Porgies are around the reef structure off Freeport if you want a bend in the rod and dinner both — clam on a standard porgy rig in 20 to 30 feet has been steady if unspectacular.

Looking ahead, I'd focus hard on that new moon spring push building through the 11th to 13th. Bigger current in the inlet should sharpen up the fluke bite in Sloop Channel on the last of the outgoing, and there's a real chance the harder flow pushes more bunker through the cut and triggers a bass show at dusk that hasn't been there in daylight. If the wind cooperates and lays down out of the south, I'd be at the inlet mouth right at last light with bucktails rigged and a stick bait tied on just in case something bigger comes up under those blues.
