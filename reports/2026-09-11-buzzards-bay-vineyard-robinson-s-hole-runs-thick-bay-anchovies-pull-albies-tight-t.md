# Robinson's Hole Runs Thick: Bay Anchovies Pull Albies Tight to the Rocks
*The new moon push has bait stacked in every rip from Quick's Hole to Menemsha, and the albies and bonito are stacked right behind it.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — September 11** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Bay anchovies and silversides are getting squeezed into every current seam between Woods Hole and Menemsha right now, and that's the whole story. The new moon on September 10th put a serious pull on the tide — bigger swings, harder current, and that means bait has nowhere to hide. It's getting pinned against the rocks at Robinson's Hole, pushed through the Quick's Hole cut, and balled up in the rip off Gay Head. Albies and bonito have been sitting on that bait like they know exactly where it's going to end up, because at this point in September, they do.

The pattern that's been building for two weeks is locked in now. Albies are working steady through Buzzards Bay and holding tight to the Elizabeth Islands chain, with the better action showing on the last two hours of the ebb at Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole, when the current really starts ripping and the bait gets pinched into that narrow gut. Bonito are mixed right in with them, a little more willing to sit shallower over the sand on the Vineyard side, working the rip off Menemsha and the drop just outside Squibnocket. We flagged this coming last week — said the albie and bonito bite would keep building as the new moon tide arrived — and that's exactly what the fleet is finding this week. Nothing tentative about it anymore.

Tackle-wise, keep it small and fast. Half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce epoxy jigs in bay anchovy patterns — olive over white, or that translucent green-and-silver — are outfishing everything else when the fish are eating anchovies, which they mostly are right now. When silversides show up thicker, switch to a slimmer profile, something like a Deadly Dix or a 3-inch Game On! EEL jig in white. Retrieve speed matters more than color out here — a fast, erratic strip with pauses is drawing more strikes than a straight burn. Fly guys working Quick's Hole and the Gay Head rip are doing well on olive-and-white Clousers and half-and-half patterns on intermediate lines, timing their casts to the boils.

Striped bass are the other half of this story, and they're finally committing the way they should this time of year. Bunker schools are tightening up against the beaches and inlet mouths from the Bay side down through Vineyard Sound, and bass are pushing them hard on the last of the outgoing tide. Cuttyhunk's west end and the rip off Canapitsit Channel have been holding fish in the mid-20s to low-30s, with bigger fish showing after dark on bunker chunks fished on the bottom near the Sow and Pigs reef. During the day, big soft plastics — 9-inch white or bunker-colored paddle tails on 2-ounce jig heads — worked slow along the current edges are getting bit. This bass push should keep building through the week as the tide energy stays elevated off the new moon.

Bluefish are thick in the same water, and they're not shy about it. Snapper blues are all through the back bays and harbors — Menemsha Pond, Lagoon Pond, the shallows off Oak Bluffs — which is the usual sign that bigger blues aren't far behind. Adult blues in the 4- to 8-pound class are showing up mixed in with the albie schools at Robinson's Hole, which means anyone throwing small metal into a blitz out there needs to be ready for a bluefish to shorten up their leader in a hurry. Wire or heavy fluorocarbon shock tippet isn't optional this week.

Fluke is the one soft spot right now, and it's worth being straight about it. The keeper-grade fish have largely cleared out of the inshore water here and pushed toward Nantucket Sound and the Shoals, chasing cooler, deeper structure as the shallow water sits in the low-to-mid 70s. What's left in Buzzards Bay and Vineyard Sound skews small — plenty of action on Gulp strips and squid strips over the sandy flats off Tarpaulin Cove, but keepers are the exception, not the rule. If fluke is the priority this week, the better bet is running out toward the Sound rather than working the same inshore drifts that produced all summer.

Looking at the next few days, the tide energy stays strong through the week as the moon eases off new phase, and that should keep bait tight and predators committed through at least the weekend. Wind looks manageable for small-boat runs to Quick's Hole and Robinson's Hole on the mornings, though afternoon sea breeze can turn that rip choppy fast — earlier starts are paying off. If there's only one window to fish this week, it's the last two hours of the ebb at Robinson's Hole with birds working — that's been the most consistent albie window all week, and there's no sign it's slowing down. Bonito should keep filtering westward with the warm edge, so keep an eye on Menemsha and the Squibnocket drop as the week goes — that's where the next wave typically shows first.
