# Captree dock talk: bunker packed the inlet, and the blitz didn't quit
*Harvest Moon tides pushed bait hard through Fire Island Inlet all week — bass and blues followed, and the fluke never got the memo about fall.*

**CAPTREE, NY — September 21** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Stood at the scale dock Thursday evening listening to a mate off one of the head boats describe what he watched from the wheel that morning — bunker balled up so thick off the Robert Moses Bridge pilings that his fish finder looked like static, and stripers coming up through the middle of it like they were shot out of a cannon. That's been the story all week out here, and it's not slowing down.

The Harvest Moon tides did exactly what big tides do this time of year — they put the bunker schools in motion. Peanuts and adult menhaden have been getting flushed out of the back bay creeks around Sayville and Patchogue on the bigger ebbs and funneled straight into Fire Island Inlet, where the bass and blues have been waiting. We flagged this trend last week, that the topwater and metal bite into those bait schools was some of the best action of the year and it would keep building as the nights cooled — and that's precisely what's happened. The water hasn't even cooled that much yet. Offshore readings are still sitting in the low-70s, which is warmer than normal for the third week of September, and that warmth is buying this bite extra time it wouldn't normally get.

Offshore has been sloppy — close to 4-foot swell on a short 5-second period rolling in from the south has kept the open beach fronts Island choppy and discolored for stretches of the week, which pushed a lot of the reliable action into the protected water. Democrat Point and the inlet itself have been the play because the jetty and the shoal break up that swell, and the bunker crowding through the cut have nowhere to hide. When the ocean's beat up like that, the inlet becomes the funnel, and everything eats there.

Stripers have been running mostly schoolie to mid-slot inside the inlet and around the Robert Moses bridges, with better fish mixed in on the harder tide stages — dawn and dusk changes especially. Bunker spoons and 2-3 ounce Ava jigs bounced off the bottom in 15 to 25 feet on the outgoing have been drawing strikes, but the real show has been topwater — Super Strike poppers and Gibbs pencils worked over visible bunker pods at first light, right along the seam where the inlet current meets the bay side. Bluefish have been riding shotgun on every one of those blitzes, chopping through the same bunker schools, and they've had size to them too — not just snappers, but 3 to 6 pound choppers that'll shred a soft bait in one pass, so wire or heavy mono leader is worth the trouble right now.

Fluke have been the pleasant surprise. Normally by the third week of September the doormat crews are packing it in, but the warm water's kept them shallow and feeding later than usual. The stretch off the Robert Moses bridges in 25 to 35 feet has still been giving up keepers on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped on 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow on the last of the incoming. Guys working the Democrat Point side of the inlet channel have reported similar action on strip baits and squid strips fished tight to bottom. It's not a wide-open bite — more a case of grinding through some shorts to find your keeper limit — but for late September, nobody's complaining.

Weakfish have shown up in small numbers mixed in with the fluke drift, mostly incidental fish on bucktails and Gulp, nothing to plan a trip around yet. Porgies are still solid around the Captree structure and the bridge rubble if you want a box of dinner fish on sandworms and squid, and blackfish season opener is still a few weeks out, but the reef spots inside the bay are worth scouting now while the water's still comfortable.

Looking ahead, the pattern that's been building should keep building for a few more days. Bait's still pushing, tides are still oversized off the Harvest Moon, and the water hasn't cooled enough to shut anything down. The next thing to watch for is false albacore working their way west along the south shore — Montauk's been seeing them, and that leading edge typically walks down the beach toward Fire Island within a week or so once the pattern's established. If that push shows up at Democrat Point or along the outer bar off Kismet, small metal like half-ounce Deadly Dicks and Epoxy Jigs worked fast through the surface splashes is the move.

The wildcard is any real cold front. Right now there's nothing forcing a hard temperature drop, but when that first true cool-down hits — even a couple of days of northwest wind — expect the fluke bite to tighten up fast and the bass to start staging harder around the inlet instead of scattering through the bay. Until then, if there's only one window to fish this weekend, put it on a dawn tide change at the inlet with the bunker pods. That's where the fleet's finding fish, and that's where the money's been all week.
