# Peconic settles into August rhythm — porgies stacked at Shelter Island, snappers take over the docks
*Warm, glassy bay water has pushed the bite into tight dawn-and-dusk windows, but the bottom fishing around Shelter Island is as reliable as it gets this time of year.*

**GREENPORT, NY — August 17** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

By mid-August the Peconic has settled into that familiar late-summer groove — the water up top is soft and warm, the bays are glassy by ten in the morning, and everything that's biting is doing it on the edges of the day or down deep where the current keeps things honest. This is the bay I know best in this stretch of the calendar: not flashy, not a blitz, just steady work if you know where to put the boat.

Porgies remain the backbone of the report. Shelter Island continues to be the epicenter — Cedar Island light, the reef off Fleets Neck, and the rock piles running out from Jessup's Neck are all holding good numbers of scup in 15 to 25 feet. High-low rigs with size 4 or 6 hooks, sandworms or clam strips, fished right on bottom on the last two hours of the incoming or the first hour of the outgoing, have been the ticket. These fish don't need much finesse this time of year — they're thick, they're hungry, and once you find the school over shell or rubble the action holds until the tide really starts to run hard. Anchor up tight, chum lightly with crushed clam, and you can fill a cooler without much drama.

Fluke are still modest, which is about what I expected. I said last week that the fluke pattern here would stay unspectacular through mid-August, and that's exactly how it's played out — the bay is holding fish, just not in the numbers or size that get anybody excited. The better drifts have been the channel edges: the cut off Jessup's Neck, the deeper stretch of Noyack Bay channel, and the flow lanes off Southold. Twenty to 35 feet, moving water, white Gulp swimming mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of squid have been out-producing straight bait rigs. Most of what's coming over the rail is short to just-keeper, so it's a pick-through-them bite rather than a limit trip, but there are enough fluke in the mix to make an afternoon drift worthwhile if you're patient about it.

Weakfish are showing the way they usually do in August — intermittent, deep, and worth a look if you're already fishing the basins. Northwest Harbor and the mouth of Three Mile Harbor have both given up a few weakfish in the 25 to 40-foot holes on the slower stages of the tide, mostly on bucktails tipped with squid or on bottom rigs with a live killie. Nothing consistent enough to plan a trip around, but if you're porgy fishing nearby and want to drop a bait into the deeper water on the way in or out, it's not a bad side bet.

The real late-summer staple right now is snapper blues, and they're thick around the docks in Greenport, Sag Harbor, and up in Three Mile Harbor. This is the bite I flagged as building last week, and it's arrived right on schedule — small silver spoons, size 00 or 0 Kastmasters, or a bobber rig with a spearing chunk fished right off the pilings will keep kids and adults both busy for an hour at a time. It's a good sign for what's coming, too. When snapper numbers build like this through late August, the adult bluefish class tends to follow into September as the bay cools and the bigger fish push in behind the bait.

Striped bass haven't disappeared, they've just gone quiet in the middle of the day the way they always do when the surface water sits in the high 70s. The better window has been right at first light, working the rip off Jessup's Neck and the edges around Robins Island on bucktails or small swimming plugs before the sun gets up. There's also been some evening action working bunker chunks on the channel edges near Orient Point as the tide turns — nothing to write home about, but enough fish moving through to make dawn and dusk worth the alarm clock. The moon is still building back toward the full on the 28th, so tidal push has been on the weaker side this week; once that current strengthens up again it should sharpen the bass bite along those current seams and give the porgy and fluke fishing an extra gear too.

Looking ahead, I don't expect a dramatic shift in the next several days — the bay is going to keep doing its warm-water August thing, with porgies as the sure bet, fluke as a grinder's game, and bass and weakfish rewarding early risers and current watchers. If the wind stays out of the south and keeps things calm, I'd spend my time at Shelter Island in the mornings for porgies, then slide into the channels midday for a fluke drift while the tide's still moving. As we build toward that full moon and the tides strengthen, watch Orient Point and the mouth of the Gut — that's usually the first sign the fall transition is starting to stir, even if it's still a few weeks off.
