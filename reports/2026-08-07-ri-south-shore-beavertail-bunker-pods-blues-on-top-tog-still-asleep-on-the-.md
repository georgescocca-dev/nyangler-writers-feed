# Beavertail bunker pods, blues on top, tog still asleep on the ledges
*Bait's stacked from Newport to Sakonnet but the bass have gone nocturnal — here's where the rocks are actually producing.*

**JAMESTOWN, RI — August 7** — _by Billy Tanaka, Zone Writer_

Peanut bunker are pouring off Beavertail right now, thick enough that you can see the ledge rock go dark under the surface at low light — a moving shadow instead of stone. That's the tell this week. Wherever that shadow shows, something's under it working the edges, and from Newport down through Sakonnet the bunker have pulled everything with teeth into the shallows.

Bluefish found them first, and they found them hard. Snapper blues are thick in every cove off Jamestown — Fort Wetherill, Mackerel Cove, the riprap below Fort Adams — light tackle guys are getting a fish every cast on 1/8-ounce Kastmasters and Gotcha plugs, no leader needed, just have fun with it. But it's the adult blues that made the week. We flagged this coming — said the cocktail blues would spike as the bunker spread, and that's exactly what showed up off Third Beach and around the Sakonnet Point rocks: 3- to 6-pound blues busting bait on top in the last hour of light, white water everywhere, gulls dropping on the pieces. Poppers and metal-lip swimmers thrown into the froth, don't overthink it. That bite's been the most reliable thing on the whole shore this week.

Bass are a different story, and it's the story we called last week too. With water sitting in the mid-70s off the ledges and tides softening out of the last push, the stripers have compressed into the strongest current hours and the deepest, coolest cracks they can find. Daytime bass fishing off the open rock has been quiet — a few follows, not much committing. Nighttime is where it's happening. Eels fished on the down-current side of the Jamestown Bridge pilings and the Newport Bridge structure have been picking up slot fish on the last two hours of the ebb, when the current stacks bait against the concrete and the bass sit tight to it. Sakonnet Point ledges are doing the same thing after dark — swim a 3-ounce bucktail with a big curl tail down along the rock face on the drop, slow, and let it bump bottom. That's producing better than anything thrown in daylight right now.

Fluke have done what they always do this time of year — they've abandoned the shallow flats for deeper structure where the bottom temp is more forgiving. The East Passage channel edges off Jamestown, and the deeper water at the mouth of the Sakonnet River, have both been giving up keeper fish to 5 pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift with the tide. Nothing gangbusters, but steady enough that a few drifts will fill a limit if you're patient and stay on the bottom.

Scup are filling out the cooler without much effort — Fort Wetherill, the Newport breakwaters, and the rocks off Second Beach are stacked with them on squid strips and clam, 15 to 25 feet of water, bottom rig. Good action if you've got kids or guests aboard who just want bent rods.

Tog, I'll be honest, are quiet. This is the dead zone for blackfish season down here — a few are showing on green crabs worked into the deeper cracks at Beavertail and around the Sakonnet Point ledges on a good slack tide, but it's a fish here and there, not a program. That fishery wakes back up as the water starts cooling in September and October, when the tog push back onto the shallower structure to feed hard before winter. Right now I'd rather spend the tide on bass or blues than grind it out on tog.

Albies haven't shown yet on this stretch, but the pieces are lining up. Offshore water's holding in the mid-70s, bait's dense along the whole coast, and the pattern that usually starts at Montauk and Block Island before sliding into our breachways looks like it's building on schedule, maybe a touch early given how warm it's run. Watch Hill and Napatree are always first in this zone — the current through the breachway concentrates bait right at the mouth, and albies push in on the last of the incoming to run it down. Weekapaug fishes the same way a few tides later once the run establishes. Nothing confirmed yet this week, but if the stable weather holds and that warm-water push keeps spreading east like it's forecast to, I'd expect the first real showing in the next week or two rather than waiting for the usual September date.

One more thing worth knowing if you're bouncing between spots — the south-facing beaches, Second Beach, Matunuck, Charlestown, have been getting pushed around by a lingering ocean swell, running 3 to 4 feet with a short period, which has kept the surf stirred and made sight-fishing tough. The Sakonnet shore, facing more east and tucked behind the point, has stayed comparatively clean and has fished better for that reason alone — worth remembering when the buoys show any kind of leftover groundswell.

For the coming week: keep working the bridges and ledges at night for bass while the daytime bite stays lockjawed, ride the bluefish blitzes on bunker while they last, and keep one eye on the breachway mouths at Napatree and Watch Hill. If that albie push arrives on schedule, it'll show there first.
