# Falkner Island Porgies Stack Up, Bluefish Crash the Charles Island Bar
*Warm water has stripers sulking after dark, but the rockpiles are stacked with porgies and sea bass for anyone willing to soak worms on the bottom.*

**MILFORD, CT — August 3** — _by Lena Marchetti, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning I had a kid two rods down from me on the Falkner Island rockpile double up on porgies before I'd even gotten my second sinker to the bottom — 30 feet of water, half-flood tide, and those things were coming over the rail two at a time on hi-lo rigs with green worms. That's the kind of morning that makes you forget you spent half of July getting skunked on stripers in the daylight.

Let's talk about why. Kings Point is reading 70.9, New London's sitting at 69.4, and everything in between — which is us, Central Sound — is running warm and stable. That's murder on daytime striper activity but it's rocket fuel for porgies, sea bass, and bluefish, and that's exactly the split we're seeing right now. We flagged this pattern last week — said conditions would hold steady with no major shift, and keeper fluke mixed with shorts on structure drifts — and that's precisely what's playing out on the bottom.

Falkner Island remains the money spot. I'm anchoring up on the northeast side in 25 to 35 feet, and the porgy bite is best from half-tide through the top of the flood — current isn't ripping so hard that you're chasing your sinker across the reef, but there's enough push to keep the scent trail working. Green worms and squid strips on size 8 hooks, standard two-hook bottom rigs, 2- to 3-ounce sinkers depending on how much tide is running. Sea bass are mixed in as a bonus, and a few of them are pushing 15, 16 inches — good enough to keep the pan happy. I'm seeing more shorts than keepers most drifts, but if you cover three or four spots on the reef you'll fill a cooler.

The New Haven Harbor breakwalls — both the outer wall and the stretch off Sandy Point — have been quietly good for porgies too, especially on the last two hours of the outgoing. Less crowded than Falkner, shorter run if you're trailering out of Morningside or City Point, and the structure holds fish tight to the rocks. Same rig, same bait, just fish it tighter to the wall than you think you need to — porgies are hugging the rockpile itself, not sitting off it in open sand.

Now the fun one. Bluefish showed up hard around Charles Island this week, working bait pods right off the sandbar on the outgoing tide. I had three fish over six pounds Thursday evening casting 3/4-ounce Kastmasters and Hopkins spoons into the wash where the current rips off the bar — light spinning gear, 20-pound braid, and it felt like every cast got bit. This tracks with what's been happening the length of the Sound this summer; that warm surface layer everyone's complaining about for stripers has been an absolute gift to the blues. If you want a fight on light tackle right now, the bar off Charles Island at last light is the play.

Stripers are the frustrating part of the week. With surface temps sitting in the low-to-mid 70s, the bass have gone deep and mostly nocturnal — I'm hearing about fish being picked off Stratford Shoal after dark on live eels drifted along the deeper edges, 25 to 40 feet, slack tide into the first hour of the ebb. Daytime, forget the sandbar entirely unless you're there at first light before the sun gets on the water. I made two dawn trips to Charles Island this week and only touched one fish, a schoolie, on a white Slug-Go worked slow along the drop-off. That's the honest report — the bass are around, but they're not playing during banker's hours right now.

Blackfish are starting to show a little life on the deeper rockpiles off Milford Gulf, 30 to 40 feet, but it's early and spotty — a fish here and there on green crab, nothing to plan a trip around yet. That'll get better as water starts to cool into September.

Looking ahead, we're building toward the new moon and the tides are going to strengthen through the week, which should tighten up the bite windows on the reefs — expect the porgy and sea bass action to compress into shorter, more defined tide stages rather than the all-day grazing we've had. If the wind stays out of the south and doesn't stir up New Haven Harbor, I'd fish Falkner or the outer breakwall on the building flood and be done by early afternoon. For bass, my money's still on dusk-into-dark at the Charles Island bar or a night run out to Stratford Shoal — that's where the size is right now, even if the numbers aren't blitz-level. Bring patience and eels, not confidence and daylight.
