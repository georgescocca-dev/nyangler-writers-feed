# New moon springs push bay fluke out the inlet as Great South Bay tops 78 degrees
*Fire Island Inlet outgoing tides are holding the best fluke of the summer while stripers set up for a sunset flood bite through the weekend.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 10** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay told us exactly what it was going to do, and it did it. Great South Bay pushed past 78 degrees — I had a thermometer reading 78.8 off my transom drifting the flats south of Nicoll Bay on Wednesday — and that's too warm for a fluke to sit comfortably on bottom mud all day. They didn't leave, they just moved. Every keeper I've heard about the last five days has come out of the channels, the bridge rips, or straight out the inlet where the water's moving and cooler. Meanwhile out front, a stretch of northeast wind did some quiet work upwelling the ocean side down into the low 70s, which is exactly where fluke and bass want to be right now. That's not a coincidence — that's a thermocline doing its job, and it's why the inlet has become the address this week instead of the open bay.

We're coming off a new moon, which means we're building into spring tides — bigger swings, stronger current, more water moving through Fire Island Inlet on every stage. That peaks through the weekend, with the tightest alignment falling Saturday into Monday, July 11-13, when the evening flood is stacking up right around sunset. That's the window I'm circling. A flood tide that peaks at dusk pushes bait — spearing, peanut bunker, whatever's left in the bay — right out through Democrat Point and past the bridges just as the light goes low and the stripers get bold. I've seen this setup before on a new moon in July, and it's one of the better recipes we get all season for a dusk bite at the inlet mouth.

Fluke first, because that's been the real story. The outgoing tide at the Robert Moses bridges — both the East and West spans — has been holding fish in that 25 to 35 foot band where the current rips through the pilings. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow right along bottom on the drift, have been drawing strikes from fish in the 3 to 5 pound range, with a few better ones mixed in — I heard of a fish pushing 7 out past the West span on Sunday's outgoing. Chartreuse and white combo bucktails tipped with a strip of squid have also been working when the current's really ripping and you need the extra scent to slow a fish down enough to commit. If you're working the ocean side, right off Democrat Point in that 15 to 20 foot depth where the upwelled water is holding, the bite's been a touch better than the bay proper — cleaner water, cooler, and the fluke are sitting tight to structure instead of spread out over mud.

Stripers have been a mixed bag, and I want to be straight about that instead of dressing it up. The daytime bite in the bay has been sluggish — too warm, too bright, fish holding deep and not eager. But the evening program at the inlet has been legitimate. Guys working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs off Democrat Point on the last two hours of the outgoing have been picking off fish in the 28 to 34 inch range, with the occasional better fish mixed in on live eels drifted through the deeper cuts near the jetty rocks after dark. That bite should only get better as we roll into this weekend's flood-into-sunset window — bigger water movement means more bait getting flushed, and stripers know how to find a buffet line.

Bluefish have shown up sporadic but violent, which is about right for July. Schools of choppers in the 3 to 6 pound class have been slashing through bunker pods just outside the inlet on the last of the incoming, and if you find the birds working you can load the boat fast on simple diamond jigs or topwater poppers before they push through and vanish. Don't overthink the bluefish — find the surface commotion, get on it quick, and be ready to move again just as fast.

Weakfish I have to be honest about: not much. The water's been warm enough for weeks now to theoretically hold them, but I haven't heard a single solid report out of the bay or the inlet, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise just because the temperature checks a box. That old rule about 70-degree water bringing the weakfish back doesn't seem to be holding this year — plenty of warm water, zero fish to show for it. If you're targeting them, I'd call that a long shot right now, not a plan.

Porgies have been steady and reliable if you want an easy box of fish — working the rock piles inside the inlet and around the Captree bridge abutments with sandworms or clam on a simple hi-lo rig, on the bottom of the outgoing, has been putting decent scup in coolers without much drama. Good option if you're bringing kids or just want dinner without chasing a puzzle.

Looking ahead, I'd spend my weekend hours at the inlet mouth chasing that flood-into-sunset striper window while the new moon springs are peaking — Saturday and Sunday evening both look good on paper if the wind stays out of the northeast and doesn't blow the water dirty. Daytime, I'd stick with the fluke drift off the bridges and out past Democrat Point where that cooler upwelled water is holding fish tight. If the bass don't show at dusk, the fluke bite is deep enough right now to be your fallback, not the other way around.
