# Bay bass bite fires as Peconic hits 72 degrees, porgies stack deep at Shelter Island
*Early flood tides producing slot stripers on bucktails while jumbo scup fill coolers in 30 feet.*

**GREENPORT, NY — June 24** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic finally hit its summer stride this week, with bay temperatures spiking to 72 degrees — a full 8 degrees warmer than the ocean side. That thermal gradient is doing exactly what it should: stacking bait on the flood tides and holding bass through the ebb.

The striper bite has been lights-out at first light, especially around the deeper channels between Shelter Island and Greenport. I've been working 3/4-ounce white bucktails with 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets, keeping them just off bottom on the drift. The key is hitting that last 90 minutes of the flood tide right at sunrise — that's when the bass are most aggressive. Slot fish from 28 to 34 inches have been the norm, with most fish coming from 15 to 25 feet of water.

What's really interesting is how the new moon spring tides are setting up the bite. The stronger current is flushing massive amounts of peanut bunker and spearing out of the back bays, creating feeding opportunities that weren't there during the neap tide cycle two weeks ago. I'm seeing bass following these bait schools right into the shallows, especially around the Jessup Neck area where the channel drops from 8 feet to 30 feet in a boat length.

The porgy fishing remains world-class, though you need to adjust your thinking from the May patterns. The big scup have moved deeper as water temperatures climbed, settling into 25 to 35 feet around the traditional Shelter Island haunts. Clam chum is still king, but I'm finding the bite more selective now. Fresh clam strips on size 4 hooks are outproducing everything else, and you need to keep that chum line steady — these fish are educated.

Weakfish are making their presence known in a big way. The 68-degree water has triggered their summer feeding pattern, and I'm finding them mixed in with the porgies during the day, then moving shallow at dusk. Chartreuse Gulp on light jigheads has been deadly, especially in 12 to 18 feet as the sun drops. Several fish over 20 inches have shown up this week, including a few pushing 4 pounds.

The bluefish situation is typical for late June — they're here, but you have to work for them. Small cocktail blues are scattered throughout the bay, with better fish showing up around the bird activity near Noyack and Three Mile Harbor. Wire leaders are mandatory, and small spoons or Deadly Dicks are producing better than soft plastics.

Looking ahead, this full moon cycle should really fire things up. The spring tides will push more bait around, and with water temperatures holding steady in the low 70s, all species should remain active. I'm particularly excited about the weakfish potential — this is prime time for the bigger fish to show up in numbers.

The weather pattern looks stable through the weekend, with light winds and calm seas. That means you can fish the deeper structure around Shelter Island without fighting the drift, and the shallow water bite should be accessible for smaller boats.

One tactical note: don't overlook the night bite. With these warm water temperatures, bass are feeding aggressively after dark, especially around the lighted docks in Sag Harbor and Greenport. Live eels or large soft plastics worked slowly along the pilings have been producing some of the best fish of the week.
