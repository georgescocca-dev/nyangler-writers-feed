# The West Cut at Moriches Narrowed Again — and the Bass Are Using It Like a Funnel
*A skinnier gut off Cupsogue is concentrating current and bait right where the full-moon springs are about to load in more push than we've seen all summer.*

**CUPSOGUE BEACH, NY — August 10** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

I walked the west end of Cupsogue on the last of Wednesday's ebb and the sandbar geometry has changed again — not dramatically, but enough to matter. The main gut through Moriches Inlet has pinched in maybe 15-20 yards from where it was running in June, pushing more volume through a tighter throat on the Fire Island side, off Smith Point. That's classic Moriches behavior — this inlet never sits still for long, and every time the channel narrows like this, the current through it accelerates and the bait gets squeezed into a tighter parade. Right now that parade is peanut bunker, and it's exactly what we flagged two weeks back — said to watch for peanuts showing in the back bay and to mark the August 11-13 springs as the window when hard current over new bar structure would sharpen things up. Here we are.

The full moon is doing its job. Tide swings are building through the weekend, and that extra push is exactly what a narrowed inlet channel needs to turn from a mediocre drift into a real one. Fluke fishing has been the backbone of the report all summer and it's holding that role — doormats stacking on the channel edges and drop-offs where the water stays a few degrees cooler than the surrounding flats. The stretch off Tuthill Point, where the bay channel drops from six feet to eleven along the edge of the marsh, has been giving up keeper fluke to white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the last two hours of the outgoing. Inside the inlet itself, the west side of the gut off Cupsogue — 18 to 22 feet — has been the better producer on the incoming, when clean ocean water floods back in and fluke slide up onto the edge to ambush bait getting pushed through that tighter throat.

Striped bass are behaving like every August striper south of the Cape right now — this heat has them locked into a strict low-light window. Daytime is mostly dead outside the cooler current-swept structure, but the inlet cut itself qualifies, and that's exactly why the narrowing matters. Dawn and dusk drifts through the new gut, working the current seam where the accelerated flow meets the flatter water on the bay side, have produced slot fish up to 30 inches on bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig, and on soft-plastic paddletails swung through the seam on the drop. If you're throwing bucktails, go heavier than you think you need — 1.5 ounces minimum to hold bottom in that squeezed current — and let it sink clean off the edge before starting a slow, rhythmic retrieve. That's a proven presentation for staged bass sitting tight to current breaks, and Moriches' new geometry is basically building you one.

Bluefish have split into two clear populations and you need to fish them differently. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay creeks around East Moriches and Mastic — small, aggressive, chewing peanut bunker imitations and tiny Kastmasters off docks and bulkheads, good action for a kid with a light rod. The adult class is a different animal, holding tight to the bait schools at the inlet mouth and blitzing sporadically at dusk when bunker get pinched through the cut. If you see the surface nervous water and diving terns off Cupsogue in the last hour of light, that's your window — don't wait, that bite can close in fifteen minutes.

Weakfish are starting to show the pattern we expected — tracking the peanut bunker into the bay rather than sitting deep. A few keeper-class weakfish have come off the drop-offs near Pattersquash Island on bucktails tipped with strips of squid, fished slow along bottom on the last of the outgoing when the water's still got some push but the light is low. It's not a stacked bite yet, more a handful of quality fish mixed into the fluke drift, but it's worth working a few extra drifts through that zone if you're already out there.

Porgies remain steady, unglamorous, and reliable — clam-baited high-low rigs over the shell and sod-bank structure inside the bay, particularly around the deeper holes off Pattersquash, are filling coolers without much drama. Not much to overthink there.

Looking ahead: the springs build through Tuesday, and if the inlet keeps funneling current the way it's been funneling this week, I'd put my time on the dawn and dusk tides through that narrowed west cut off Cupsogue — that's where the bass and the bigger bluefish are going to stage while the extra push holds. Once the moon rounds off and the current eases back to normal, I'd expect the bite to spread out again and lose some of that concentrated edge, so if you've got a day this weekend, don't wait for it.
