# Mackerel Push Into the Shoals, Cod Hold Tight on the Appledore Ledges
*Thirteen miles of coastline is plenty when the groundfish are stacked at White Island and the bass are starting to key on bait in the Piscataqua.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — September 7** — _by Cole Robinson, Zone Writer_

Folks from away hear thirteen miles of coastline and figure there's not much to work with. That's fine by me. It means less pressure on the ledges nine miles out where the haddock have been stacked all August, and it means the Piscataqua stays a locals' game while the rest of the coast chases bunker schools they can see from the road. You don't need Montauk's real estate to catch fish. You need to know where the bottom breaks at the Shoals and which stage of the tide turns the current on Henderson's Point. That's the whole trick out here.

This week's setup is about bait where it should. Mackerel are working back into the Isles of Shoals in decent numbers, which is the first real signal that the water's turning over toward fall. Pogies are still scattered along the beach front from Hampton to Rye, and stripers that have been sulky and scattered all August are starting to show real interest instead of just picking at stragglers. We're coming off the full moon that hit Sunday into Monday, which tightened the bait up nicely, and now we're sliding toward the new moon on the 10th — that's the tide that should get things moving for real. Bigger swings, more current, more urgency in the fish. If there's a week to watch, it's this one heading into next.

Out at the Shoals, the groundfish bite has been steady, not spectacular, which for early September is about right. Haddock are holding on the ledges off White Island and around Duck Island in 90 to 130 feet, mostly on the last two hours of the ebb when the current lays down enough to fish a jig without getting swept sideways. Diamond jigs tipped with a piece of clam or a Berkley Gulp strip have been outproducing bare metal — the fish are eating but not aggressively enough to chase flash alone. Cod have been holding tighter to structure than the haddock, sitting right on the Appledore ledges and the rockpile north of Star Island. Slack water is the window there — twenty, thirty minutes either side of the turn — and jigging bucktails tipped with squid strips has taken fish to eight pounds. Nothing to write home about size-wise, but a solid box of chowder-grade cod and haddock has been a normal trip for boats running out of Rye Harbor this week.

Mackerel jigging has been the wildcard and honestly the most fun anybody's had out there. Schools have been moving through the deeper holes around the Shoals on the tide changes, and a string of mackerel jigs dropped straight down has been good for fast action when you find them stacked on the sounder. Worth keeping a rod rigged for that even if you're targeting bottom fish — mackerel make excellent bait for the bigger stripers starting to show interest inshore, and fresh is fresh.

Inshore, the striper story is starting to turn. Hampton Harbor has had bass working the inlet on the last two hours of the incoming, chasing bait that's getting pushed up against the jetty rocks. Live mackerel and pogies on a fish-finder rig, fished tight to the rock pile on the north side, have taken fish in the 20 to 28 inch range with the occasional better fish mixed in. Bucktails in white or chartreuse, three-quarter ounce, worked slow along bottom on the drift through the channel, have also produced. The Piscataqua has been the more consistent player of the two rivers — bass working the current seams off Fort Point and down through Portsmouth Harbor on the ebb, with soft plastics and small swimming plugs getting hit as the tide starts to really move. Bluefish are mixed in wherever the bass are, and this week they've actually outnumbered stripers in some of the Rye Harbor breakwater reports — nothing giant, mostly two to four pounds, but they'll flat out wreck a bucktail if you're not paying attention.

Odiorne Point has been quieter than it should be for this time of year, but that's typical before the bigger bait pushes arrive. A few eel-soakers working the rocks at last light have picked off single bass in the slot, nothing consistent yet. Great Boars Head has had better action on the outgoing tide right at dusk, mackerel chunks fished on the bottom picking up bass working the wash.

Looking at the next few days, the wind and tide line up for a legitimate push. As the moon builds toward new phase on the 10th, expect the bait to tighten further and the first real blitz-style activity of the fall to start showing along the beachfront and in the harbor mouths — that's the pattern up and down this whole stretch of coast right now, and there's no reason NH sits it out. If you've only got one window this week, fish the last two hours of the incoming at Hampton Harbor inlet or the start of the ebb in the Piscataqua off Henderson's Point — that's where the bait's going to get squeezed first. Offshore, keep working the slack tides at the Shoals while the groundfish are still cooperating; that bite tends to hold steady through September before it really turns on in October.
