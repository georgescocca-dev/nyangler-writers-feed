# Wilmington still waiting on a clean break as SST pipeline lags behind the fleet
*With current thermal imagery thin and no fresh eddy confirmation, the canyon bite hinges on finding the 72-degree edge along the 100-fathom line.*

**INDIAN RIVER, DE — June 5** — _by Capt. Dana Cooper, Zone Writer_

The canyon enters the first full week of June without the clean oceanographic picture captains want at this stage of the season. The most recent SST package in the pipeline carries a June 3 date, and the catalog before that thins out to scattered late-May imagery, meaning anyone running today is working off charts that are at minimum 48 hours stale on a system where warm-core eddies can rotate meaningful structure across ten miles in that window. No fresh buoy temperature reading has been logged into the current analyst package, and the offshore tide and current stations referenced yesterday were all inshore northern grid points — Kings Point, Montauk, Battery, Sandy Hook — none of which speak to what is happening over 500 fathoms at the Wilmington. The honest read is that the fleet is flying partially blind and will be relying on sea-surface temperature gauges at the boat, color changes by eye, and shared radio intel until the next thermal package drops.

What that means for this beat is straightforward: do not commit to a number on the chart, commit to a temperature and a color. Early June at the Wilmington is the window when the shelf water finally pushes through the mid-60s and the offshore side climbs into the low 70s, and the productive edge is almost always the tightest gradient you can find between roughly 68 and 74 degrees. Without a current SST overlay, that edge has to be hunted. The east wall and the southeast notch out toward the 500-fathom curve are the high-percentage starting points, and the tide that matters out here is the lunar pull on the shelf-break current, not anything an inshore gauge will tell you.

The signal coming back from boats that have made the run over the last several days is consistent with a season that is building rather than peaking. Yellowfin are the realistic target class right now, with the first credible bigeye reports typically tied to whichever eddy finger pushes closest to the 100-fathom line in the second and third weeks of June. Mahi numbers around any floating structure — high fliers, pot markers, weed lines — have been the most reliable bend in the rod, which is the classic tell that the warm water is close but the concentrated bait pods that pin tuna in one zip code have not fully set up. Wahoo are a bycatch story at this point in the calendar, not a target, and the white marlin window is still two to three weeks out under a normal thermal progression.

Technique should match the uncertainty. Trolling is the right tool when you are still looking for the edge: a spread of ballyhoo under sea witches, a couple of green machines in the shotgun, and a deep-running plug or planer to cover the column. Once a temperature break is located and marked, the play in the afternoon and into the evening shifts toward setting up on the high side of the edge in 50 to 100 fathoms and putting a chunk slick in the water. Night chunking has not yet hit its stride here — water temps at depth are still cool enough that the bigeye bite under the moon has not lit up — but anyone making the overnight run should be prepared for it if the surface temp at the boat reads north of 72.

The next three to five days hinge on two things. First, whether the next SST package lands and reveals a warm finger pushing toward the 100-fathom line on the east side of the canyon — that would change the calculus and concentrate the fleet quickly. Second, the wind. Anything sustained out of the southwest will help drag warmer water up the shelf and tighten the break; a hard northeast pulse would stall the progression and push the productive edge back offshore. Moon is building toward full later in the month, which historically aligns with the first real bigeye window at the Wilmington.

Until the imagery catches up, the canyon rewards captains who run with a thermometer in their hand and a willingness to keep moving. The fish are out there; the map just is not finished yet.
