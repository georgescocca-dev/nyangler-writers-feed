# Barnegat Inlet rip still paying on fluke while the beachfront turns into a bluefish game
*A new cut cut into the Holgate flat this week, and it's telling you everything about where the bait — and the blues — are stacking up.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — July 31** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

I walked the Holgate stretch Tuesday morning at first light, low tide bottoming out, and there's a new cut carved into the outer bar that wasn't there two weeks ago. Leftover groundswell — we've had 3 to 4 foot stuff rolling through on a short 5-second period most of last week — has been chewing on that flat, and it finally broke through in a spot about 200 yards south of the last parking access. That's a real feature now: deeper water pinched between two humps, current funneling through it hard on the ebb. I marked it on my phone, because that's exactly the kind of structure that holds bait when everything else on this beach is getting hammered flat and featureless by swell.

And bait is the whole story right now. Water's sitting in the mid-70s to pushing 80 in the shallows, which is above where striped bass want to be, but bluefish love it, and they've got it. We flagged this a week ago — said the bunker-bluefish blitz working up from South Jersey would spread into our stretch, and it has. Beach Haven north through Ship Bottom has had legitimate blitz mornings, bunker pods getting pinned against the bar and blues busting on top hard enough to see the spray from the dune line. Bring metal — Deadly Dick or a 1.5-ounce Ava jig in chrome — and don't overthink it, just get it in front of the school before they slide down-beach. Some bigger blues have mixed in since late last week, mid-teens mixed with the choppers, so if you're throwing bucktails for something else and get bit off, that's why.

Fluke are still the more dependable program, and Barnegat Inlet keeps paying like we said it would. The rips off the north jetty and the reef structure on the ocean side are holding keeper-class fish on bucktail and fluke-belly combos — 3/4-ounce white or chartreuse bucktail, six-inch strip of fluke belly or a swimming mullet trailer, worked slow along the bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing. Depth's the key: 15 to 25 feet where the current's ripping over structure, not the flat sand in the middle of the channel. The full moon tides we've been riding have been building nicely into that window, sharpening up the dawn push through the inlet, and that's when I've heard of the better counts — multiple keepers to 22 inches, mixed with plenty of shorts you'll cull through.

Inside the bay, it's a quieter game but not a dead one. Channel edges off Ship Bottom and the bridge structure at Manahawkin have fluke sitting tight where there's a slight thermal break from the current, and a Gulp swimming mullet on a light jighead, three-eighths ounce, dragged slow through those seams on the last of the incoming has been worth the trip for a few guys I talked to at the shop. Weakfish are showing incidentally in the deeper holes on the bayside — Graveling Point and up into the lower Mullica have produced a few in the 18 to 20 inch class for guys soaking bloodworms or a small Gulp shrimp on bottom around dusk. Nothing to build a trip around yet, but worth a rod in the spread if you're anchored up for stripers or drum.

Speaking of stripers — that bite has gone almost entirely nocturnal, and that's not going to change until the water backs off. With surface temps sitting where they are, the resident bass around Barnegat Inlet and the nearby wrecks are holding deep during daylight and only really coming up on bunker schools after dark or right at the tide change. If you want a shot at a bigger fish, soak a whole bunker on a fish-finder rig, 2 to 3 ounce sinker depending on the drift, tight to the inlet rocks or the wreck structure just outside on the last two hours of the ebb into the top of the flood. That's been the play, and it's a slow, patient one — a handful of quality fish a night rather than a blitz.

Black drum season's essentially closed the book on itself for this stretch — that window was April into May around the inlet and Little Egg, and those fish have long since pushed north and offshore. Tautog's closed for the summer too, so if you're structure fishing the reef sites, know you're targeting fluke and bass only right now, nothing to keep off the bottom otherwise.

Out in the surf, kingfish have been showing on the outer bars in decent numbers — small but steady, biting bloodworm or Fishbites on a high-low rig in that 2 to 4 foot zone right where the wash meets the bar. Good action for kids or anyone who wants a rod bent between the bigger blitz windows.

Looking ahead: tides are easing off the full moon push toward neap over the next few days, which should calm the current enough that bunker schools spread out a little and the blitz activity gets patchier rather than pinned in one spot — cover water instead of camping. If an afternoon thunderstorm rolls through, which is typical this time of year, expect a short pause in the bite followed by a reset. The next real trigger is the build back toward the August 12 new moon, when I'd expect bait and predators to reconcentrate hard around the inlet and that new Holgate cut. I'll be watching that cut closely — it's early, but it's the kind of structure that turns into a real producer once the current really carves it out.
