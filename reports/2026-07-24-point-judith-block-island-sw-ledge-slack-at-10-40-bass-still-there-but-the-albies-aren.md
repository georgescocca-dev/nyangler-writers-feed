# SW Ledge slack at 10:40: bass still there, but the albies aren't yet
*The bite's gone nocturnal on the Ledge, sea bass are bailing out the daylight charters, and the water's inching toward that first bonito flash off the Block.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 24** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack tide at SW Ledge hit 10:40 this morning and I had two rods bent inside four minutes — both fish came off before the leader, which tells you everything about the size class we're dealing with right now. Big stripers, working the last of the ebb before the turn, using that current break the way they always do this time of year: staged deep, waiting for the moment the water goes still and the bait gets confused. That window — call it fifteen minutes either side of slack — has been the whole program at the Ledge for the last ten days. Outside that window, you're just soaking bait in open water.

Here's the thing nobody wants to hear in the third week of July: the daytime bass bite off Galilee has gone quiet, and it's not coming back until the water cools or the sun goes down. Surface temps pushing into the mid-70s have shoved the better fish onto the thermocline, and at SW Ledge that means working the deeper edge of the rip — 35 to 55 feet — with big paddletails or a fresh chunk of bunker on a fish-finder rig, bounced right on the bottom where the current stacks bait against the ledge structure. I had a 34-pound fish come up on a live eel two nights ago, fished on the outgoing around 9:30 PM, about a half mile off the Ledge in that deep slot where the current really rips. That's been the pattern — dusk into full dark, not midday. If you're only fishing this spot from 10 to 2, you're leaving fish on the table.

Fluke has been the honest bite. Not a spectacular one, but an honest one. We've been picking through a lot of shorts drifting the deeper humps south of the Ledge and around the mouth of the Harbor of Refuge, but the keepers — fish to 5 and 6 pounds — are coming from the channel edges in 40 to 60 feet, on the stronger half of the tide. White Gulp Swimming Mullet on a bucktail, 3/4 to 1 ounce depending on drift speed, tipped with a fresh squid strip. Slow-dragged, not ripped. The warm shallow water has fluke doing exactly what Hooper's crew flagged coastwide — pushing quality fish deeper and making you work for them instead of just anchoring up anywhere and waiting.

Black sea bass has quietly been the best deal going for anybody who wants steady rod-bending without overthinking it. The reef structure off Block Island — the humps north and east of North Light, and the rockpiles working out toward Southwest Ledge itself — have been stacked. Squid and clam belly on a two-hook bottom rig, 60 to 80 feet, and you're not waiting long between bites. Not the biggest fish of the year but plenty of 16- to 18-inchers mixed in, and it's been a reliable fallback on days when the bass program requires patience you don't have.

Now — the story everybody's actually asking me about at the dock. Bonito and albies. Nothing confirmed yet off the Block, but I'll tell you straight: we flagged this exact scenario a few days back, watching the same warm-water breaks that are now showing up on the surface temp readings creeping toward that trigger range. I was out past Sandy Point yesterday afternoon watching for nervous water and diving terns, and saw bait balling up tight off Clay Head — small silversides, maybe some rain bait mixed in — but no speed demons crashing through it yet. That's usually the tell 48 to 72 hours out. If the pattern holds and this stretch of stable weather doesn't get knocked sideways by wind, I expect the first scout schools to show around the Block — probably off the rips at Southeast Light or out past North Rip — sometime in the next few days. When they do show, small Deadly Dick or a 7/8-ounce Kastmaster, fished on the fastest retrieve you can manage on light spinning gear, is exactly what you want rigged and ready.

Bluefish have been the reliable chaos machine everywhere else on the coast, and while we haven't had the same beach-blitz scenario Galilee sometimes sees, there's been solid blue activity mixed in around the bunker schools working the mouth of the Harbor of Refuge and along the breachway. If you're marking bunker on the sounder, there are blues under them, and a wire leader will save you a lot of bucktails.

Tide-wise, we're working with a moon that's keeping the current honest — good push through the Ledge, good drift speed for the fluke game, and that slack window at SW Ledge staying predictable enough to plan around. My read for the coming week: keep the daytime hours for fluke and sea bass, shift the bass program to first light and last light at the Ledge, and keep your eyes on the water off Sandy Point and Clay Head for that first real albie flash. If the wind stays out of the south-southwest and this warm stretch holds, I wouldn't be surprised if we're writing about confirmed bonito by next week's column. Until then, work the tides you know and don't burn daylight hours chasing a bite that's moved to the dark.
