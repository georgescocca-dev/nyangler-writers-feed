# Bay water's cooking — fluke slide to the inlet as new moon springs build
*Jamaica Bay's flats are too warm to hold fish, but the inlet channels and the Bridge current are picking up the slack.*

**BROAD CHANNEL, NY — July 8** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay sat under that thick July haze — light southwest air, barely a ripple most afternoons, and the flats cooking in the sun. That kind of stagnant, warm-water stretch is exactly what pushes fluke off the shallow bay bottom, and that's what happened. The moon's coming off full and sliding toward new on the 11th, which means the tides have been slack and unremarkable the last several days — small swings, weak current, water sitting instead of moving. That's the setup that lets bay water climb into the mid-to-upper 70s on the flats, and fluke don't want any part of that once it gets much past 75. They've been sliding toward the channels, the inlet, and anywhere there's a little more exchange and a little less heat.

This week changes that picture. New moon lands July 11, and that means building spring tides right through the weekend — bigger highs, lower lows, and a lot more current pushing through Rockaway Inlet and under the Marine Parkway Bridge. Bigger tide swings flush the bay harder, pull cooler, cleaner ocean water in on the flood, and get everything moving again. I'm watching Friday through Monday, the 10th through the 13th, as the best stretch of the month so far — that's when the evening flood starts stacking up right around sunset, and that's prime time at the Bridge.

Fluke has been the real story, but it's a moved story. The bay flats — Grassy Bay, the flats off Ruffle Bar, the stuff that's usually money in June — have gone quiet as the water's warmed. The fish that made the trip didn't disappear, they relocated to the inlet channel and the deeper cuts around Breezy Point and the Rockaway Inlet buoys, where there's current and cooler water pushing in off the ocean side. I've been dragging white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails through 18 to 25 feet in the inlet channel on the outgoing, tipping with a strip of squid, and connecting with keeper-class fish in the 18 to 21-inch range, with the occasional better one mixed in — nothing like that 12-plus-pounder they weighed in at Bayonne a couple weeks back, but solid, legitimate fluke fishing if you're willing to leave the flats behind. The bite's been best on the first two hours of the outgoing, when the current is still building and dragging bait out of the bay past the bridge pilings.

Striped bass have been a dusk-and-dawn proposition right now — daytime heat has them tucked deep and sluggish, but the Marine Parkway Bridge is still doing its thing on the outgoing tide, especially the last two hours before low. Bunker chunks fished on the bottom near the pilings have been picking up schoolies and the occasional mid-20-inch fish, and guys throwing swimming plugs and paddletails through the current seams after dark have had better luck than the bait guys some nights. With the new moon building the tide swings this week, I expect that bridge bite to sharpen up — more water moving means more bait getting pushed through the current lanes, and bass stack up right where that funnel narrows.

Bluefish have been scattered but present. Rockaway Beach surf has had pockets of activity early and late in the day when bunker schools push close to the beach — metal like a Deadly Dick or a Kastmaster worked fast through the wash has been the ticket when the blues show, though it's not an every-day thing yet. Inside the bay, Canarsie Pier has had a decent run of snapper blues for anyone with a light spinning rod and small Kastmasters or spearing — good action for kids and a nice change of pace, even if nobody's filling a cooler with them.

Porgies are around Cross Bay Bridge and the humps off Beach Channel in modest numbers — nothing spectacular, but a bucket of bait, some sandworms or clam, and a couple hours on the bottom during the tide change will put a mess of them in the cooler. Weakfish, on the other hand, have basically been a non-event. Water's warm enough on paper, but the reports just aren't there this year — same story up and down the coast. I wouldn't plan a trip around them right now.

Looking ahead, I'd put my time at the Marine Parkway Bridge on the outgoing this weekend as the new moon builds those spring tides — Friday through Monday evening, right as the flood stacks up with sunset, is where I'd expect the bass bite to firm up. If that current gets moving the way I think it will, the inlet fluke bite should get a boost too, with cleaner ocean water pushing further into the channel on the flood. If the bass don't cooperate, I've got the fluke drift in the inlet as my backup — either way, the bay's telling you plainly where the fish went. Follow the current, not the flats, until this heat breaks.
