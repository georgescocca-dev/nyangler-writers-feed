# Springs building toward the new moon — bass setting up on the Stratford Shoal rip
*Fluke slide out to the inlets, bluefish chop bunker off Charles Island, and the first PM flood window in weeks opens this weekend.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — July 8** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

Last week the Sound sat under a light southwest breeze most days — nothing that tore the water up, but enough to keep a chop running through the afternoons and push surface temps up another notch. That's typical for early July here: the middle Sound narrows between Port Jeff and the Connecticut shore and holds heat once it arrives, and by the weekend the harbor thermometers around Oyster Bay and Norwalk were reading properly summer-warm. The moon's been waning toward new, which this week turns into building spring tides — bigger swings, more current through the pinch points, and that's the piece that matters most for how I'm going to fish the next seven days.

We're heading into new moon on the 13th, and the springs build hardest Friday through Monday. That's significant because the PM flood now lines up with sunset for the first time since the June full moon — the tide stage and the light are stacking instead of fighting each other. On a beat like this, where Stratford Shoal and Middle Ground Light live and die by how hard water rips over the structure, that alignment is the difference between a slow drift and a real bite window. I'd circle Friday evening through Monday evening as the best stretch to be on the water for stripers.

Speaking of stripers — the reports out of Stratford Shoal have been honest, not spectacular. Guys working bucktails and swim shads on the last two hours of the outgoing have picked at fish in the 24-30 inch range, holding tight to the boulder edges on the west side of the shoal where the current breaks and folds. Slack water there has been mostly dead; you need the rip actually moving to pull fish up off the bottom. Same story at Middle Ground — a few keeper bass came off bunker chunks fished on the bottom during the last hour of outgoing, but it's been a grind, not a blitz. With the springs building this week, that current is going to run harder and longer through both spots, and that usually concentrates bait against the rock piles better than a lazy neap tide does. I'd fish the last two hours of the ebb at Stratford and the first hour of the new flood at Middle Ground, working 5-inch white or bone Storm Shads on 3/4-ounce jig heads, bounced slow along bottom.

Bluefish have been the more reliable player. Peanut bunker have been thick around Charles Island and pushed into the coves off Norwalk, and the blues have been chopping them on the surface most mornings before the wind fills in. Poppers and metal — Deadly Dicks, half-ounce Kastmasters — have worked when you can find the surface activity, but a lot of the better fish have come on bunker chunks fished mid-column off Charles Island reef, especially on the first two hours of the incoming when bait gets pushed up against the structure. Nothing huge, mostly 3-5 pounders, but steady enough that it's worth having a rod rigged for them any time you're out.

Fluke have done exactly what you'd expect with the water this warm — they've slid out of the shallow bay pockets and settled onto the channel edges and drop-offs near the inlets. Port Jeff Harbor mouth has been better than the inner harbor all week, with keeper-class fish coming off the 20-25 foot drop just outside the breakwater on white Gulp Swimming Mullets teamed with chartreuse bucktails, fished on the last of the outgoing when the current sweeps bait off the flats. Norwalk Islands has produced similarly — the channel between Sheffield and Copps has held fish on the drift, though you have to cover water; it's more a fifteen-drift-for-three-keepers pattern than an easy pick.

Porgies have been steady and honest, which is about all you can ask from scup in July. Sandworms and clam on high-low rigs over the rock piles at Middle Ground and around Charles Island have kept rods bent for anyone willing to anchor up and chum a little. Not glamorous, but it's a bite you can count on when the bass and bluefish go quiet mid-day.

Weakfish — I have to be straight with you here — still aren't showing. Water's plenty warm enough on paper, but that threshold alone hasn't meant anything the last few seasons, and this year is no different so far. I'm not spending tide time chasing them until someone actually puts one in a cooler.

One thing worth watching quietly: the warm-water corridor that's been pushing bonito and Spanish mackerel toward Rhode Island and eastern Long Island has widened this week as that cold wall out that way broke down. Central Sound bonito are an August event historically, not a July one, but with water this warm this early, I wouldn't be shocked if we get an early scout report from the Norwalk Islands or Charles Island in the next couple weeks. Nothing confirmed yet — just something I'm keeping an eye on.

For this week: I'd build around the Friday-through-Monday PM flood window for bass at Stratford Shoal and Middle Ground, work the bluefish early before the wind builds off Charles Island, and treat fluke as an inlet-and-channel game rather than a bay game until the water cools back down. If the wind stays manageable and the springs build the way they should, this is the best current setup we've had in weeks — worth putting a full evening into it.
