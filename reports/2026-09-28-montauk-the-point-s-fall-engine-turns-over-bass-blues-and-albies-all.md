Bunker chunks and fresh-cut bait fished on a fishfinder rig off the rocks or from an anchored boat in the Rip are also producing, particularly on the bigger bluefish, which have been running heavy and aggressive with all this bait density pushing through.

This isn't a guaranteed connection — albies are albies, they'll test your arm and your patience before they test your knots — but the volume of fish showing has been real, not a rumor.

Fluke are winding down as expected this time of year; there's still a scattered holdover bite in deeper water off the south side, but this isn't the primary play anymore and effort should be going elsewhere. Weakfish are mixed into the same current edges as the bass, though in smaller numbers, and porgies and blackfish are still available for anyone working structure in Fort Pond Bay or Turtle Cove on quieter days, with the tautog opener quality something worth watching as water temps keep sliding.

Honesty check: this is not an easy, walk-up-and-catch-anything week. The ocean-side swell has kept the exposed rock structure dangerous and, at times, unfishable on the outside, pushing a lot of the productive water into current seams that require reading the rip correctly rather than just casting at open water. The bite has been strong where anglers found the current edges and the boils — it's been quiet everywhere else. That's the honest shape of it.
