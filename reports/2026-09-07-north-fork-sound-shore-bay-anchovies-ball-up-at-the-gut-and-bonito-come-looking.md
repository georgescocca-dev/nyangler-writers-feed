# Bay Anchovies Ball Up at the Gut, and Bonito Come Looking
*Porgy stayed steady all week while the first bonito and albies showed at Plum Gut and Orient Point on tightening bait, and bass are staging for what's coming.*

**ORIENT, NY — September 7** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

There's an old trick to reading September on this stretch, and it doesn't take a boat. Stand on the rocks at Orient Point on a still morning, watch the terns. When they start working tight little circles instead of long lazy passes, that's bay anchovies getting squeezed into a ball by something underneath them. This week those circles got tight, and they got tight often — Plum Gut approach through the Orient rocks, most mornings before the wind came up. That's not a guess, that's just what the birds were telling anybody who bothered to look.

We flagged this back in late August — said this stretch sits close enough to the Gut and the Race to catch the overflow when the bonito push starts building out of Rhode Island. Here it is. Bonito and the first false albacore have shown at Plum Gut and around Orient Point, chasing those anchovy balls in classic late-summer fashion. It's not a blitz yet, more a scattered, moving thing — pods showing for ten, fifteen minutes at a crack, then sliding off with the current. But it's real, and it's the leading edge of what's typically a building pattern into mid-September.

The water's cooperating for it. Central Sound is still carrying decent heat — Kings Point up near 71, New London holding around 69 — and that west-to-east gradient means the warmer water pushing east keeps the bait comfortable and the predators interested. Out toward Montauk it's sitting close to 69 as well, which tells you the whole eastern end is still in that transitional late-summer window rather than a hard fall turnover. Give it another week or two of cooling nights and that changes fast.

The moon's been full the past couple nights, which means good strong tides through the Gut and around Plum Island — that's exactly the kind of current that concentrates bait against structure and gives predators a clean shot at it. If you're going to fish the albies or bonito, the last two hours of the ebb through the Gut, when the current is really moving water past Plum Island, has been the more reliable window than the flood. Small profile is the whole game — 1-2 ounce Deadly Dick or a green machine on light wire, cast into the boil edges rather than the middle of it, and don't stop the retrieve. These fish don't forgive hesitation.

Striped bass are still mostly a staging story rather than a catching story right now. Bunker schools have been tightening up from the western Sound down through Raritan way, and the fish are starting to show real interest instead of just picking at stragglers — that's the pattern building toward the season's first legitimate blitz activity, and Horton Point and Rocky Point are exactly the kind of structure that lights that up when it arrives here. It hasn't fully arrived yet on this stretch. What's been working is smaller stuff — bass in the mid-20s to low 30s taken on bucktails, half-ounce to an ounce, white or bone, worked slow along the drop at Horton Point on the last of the ebb, and a few better fish coming off live eels fished after dark off Rocky Point. Nothing to write home about, but steady enough to be worth the trip if you're patient about it.

Bluefish have been the more dependable customer. They've been running with the bunker pods off Mattituck Inlet and drifting east, and they'll hit just about anything moving fast — poppers, metal, bucktails, doesn't much matter. Fish in the 4-8 pound range, with the occasional bigger one mixed in, chopping bait on the surface in the early hours. If you want a guaranteed bend in the rod this week, that's your best bet.

Porgy fishing has been the quiet workhorse all season and this week was no different — bottom rigs with squid strips or clam, fished on the structure off Horton Point and around the Mattituck breakwater, still producing steady numbers of scup in the half-pound to pound-plus class. It's not glamorous but it's honest fishing, and on a day when the albies won't cooperate and the bass are ghosting you, dropping bait on the porgy grounds will save the trip. Blackfish season isn't quite in gear yet on this stretch, but the rockpiles off Rocky Point are worth keeping in mind as the water cools further into October. Weakfish have been scarce, which tracks with the pattern most of the season — the odd fish here and there on soft plastics worked slow near the inlet mouths, but nothing to plan a trip around.

Looking ahead, the next few days should hold mostly steady — moderate winds, tide still strong off the full moon, water temps easing a degree or two as nights cool down. That's the setup for the bonito and albie action to keep building rather than fade, and for the bass to keep tightening up on bait without quite breaking into a full blitz just yet. If you've only got one morning, work the Gut on the back half of the ebb for the albies and bonito, then swing over to Horton Point structure for bass and porgy as the tide slacks. The real fireworks on stripers are probably still a week or so off, once the nights cool further and that bunker gets pinned tight against the shoreline — but the anchovy-ball pattern at the Gut is worth checking every clear morning between now and then.
