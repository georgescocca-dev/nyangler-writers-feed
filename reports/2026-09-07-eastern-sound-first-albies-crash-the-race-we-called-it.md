# First Albies Crash The Race — We Called It
*False albacore finally show in the rips off Fishers Island as bunker schools tighten under the full moon and bass start staging for the fall run.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — September 7** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The first false albacore of the season showed up in the rip off Fishers Island Sound this week, and the tackle shops from Orient to New London with the news fast. Small pods, nothing like the November blitzes yet, but they were there — busting on bay anchovies and rain bait in the fast water off the Race and pushing into the eastern end of Fishers Island Sound on the last two hours of the ebb. We flagged this zone back on August 31 as the spot to watch for the season's first bonito and albies on the New York side of the Sound, and here they are, right on schedule. That's the headline, but it's not the whole story this week.

The bigger picture is bait. Bunker schools are stacking tight against the reefs from Plum Gut down through Orient Point, and that's pulling everything with them — bass, blues, and now the first speedsters of fall. Water temps are still holding warmer than you'd expect this deep into the calendar — New London reading 69.4, Montauk at 69.1 — which means the thermal window for albies and bonito to keep pushing west along the Sound stays open. Expect that pattern to build over the next week, not fade.

Striped bass are in the staging phase right now, not the blitz phase. The full moon on the 6th and 7th tightened bunker schools around The Race and Plum Gut, and bass are shadowing them, but the feeding is still more opportunistic than explosive — a few fish picking off stragglers at first light rather than sustained surface chaos. That changes fast once the new moon hits September 10. Bigger tides, harder current, tighter bait — that's usually the trigger for the first real blitz of the fall, and with bunker already balled up from Norwalk clear through the eastern Sound, the pieces are in place. If there's one window to circle on the calendar this month, it's the four or five days bracketing that new moon.

For now, the most consistent action on bass is coming off the ebb at Plum Gut and the western edge of The Race, working 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of pork rind or a swim shad trailer, dropped to the bottom on the edge of the rip and worked with a slow, rhythmic hop back up through the current seam. Fish are holding deep in the eddies where the water slows on the inside of the main flow — not out in the screaming middle of the Gut where the current's ripping at 4-5 knots. Early morning and the last hour of daylight are still the windows; midday sun with bright, high water has been a dead zone.

Bluefish are the one species that hasn't slowed down at all. Jumbo blues are working the bunker schools hard around the reefs off Orient Point and out toward Fishers Island, and they're not subtle about it — surface explosions visible from a half mile out. Poppers and metal-lip swimmers worked fast through the froth are drawing violent strikes, and bunker chunks fished on the bottom near the reef edges on wire leader are picking up fish in the 8 to 12-pound class with some bigger ones mixed in. If you want a guaranteed bend in the rod right now, this is it — blues are the most reliable game in the zone.

Fluke are winding down but haven't shut off completely. The deeper holes off Plum Gut and the flats around Orient Point are still giving up keeper-class fish on white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift in 25 to 35 feet. Expect this bite to keep tapering hard through the rest of September as water starts its seasonal slide and fish push toward the deeper basins ahead of their offshore run. Porgies remain a solid backup in the same depths on bloodworms or squid strips fished tight to bottom structure, and blackfish season isn't open yet in New York waters, but the scouting reports on wrecks and boulder fields around Fishers Island are already starting — worth marking spots now for when the season flips.

Looking ahead, the next several days are about watching that new moon build. If the bunker schools stay tight against the reefs through the weekend and the current keeps running hard through the Gut and the Race, the first legitimate fall blitz on bass should be close behind the albies — probably within the next seven to ten days as nights keep cooling. Best case, that means surface chaos at dawn tides off Plum Gut and Orient Point with bass, blues, and albies all mixed into the same bait. Worst case, the bait scatters west with the tide push and the timing slides a few days. Either way, this is the stretch of September where the zone earns its reputation — worth being on the water for the early tide, not the late one.
