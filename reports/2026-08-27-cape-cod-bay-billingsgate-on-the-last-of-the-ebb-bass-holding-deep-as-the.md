# Billingsgate on the last of the ebb: bass holding deep as the moon builds
*Warm surface water has the bay's stripers pinned to structure and night tides, while bluefish keep the rods bent through the heat of the day.*

**PROVINCETOWN, MA — August 27** — _by Donny Costa, Zone Writer_

The fleet working Billingsgate and the deeper humps off Race Point this week has settled into a pattern that's become familiar this time of year — bass sliding down off the sand and holding tight to bottom structure through the heat of the day, then coming up to feed hard on the moving tides around first light and last light. Surface water in the bay has stayed stubbornly warm through most of August, and that's pushed the bigger fish into a late-summer holding mode rather than anything resembling a migration push. It's not a bad bite. It's a specific bite, and knowing when and where to show up matters more right now than it did in June.

We flagged this pattern building last week — said the moon coming up on full August 28 would strengthen the current through the rips and tighten the bait concentrations, and that's exactly what's setting up now. The tide is running harder through Peaked Hill Bars and off the Billingsgate edge than it was two weeks ago, and where there's harder current there's more bait getting funneled and more bass willing to commit. The last two hours of the ebb off Billingsgate has been the standout window, with fish holding in 18 to 25 feet on the drop where the shoal falls into deeper water. Live mackerel on a light fish-finder rig, or a chunk of the same fished slow along bottom, has been the most consistent producer for fish in the 20- to 34-inch range, with a scattering of bigger fish pushing past 36 inches coming on the same presentation worked right at the base of the drop as the tide starts to slack.

Race Point itself has been more of a dawn program. The rip there sets up hardest on the early stages of the incoming, and boats working live mackerel or trolling large bunker-colored umbrella rigs through the moving water have connected with better-grade fish holding just off the main current seam, generally in 20 to 35 feet. Once the sun gets up and the tide slows, the bite there shuts down fast — this is a tide-window fishery right now, not an all-day proposition, and anyone planning a trip around midday slack is going to be disappointed.

Bluefish have been the one constant nobody's complaining about. Snapper blues are thick in Provincetown Harbor and up into Barnstable's inner reaches, keeping light-tackle anglers and kids on the docks busy on small Kastmasters and Gulp shrimp under a float. The bigger cocktail blues, fish in the 3- to 6-pound class, are mixing right in with the striper catches on the rips and on the troll — anyone dragging a spoon or a small tube-and-worm rig through Peaked Hill Bars this week has had to fight through blues to get to bass, which is not the worst problem to have in late August.

Fluke have been the soft spot in the mix, and it's the same story playing out from here down through the mid-Atlantic — warm, thin water has the fish spread out across the flats instead of holding tight, and that's softened catch rates everywhere except the channel edges and deeper structure where a cooler bottom layer still holds. Barnstable Harbor's main channel and the drop-offs along Sandy Neck have kept producing keeper fish to 4 pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with squid strips, fished on 3/4-ounce bucktails dragged slow along the bottom on the last of the outgoing. Anyone working the open flats in the middle of the bay this week has mostly found short fish and a lot of nothing.

Cod are still a deep-water proposition and it's early yet for the real showing — that pattern typically doesn't build until the water cools further into fall — but there's been a scattered presence on the deeper structure off Race Point and along the edges of the Bars, fish in the 20-inch class coming up on diamond jigs worked slow along bottom in 60 to 80 feet during the slower tide stages. Worth a stop if you're already out there working the rip, not worth a dedicated trip yet.

Looking at the next several days, expect the pattern to hold and likely sharpen. The moon hits full on the 28th, which means bigger tidal swings and stronger current through all the rip structure — Billingsgate, Race Point, and Peaked Hill Bars should all fish a little harder on both the ebb and the flood as a result. That's good news for anyone working the tide-window program, less good news for anyone hoping for an all-day bite. Southwest winds in the 8- to 12-knot range should keep conditions comfortable for drifting through the weekend, with water color staying clean along the outer structure. The real change everyone's watching for — the first hint of falling nighttime temperatures that starts nudging bass into more aggressive daytime feeding — probably doesn't show itself for another two to three weeks. Until then, the smart play is the same one that's been working: fish the moving water, work the drop-offs and rip edges, and don't waste good tide on the flats.
