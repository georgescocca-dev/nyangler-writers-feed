# Double Creek Fluke Crowd the Inlet While Snapper Blues Bulk Up at Tices Shoal
*The bay's gone thick and warm upstairs, so the good fish are pushing down toward the moving water — and the bait's telling on them.*

**FORKED RIVER, NJ — August 17** — _by Gus Papadakis, Zone Writer_

Forty-some Augusts ago I was still a kid mating on a Point Pleasant boat, and there was an old captain named Ernie who used to tell me the dog days turn fluke into a different animal. "They ain't hunting no more, Gus," he'd say, working a cigarette out of the corner of his mouth while we idled over a lump off the inlet. "They're laying in the current with their mouths open, waiting for something to fall on their head." I didn't get it then. I get it now, every single August, and this one is no exception.

The upper bay has gone flat and warm — I'd put it high 70s pushing 80 in the skinny water up around Toms River, and fish don't want to be in that stew if they don't have to. What's happened, same as every summer this stretch, is the fluke have pulled off the mud basins and pushed toward anywhere the tide keeps moving and the water stays a few degrees honest. That means Barnegat Inlet and Double Creek Channel right now are doing the heavy lifting for this whole zone.

I've had three trips through Double Creek this past week working the deeper bends on the last two hours of the outgoing, and the pattern's been consistent — not a banner bite, but a real one. Four-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets in white or pearl on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged dead slow along the channel edges in 12 to 16 feet, have been outproducing anything finesse-sized. Ernie was right — these fish are lazy right now, and a bigger profile gets bit when a small one gets ignored. Best fish of the week went 6 pounds off the elbow where Double Creek dumps toward the ICW, taken on the last of the outgoing with the current really ripping. Most days it's two to four keepers mixed with a pile of shorts, which for mid-August in this bay is honest work and I won't oversell it as more.

The inlet itself, right through the Coast Guard station and out toward the rocks at Barnegat Light, has been holding fluke tight to structure — the same story you're hearing up and down this whole stretch of shore right now. Bigger baits, slower presentations, fish sitting in the current seams waiting on the tide to bring dinner to them instead of chasing it down. If you're drifting the inlet, time it to the last of the incoming into the top of the tide — that's when the bait gets pushed hard against the rocks and the fluke stack up on the downcurrent side.

Bluefish have been the most dependable thing in this bay for two solid months now, and I'll say it plain — we called this one. Back in the middle of the month the snapper blues in these back creeks and river mouths were still pencil-thin, and the read then was that they'd bulk up fast through late August. Well, here we are. I was up at Tices Shoal and around the mouth of Oyster Creek this week and those snappers have put on some size — plenty of them pushing 12 to 14 inches now, and mixed in with them are adult blues in the 3 to 5 pound class that weren't there two weeks ago. Small strips of bunker or a silver spoon behind a light wire leader is all you need. They're aggressive, they're everywhere in the back bay right now, and if you've got kids or a slow afternoon, this is your bite.

Striped bass are around but they're not making it easy. With the bay running warm the way it is, the bass have compressed hard into dawn and dusk, same as everywhere south of the Canal this summer. I've had my best luck working the rocks at Barnegat Light right at first light, and along the deeper edge of the Double Creek Channel right at last light, on bunker chunks fished on the bottom with just enough weight to hold in the current. Nothing to write home about in numbers, but the fish that are coming are decent — mostly slot-range, a few over. Don't expect much from the middle of the day. The water's too warm and they know it.

Blowfish and kingfish are both doing their usual late-summer thing in the sod bank creeks and the sandier stretches down toward Island Beach. Blowfish on small hooks with clam or squid strips in three to six feet of water off the marsh edges near Double Creek have been steady for anybody patient enough to sit on them. Kingfish are showing in the surf and inside the inlet on sandy bottom, bloodworms or Fishbites on a simple high-low rig doing the job. Weakfish, if you want the honest truth, are mostly a memory of May right now — I'm hearing the occasional one taken after dark around the lights at Barnegat Light, but that's a bonus fish, not a target this time of year.

We're building toward the full moon on the 28th, and that extra tidal push should sharpen up the current-driven spots — Double Creek, the inlet mouth, the rips off Barnegat Light — right when we need it, as the upper bay stays stuck in this warm holding pattern. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd take the last two hours of the outgoing through Double Creek Channel for fluke, then slide out to the inlet rocks for the last light bass bite. That's where I'm putting my time, and where I'd tell any of you to start.
