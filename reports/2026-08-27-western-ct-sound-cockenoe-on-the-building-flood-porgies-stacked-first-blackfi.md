# Cockenoe on the Building Flood: Porgies Stacked, First Blackfish Show on the Cows
*The moon goes full tomorrow night and the current is already sharpening up the reef bite from Greenwich to Bridgeport.*

**NORWALK, CT — August 27** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Tomorrow night's full moon is the whole story for the next 72 hours on this stretch of the Sound, and the smart play right now is picking your reef around how much current you want to fight rather than how much you want to sit still. Cockenoe Reef and the rockpile off Sheffield Island are drawing the bulk of the porgy and blackfish talk this week because the building tide is finally giving those spots the push they've been missing through the flat, sticky stretch of August. We flagged this last week — said the current toward the full moon should sharpen the bite around the reef structure — and that's exactly what's playing out as the swings get bigger through the weekend.

Water's still warm for the calendar. Kings Point is reading 70.9, and that west-end number matters for this zone because Greenwich to Bridgeport sits right in that warmer pocket compared to the cooler water working its way in from the east end of the Sound. That temperature is what's keeping this fishery locked into its late-August personality — a porgy-and-snapper-blue program during the day, with striped bass activity mostly pushed to structure at night or into the last two hours of ebb when there's a current edge and some shade. Daytime bass on the reefs have been a grind. Nobody's saying otherwise. The fish are there, they're just not eager to chase in 70-degree water with the sun straight overhead.

Penfield Reef is fishing the classic late-summer split. On the last two hours of the ebb, working the deeper edge off the lighthouse in 18 to 25 feet, blackfish are starting to show — not thick yet, but enough 2-to-4-pounders on green crab and Asian crab to tell you the pattern is turning over a few weeks early. That's worth watching into September. The porgy program on Penfield is more reliable right now: sandworms and clam strips on the high-low rig, fishing bottom in that same 18-to-25 foot band on the tail of the flood, with fish running a half-pound to a pound and a half, occasionally better. Boat traffic on Penfield gets thick on weekend mornings with the marina crowd running out of Black Rock and Fairfield, so the better window there has been first light through mid-morning before the pleasure boats stack up on top of the structure.

The Cows, off Stamford, have been the most consistent porgy spot in the zone all week. Anchoring up-current of the rockpile on the last of the ebb and first hour of the new flood, with clam on a two-hook bottom rig, has been putting good numbers of porgies in the box, mixed with cocktail blues that are chasing the same bait balls. Those blues have been a dependable presence everywhere from the Cows to Greenwich Point — snapper-sized fish thick in the harbors and coves, with the occasional 2-to-3-pound cocktail mixing into the porgy catches out on the reef itself. If you want bluefish action for kids or for numbers, small metal or a bucktail teaser above the porgy rig will get bit constantly right now.

Striped bass are the harder conversation. The daytime bite on Cockenoe and the Norwalk Islands rockpiles has been soft, which lines up with what's happening across the whole region — warm surface water is pushing bass activity to the edges of the day. The better program has been working the Norwalk Islands and the rocks off Sheffield Island after dark, drifting or slow-trolling live bait — peanut bunker or a chunk on a fishfinder rig — tight to the structure on the last two hours of the ebb when the current is pulling bait off the rocks into deeper water. Fish have been mostly schoolie to mid-20s class, with the occasional better fish mixed in on nights with a strong tide push. Nobody's stumbling onto slot-and-up numbers consistently yet, and that's expected — the real fall push doesn't usually show here until nighttime temperatures start knocking the surface water down in earnest, likely mid-September.

Fluke have been the toughest read of the summer. Warm, thin water across the flats has spread the fish out instead of concentrating them, and that's held true here — the channel edges around the Norwalk Islands and the deeper drop-offs near Sheffield Island have been holding a cooler bottom layer and are outfishing the open flats by a wide margin. Gulp or squid strips on a bucktail, worked slow along the edge where the depth breaks from 12 to 20 feet, has been the more productive approach than drifting the shallow flats blind.

Looking ahead, the full moon tomorrow night is going to keep building the tidal swings through the weekend, and that should keep sharpening the reef bite on Cockenoe, Penfield, and the Cows — bigger current means more bait pushed off structure, and that's when the porgies and blues really pile up. If the pattern holds and overnight temperatures start trending down even slightly next week, expect the first real crack in the daytime striper lull. Until then, play the tide windows tight, fish the last two hours of the ebb on the deeper reef edges, and save the open flats for another few weeks.
