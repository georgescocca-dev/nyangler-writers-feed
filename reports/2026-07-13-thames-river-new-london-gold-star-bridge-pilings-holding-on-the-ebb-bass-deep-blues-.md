# Gold Star Bridge Pilings Holding on the Ebb: Bass Deep, Blues Working the Surface
*Cooler water pouring out of the Thames is stacking bait against the bridge structure, and the stripers haven't left the neighborhood.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — July 13** — _by Marcus Webb, Zone Writer_

There's a spot about two hundred yards downriver of the Gold Star Bridge, on the Groton side, where the current pinches between the third and fourth pilings and forms a seam you can see just by watching the surface — a thin ribbon of slick water running against a wall of standing riffle. I've fished that seam for twenty years and it still tells me more than any app on my phone. This week it's been loaded with peanut bunker getting pushed and turned by the tide, and there are bass sitting right on the downcurrent edge of it, 18 to 22 feet down, waiting for that bait to get disorganized.

The water coming out of the Thames right now is running cooler than what they're seeing over in the western Sound — we're reading in the mid-60s up here compared to low 70s down toward Kings Point — and that gradient matters more than people think. It means the bigger fish that pushed west into the warmer water aren't necessarily our fish. What we've got right now up around New London and Groton skews toward resident and staging bass, mostly schoolies up through low-slot, holding tight to structure rather than roaming open water chasing bait balls the way they will once things warm through the month.

The pattern that's produced for me and for the guys I talk to at the ramp has been simple: fish the last two hours of the ebb at the bridge pilings, when that current seam is fully formed and pushing hard, then shift down to Fort Trumbull as the tide goes slack. A 1-ounce white bucktail with a small strip of pork rind or a 4-inch white paddletail trailer, bounced right off the base of the pilings and let it swing through the seam — that's been the money presentation. Don't overwork it. Let the current do the job and just keep contact. Guys throwing swimming plugs, Doc's Goggle-Eyes and Yo-Zuri Mag Darters in bunker patterns, have been picking off blues on top in the same stretch, especially in that last hour of daylight when the bunker pods get nervous and start skipping on the surface off the Groton shore.

Fort Trumbull is still doing what it always does on the tail end of the ebb — that rip off the point where the river current collides with the incoming harbor swell builds a defined edge, usually 12 to 15 feet, and it's held bass consistently through the week, though I'd call the action mixed rather than steady. Some tides you hit it right and get six or eight fish in an hour, mostly 22 to 26 inches, and other tides you stand there for ninety minutes and manage two. The difference has been wind. When we get any south or southwest breeze pushing against that ebb, the rip gets a chop on it and the bite goes quiet. Calm evenings, it's been dependable.

Down at Ocean Beach and Pleasure Beach, the porgy fishing has actually been the more consistent story this week. The rock piles off Pleasure Beach in 15 to 20 feet have porgies stacked up, and sandworms or green crab on a simple high-low rig has been putting fish in the cooler steadily on the incoming tide, when clean water pushes back in off the Sound. Nothing huge — most going 9 to 11 inches — but for a guy who wants a bend in the rod and dinner in the bucket, it's about as sure a thing as we've got on this stretch right now.

The hickory shad run up through the Thames toward the Mohegan stretch has pretty much wound down for the season — that window was April into May, and by mid-July those fish have scattered or moved on. If you're still swinging small flies or shad darts up there hoping for one more push, I wouldn't hold my breath. And blackfish, obviously, are still closed this time of year in Connecticut waters, so leave the crab pots in the garage a little longer.

Looking ahead, I'd keep my expectations honest rather than excited. This isn't a blow-it-up week — it's a work-the-structure week. If the wind stays out of the north or lays down entirely through the weekend, I'd put my time on the last two hours of ebb at the Gold Star Bridge pilings and then slide down to Trumbull as it goes slack, betting that the cooler water holding here keeps concentrating bait against structure rather than letting it spread. If we get a stretch of south wind chopping up that rip, I'd skip the point entirely and just spend the tide on the porgies off Pleasure Beach — steady beats stubborn most weeks on this river.
