# Niantic Fleet Split: Sea Bass Carrying Black Point While Fluke Scatter in the Warm Water
*The moon builds to full Friday and the fleet is betting the current comes back strong enough to save a soft fluke bite and wake the bass up at night.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 27** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Talk at the counter this week has been mostly about what's not happening rather than what is. A couple of the Waterford captains have been saying the same thing separately, which tells you something — the fluke have quit stacking up the way they were in July. Water's still warm, New London reading in the high 60s and pushing 70 in spots, and that's just enough heat to spread the fluke out across the flats instead of holding them tight to structure where the boats can work them. The better fish are still there, they're just harder to find, and the fleet has adjusted by leaning hard into Black Point and the deeper edges off Twotree Island Channel where a cooler bottom layer holds up even when the surface is soup-warm.

The good news is sea bass have picked up a lot of the slack. Boats working the rock piles off Black Point and out toward Hole-in-the-Wall have been putting together respectable sea bass counts on Gulp squid strips and clam-tipped bucktails, fished tight to structure in 30 to 45 feet. That's been the more dependable half of the mixed bag this week — steady enough that a few skippers have started calling it before they even leave the dock, telling anglers to bring the heavier jigging setups because the sea bass are the play in the middle of the day while the fluke bite windows shrink down to first light and the last two hours of the outgoing.

Striped bass are exactly where you'd expect them for the tail end of August — not gone, just quiet during the day. This is the predictable late-summer holding pattern, not the fall push, and the smart money in the fleet has moved to structure and low-light programs rather than daytime blind-casting. We flagged this pattern building last week — said the current would tighten up and concentrate bait as the moon swung toward full on the 28th — and that's exactly the setup coming into play right now. The moon builds to full Friday, and that means bigger tidal swings starting to stack through Niantic Bay and around McCook Point. That extra push of water should sharpen up the current seams the bass like to sit on, particularly on the last of the ebb out of the Niantic River where warm water dumps out into cooler bay water and stacks bait against the edge. Anglers working live eels or bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs after dark around the river mouth and the bay-side rock structure have had the better shots at keeper bass, with a few overslot fish mixed in on the darker nights.

Bluefish have been the one constant nobody's arguing about. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay off the Niantic River, and cocktail blues in the two-to-four-pound class are mixing into both the fluke drifts and the striper spots, especially anywhere there's bait getting pushed around by current. If the fluke bite goes cold on a drift, it's not unusual for the rod to bend anyway courtesy of a blue jumping on a Gulp mullet meant for something else. Not glamorous, but it's kept a lot of otherwise slow mornings from being total blanks.

Porgies remain the honest bottom fishery this zone leans on, and that hasn't changed. Boats anchoring up on the rubble and mussel beds around Black Point and off McCook Point are still filling coolers on standard porgy rigs with squid and clam, fishing the bottom third of the water column on the slower stages of the tide. Worth watching over the next couple weeks — as the season starts to turn, this is usually when scup fishing gets even better before the first cold nights push everybody's attention toward blackfish season.

One more thing worth keeping an eye on for the guys who run farther east: the water off the Race and toward Fishers Island has historically been one of the earlier tells for bonito and false albacore showing up in Connecticut waters, and the current there is strong enough to hold bait tight even through a warm stretch like this. Nothing confirmed yet in this zone, but with squid already building around Rhode Island and the Cape, it's not a bad idea to keep a light spinning rod rigged with a small metal in the rocket launcher just in case something silver shows up chasing bait through the rip.

Looking at the next few days, expect the pattern to hold close to what it's been — southwest breeze in the 8 to 12 knot range, warm surface water, and fluke that reward patience over volume. The building moon into Friday should be the difference-maker for striper anglers willing to fish the dark hours around the river mouth and bay structure, and it should also sharpen the fluke bite windows around the tide changes even if it doesn't fix the spread-out pattern completely. If there's only one window to fish this weekend, the last two hours of the outgoing around McCook Point or Twotree Island Channel, right as the moon tips full, is the one worth building the day around. Keep the sea bass gear rigged as the backup plan — right now, it's the fishery not letting anybody down.
