# 120 feet on the Stellwagen gravel: cod chewing, mackerel schools pulling bluefin up top
*Steady bottom bite on the Bank this week, and the first real tuna showing on the surface chasing mackerel — but you have to find the birds first.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — August 7** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

128 feet of water on the northeast corner of Stellwagen, gravel bottom, and my sounder was stacked with marks sitting right on the bottom third — that's the read I've been getting all week and it's held up. Cod are there, they're just not stupid about it. You put a clam bait or a diamond jig with a teaser dropper down through that column and you'll know within the first ten feet off bottom whether they're eating.

We've been working the edge where the gravel transitions into softer bottom, that seam that runs along the northeast face toward Tillies. Cod to 15 pounds, most fish running 6 to 10, and they want bait moving — jigging a 12-ounce diamond jig with a red tube teaser well off bottom, or drifting whole clams on a high-low rig if the current's slack enough to hold bottom without a ton of lead. When the tide's ripping over the Bank, which it has been on this cycle, you need 16 to 20 ounces just to feel the bottom, and that's when the bite can shut down fast — the fish don't want to chase in hard water. Best windows have been the last two hours of the ebb into the first hour of new tide, when things settle down enough for a clean drift.

Haddock have been more scattered than I'd like for early August, but where we've found them it's been good — soft mud pockets in 140 to 160 feet on the western side of the Bank, that classic mud-line country south of the Basin. These fish want a smaller hook, size 6 or 8, tipped with clam, fished right on bottom with minimal weight so the bait sits still instead of dragging. When you find a pod of haddock they're usually tight together, and you can load the box in twenty minutes and then it goes quiet — move the boat 200 yards and start over. That's been the pattern all week: find them, hammer them, they go quiet, you pick up and hunt again.

Now the fun part. Bluefin have shown up on the Bank chasing mackerel, and that's exactly what you want to see this time of year. Warm water's been pushing north and it's dragging bait with it — mackerel schools have been thick on the surface over the southwest corner and out toward Cape Cod Tails, and where you find the mac balls getting nervous, birds diving, whales working the same bait, that's where the bluefin have been showing. I had a morning last week where we ran up on a school of maybe forty fish busting mackerel on top, all in the 80 to 150 pound class from what we could see rolling — didn't stick one, but we had shots. Green machines and small poppers thrown right into the boil have drawn strikes, and if you can get a bait pinned or a spreader bar through the middle of a busting school, you've got a real shot.

The key with these fish is patience and not running the school over. They'll show, sound, and pop up again a quarter mile off — chase them and you'll blow the whole pod down. Better to set up ahead of where you think they're moving and let them come to you. Whale activity has been a good tell all week — humpbacks and finbacks working the same bait balls as the tuna, so if you see whale spouts and diving birds together, that's worth a long look through the glasses before you commit fuel to it.

Weather's been giving us a stable window — light southwest winds, seas laying down enough to fish comfortably out on the Bank and even push out toward Tillies without getting beat up. That's the kind of stretch you want to take advantage of before anything changes, because we're deep enough into hurricane season now that any tropical development needs watching. If the pattern holds through the weekend, I'd be splitting time — work the gravel early for cod while the tide allows a clean drift, then run out looking for mackerel schools and birds once the sun's up and the surface bite has a chance to show itself.

One thing I'll say plainly: the haddock bite isn't wide open right now. You have to hunt for it, and some drifts you'll mark fish and get nothing, other drifts you'll load up. That's just where we are in the season on the mud — it comes and goes in pockets rather than a blanket bite across the Bank. Cod on the gravel has been more consistent, which is a nice change from earlier in the summer when everything felt scattered.

Looking ahead, if this stable weather holds another few days I expect the mackerel schools to keep building on the Bank, which means more shots at bluefin showing on top. Keep an eye on the whale activity — where the big boys are working, the tuna usually aren't far off. I'd rather run out on a calm morning and spend an hour glassing for birds and spouts than blind-troll the whole Bank hoping to bump into fish. Bring the jigging rods regardless — even on a slow tuna morning, that gravel edge in 120 to 130 feet has been good enough to fill a cooler with cod while you wait for the surface to light up with bait.
