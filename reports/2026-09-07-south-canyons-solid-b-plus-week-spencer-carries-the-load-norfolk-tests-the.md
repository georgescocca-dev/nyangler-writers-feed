# Solid B-Plus Week: Spencer Carries the Load, Norfolk Tests the Fuel Budget
*The warm filament held its ground all week, and the canyon that got hit mattered more than the canyon you wanted to hit.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 7** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Grade for the week across the southern run: solid B-plus, with an asterisk for anybody who burned diesel getting to Norfolk on the wrong day. Spencer and Lindenkohl did the heavy lifting, Poor Man's stayed stubborn and inconsistent through the middle of the week, and Norfolk rewarded the boats that had the range and the patience to make the long ride pay off. That's the story of early September down here — it's never one canyon carrying the whole fleet, it's whoever's closest to where the warm water decided to park itself that day.

Here's the why. The Gulf Stream filament pushing water into this stretch has held its position better than it usually does this time of year, and that's kept this zone running warmer than the water up north — which is exactly the setup that keeps yellowfin and mahi stacked and starts teasing the idea of an early wahoo run as we push toward peak temps in the next couple weeks. When that filament sits still instead of sliding east or getting pinched by a cold eddy, the bite doesn't just hold — it gets more predictable, and predictable is what you want when you're deciding which of four canyons to burn fuel toward at 4 a.m.

Spencer's been the go-to because the edge there has been holding a clean temperature break, and clean breaks are where bait piles up and tuna sit underneath it working the current line. The chunk bite along that edge has been the more consistent play — butterflied bunker fished on a slow bump-and-drift over the drop, with the boat working the 50 to the deeper edge depending on where the bait shows on the machine. Yellowfin have been the target and the producer, with the chunk accounting for a mixed bag that includes a few bigeye showing up after dark when the boats stay on station and keep the chunk line going through the overnight soak. Mahi have been mixed in on the troll during daylight, mostly picked up incidental to the tuna spread rather than a dedicated mahi run — small stuff around any floating structure or weed line, bigger fish scattered and harder to pattern.

Lindenkohl's played the same tune but a notch quieter — the troll has been the better bet there over the chunk, with a mixed spread of cedar plugs, green machines, and skirted ballyhoo working the temp break where the warmer canyon water meets the cooler shelf water pushing up from the bottom. That temperature seam is the whole game right now — find where it sits, usually somewhere in the transition zone off the edge in the mid-70s water, and that's where the spreads have been getting hit. Wahoo haven't shown in numbers yet, but the ingredients are there — the warmer water pushing this deep into September is exactly the kind of setup that produces an early wahoo bite once the temps peak, so any boat running a high-speed spread with wine-colored baits or a planer down the temp break should keep an eye out. It wouldn't take much for that to turn into the better story of the next two weeks.

Poor Man's has been the tough grade — closer for the Ocean City boats, but the bite there has been spotty enough that more than one crew came back with a story about seeing bait and marks on the screen without much to show for it. When it's produced, it's been white marlin on the dredge and rigged ballyhoo along the edge, worked slow with a long dredge spread and naked ballyhoo skipping just off the rigger clips. That's a finesse bite, not a numbers bite, and this week it stayed finesse.

Norfolk's the long-range gamble that's paid off for boats willing to make the run — but it's not a canyon you hit on a whim. The extra fuel and time only make sense when the intel lines up, and this week it did more than once, with the troll bite producing yellowfin on a mixed spread similar to what's working at Lindenkohl. The tradeoff is real: burn the extra hours getting there and you'd better have a weather window that holds, because Norfolk punishes anybody who gets caught out there when the wind turns.

Looking ahead, the moon builds toward new phase on September 10, and that's typically the pivot point down here — expect the pelagic bite to hold steady through the next few days provided that warm filament stays put, which right now it's showing every sign of doing. If you've only got one shot this week, Spencer's still the safer bet on the strength of that clean edge and the chunk bite that's been the most repeatable producer. Keep a Plan B toward Lindenkohl if the drift at Spencer gets crowded or the current doesn't set up right — the troll spread there has been nearly as good. Norfolk's worth the run if you've got a multi-day weather window and want to be first on fish that haven't seen a hook yet, but don't force that trip against a marginal forecast. Poor Man's needs another week to sort itself out before it's worth prioritizing over the other three.
