# 38 Pounds on the Ebb — The Race Is Doing Work Again
*Bunker pods stacked from Plum Gut to Fishers Island have big bass feeding hard on the new moon current, while the albie watch heats up east of Orient.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 14** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Three a.m., chest-deep off the Orient rocks, and a 38-pound bass nearly ripped the rod out of my hands on the third cast of a bunker-colored Danny plug. That fish came off a rip line where the current was still ripping at close to five knots even on the tail of the ebb — the kind of push you only get a couple days after a new moon. I landed her, unhooked her in the wash, and by the time I got back to the truck my hands were shaking from more than the cold water. That's the eastern Sound in mid-August when it's right.

We flagged this last week — said building tidal flow into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what's happening. The Race and Plum Gut are both running harder than they have in weeks, and the bass know it. Peanut bunker pods are getting squeezed against the rock piles and current seams, and every incoming tide is stacking predators on top of them. I've seen bass anywhere from 28 to 40 inches busting bunker on the surface at both ends of Plum Gut, especially on the last two hours of the ebb when everything funnels through that gut like water through a fire hose.

Water's holding a real gradient right now — Kings Point up around 70.9, New London sitting cooler at 69.4, Montauk close behind at 69.1. That west-to-east drop means the bait and the bass are staging tighter to structure out here than they are further up the Sound, which is exactly why The Race and the Gut are outfishing the open water. Bunker don't like getting pushed into cooler, faster water, so they bunch up hard against the rips — and that's a dinner bell for anything with teeth.

Tactics that are working: bunker spoons trolled just off the bottom through the heart of the Gut on the last of the incoming, umbrella rigs with hoses in bunker and chartreuse combos worked slow through The Race on the drift, and — if you can find surface activity — a big topwater or a 2-ounce bucktail with a white curl-tail trailer bombed right into the boil. I've had my best luck working the eddies on the Orient side of the Gut where the current backs off just enough to hold fish without blowing your line out straight. Depth there runs 15 to 25 feet on the shallow shoulders, dropping to 40-plus in the main channel — fish the shoulders on the up-current side first, then work deeper as the tide slacks.

Bluefish are riding shotgun on the same bunker schools, and they're not small. Jumbo blues in the 8 to 12-pound class are tearing through bait balls off Fishers Island and along the rip lines east of Plum Gut, especially in that last hour of daylight when the light goes flat and everything gets aggressive. Wire leader is not optional right now — these fish are angry and they've got teeth to match the size.

Fluke fishing is holding steady but modest — nothing to write home about, but enough to keep the cooler from being empty. I've been picking up keepers to 4 pounds drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails in 25 to 35 feet off the Orient reef structure, working the last of the incoming when the drift slows down enough to keep bottom contact. Porgies are thick around the same structure if you want numbers over size — squid strips on a hi-lo rig, standard stuff, but it's a solid backup when the current's too hard for anything else.

Weakfish have been a no-show this year in this stretch, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. If you're specifically targeting weakfish out here, you're better off putting that time into the bass and blues that are actually showing up.

Here's the thing I'm watching hardest right now: the warm water and bait density building around Fishers Island Sound has all the ingredients for the first false albacore push of the season. I haven't seen one yet, and I'm not going to claim otherwise, but the setup is there — dense peanut bunker, clean current, and warm surface temps holding in the high 60s to low 70s. If you're fishing east of Orient this week, keep a light spinning rod rigged with a small epoxy jig ready. When those albies show, they show fast and they don't stick around long.

Look-ahead: if this current pattern holds through the week, I'd keep hammering The Race and Plum Gut on the last two hours of the ebb — that's where the bait's getting squeezed hardest and where the biggest bass are showing. If the wind stays out of the southwest under 15 knots, the surface bite off Orient at first light should stay consistent. I'd also keep half an eye east toward Fishers Island Sound — if the albie run starts anywhere in this stretch of the Sound, that's where it breaks first, and you don't want to be the guy who heard about it two days late.
