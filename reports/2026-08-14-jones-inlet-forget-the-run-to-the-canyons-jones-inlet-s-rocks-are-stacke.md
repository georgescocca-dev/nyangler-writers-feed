# Forget the run to the canyons — Jones Inlet's rocks are stacked with bunker and blues
*While the fleet chases albies out east, the current lines inside Jones Inlet are doing all the work on bass, blues, and a fluke bite that won't quit.*

**JONES BEACH, NY — August 14** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Everybody and their brother's got their eyes on the offshore reports this week — bluefin talk, albie watches, canyon chatter. That's fine for guys with a 30-footer and gas money to burn. Me, I'm standing on the rocks at the inlet at 5:15 in the morning watching peanut bunker get pushed through the cut like they're running from something, because they are.

This is the pattern I wait all summer for. Mid-August, the bait gets thick along the south shore, and Jones Inlet turns into what it always turns into this time of year — a bunker highway with teeth on both ends. I flagged this a couple weeks back, said the building tidal flow into the new moon would sharpen up the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what's happened. We had that new moon on the 12th, and the extra push on the tides has lit a fire under everything holding on the current lines — the jetty rocks, the mouth of Sloop Channel, the deep edge off Short Beach.

Striped bass are the headline, but don't go thinking it's blitz fishing every trip — it's not. What I'm seeing is first-light schoolies, mostly 20 to 26 inches, working the same rips where the bunker get squeezed through the inlet on the last of the outgoing. I've had my best luck casting bunker spoons and 2-ounce Ava jigs with a green tube right into the white water off the north jetty, letting it sink on the swing and ripping it back through the seam. You get a few fish, then it shuts off for twenty minutes, then it turns back on when the next wave of bait gets pushed. That's the rhythm right now — not a steady blitz, but reliable windows if you're willing to put in the time and read the current instead of just chucking and hoping.

Bluefish showed back up in real numbers this week, and they're not shy about it. Snappers are thick in the back bay creeks off Freeport and up around the Wantagh Bridge pilings — kids with bobbers and spearing are having a field day — but the adult class blues are the ones I care about, and they're hammering bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig right in the main channel of the inlet on the last two hours of the ebb. Bring wire leader or you're donating hooks. These fish are riding the same bait wave that's been driving stripers from here up through the Sound, and if the bunker keep pouring off the beach the way they have, I expect the blues to keep building through the back half of August.

Fluke fishing has actually been the pleasant surprise. Guys have been sleeping on it because of all the bass talk, but the drift through Sloop Channel and the deeper holes off Short Beach has been putting keepers in the box on the outgoing tide, when the bait gets swept off the flats and funnels into the channel edges. I'm running 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullet in chartreuse, working 12 to 18 feet of water, bouncing bottom nice and slow. Nothing giant — most fish are running 17 to 19 inches — but it's a consistent pick if you're willing to make repeat drifts through the same stretch instead of running all over the bay burning gas.

Now here's something worth watching close — weakfish are trickling back into Hempstead Bay and South Oyster Bay, and I mean that as a real signal, not a fluke catch here and there. Guys working soft plastics on light jigheads around the deeper holes off the flats at dusk have been picking up weakies in the 16 to 20 inch range mixed in with the fluke. It's not a stampede yet, but after years of hearing guys complain about weakfish being a memory around here, seeing them show consistently enough to mention is a good sign for where this fishery's heading.

Water's still warm — mid 70s inshore, low 70s out at the edge of the bight — and that's kept things stable, not pushing fish out early. The ocean's been a little sloppy with some leftover swell rolling through, which is exactly why I'm not wasting time on the open beach right now. That energy is getting absorbed by the bar and the inlet mouth, and it's concentrating everything — bait, bass, blues — into the protected current seams instead of spreading it thin along miles of surf.

Looking ahead, if this tidal push holds and the bunker keep working down the beach, I'd expect the early-morning bass bite at the inlet to hold steady through this week, maybe build a little as we work toward the next tide cycle. The blues should keep riding shotgun on the same bait. Best case, we get one more good week of this before the fall push really starts stacking up. If the wind swings hard out of the southwest and messes with the water clarity inside, I'd shift over to the fluke drift in Sloop Channel until it settles back out. Either way, I'm not driving to the canyon for this. Everything I need is inside that inlet.
