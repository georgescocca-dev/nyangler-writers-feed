# Kayak Over Surf This Week: Fluke and Weakfish on the Bay's Deep Edges
*With bass scarce and the ocean chopped up, the smart play in Jamaica Bay this week was staying inside and working the channels.*

**CANARSIE, NY — August 9** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday morning to leave the surf rods in the truck and put the kayak in off the Motorless Boat launch instead. The swell rolling into Rockaway and Breezy Point has been messy all week — nothing dangerous, but enough push and period to turn the beach into a washing machine and make Marine Parkway Bridge's usual outgoing bass show a non-event. When the ocean's stirred up like that, the bay is where the fish and the fun are, and paddling lets me sneak into the channel edges and holes that the bigger boats can't work as tight.

We flagged this a few days back and it's held up: striped bass have mostly cleared out of Jamaica Bay for cooler water elsewhere in the Bight, and there's just not much resident action here right now. I worked the usual staging water off the bridge on two different outgoing tides this week and came up with nothing but a couple of small blues for my trouble. If you're set on bass, you're better off waiting for that first real cool pulse in September than beating this water right now — it's warm, it's shallow, and it tracks the air temperature closely, exactly the pattern we expected to hold through this stretch of summer.

What IS working is fluke, and it's been a legitimate bright spot. I've had my best drifts in Grassy Bay and along the edge of Duck Point Meadow, working 8 to 14 feet of water on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current is still moving bait off the flats but hasn't gone fully slack. Four-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow right along bottom, have been the ticket — most fish in the 15 to 18 inch range with a scattering of keepers pushing 20. The channel edge off Ruffle Bar has produced too, though it's spottier — you need to hit it right on that tide change or you're wasting drifts.

Weakfish are still around in decent numbers, but you have to fish the last hour of daylight into dusk to connect consistently. I've been anchoring up on the channel edges near Silver Hole and working a bucktail tipped with a strip of squid, letting it settle and just twitching it along bottom. Nothing huge — mostly 16 to 20 inches — but it's a real fishery right now if you're willing to fish the light-fading window instead of the middle of the day.

Snapper blues are absolutely thick in the shallows off Canarsie Pier, Hamilton Beach, and along the bulkheads by Motorless — this is prime kid-fishing right now, small Kastmasters or spearing under a bobber, and it's a good early read on the broader bluefish comeback we've been watching build. The bigger blues haven't fully pushed into the bay yet, but I've heard of some heavier fish showing on bunker schools right at Rockaway Inlet, which tells me there's real bait moving through and it's only a matter of time before that filters back into the bay proper.

Porgies are holding on the humps and channel edges near Yellow Bar — sandworms or clam on a simple bottom rig, 10 to 14 feet of water, and they've been steady if unspectacular. Blackfish are still off-limits until the fall season opens, so leave those spots alone for now and save them for October.

Tide-wise, we're building toward the new moon on the 12th, and the extra push in current strength should sharpen up the bite at every current-driven spot in the bay — Cross Bay Bridge, the mouth of Grassy Bay, the deeper cuts by Ruffle Bar. That's usually when the fluke and weakfish bite gets a little more aggressive and a little more predictable, because the moving water concentrates bait right where the fish are already sitting.

Looking ahead, I'd keep working the kayak or a shallow-draft boat through the channels rather than fighting the surf until that swell settles down. If you want bass, patience is the better strategy than effort right now — wait for the first real cool nights and watch the bridge current again once that water starts to turn. For everything else, this coming week with the building new-moon tide should be some of the better fluke and weakfish fishing we've had all summer, provided you're willing to work the tide changes and not just show up whenever. The Rockaway blitz everyone's already asking about is still weeks off — but bunker showing at the inlet and blues building underneath them is exactly the kind of early signal that gets me excited for what October's going to look like.
