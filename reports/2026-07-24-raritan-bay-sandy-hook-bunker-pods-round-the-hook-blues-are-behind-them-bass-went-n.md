# Bunker Pods Round the Hook: Blues Are Behind Them, Bass Went Nocturnal
*The bait finally made the turn at Sandy Hook, and now it's a question of fishing the blitz by day or the bass by dark.*

**HIGHLANDS, NJ — July 24** — _by Marisol Duarte, Zone Writer_

I told my father Wednesday morning I was skipping the Flats and running straight to the Hook, and he looked at me like I'd lost it — July, flat calm, everybody parked on Keyport chasing porgies. But I'd been watching the bunker push north out of the Belmar stretch for a week now, and I said it right here last week: once those pods round the Hook, the blues and bass follow. That's exactly what's happening, and it's the only call to make right now if you want to be in the middle of visible, violent fishing instead of soaking bait and hoping.

I ran out past Romer Shoal Tuesday morning on the last of the incoming and found bunker balled up tight off the Hook's ocean side, maybe a quarter mile off the beach, gulls already stacking overhead before I even cut the motor. Blues came up under them in waves — five, ten pound class fish, some bigger — just demolishing pods on the surface. Didn't need anything fancy. Diamond jigs, the 2-3 ounce silver ones, cast right into the froth and let it flutter down eight, ten feet before you start the retrieve. Poppers worked too when the surface stuff calmed for a second and they were still under there sipping scraps. This is the most reliable visible bite in the bay right now and I expect it to keep building through the weekend as more bait rounds the point. If you're set up to run and gun along the Hook's ocean beach or drift the edge of Romer Shoal on a dropping tide, you're going to find them.

Stripers are a different story right now, and I want to be straight about it — daytime bass fishing in the bay has gone quiet. Water's pushing into the mid-70s on the flats and even out around Flynn's Knoll, and the fish know it. They're sliding down onto structure and holding through the heat of the day, not interested in much until the light drops. I fished the Knoll at first light Saturday on bunker chunks, soaked two rods on fish-finder rigs in eighteen feet of water on the last of the outgoing, and had one decent take — a 28-incher that came unbuttoned at the boat, which is about the story of my week. If you want bass right now, you fish the night tide or you fish the last hour of light into full dark. The Highlands bridge pilings and the mouth of the Shrewsbury have both been giving up fish after 9 PM on live bunker drifted on the current, weighted just enough to hold bottom without dragging.

Fluke have been the honest, unglamorous producer all month and that hasn't changed. The channel edges off Keyport Flats and the deeper water running along the Verrazano-adjacent structure toward the bay mouth are still holding keeper-class fish, though you have to work for them now that the shallow water's warmed through. I've been dragging white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, right along the drop where the flats fall into the channel — 20 to 28 feet, working the drift on the tail end of the incoming when the current's still got some push but isn't ripping. Shorts are everywhere in the shallower water, which tells you the fish are there, just not the right size until you find that depth change. Patience and boat position matter more than lure choice right now.

Porgies are still stacked up thick around Romer Shoal and the reef structure inside the bay, and if you need a box of fish to show for the trip that's your play — small hooks, squid strips, bottom rigs, anchor up and let them come to you. Blackfish season's closed this stretch so leave the tog gear home, and weakfish have been scarce here even with the better reports building down in Delaware Bay — I haven't heard of a legitimate weakfish catch in this zone in weeks, so I wouldn't plan a trip around them.

Tide-wise, we're working through a slack lunar period, current's not doing anything dramatic, which actually favors the blitz conditions — calmer water keeps the bunker balled tighter instead of scattering them, and that's part of why the blues have been able to camp on them instead of chasing broken pods all over the bay. Wind's been cooperative too, nothing pushing hard enough out of one direction to break up the bait concentrations along the beach.

Looking ahead, I expect the bunker-blue show at the Hook to keep intensifying through the weekend as more bait funnels north — this is going to be the story of the next several days in this zone, and I'd rather be out there with diamond jigs than sitting on the flats waiting for something that isn't happening in daylight. For bass, I'd shift my whole game to last light and full dark this week: fish the bridge structure in the Highlands, fish the Shrewsbury mouth, and don't bother running out at 6 AM expecting daytime bass unless you're willing to go deep and slow on the Knoll and accept a long wait for a short bite window. Fluke guys, keep working those channel edges on the moving water — it's not exciting fishing but it's honest, and honest still fills a cooler if you put in the drift time.
