# Great Point Rip: Blues Crashing, Terns Working, Albies Still a No-Show
*Two weeks of watching the Point for the first bonito and all I've got so far is chopper blues and a lot of hope.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — July 31** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning, half-light, I'm idling off Great Point with the ebb just starting to run, and the terns go from lazy circles to a full stoop in about four seconds flat. Bait balls up tight against the rip line, gets pushed and pinned by the current seam, and something underneath starts working it from below. I'm easing the boat in thinking this is it, this is the morning — and it's blues. Good blues, mind you, four to eight pounds, absolutely stacked on peanut bunker getting squeezed against that rip. But blues all the same. No green backs, no torpedo shapes knifing through the bait. Not yet.

I've been watching for bonito and albies for two weeks now, same as every August. The water's right for it — Nantucket Sound is holding real heat this year, pushing into the mid-70s in the shallows and even brushing up near 80 in some of the back pockets off Monomoy on a sunny afternoon slack tide. That's warmer than the stripers want, and it shows. We flagged this a week back — said the bass would keep sliding deep and staying nocturnal as long as this warm, stable stretch held, and that's exactly the program right now. Daytime, the bass have all but vanished off the visible structure. Come dusk and through the night, they're stacking on bunker chunks and eels around the elbow at Chatham Inlet and along the Monomoy bar, working 20 to 30 feet of water where the bottom stays cool even when the surface is soup-warm. If you're on Monomoy after dark, fresh bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig, 3 ounces of lead to hold bottom against that current, and patience — that's the program. Not fast fishing, but real fish, mostly mid-30s to low 40-inch class.

Fluke have been the most honest fish in the Sound. Steady, not spectacular, but you can set your watch by it. Horseshoe Shoal and the edges around Muskeget Channel are holding fish in 25 to 35 feet, and the drift over the sandy cuts off Tuckernuck has been kicking out keepers mixed with a lot of shorts — four-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow with the current rather than against it. Scup are riding shotgun on the same drifts, which tells you the bottom's still cool enough down there to hold bait, even with the surface running warm.

Now, about the albies and bonito — here's where I have to be straight with you. Nothing confirmed in the Sound yet. Not off Great Point, not off Sankaty, not around Monomoy. But the signs are stacking up the way they do right before the switch flips. Squid have been showing in real numbers pushing into the Sound and around the Vineyard side, which is usually the tell — bonito track squid and silversides, and when the bait shows, the fish aren't far behind. We called this the week before last, saying the thermal setup at Montauk, Block Island, and the Buzzards Bay corridor had crossed the seasonal threshold and just needed the tide cycle to trigger the first real push. The tide's building back now toward the August 12 new moon, current's getting stronger through the rips every day, and that's usually the ingredient that's been missing. If I had to bet, I'd say the first scout showing in this zone comes off the Sankaty rip or the tip of Great Point on a strong ebb sometime in the next week to ten days — small, single fish first, testing the rip, before it turns into anything you'd call a blitz.

Wind's been the easy part of this whole stretch — stable out of the southwest, nothing over 15 knots most days, which means the rips have been fishable top to bottom instead of getting blown out and turned to washing machine chop. That's kept the blue run consistent at the Point. If you want the most reliable fish in the Sound this week, that's it — work the rip on the first two hours of the ebb, cast a Hogy or a Deadly Dick past the seam and let it swing through, and expect blues in the 5 to 8 pound range with the occasional bigger chopper mixed in. It's not subtle fishing. They hit like a freight train and you'll go through a few leaders.

Look, I'd love to tell you the albies showed up this week. They didn't. But every piece of this puzzle — the squid, the building tide, the thermal edge sitting right off the Vineyard and pushing toward us — says the window's close. I'll be out there every clean morning with the SW wind, watching that rip off Great Point for the first torpedo shape under the terns. If the tide keeps building the way it should into next week, and a scout or two starts showing at Block Island or Montauk like the pattern usually goes, don't be surprised if the first Nantucket albie report breaks within the next several days. Until then, work the blues, work the fluke, and fish the bass at night. That's the honest state of the Sound right now.
