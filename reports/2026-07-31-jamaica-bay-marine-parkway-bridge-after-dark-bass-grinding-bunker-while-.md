# Marine Parkway Bridge After Dark: Bass Grinding Bunker While the Bay Runs Warm
*With the bay pushing near 80 degrees, the bass have gone nocturnal and the blues have taken over the daylight shift on Rockaway's beachfront.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — July 31** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

I took the kayak out under Marine Parkway Bridge at 4:45 Tuesday morning, before the sun even thought about coming up, and I'll tell you why — this bay is running warm enough right now that daytime bass fishing inside Jamaica Bay is mostly a waste of gas or paddle strokes. When the surface temps push into the mid-70s and flirt with 80 in the shallows, stripers want no part of it during daylight. They slide down onto the current-scoured bottom under the bridge, in that deeper cut where the tide rips through on the outgoing, and they wait for dark to come up and feed. So that's where I went, and that's where you should be going too if you want a shot at a real fish right now.

It paid off. Fished bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig, 3-ounce sinker to hold bottom against that current, right off the pilings on the last two hours of the outgoing tide. Picked up two bass in the 26 to 30-inch range and missed a third that felt heavier before it pulled off. Nothing that's going to make the cover of a magazine, but solid, honest fish, and exactly the pattern I expected given how hot this bay has gotten. The bunker schools pushing through the bay right now are the whole story — where you find pods of peanut bunker stacked up near the bridge or along the Cross Bay Bridge pilings, there's usually a bass or two shadowing them after dark. Eels work too if you want to cover more water — a live eel drifted slow along the bridge fenders on the incoming has been my second option when the chunk bite goes quiet.

During the day, the action has flipped almost entirely to bluefish, and that's not a surprise — warm water is bluefish water, and they're the ones cashing in while the bass hide out deep. We flagged this pattern last week, that the blitz running the Jersey and Long Island beachfront would keep spreading north toward the Sandy Hook and Raritan Bay side, and sure enough it's pushed right up into our stretch. Rockaway Beach has had legitimate blitz mornings this week — peanut bunker and small mullet getting run up onto the sand from Beach 90th down toward Beach 116th, with blues busting bait right in the wash. If you're fishing the surf, get there at first light before the joggers and the sun push the bait back out. Metal — a 1-ounce Kastmaster or a Deadly Dick — cast right into the froth will get bit almost every cast when it's happening. Poppers work too if you want the visual, but honestly the blues aren't picky when they're plowing through bait like that.

Fluke fishing has been steady, not spectacular, which is about right for the end of July. The channel edges off Cross Bay Bridge and the deeper cuts around Grassy Bay and Ruffle Bar are holding fish, but you've got to work for keepers. I've been drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, along the drop-offs where the mud flats fall into the channel — anywhere from 15 to 25 feet. Getting a lot of shorts mixed in with the occasional keeper, so it's a numbers game more than a guaranteed box of fish. Best window has been the last two hours of the incoming, when clean water is pushing back into the bay off the ocean side and the fluke seem to key up on it before the flat slack kills the bite.

Weakfish have been a footnote — a few scattered reports of small ones coming up near the bridge structure at night mixed in with the bass bite, nothing worth planning a trip around. Porgies are around Canarsie Pier if you want an easy family outing — bloodworms or clam on a hi-lo rig off the pier at half-tide will keep the rods bent for the kids even if nothing else is happening. Blackfish are still a month or so off from being worth chasing here.

We're coming off the full moon and those tides are still running strong, which is part of why the outgoing current under the bridge has been pushing so hard and holding the bass tight to structure rather than spreading them out. As we ease toward the neap tide over the next several days, expect the current to slacken a bit, which could actually spread the bunker schools out more and make the bass bite a little less predictable — I'd fish the bigger tide swings while they last if your schedule allows it.

Looking ahead, I don't expect a major shift unless we get a real thunderstorm system roll through, which is typical for early August and could knock the surface temps down a degree or two and briefly scatter the bait. If that happens, watch the bay right after — a cooling rain can actually trigger a short window of daytime bass activity before things settle back into the nocturnal pattern. Otherwise, my plan for the coming week is the same as this one: dawn and dusk on the bridges for bass, first light on the Rockaway sand for blues, and channel drifts for fluke in the middle of the day when nothing else is biting anyway.
