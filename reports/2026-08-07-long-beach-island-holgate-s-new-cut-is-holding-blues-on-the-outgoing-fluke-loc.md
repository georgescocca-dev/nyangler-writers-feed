# Holgate's new cut is holding blues on the outgoing — fluke locked to the bridge shadows
*Building tidal range ahead of the Aug. 12 new moon is starting to sharpen the bite at every piece of structure on the island.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — August 8** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Walked the south end at Holgate Tuesday at first light and the beach had changed shape on me again. That three-to-four foot swell that rolled through off the Bight over the weekend chewed a new cut about 150 yards north of the old one — deeper, narrower, with a hard drop-off on the south shoulder where the trough dumps into the bar. I could see the color change from the dune line, that dark green gut cutting against the lighter sand flat. Spots like that don't stay secret long once the bait finds them, and by Wednesday morning there were peanut bunker getting pushed around in the wash and small stripers — mostly 18 to 24 inches — slashing through them on the last of the outgoing.

That's been the pattern up and down the island all week: small, scattered striper action working bait pods in the surf, mostly a dawn-and-dusk deal, nothing you can set your watch to but enough to keep a plug bag interesting. I've been doing best with a darter or a needlefish, something slim that reads as a peanut and not a full-grown menhaden, worked through the trough on the last hour of the outgoing and first hour of dead low. Ship Bottom and Beach Haven beaches have both been giving up a few, same story — small fish, tight to the wash, gone once the sun gets up over the dunes.

The better, more consistent story right now is inside. Fluke have settled onto the channel edges and bridge structure where there's current and a shot of cooler bottom water, and that's exactly where I've been finding them. The Beach Haven and Ship Bottom bridges are both holding fish in that 8 to 12 foot zone on the pilings' shadow line — white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow through the slack right at the tide change, has been the money presentation. Don't work it fast. These fish are sitting tight to structure in warm, shallow back-bay water and they want it crawled past their nose, not ripped through. Legal fish have been mixed with a lot of shorts, so it's a pick, but a 4- to 5-pound fluke off the Ship Bottom span on Sunday's high slack tells me the bigger fish are still around if you're patient and fishing the right three feet of water.

Weakfish have been spotty — that's the honest read. A few fish showing on the channel edges up around Graveling Point and into the Mullica system on the incoming, taken on small swim shads and bucktails tipped with Fishbites, but it's nowhere near a sure thing. If I had to put money on it I'd fish the deeper bend just off Graveling Point on the front half of the incoming, when that cooler Mullica water is still pushing out and meeting the flood — that seam has produced for me the last two Augusts running.

Bluefish are the piece that's actually improved. Barnegat Inlet has been showing more consistent blues on the tide change, both ebb and flood, fish in the 3- to 6-pound range busting bunker pods right in the rips off the north jetty rocks. Metal — Kastmasters and Deadly Dicks in the 1- to 2-ounce range — worked through the white water on the outgoing has been getting hit hard for short windows, then it shuts off until the next push of current. I think these are fish getting squeezed south out of the bluefish build that's been developing up toward Sandy Hook and the western Sound — bait's tight to structure right now and the blues are following it down the beach.

Kingfish have shown in small numbers on the outer bars at Holgate and down toward the inlet on bloodworm-tipped high-low rigs in that 3- to 5-foot sandy wash — nothing to write home about yet, but worth a rod in the sand spike if you're already surfcasting for stripers.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the deep, current-driven bite would hold and sharpen once the tide range started building into the new moon, and that's exactly what's happening. The moon's five days out from new on the 12th, tidal range is stacking day over day, and every piece of structure on this island — the bridges, the inlet rips, the Holgate cuts — is starting to fish harder on the swings. That's not an accident. More water moving means more current pinning bait against structure, and that's when these fish stop scattering and start feeding on a schedule.

Looking ahead, I'd focus the next few days on the moving tides at Barnegat Inlet for blues, the bridge shadow lines on the slack for fluke, and I'd keep half an eye on the surf for anything unusual working bait — this time of year, with warm current pushing down from the Bight, an early bonito or albie showing up off the beach wouldn't shock me, though I haven't seen or heard of one yet on this stretch. If the tidal range keeps building clean through the weekend without a big blow messing up the water, I'd bet the fluke bite on the bridges gets better before it gets worse, and Holgate's new cut is worth another look once this next round of swell settles down and lets you read it properly.
