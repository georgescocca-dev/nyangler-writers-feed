Cooling water is doing the driving. As surface temps keep sliding, peanut bunker and mackerel schools are being squeezed tighter against structure, and bass are stacking behind them instead of chasing wide open water. That's why the bite off the outer bay has felt more like a slow pressure cooker than a blitz — fish are there, they're feeding, but they're not always showing themselves on top.

Live mackerel remains the go-to bait for the bigger fish working the rip at Race Point, fished on a simple fishfinder rig and allowed to swim naturally through the tide line rather than retrieved. Boats slow-trolling tube-and-worm along the deeper edges off Peaked Hill Bars have also connected with some better-grade bass, particularly on the last two hours of the ebb when the current compresses bait against the drop. Diamond jigs and bucktails are still drawing bluefish into the mix, but numbers are thinning as the water cools — bluefish tolerate cold water worse than bass, and that pattern is holding true again this fall. If you're specifically hunting blues, the window is shortening fast; don't be surprised if most of what you connect with from here out is bass with the occasional straggler bluefish mixed in.

Closer to shore, Sandy Neck and the flats off Barnstable and Brewster have been producing smaller schoolie bass on peanut bunker-profile soft plastics and small swimming plugs worked on the last of the outgoing water, when bait gets pushed off the flats into the channel edges. It's not a numbers game out there right now, but it's a reasonable option for shore-bound anglers who don't have a way to get out to the deeper rips. Billingsgate has also had some action on the drop, mostly smaller fish picking off bait pushed out by tide, worth a look on a moving current rather than slack.

Cod are starting to show on the deeper structure out toward the outer bay as the thermocline breaks down and bottom temperatures fall into a range cod tolerate. It's early for that pattern, and nobody should expect a return to the heavy cod years, but diamond jigs and bait rigs bounced along bottom near the deeper ledges have picked up a handful of keeper-class fish mixed in with the usual dogfish. Worth a few drifts if you're already out there working structure for bass and want to diversify the cooler.

Fluke are winding down fast in the bay as water drops out of their preferred range — this is the tail of that fishery, not the peak, and effort is rightly shifting toward the migratory bass and bottom species that tolerate the cold better. If you've got a fluke rod rigged, this is close to the last call for the season inshore.
