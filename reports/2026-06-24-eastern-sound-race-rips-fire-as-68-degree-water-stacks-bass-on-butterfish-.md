# Race rips fire as 68-degree water stacks bass on butterfish and squid
*Current-swept edges holding trophy stripers while Plum Gut produces steady slot fish on three-way rigs.*

**THE RACE, NY — June 24** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The eastern Sound is absolutely on fire right now, and it's all about reading the thermal breaks and current edges that are stacking bait like a dinner bell for big bass. Water temps hit 68 degrees this week — that magic number that turns stripers from picky to aggressive — and the results speak for themselves.

The Race has been the star performer, especially on the ebb when current screams through at 4-plus knots. I'm seeing bass to 40 inches crushing butterfish and squid in the deep water eddies where the rip breaks around structure. The key is positioning your drift to hit those current seams where fast water meets the back-eddies. Three-way rigs with 8-ounce cannonballs and 6-foot leaders are mandatory — anything lighter gets swept away before it finds bottom in 100-plus feet.

Swim shads are absolutely destroying bucktails right now for the bigger fish. I've been running 7-inch Tsunami shads on the three-way setup, and the size difference is dramatic. Where bucktails are pulling 28-inch slot fish, the shads are consistently producing 32-to-38-inch bass. The bigger profile mimics the butterfish that are thick in the water column, and these Hudson River fish that pushed in this week are keyed on size.

Plum Gut continues to be the consistent producer for numbers. The flood tide has been money, especially the last two hours when current really starts pushing. I'm working the 80-foot edge where it drops to 40 across the main rip, then falls back to deep water. The bottom contour creates a perfect ambush zone, and bass are stacked there waiting for bait to get swept over the hump. Same three-way rig setup, but I'm using 80-pound leader material — the stiffness helps stand the bucktail off bottom and reduces hang-ups on the rocky terrain.

The butterfish presence is unusual for June — typically we don't see them this thick until July — but they're here in force and the bass are taking full advantage. I'm also seeing squid mixed in, which explains why topwater has been productive during slack tide periods. When the current dies, switch to pencil poppers and walk-the-dog presentations. The fish are feeding up in the water column on the squid, and surface action has been spectacular at first light.

Bluefish have crashed the party in a big way. These aren't the cocktail blues we usually see early season — these are gorilla blues to 15 pounds that are absolutely wrecking expensive plugs. Wire leaders are mandatory now, and I'm seeing guys lose $30 glide baits to these things. The upside is they fight like freight trains and provide non-stop action when the bass bite slows.

Current speed is everything right now. The new moon spring tides are creating 5-knot rips that are both blessing and curse. When you can control your drift and stay in the strike zone, the fishing is phenomenal. But get caught in the main rip and you'll burn through sinkers trying to maintain bottom contact. I'm timing my drifts to hit the edges where current is strong enough to position bait but not so violent that presentation becomes impossible.

Weather has been cooperative with light winds, but that changes this weekend. Southwest winds to 15 knots will make the eastern spots challenging, especially The Race. When conditions deteriorate, the western edges of Plum Gut and the Connecticut side drops become the play. The fish are there — it's just a matter of finding fishable water.

Looking ahead, this 68-degree water is the sweet spot we've been waiting for all season. The thermal structure is set up perfectly with cooler water offshore creating a gradient that's concentrating bait. As long as water temps hold in the mid-to-upper 60s, this bite should continue strong. The full moon Friday means maximum current flow — perfect for The Race if you can handle the conditions, and prime time for the Gut edges where current creates the feeding zones these bass can't resist.
