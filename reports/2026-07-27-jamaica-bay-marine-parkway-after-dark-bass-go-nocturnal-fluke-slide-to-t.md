# Marine Parkway After Dark: Bass Go Nocturnal, Fluke Slide to the Rip
*With the bay running warm and slick, the real bite in Jamaica Bay this week happens after the sun drops and in the deep water the fish are hiding in.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — July 27** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday night to leave the kayak on the roof rack and fish Marine Parkway Bridge from the rocks instead, and it was the right move. When the bay's sitting this warm — surface temps pushing into the low 70s and barely moving on the calm days we've had — you want to be somewhere with current, depth, and shade, and the bridge gives you all three once the light goes. I got there an hour before the tide turned to outgoing, set up on the Rockaway side where the current sweeps hard past the pilings, and waited.

The bass showed up right on schedule, but not the way they were a month ago. These fish have gone nocturnal on us, plain and simple. Daytime, they're down deep on the structure and current seams, sulking in whatever cooler water they can find. After dark, with the outgoing water ripping past those pilings, they came up and hunted. I fished a black Slug-Go on a 3/8-ounce jighead, worked slow and dead-drifted through the seam, and picked up two fish in the 26 to 28-inch range plus a handful of shorts that never left the water. A friend fishing bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig, bounced right off the base of the pilings, had a better night than me — three keepers before midnight. If you're set up to soak bait, that's still the more consistent play at the bridge right now. Eels work too, especially on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current really loads up against the structure.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the stripers would stay locked into this deep, after-dark rhythm as long as the warm, stable weather held, and that's exactly what's playing out. Nothing about this bay says it's going to break until we get a real cold front or a string of blustery days to mix the water column. Until then, plan your trips around dusk and the outgoing tide, not the middle of the afternoon.

Fluke has been the tougher conversation this week. The open-flat drifting that was so good back in May and June has gone quiet — those fish have pushed off the shallow mud and grass into the deeper channel bends and inlet-adjacent structure where the water still moves and stays a few degrees cooler. Rockaway Inlet is still your best bet, particularly the channel edge running past the old Coast Guard station, and the deep bend just inside Cross Bay Bridge. I've been dragging 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails right along the bottom there on the last of the incoming, and it's producing — not a banner bite, but enough keepers in the 18 to 20-inch range to make the trip worthwhile if you're covering ground and not parking on one drift. If you fish the open bay flats right now and come up empty, don't be surprised. That bite has softened considerably and I don't expect it to turn back on until the water starts cooling toward September.

The best news of the week, honestly, has been the snapper blues. Juvenile bluefish have shown up in real numbers along the marsh edges — Canarsie Pier, the flats off Bergen Basin, anywhere there's grass and a little current. These are small fish, six to nine inches, but they're aggressive and they're everywhere, which is exactly what you want to see this time of year. It's the leading indicator for a healthy fall bait cycle, and it's also just plain fun — light spinning gear, small silver spoons or a strip of spearing under a bobber, and you can keep a kid entertained for hours off the pier without needing a boat or a kayak. The adult bluefish are still out there too, mostly working the inlet and the surf line off Rockaway Beach when bunker schools push through, though I haven't seen a full-blown blitz inside the bay itself this week — that action has stayed concentrated more toward the open beachfront.

Weakfish have made a quiet appearance in the deeper channels — nothing to build a trip around, but a few incidental catches on bucktails and soft plastics fished deep near Cross Bay Bridge tell me there's a trickle of fish moving through. Worth keeping an eye on if that regional resurgence keeps building.

Looking ahead, I don't see much changing in the next several days. The moon's working toward full in early August, which should start sharpening the current through the inlet and around the bridge — that's usually when the bass bite gets a little more aggressive and a little more predictable. If the heat and calm winds keep stacking up the way they have, I'd also keep half an eye on the shallow back-bay corners — Bergen Basin, Head of Bay — for signs of stressed water on the hottest, stillest afternoons. That's not a reason to panic, just a reason to fish smart: mornings, evenings, moving water, and don't be afraid to go deep or go dark to find fish that are avoiding the heat just like we are.
