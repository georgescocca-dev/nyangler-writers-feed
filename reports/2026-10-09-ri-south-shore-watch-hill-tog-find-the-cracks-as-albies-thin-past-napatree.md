The mechanics here are straightforward. The October 6 full moon pushed a strong tidal exchange through the breachways all week, and a cold front right behind it knocked surface temps down into the low-to-mid 60s across the regional gradient — Montauk's reading 64.2, the open Sound mid-60s, nothing dramatic but enough to tip bait behavior. Baitfish don't like getting shoved around in open water when it's cooling fast, so they tuck tight against structure: jetty rocks, breachway mouths, the lee side of points. That concentration is exactly what's pulling striped bass and bluefish onto the visible boils anglers are reporting from Sakonnet to Napatree, and it's exactly what we flagged last week — we said the full-moon tide would pin bunker and mullet tight to structure with stripers and blues running them over at dawn and dusk, and that's precisely the pattern playing out right now.

Striper action along the open beaches and breachway mouths has been genuinely good, not just good-on-paper. The pattern favors first and last light, when bait is pushed hardest and bass are least shy about showing on top. Bucktails in the 1-to-1.5 ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked with a slow bottom bounce off the current seams at Sakonnet Point and the Napatree rip, are producing slot fish and the occasional overslot. Where the blitzes go fully topwater — and they have been, sporadically, mid-morning when the sun gets on the bait — a Hogy Pencil or a bone-colored Super Spook thrown just past the froth and worked back through it has been the move. Bluefish are mixed right into these same schools, chopping up peanut bunker with no subtlety at all, so wire or a heavy fluorocarbon leader is worth the hassle if you don't want to donate lures.

Tautog is the other half of this week's story, and it's building the way it usually does once the scup bite starts thinning out. Green crabs are the bait of choice on the rock piles around Jamestown, the Newport Bridge abutments, and the Watch Hill reef complex, fished on a simple knocker rig or a jig-head crab combo dropped straight into the cracks. Early reports have fish in the 4-to-8 pound range with a few better ones mixed in, which is about right for this point in the season — not a stacked bite yet, but a real one, and it should only get stronger as water temps keep sliding and tog move shallower off the open structure. If you're switching gears from bass to tog this week, that's a sound call; the window is opening, not closing.

False albacore and bonito are the harder read right now, and I want to be straight about it rather than oversell it. They're still around — Napatree and the open water off Watch Hill have had flurries, usually brief, usually tide-dependent — but the numbers are thinning as temperatures slide toward that upper-50s/low-60s mark where these fish typically clear out of Rhode Island water. The stronger, more consistent albie and bonito action right now is reported further east, around Buzzards Bay, the Vineyard, and Nantucket Sound, where water's holding a touch warmer longer. If you're making a dedicated trip for albies this weekend, Napatree and the Sakonnet rips are still worth a cast with a half-ounce epoxy jig or a small Deadly Dick worked fast through visible nervous water, but treat it as a bonus rather than the plan. The window here is closing over the next week to two, not gone yet.

Looking at the next 72 hours, the pattern that's been working should hold with the moon's tidal push still carrying some strength and water continuing its gradual cool-down. That favors the bass-and-blue blitz pattern staying the headline story along the open beachfront and breachways, with dawn and dusk remaining the best windows as bait gets pushed hardest on the moving tide. There's a potential frontal system reported for midweek that could trigger another round of blitz intensification similar to what's been happening — worth watching the forecast and being ready to move if the wind lines up. For tog, the better call is patience: the bite is building, not peaking, so the next couple weeks should only improve as more fish settle onto the structure. If you've only got one morning to spend, I'd put it on the bass blitz at first light over a tog session right now — but keep the crab bucket in the truck, because that trade is going to flip before long.
