# Quick's Hole Turns Bonito Green — Albies Stacking Right Behind Them
*Bait's balled up tight in the Elizabeth Island rips and the first real albie push of the season is riding the building moon into Buzzards Bay.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — September 4** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

The bait told the story all week before a single rod bent. Peanut bunker and silversides have been getting squeezed into fists in the rips off Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole, pinned against the current by bonito working underneath and gulls marking the surface from above. That's the setup we flagged a week ago — said the bonito and albie signals were building alongside the striper and bluefish pattern, and here it is, right on schedule, with bonito now showing consistently through the Elizabeth Islands chain and the first legitimate false albacore reports trickling in behind them.

Water's still holding in the low-to-mid 70s through Buzzards Bay and Vineyard Sound, which is exactly the kind of warm, current-scrubbed water that keeps bait schools tight and predators committed rather than scattered. The moon's building toward full on the 6th and 7th, and that's the number to watch — bigger tidal swings mean harder-running water through Woods Hole, Robinson's Hole, and Quick's Hole, and harder-running water is what turns loosely balled bait into the kind of pinched, panicked schools that get albies and bonito to commit instead of just cruising through.

The rip fishing itself has been honest work, not a gimme. Bonito are showing best on the last two hours of the ebb through Robinson's Hole, when the current funnels bait out of Buzzards Bay into the Sound and stacks it against the western shore of Naushon. Small profile is the difference-maker right now — half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce epoxy jigs in green-and-white or bone, and Deadly Dicks in the smaller sizes, are outfishing the bigger metal that worked earlier in the summer. These fish are eating small right now, likely keyed on tiny silversides rather than anything with size to it, so if you're throwing standard Hopkins or Kastmasters and getting refusals, drop down before you give up on the spot.

False albacore reports are still spotty rather than steady — a few fish busting through Quick's Hole on the last of the outgoing, a handful more working the rip off Cuttyhunk near Sow and Pigs Reef on slack into the first of the flood. That's honest albie behavior for this early in September: they're here, they're feeding, but they're not locked in yet. The pattern usually tightens fast once that full moon current gets moving, so the next week is when this typically flips from occasional to reliable. Albie Snax in bone or pink-and-white, and small Deceivers in the 2-inch range on 20-pound fluorocarbon leader, have been the better calls when the fish do show — these are line-shy, fast-moving targets and a long cast into the boil beats working the edges.

Striped bass are still mostly a bait story rather than a blitz story in this zone. Bunker pods are thickening up inside Buzzards Bay, particularly off the mainland side toward New Bedford and down through the Bay proper, but the fish sitting under them are staged and non-committal rather than smashing the surface — same pattern playing out from Raritan Bay to Narragansett Bay right now. That should change as nighttime temps start dropping through September and the building moon tightens those bunker schools further. Worth working live bunker on a fishfinder rig or bucktailing the edges of visible pods around Cuttyhunk and the western Elizabeth Islands, but don't expect the top-water chaos yet — that's a look-ahead item, not a this-week item.

Bluefish have been the one bite you could count on without qualification. Blues are running thick through Woods Hole and along the south side of the Vineyard, chopping up peanut bunker and taking anything shiny thrown into the melee — poppers, small metal, bucktails, doesn't much matter. This has been consistent basin-wide through Labor Day week, and there's no reason to think it slows before the water starts dropping into the 60s.

Fluke are on the expected early-September fade. Back-bay and inlet numbers are thinning as the bigger fish slide out toward deeper structure ahead of the fall push. Vineyard Sound edges and the deeper water off Woods Hole are still worth a drift with a white Gulp swimming mullet on a bucktail if fluke is the priority, but this bite is winding down, not building — treat any keeper as a bonus rather than the plan.

Scup remain the reliable bread-and-butter fish for anyone who wants a bent rod without chasing moving targets. Oak Bluffs pier and the Woods Hole drawbridge current have both been producing steady scup on squid strips and small pieces of clam, mixed with snapper blues for anyone fishing bait on the bottom.

Looking ahead: the next several days should sharpen the albie and bonito bite as the moon builds. If the tide lines up right — start the ebb at Robinson's Hole two hours before dead low, or hit Quick's Hole on that same window — the odds of connecting go up considerably. Best case, the current strengthens enough by midweek to turn these scattered albie sightings into a real, sustained bite through the Elizabeth Islands chain. If the fish slide off the pace, Cuttyhunk's rip and the Menemsha side of the Sound are the backup plays. Either way, keep the small profile jigs rigged and ready — that's been the difference this week, and it's not likely to change until the bait size changes with it.
