# Captree drift fires as bay water warms — fluke to 6 pounds on bucktails
*Mixed bags of sea bass, porgies, and ling round out the action as June heat kicks in.*

**CAPTREE, NY — June 12** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The bay finally feels like June. Water temps hit 68 degrees this week, and you can feel the difference in everything from the bait movement to how aggressive the fish are feeding. The Captree fleet has been crushing it on the ocean side, with fluke up to 6 pounds coming over the rails on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and chartreuse bucktails.

The Robert Moses bridge drift has been the money spot for keeper fluke. I've been working 25 to 35 feet of water on the outgoing tide, dragging 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with 5-inch white Gulp. The key is keeping contact with bottom but not anchoring your jig — let it bounce and hop with the current. The bigger fish are holding in the deeper pockets near the bridge pilings, so don't be afraid to work tight to structure.

What's really impressive is the mixed bag action. While you're targeting fluke, you're picking up quality sea bass, fat porgies, and some surprising ling. The sea bass bite has been particularly strong — limits are common on diamond jigs and high-low rigs with clam strips. I watched one angler switch from bucktails to clams after limiting out on sea bass and immediately start hooking ling in the 3 to 5 pound range.

The ocean structure has been producing better quality fish than the bay this week. The warming water is pulling bait offshore, and the fluke are following. I'm seeing boats work the 40 to 50 foot depths south of the inlet with consistent action. Sabitiki rigs are deadly for the sea bass when they're stacked up, and I've seen guys pull three at a time.

Bait presentation matters more as the water warms. The fish are getting pickier, so fresh bait is crucial. Squid strips on the bucktails are outfishing old bait, and if you can get fresh spearing or peanut bunker, live-line them near structure on fishfinder rigs. The current has been moderate — perfect for controlling your drift without fighting it.

Night fishing has started to turn on for stripers in the bay. I'm hearing reports of slot fish on clam bellies around the bridges and channel edges. The key is fishing the moving water — incoming tide pushes bait through the inlet, and the bass stage up to ambush. Bass Assassins and soft plastics are working when the clam bite slows down.

The new moon is Friday, which means spring tides and strong current flow. This should flush more bait out of the bay and concentrate fish at the inlet mouth. I'm watching for the evening drain — that's when the weakfish typically show up for their first real push of the season. The water temperature gradient between bay and ocean is setting up perfectly for it.

Looking ahead, this warming trend should continue the fluke bite and start bringing the summer species inshore. The porgies are already thick, and I expect the sea bass action to stay strong through the weekend. If you're planning to fish, focus on the ocean side structure during the day and the bay edges at night. The fish are there — you just need to put your bait where they're feeding.
