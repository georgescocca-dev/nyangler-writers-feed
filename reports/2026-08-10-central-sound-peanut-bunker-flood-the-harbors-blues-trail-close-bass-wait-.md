# Peanut Bunker Flood the Harbors — Blues Trail Close, Bass Wait for Dark
*Watching the bait pile into Port Jeff and Oyster Bay this week told me more than any buoy could — here's what's chasing it.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — August 10** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

I spent Tuesday morning at the mouth of Port Jefferson Harbor just watching, not fishing, because the bait was doing something worth paying attention to. Peanut bunker — thick pods of them, maybe two to three inches, packed tight enough to shadow the bottom — were pouring out of the harbor mouth on the last of the ebb and scattering along the edge where the channel drops to 15 feet. Terns were working them hard, diving in tight little circles, and under that commotion I watched three separate blowups that weren't terns. That's blues, and they're right where the bait says they should be.

We flagged this two weeks back — said the bluefish would track the peanut bunker as it spread and that back-bay cocktail blues would spike within two weeks. Here it is. Northport Bay, Oyster Bay, and the Port Jeff inner harbor are all holding schoolie blues in the 12 to 16-inch range hammering that bait on the outgoing tide, and they're not hard to find once you spot the birds. Small Deadly Dick spoons, half-ounce Kastmasters, or three-inch Albie Snax in bunker patterns — fast retrieve, no finesse needed. Kids-on-the-dock fishing at its best, and it's a good early warning system for what's about to show up on the bigger structure.

Because where the peanut bunker concentrates, the bigger fish follow eventually, and the bass are already staging on it after dark. This time of year, with the Sound sitting warm — Kings Point read 72.5 the other day, a good four degrees ahead of what New London's showing to the east — the stripers just aren't interested in daytime work outside of moving current over structure. Stratford Shoal at night has been the play. Anchor up-current of the rip on the last two hours of the incoming, soak fresh bunker chunks or live peanuts on a fish-finder rig, 3/0 circle hook, and be patient. Guys working that shoal after 9 PM have been putting slot fish in the boat, along with the occasional 30-incher that decided to stick around instead of running the rip through to the Race. Middle Ground Light has fished similarly at dusk — bucktails, an ounce and a half, bounced slow along the drop just as the light fails, has drawn some quality fish off the rocks.

Daytime, honestly, the shoal bite has been a step slower than it was three weeks ago, and that tracks with what we expected — the neap tide early last week took the current down a gear and the daytime bite went with it. Full moon current is building back now, and I felt it Tuesday — more push through the rip than I'd seen in ten days. That should start sharpening the structure bite again by midweek, particularly at Stratford Shoal and around the Norwalk Islands where the porgies and sea bass have been holding steady through all of this regardless of tide strength. Sandworms and clam strips on a simple high-low rig, 20 to 35 feet around the rockpiles off Sheffield Island and the outer Norwalks, has been dependable — not exciting, but dependable, and that's worth something in August when a lot of other things are inconsistent.

Fluke have been the most honest fish in the zone. Channel edges and current-swept drop-offs are holding doormats where the water stays a few degrees cooler than the flats around them — the Port Jeff channel mouth, the drop just south of Charles Island, and the deeper cuts through the Norwalk Islands have all produced fish to five pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets rigged on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift with the tide, not against it. The full moon swing this week should sharpen those tide-change windows even more — the first hour after the turn has been when the bigger fish show.

Now, the thing I'm actually watching closest right now isn't in my zone yet, but it's close enough that I keep checking. Bonito season is right on schedule for a mid-August push, and the early word out of the Race and Fishers Island Sound is that reports are starting to firm up as water temps sit in that low-to-mid-70s sweet spot. That's the same water sitting off Stratford Shoal and Middle Ground right now. Every August I've fished this stretch, the bonito show up on bait first — small, tight, silver flashes chasing rain bait or tiny peanuts — days before anyone's marking them on a fishfinder. If you're out on the shoal and see something moving faster and more erratic than the blues, don't assume it's another cocktail blue. Have a small metal ready.

Looking at the week ahead: current strength is building back toward the full moon, which should put some snap back into the daytime shoal bite by Wednesday or Thursday if the wind cooperates. A stiff southwest blow would undo some of that, especially on the western end near Oyster Bay, but the central basin around Stratford Shoal tends to handle wind better than the western approaches do — it took the last blow in better shape than Huntington did. If I had one trip to make this week, I'd fish the Stratford Shoal rip on the building tide after dark, bunker chunks out, and keep a bucktail rigged for whatever shows up chasing that peanut bunker when the sun's still up.
