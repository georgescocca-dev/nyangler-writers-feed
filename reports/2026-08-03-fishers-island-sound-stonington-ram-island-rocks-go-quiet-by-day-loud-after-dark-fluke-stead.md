# Ram Island Rocks Go Quiet by Day, Loud After Dark — Fluke Steady, Albies Still a Rumor
*The current's easing toward the neap and the bass have followed it down into the rockpiles, where the real work now happens after the sun drops.*

**STONINGTON, CT — August 3** — _by Manny Pereira, Zone Writer_

There's a window at Wamphassuc Point right at first light where the peanut bunker come off the boulders in a nervous sheet, and if you're not already positioned when that happens you've missed the best twenty minutes of your day. I was out there Tuesday before 5:30, poling the skiff along the edge where the granite drops from three feet to eleven, and watched three separate bunker showers get worked over from below before the light was even good enough to see my leader knot. That's the tell right now — the bait's holding tight to the rock, and the bass are coming up to get it in the low light, then sliding back down into the structure once the sun's on the water.

We flagged this a couple weeks back — said the softening tide heading into the neap would widen the window and favor slower presentations, and that's exactly what's playing out. The moon's pulling toward neap around August 5-6, and you can feel it in the rips already. Fishers Island Sound isn't ripping the way it was three weeks ago. The Race current still moves plenty of water, but through the Sound itself — Ram Island, the gut off Wicopesset, the flow around Wamphassuc — it's softened enough that I've gone from 2-ounce bucktails to 1-ounce and 3/4-ounce without losing bottom contact. A white bucktail with a chunk of sea worm or a 4-inch white Slug-Go trailer, dead-drifted right along the boulder seams on the last hour of the outgoing, has been the money presentation. Slower water means the bass have more time to look at what you're throwing, so I've slowed my retrieve to almost nothing — just enough hop to keep it off the rocks.

Daytime, the bigger fish have gone deep and sullen, which is typical for early August with water in the upper 60s to low 70s out here. New London's reading around 69.6, and that's cool enough to keep fish comfortable holding tight to structure through the heat of the day rather than roaming. I'm finding stripers stacked on the deeper edges around Ram Island and the outer breakwater rubble in 15 to 20 feet, mostly uninterested until the tide starts moving again. Come evening, though, they push up onto the shallower boulder fields — 4 to 8 feet of water — and that's when eels fished on a light fishfinder rig, drifted right through the wash off the breakwater at dusk and after dark, have been putting slot fish and a few overslots in the net. Nothing giant, mostly 28 to 34 inches, but consistent if you're willing to fish the dark.

Fluke fishing has stayed the most dependable thing going. The channel edges through the Sound — between Fishers Island and the mainland, and the deeper cuts off Ram Island — are holding fish through the peak of the season the way they're supposed to. Drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, over 20 to 30 feet of water on the moving tide has produced steady keepers, with a few pushing 4 and 5 pounds mixed into the shorts. Black sea bass are riding shotgun on the same drifts wherever there's any rock or shell mixed into the bottom — no secret to it, just find hard bottom on the fish finder and drop.

What I haven't seen yet, and what everybody keeps asking me about at the dock, is albies. Nothing. Not a fin, not a nervous bait ball getting hammered from below, not a single confirmed sighting from anyone I trust out past Watch Hill Reef or along the outer rips. That's not unusual for the first days of August — the pattern up and down this coast has warming temperature breaks starting to show at places like Block Island and the Vineyard, and that's the kind of signal that typically precedes the first scout fish showing up here by a week to ten days. I'd expect the first real sighting somewhere out around Watch Hill Reef or the rips off the eastern end of Fishers Island before the middle of the month, not before. If you're itching to start throwing epoxy jigs and Albie Snax, I wouldn't blame you, but I'd save the gas money for another week.

Tog are holding on the same rockpiles as always — Ram Island, the breakwater rubble, the boulder fields off Esker Point — but I'm leaving them alone this time of year and so should you; there's no reason to stress fish sitting tight to structure in warm water when there's nothing to gain from it.

Looking ahead, if the current keeps softening into the neap like it's doing, I'd expect the low-light bite on the rocks to stay the most reliable window through the weekend — eels after dark at the breakwater and Ram Island, bucktails on the last of the outgoing at Wamphassuc. Fluke should hold steady on the channel edges regardless of tide strength. And I'd keep one eye east toward Watch Hill and Fishers Island Sound's outer edge for that first bait-nervous water that tells you the albies have finally found the temperature break they're looking for.
