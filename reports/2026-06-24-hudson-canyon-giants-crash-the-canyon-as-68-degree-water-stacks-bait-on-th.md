# Giants crash the canyon as 68-degree water stacks bait on the 100-fathom line
*Bluefin to 96 inches and yellowfin pushing 60 pounds light up the Hudson on jigs and stickbaits.*

**HUDSON CANYON, NY — June 24** — _by Capt. Tony Rosetti, Zone Writer_

The Hudson Canyon is firing on all cylinders right now, and it's the kind of bite that reminds you why we make the long run out here. Water temps hit 68 degrees on the shelf this week — that's the magic number that brings everything together. The thermocline set up perfect at 45 feet, creating a conveyor belt of bait that's stacking bluefin and yellowfin from the Claw all the way down to the Dip.

Bluefin are the story right now. We're seeing giants — fish pushing triple digits — crashing bunker schools in spectacular fashion. These aren't the usual deep-water grinders. They're up top, feeding aggressive, and they want it loud and fast. The UVT stickbaits are absolutely deadly when you find them blowing up on bunker. Cast into the white water and hang on. I've been running 280-gram jigs with beefed-up hooks when they drop down to 75 feet after the surface feed dies. Work them slow through the water column — these fish are sitting just off the bottom, using that thermocline as a highway.

The yellowfin bite has been more consistent but requires more finesse. They're holding in 68-degree water along the canyon edge, and they're particular about presentation. Ballyhoo on the troll is producing fish to 60 pounds, but you need to match the bait size to what they're eating. The bunker schools are thick but small — peanuts mostly — so downsize your spread. Four-inch ballyhoo rigged on 8/0 hooks, pulled at 6.5 knots, has been the ticket.

Mahi are scattered but quality when you find them. The grass lines 15 miles southeast of the canyon mouth are holding decent fish. Feather rigs behind a teaser spread work, but don't overlook the old-school approach — a simple ballyhoo on a circle hook under a grass mat will get bit. The key is staying mobile. These fish are moving with the current, following the temperature breaks.

The bite timing has been crucial. Early morning — first light to 8 AM — is when the bluefin are most active on top. As the sun climbs, they sound, and that's when the jig bite turns on. The yellowfin seem less affected by light penetration, staying active through midday if you're on the right temperature water.

Current has been moderate but consistent, running northeast at about 1.2 knots along the canyon wall. That's perfect for holding bait against the structure without making boat control impossible. The new moon spring tides earlier this week flushed a lot of fresh bait out of the Hudson River system, and it's been stacking up where the current hits the canyon edge.

Weather looks cooperative for the next few days — light northwest winds shifting to southwest by the weekend. That should keep the surface conditions fishable and maintain the temperature structure we've been seeing. The 68-degree water is the key to this whole bite. It's warm enough to keep the tuna active but cool enough that they don't push deeper.

Looking ahead, I expect this pattern to hold through the weekend. The full moon Friday will bring bigger tides, which should push even more bait into the canyon system. If you're making the run, launch early — the best fishing has been happening before the fleet arrives. Bring heavy tackle and sharp hooks. These fish are not playing games.
