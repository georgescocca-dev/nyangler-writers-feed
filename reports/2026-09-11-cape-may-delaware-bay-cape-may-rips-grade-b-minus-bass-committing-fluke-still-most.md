# Cape May Rips Grade B-Minus: Bass Committing, Fluke Still Mostly Shorts
*The new moon push finally put stripers on the beach and in the rips — the bay's weakfish run is right behind them.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 11** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Give the Rips a B-minus this week. Not a flunk, not an A, but honest, fishable water with real fish mixed into the junk — and that's more than we've had most of August. The new moon on the 10th put the strongest tide of the early fall season through Cape May Point, and that current did exactly what a September tide is supposed to do: it squeezed bunker and peanut bunker tight against the beach, the inlet mouths, and the edges of the Rips themselves. We flagged this last week — said bunker and spot would start filtering out with the first cooler nights and stripers would start staging at the bay mouth — and that's precisely what showed up on the meter and in the chum slicks this week. Not a blitz. Not yet. But real committed feeding, not the half-hearted picking we were seeing through late August.

The Rips fish on the moving water, always have, always will. My father used to say the Rips don't care what the calendar says, they care what the tide's doing, and this week proved him right again. The better window has been the last two hours of the ebb into the first hour of the new flood, when the sand humps off the Point stack current against current and everything holding bait gets funneled through that seam. Boats working live peanut bunker on fish-finder rigs, 3/0 circle hooks, 20-30 feet of water, have been picking at keeper stripers in the low-to-mid 20s with some bluefish mixed in on the same drift. Bucktail guys — half-ounce to one-ounce white or chartreuse, tipped with a strip of squid — have done fine on the same tide stage, working the bucktail slow along bottom where the rip lines meet the flat. It's not a rod-bending free-for-all. It's a pick. But it's a pick with real fish in it, which is the first time we can say that with a straight face since the dog days set in.

Hereford Inlet has been the other bright spot, mostly for the little guys. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay channels off Wildwood right now — a good sign, because a snapper blitz almost always means adult blues aren't far behind as the water cools another few degrees. We're starting to see a few keeper-class blues mixed in with the stripers out at the Rips, and that number should build through the month. If you've got kids in the boat, small Kastmasters or Gotcha plugs on the incoming tide inside the inlet will keep them bent all afternoon on snappers, no complicated rig needed.

Fluke has been the honest disappointment of the week, and I'm not going to sugarcoat it. The keeper-grade fish that were around in July have mostly slid off toward deeper, cooler structure the way they do every September — that's a textbook late-season move, not a mystery. What's left inside Hereford Inlet and along the back bay edges is mostly short fish, one-and-out on white Gulp Swimming Mullets over 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted on the last of the incoming near the inlet mouth. If you want a fluke dinner this week, fish early on the push and expect to cull through a lot of dinks for every keeper. The better fluke bite has moved well north of us — nothing wrong with our water, the fish just aren't holding here anymore this time of year.

Delaware Bay is where the real story is building. Water's holding low-to-mid 70s from Fortescue down toward Brandywine Shoal, and the fall weakfish and croaker run is starting to load into the back bay channels the way it does every September once the new-moon tide gets the current moving harder. Bloodworms and Fishbites on a simple high-low rig, fished tight to the channel edges around Fortescue on the moving tide, have started producing weakfish in the 16-20 inch range with croaker mixed in — not a stacked bite yet, but building, and it's the kind of pattern that tends to snowball through the back half of September. If you're planning a bay trip, target the two hours around the tide change rather than dead low or dead high — that's when the bait gets pushed and the weakfish stack up on it.

Worth saying plainly: this is not drum season. Fortescue's black drum run is a May event, and there's nothing happening on the drum grounds right now — anyone telling you otherwise is selling something. What Delaware Bay is doing right now is the weakfish and croaker handoff, and that's worth the trip on its own if you fish it on the right tide.

Looking ahead, the tidal push from this new moon should keep building bait concentration through the next several days, and the bass and bluefish should keep tightening up on it at the Rips and along the Point beaches. If the wind stays reasonable out of the northwest, the water should stay clean enough to keep the bite visible on top some mornings. Best bet for the next few days: fish the moving tide at the Rips with bunker chunks or bucktails, and check the bay weakfish bite on the change of tide around Fortescue. Both patterns are still building, not peaking — worth the gas, but keep expectations at a B-minus, not an A.
