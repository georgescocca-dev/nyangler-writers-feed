# Nyatt Point on the flood: bunker pods bring blues topside, scup stack Rocky Point ledges
*The herring are long gone up Buckeye Brook, but the bunker showing up in the upper Bay are pulling bluefish to the surface and keeping the scup and fluke patterns honest.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — August 7** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Stood at the rail off one of the piers below the Point Street Bridge last week and heard two guys arguing about whether the blitz off Nyatt was bunker or peanut bunker. Doesn't matter much once the blues are in it — that water was churning white for a good twenty minutes before it shut off like someone flipped a switch. That's August on this Bay. The herring run that fed the spring bass bite up Buckeye Brook wrapped up months ago, and now it's bunker pods doing the work, dragging bluefish up top and holding stripers underneath them on the edges.

The pattern right now is thermal, plain and simple. Upper Bay water is warm, mid-70s and pushing higher on the flats, and that's stacking bait tight against current breaks where there's still some cooler water moving. Conimicut Point is still doing what it does on the last two hours of the ebb — that flat off the light dumps into a channel edge that holds fluke in 12 to 18 feet, and the drift with a 3/4-ounce white bucktail tipped with a strip of squid or a chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullet has been the play. Nothing gigantic being reported, mostly keeper-class fluke in the 18 to 20-inch range, but enough of them dropping to make the drift worth repeating.

Rocky Point ledges are holding scup thick right now, which is exactly where they should be this time of year — those rock piles and the drop just off the point warm up fast in the afternoon sun and the porgies pile on top of them shoulder to shoulder. Small hooks, size 6 or 8, squid strips or sandworm on a high-low rig, right on the bottom in 15 to 25 feet. Bring a five-gallon bucket of patience because once you find the school it's a fish every drop, but you've got to hunt for them — some days they're stacked tight to the rocks, other days they slide out deeper and you need to work the drift wider to find them again.

Greenwich Bay has been a mixed bag. Fluke are in there but scattered — the mud bottom in the middle of the bay holds some, and there's a scup bite along the edges near the marinas, but nobody's filling coolers. That's honest talk: the water's warm enough that a lot of that structure fishing has gone soft in the middle of the day, and the better windows are dawn and the last light of evening when the surface temperature drops a degree or two and fish move up onto the flats to feed.

Striped bass in the upper Bay are doing exactly what warm-water bass do — they're not sitting still in open water, they're tucked against current edges and structure where there's moving water and shade. Nyatt Point and the pilings around the Providence River bridges have been holding fish on the bunker pods, and that's where the bluefish blitzes have been breaking loose. Topwater plugs — a Doc or a Zara Spook worked erratic — will draw blues up when they're actively working the pod, but once the school sounds, switch to a bucktail or a leadhead jig worked just under the bait ball because that's usually where the bass are sitting, letting the blues do the dirty work up top. We flagged this pattern a few days back — said as long as the bunker stayed thick and the weather held stable, the topwater feeds on those schools would keep firing, and that's exactly what's playing out off Nyatt and the river mouths this week.

Prudence Island's shoreline has been quieter than I'd like this time of year, but the east side rips still deserve a look on the incoming tide — that's classic staging water for bass working bait pushed by current, and a live scup or a chunk of bunker fished on a fish-finder rig near bottom has been the better bet over artificials there.

Weakfish reports have been thin, which tracks with where we are in the season — they're around in pockets but not showing in numbers worth chasing specifically. Black sea bass are holding tighter to the rockier structure down toward the lower Bay, and while that's more Newport-adjacent water than pure Bay structure, there's enough rock around Prudence and the deeper ledges to hold a few if you're jigging bucktails or diamond jigs tipped with squid.

Looking ahead, if the stable weather pattern holds through the coming week like it has, I'd expect the bunker-driven bluefish blitzes to keep showing up wherever those pods concentrate — Nyatt, the river mouths, maybe pushing down toward Conimicut if the bait drifts that way on the tide. The scup bite on Rocky Point should stay dependable as long as that structure keeps holding bait, and the fluke drift off Conimicut on the ebb remains the most consistent read-and-react option in the upper Bay right now. If a front comes through and knocks the water temp down even a couple degrees, watch for the bass to spread out of the tight current seams and get more aggressive up top — that's the kind of shift that can turn a good week into a very good one fast.
