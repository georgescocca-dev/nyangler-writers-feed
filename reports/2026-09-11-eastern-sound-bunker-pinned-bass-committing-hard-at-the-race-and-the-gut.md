# Bunker Pinned, Bass Committing Hard at The Race and The Gut
*The new moon jammed bait against the rocks from Orient to Fishers Island, and stripers are finally eating with conviction instead of picking at scraps.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — September 11** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Guys working the rocks at Orient before first light this week are all telling the same story: bass crashing bunker pods stacked tight against the beach, committing on the first cast instead of nosing around and vanishing. That's the real tell. This isn't scattered interest anymore — it's fish making a decision and eating. The September 10 new moon delivered the hardest tidal push of the early fall so far, and it's done exactly what a big push is supposed to do: squeeze bunker schools into the beaches, inlets, and current seams from Fishers Island Sound down through The Race and Plum Gut. We flagged this pattern last week — said bunker would tighten under the building moon and stripers would start committing rather than just picking at stragglers — and that's precisely what's showing up on the rocks and in the boats this week.

Water's holding in the high 60s across the zone — New London reading around 69.4, Montauk close behind at 69.1 — which is still warm enough to keep bait active and moving but cool enough that predators are feeding with urgency instead of lounging. That's the sweet spot for this time of year. Bunker are pushing hard against structure, and every rip line from the Gut to the Fishers Island side is loaded with bait balls getting worked from above and below.

The Race is fishing textbook for early fall: strong current, bait getting squeezed against the edges, and bass using the eddies on the down-current side of the reef to ambush without burning energy fighting five knots of water. Best window has been the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the flood, right at the color change where the rip meets slicker water. Bucktails in the 2 to 3-ounce range — white or chartreuse — dropped to the bottom and worked with a slow lift-and-drop through the seam are drawing the most consistent strikes. If you're running bunker chunks or whole peanut bunker on a fishfinder rig, fish it right on the edge of the reef structure, not out in open water — the fish are hugging the transition line hard right now, not roaming.

Plum Gut has been the more explosive bite after dark. Guys walking out to the rocks at Orient in chest waders around 2 and 3 a.m. are connecting on big pencil poppers and darters worked through the rip on the last of the outgoing. These are not small fish — slot and overslot class bass are dominating the reports, with very few shorts mixed in, which tracks with what's been showing up along the whole eastern Long Island surf complex this week. When bunker get pinned against the beach in the dark, the bigger fish push the juveniles off the bait and take over the buffet. That's who's eating right now.

Bluefish are riding the same bunker wave. Snapper blues are thick in the back bays and creeks, which is the reliable early signal that adult-class blues are close behind as water keeps cooling. Montauk already produced an 18.49-pound blue that took down a $25,000 derby prize this week, which tells you the size class pushing through the region right now is real, not incidental. Expect that same weight class to start showing on bunker schools working through the Gut and along the Fishers Island rips over the next several tides as the bigger blues follow the bait east.

Fluke is the one piece of this puzzle that's fading fast, and that's not a surprise — we called that taper last week too. The keeper-grade fluke have largely abandoned the shallow structure around here and pushed out toward deeper, cooler water, with the better-grade fish now concentrated up around Nantucket Sound and the Shoals. What's left inshore in the Sound is mostly short fish picking at bait in the shallows. If fluke is your main target this week, it's a low-percentage play in the Race/Gut corridor — better to put your time into the bass and blues while they're stacked and feeding hard.

Porgies are still a dependable backup around the reef structure off Fishers Island — high-hook rigs with squid strips on the bottom in 25 to 40 feet will keep a rod bent between bass drifts, especially if the current's too hard to hold bottom cleanly for anything else.

Looking ahead: squid are building steadily up around Rhode Island and Nantucket Sound as that water pushes into the high 50s in the protected coves, and that bait wave is going to supercharge the rip patterns from Montauk right on through the Race and the Gut over the next week or two. Expect the bass and blues to hold their current aggression through this weekend as the new moon's tidal energy stays elevated, with a gradual settle starting as the moon pulls away from new phase — figure the strongest window closing out by midweek before the pattern resets on the next push. If you've only got one shot, take the ebb at the Gut after dark or the last two hours of ebb into the flood at the Race — that's where the bait, the current, and the fish are all lining up right now.
