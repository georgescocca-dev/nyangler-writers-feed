# Conimicut on the ebb: stripers go dark, fluke and scup carry the bay
*The bass slid into the channel and started feeding after sundown, but scup on the ledges and a steady fluke drift are keeping rods bent through the heat.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — August 3** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Ran into Sal down at the Fields Point ramp Tuesday morning, hosing off a cooler that had more ice in it than fish, and he says to me, "Vinny, where'd all the bass go?" I told him the same thing I've been telling everybody who'll stand still long enough to listen — they didn't go anywhere, they just got smart. Buckeye Brook herring run fed those fish back in April and May, pushed a fresh wave of stripers up through the Providence River and into the upper bay. Now it's August, the surface water's sitting in the low-to-mid 70s from Conimicut down past Nyatt Point, and those same bass have dropped into the channel edges and gone nocturnal on us. That's not a mystery, that's just how it works when the top of the water gets too warm for a fish that likes to work.

If you want stripers right now, you're fishing after the sun goes down, and you're fishing deep structure — the Conimicut Point drop-off on the last two hours of the ebb, the channel edge off Nayatt Point, or down around the Bullock's Point Light rip where the current stacks bait against the rocks. Live scup on a fish-finder rig, or a chunk of fresh bunker soaking on the bottom in 20 to 25 feet, has been the trick. A couple guys working the Mount Hope Bridge piers after dark on eels have done alright too, nothing overslot, mostly schoolies to mid-20s, but enough to keep you out there past midnight. Daytime, forget the open flats — those fish just aren't up there feeding when it's 75 degrees on top. Work the shade lines under the bridges, the Jamestown-Verrazzano included, on the front of the incoming.

Fluke are the fish keeping this bay honest right now, and honestly we called this — said last week the fluke bite would stay steady rather than blitz-level, and that's exactly the shape of it. Drifting Greenwich Bay off Warwick Neck and down along the edges of the Bay Channel toward Prudence Island, guys are picking through a lot of shorts to find their keepers, but the keepers are there. Four-inch white or chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets on a half-ounce bucktail, tipped with a strip of squid, dragged slow on the bottom through 15 to 25 feet — that combo has been putting fish in the box on the last of the flood into the top of the ebb, when the current's got enough push to keep your bait moving but not so much you're bouncing bottom like a jackhammer. Sabin Point and the flats off Colt State Park have both been giving up two- and three-pound fish mixed with the occasional four, nothing to write home about size-wise but a legit, repeatable bite if you put in the drift time.

Scup are stacking hard on the rocky ledges right now, which is exactly where you'd expect them this time of year — Rocky Point, the Ohio Ledge off Prudence, and the rock piles around Fox Island are all holding good numbers. Sea worms or squid strips on a high-low rig, small hooks, light sinker just enough to hold bottom in that current — that's a fish-a-drop deal some afternoons, and it's the kind of action that's perfect if you've got kids in the boat or you just want to fill a cooler without overthinking it. Black sea bass are mixing in on some of those same rocky spots, especially the deeper structure off Prudence and down toward the East Passage, and they're a nice bonus on squid strips fished tight to the rocks.

Bluefish are working the warmer flats same as everywhere else along the coast right now — that warm surface mass has them fired up, and the bay's no exception. Greenwich Bay and the flats off Rocky Point have had bluefish blitzing bait on the surface some mornings, and if you see the birds working and the water nervous, throw a Kastmaster or a Hopkins and don't overthink your retrieve, just get it moving. Sizes have been mixed, a lot of cocktail fish in the 2- to 4-pound range with the occasional bigger one mixed in, similar to what's being seen up and down the coast right now as a fresh push works south.

Weakfish, I'll be straight with you, have been quiet. A few reports of fish taken incidentally while fluke fishing down around Prudence, nothing consistent enough to call a pattern. If you're specifically hunting weakfish this week, I wouldn't build a trip around it.

Looking ahead, the tides are starting to build toward the new moon, and that extra current should help both the fluke drift and the night bass bite — more water movement means more bait getting pushed around structure, and that usually wakes fish up on the change. If we get a stretch of cooler nights or a front comes through and knocks that surface temp down even a couple degrees, don't be surprised if the bass start showing daytime activity again around the bridges. Until then, I'd fish the ebb at Conimicut after dark for bass, hit Greenwich Bay on the tide change for fluke, and swing by the ledges at Rocky Point for a guaranteed bent rod on scup. That's a full day, and it's the kind of week where the bay rewards patience over hope.
