# Captree dock talk: bunker in the bay, bass leaning on the inlet current
*The ocean's been ugly all week, but Fire Island Inlet and the bay structure are holding up their end of the bargain.*

**CAPTREE, NY — August 14** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Had a guy at the dock Tuesday morning, coffee in one hand, casting rod in the other, ask me if the bunker showing up off Democrat Point meant the blitz everybody's been posting about up in the Sound was finally coming our way. I told him what I've been telling everybody this week — we don't need the blitz. We've got our own thing going, and it's been quietly good if you fish it right.

The ocean's been a mess. Buoy readings out front have been running close to 4-foot seas on a short, choppy 5-second period, which is exactly the kind of sloppy, confused water that makes surfcasting off the ocean beaches a grind. Democrat Point looked like a washing machine most mornings this week, and I watched guys give up after an hour of getting hammered by whitewater with nothing to show for it. If you want to fish the surf right now, you're better off tucked in behind the point where the jetty breaks up the swell, or working the calmer stretches inside the inlet itself rather than the open beach.

That rough water out front has done something useful for us, though — it's pushed the bait and the bass activity inside, tight to structure, right where we like them. The inlet seam off Democrat Point has been holding stripers on the last two hours of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing, right where that current rips past the rock pile. Guys live-lining peanut bunker on a simple fish-finder rig, 20-pound leader, circle hook, have been picking off slot fish in the 26 to 32-inch range along that seam, with a few better fish mixed in on the deeper edge where the channel drops off. If you don't have bunker, a bunker-colored Doc's Goofy Jig or a white bucktail with a chunk of pork rind gets it done fished right along bottom, slow, right through that hard current.

We flagged this a week ago — said the building tidal flow running into the August 12 new moon would sharpen up the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what's played out. The moving water through the inlet and around the bridges has been stronger and more consistent than it was two weeks back, and the fish have responded by tightening up right on the seams instead of scattering through the open bay. That's the pattern I'd lean on through the rest of this week while the tides are still running big off that new moon.

Fluke have been a mixed bag — not hot, but not dead either. The Robert Moses bridges are still the go-to, working the west channel in 25 to 35 feet on the tail end of the outgoing when the current's still got some push to it. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along bottom, have been the ticket, with a few keepers to 22 inches coming out of there most days. It's been more of a "grind out your limit" fishery than a "load the cooler" one right now — expect to cover ground and cull through some shorts before you find your keepers.

Bluefish have shown up with some teeth behind them. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay creeks around Patchogue and Sayville, which is a good early sign for the adult class fish that'll follow them in over the next few weeks. Out in the bay proper and around the inlet, we've had decent runs of cocktail blues to 3 pounds crashing bunker pods on the outgoing tide, and they'll hit anything shiny moving fast — a diamond jig or a Kastmaster worked through the melee will get bit in a hurry.

Weakfish have been a quiet story, which is about all you can say for weakfish these days. A few have turned up mixed in with the fluke drift off the bridges, mostly on the smaller side, and with the strict slot regulations still in place, most of what's coming over the rail is going right back over. Worth keeping a rod rigged with a light jig if you're already drifting that stretch, but I wouldn't plan a trip around them.

Porgies have been solid around the bridge structure and the deeper holes off Captree, working clam on a simple high-low rig in 15 to 25 feet. Nothing glamorous, but it's steady action if you want to fill an afternoon and bring home dinner.

Looking ahead, I'd keep leaning on the inlet current and the bridge structure while this bigger tidal swing holds through the week. If the ocean lays down even a little, Democrat Point surf could turn on fast once that swell drops below 3 feet — that's when the bass tend to slide back out toward the open beach to chase bait pushed around by the current. Until then, I'm fishing the seams, not the sand, and I'd tell you to do the same.

The bunker schools are still building in the bay, and where there's bunker, there's eventually bigger bass behind them. I wouldn't be shocked to see some real size show up on the inlet current in the next week or two if that bait holds. Worth checking in before you plan your next trip out — I know I'll be watching it close.
