# Race and Gut firing on swim shads as 68-degree water stacks bait
*Big bass crushing butterfish in the rips while bluefish wreck everything else.*

**PLUM GUT, NY — June 24** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The eastern Sound is absolutely on fire right now, and it's all about reading the thermal breaks. Water temps hit 68 degrees this week — that magic number where everything clicks. The Race and Plum Gut are holding massive concentrations of bass, and they're not messing around with small offerings.

I've been running three-way rigs with 8-ounce cannonballs and 80-pound leaders, but here's the kicker: swim shads are absolutely destroying bucktails for size. Seven-inch Tsunami swim shads on the same three-way setup are pulling overslot fish while bucktails are getting the slot fish. It's not even close. The heavier leader material stands the bait off the bottom better in that screaming current, and the swim shad's profile is triggering the bigger fish.

The Gut's been producing on both flood and ebb, which tells you the bait is stacked thick. I'm seeing butterfish and squid getting hammered in 80 to 40 feet across the main rip. The key is following that bottom contour and taking preemptive cranks as you come up the mountain — these fish are sitting right on the structure, and you need to keep that bait dancing just off the rocks.

But here's your warning: the bluefish have arrived in force, and they're absolute savages. Big gator blues are cutting through everything — topwater plugs, expensive glide baits, you name it. Run wire leaders or kiss your tackle goodbye. I watched three guys lose bucktails in ten minutes before they figured it out.

The Race is producing similar action, especially on the slack tide periods when you can work topwater. Yo-Zuri Hydro Pencils and X-Walks are getting crushed at first light when the current eases up. Once the tide starts ripping, it's back to the three-way rigs and deep presentations.

What's driving this bite is the thermal structure. That 68-degree water is holding against cooler ocean water, creating a perfect feeding zone. The butterfish showed up early this year — usually don't see them thick until July — but they're here now and the bass are locked onto them.

Current's been running 2.5 to 3.5 mph through the Gut, which is perfect for drift fishing but brutal if you're not prepared. Double-drop your rigs to remove line belly, and don't fight the drift — let it carry you through the productive water and reset for another pass.

Fishers Island drops are also holding fish, particularly the deeper structure in 55 to 70 feet. Same three-way approach works, but the fish seem more spread out compared to the concentrated action at the main rips.

Looking ahead, we've got a new moon Friday which means spring tides and serious current. That's going to flush more bait out of the back bays and really fire up the inlet mouths. The thermal gradient should hold with this weather pattern, so expect the Race and Gut to stay hot. Just make sure you've got plenty of terminal tackle — between the structure and those bluefish, you're going to lose some gear.
