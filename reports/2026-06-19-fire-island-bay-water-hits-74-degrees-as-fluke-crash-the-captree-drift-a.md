# Bay water hits 74 degrees as fluke crash the Captree drift and bass stage at inlet
*Seven-degree thermal gap between bay and ocean is stacking bait and firing the bite from Robert Moses to the inlet mouth.*

**CAPTREE, NY — June 19** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The numbers don't lie — Great South Bay hit 74.2 degrees this week while the ocean side is holding at 67.7, and that seven-degree thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do. It's concentrating bait on the flood tide and holding fish on the ebb, creating the kind of conditions that make you forget about sleep and start planning your next trip before you've even cleaned the current catch.

The Captree fleet has been crushing it on the fluke bite, with boats coming back loaded after hitting the ocean-side drifts in 25 to 35 feet. The party boats are seeing limits of sea bass mixed with keeper fluke, and the diamond jig bite has been absolutely insane for ling. I'm talking about guys switching from jigs to clams after limiting out on sea bass in the first hour, then picking up three or four ling before the tide turns. The 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are producing the best fluke, dragged slow on the drift with just enough weight to stay in contact with bottom.

What's really got my attention is the bass staging at Fire Island Inlet. The DEC has been active around the party boat fleet — I won't get into details, but let's just say they're paying close attention to size limits and passenger counts. That enforcement pressure usually means one thing: the fishing is good enough that people are getting greedy. And from what I'm seeing, they have reason to be.

The inlet mouth has been producing on the outgoing tide, especially that first hour when the current really starts to rip. Clam bellies on fishfinder rigs are taking bass in the slot, with a few overs mixed in. The key is positioning yourself where the current sweeps bait out of the bay — think about the north side of the inlet where that thermal gradient creates an edge. The bass are sitting right on that temperature break, picking off bunker and spearing as they get flushed out.

Speaking of bait, the bay is absolutely loaded. I'm seeing massive schools of spearing and sand eels from the Robert Moses bridges all the way back to the Wantagh channels. The birds have been working overtime, diving on bait balls that are so thick they're showing up on fishfinders as solid marks. This is textbook summer fishing — warm bay water holding bait, cooler ocean water creating edges, and fish positioned to take advantage of both.

The southwest wind pattern we've been locked into is playing perfectly into this setup. Those 13-knot winds out of 260 degrees are pushing surface water into the bay on the flood, concentrating that warm water and everything living in it. When the tide turns and starts draining, all that bait gets funneled through the inlet like a dinner bell for anything with fins.

One thing to watch: the weed situation is getting thick in some of the back channels. If you're hitting Jones Inlet or working the Wantagh area, green bucktail teasers about a foot above your sinker are cutting through the grass and still producing. The local shops are pushing green for a reason — it's the color that's been consistent when everything else gets fouled.

Looking ahead, we've got a full moon Friday, which means spring tides and serious current flow. That's when this thermal gradient really comes alive, pushing massive amounts of water and bait through the system. I'm expecting the inlet bite to go off at sunset as the ebb really starts cranking. The Robert Moses bridge pilings should be holding bass on the flood, especially if this southwest wind holds and keeps pushing warm water through the system.

The ocean side is fishable but sporty — seas are running 4 to 5 feet with a southwest groundswell, nothing that'll keep the boats in but enough to make the ride interesting. The fluke bite is worth the bounce, especially if you can time it with the slack water windows.

Bottom line: this is prime time Great South Bay fishing. The thermal setup is textbook, the bait is everywhere, and the fish are responding exactly like they should. Get out there before this pattern breaks.
