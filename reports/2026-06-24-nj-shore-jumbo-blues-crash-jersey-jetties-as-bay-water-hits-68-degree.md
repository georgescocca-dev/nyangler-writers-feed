# Jumbo blues crash Jersey jetties as bay water hits 68 degrees
*Raritan Bay stripers still feeding on bunker while fluke action picks up in the inlets.*

**MANASQUAN, NJ — June 24** — _by Joey Napolitano, Zone Writer_

The water finally warmed up enough to flip the switch on our summer bite, and man, did it flip hard this week. Bay temps hit 68 degrees — a solid four-degree jump from the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is stacking bait like crazy on every flood tide. The result? Some of the best mixed-bag action I've seen in years.

The big story is jumbo bluefish returning to Monmouth County waters in force. These aren't your typical snapper blues — we're talking 8 to 12-pound choppers that'll test your drag and your wire leader. I've been finding them crashing bait off the jetties from Sandy Hook down to Manasquan, especially on the outgoing tide when bunker get flushed out of the bay. White metals and large spoons are absolutely crushing them, but you better have 40-pound wire because these fish are slicing through everything else.

The jetty bite has been phenomenal for mixed species. Small fluke are everywhere in the inlet mouths — mostly shorts, but there are some keepers mixed in if you work the deeper holes on the drift. Three-eighths-ounce jig heads with white Gulp Swimming Mullets are the ticket, dragged slow along the bottom on the last two hours of incoming water. The key is finding that sweet spot where the current creates an eddy behind the rocks.

Raritan Bay is still holding stripers, and the bunker schools are thick as thieves. These aren't the monster spring fish, but solid 24 to 32-inch bass that'll bend your rod and put up a real fight. Live bunker on a fishfinder rig is money, especially around the Raritan Bay Bridge pilings on the outgoing tide. The bass are using the current to ambush bait getting swept out of the shallows.

What's really got me excited is the water clarity. After weeks of dirty water from all that southwest wind, we finally got a few days of northwest breeze that cleaned things up. You can actually see bottom in 8 feet of water, which means the fluke are more aggressive and the bass are more willing to chase lures.

The thermocline is setting up beautifully offshore. Surface temps are running 68 to 70 degrees, but there's a sharp drop to 58-degree water at about 45 feet. That temperature break is holding bait, and where there's bait, there's fish. I'm hearing reports of thresher sharks working the nearshore waters, which makes sense with all this bait around.

Moon phase is working in our favor too. We're coming off a new moon, so the spring tides are really moving water through the inlets. That current is key for everything we're targeting — it positions the bait, triggers feeding, and gives us those defined bite windows.

Looking ahead, I'm watching the full moon on Friday. Those big tides are going to really flush the inlets, and I expect the first real push of weakfish to show up. The water temp is right, the bait is there, and those lunar tides should trigger some serious feeding. I'll be working the inlet mouths at sunset with bucktails and soft plastics, looking for that telltale weakfish bite.

The Cape May area is also starting to fire up. Water temps down south are running a few degrees warmer, and the fluke bite is more consistent. If you're willing to make the run, the back bays around Great Bay are holding some real doormats.

Bottom line: this is prime time Jersey fishing. The water's warm, the bait's thick, and the fish are feeding. Whether you're working the jetties for blues, drifting the inlets for fluke, or live-lining bunker for stripers in the bay, there's something biting. Just make sure your tackle is up to the task — these fish are healthy and hungry.
