# Bay water hits 74 as fluke crash the Captree drift and bass stage for the full moon
*Seven-degree thermal gradient stacking bait in the inlet as southwest winds set up the weekend bite.*

**CAPTREE, NY — June 19** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The numbers tell the story this week: Great South Bay water hit 74.2 degrees while the ocean side sits at 67.7 — that seven-degree gradient is what's been stacking bait in Fire Island Inlet and firing up the fluke bite from the Robert Moses bridges all the way back to the Captree channel. When you get that kind of thermal difference, especially with these southwest winds pushing 15 knots, you're looking at a classic setup.

The Captree fleet has been cashing in hard. Party boats are coming back with limits of keeper fluke, and the bite's been consistent from the inlet mouth out to 30 feet of water. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are the ticket — drag them slow on the drift, let the current do the work. Green bucktails with a white teaser 12 inches up the line have been producing the bigger fish, especially on the start of the outgoing tide when the inlet choke point concentrates everything.

What's really got my attention is how the sea bass bite has exploded alongside the fluke action. Diamond jigs in 4-ounce are crushing them in 25 to 35 feet, and I'm hearing about limits coming easy on the party boats. The ling are mixed in thick too — switch to clam bellies when the jig bite dies and you'll load up. That's classic June fishing when the water temps stabilize in the low 70s.

The striped bass are staging for something big. I've been watching the bait movements, and with the full moon hitting Friday, we're looking at spring tides that'll flush massive amounts of bunker and spearing out of the bay. The bass know it's coming — I'm seeing scattered fish from 28 to 35 inches holding in the deeper cuts around the inlet, waiting for that moon tide to trigger the feed.

Surf side has been trickier with these southwest winds, but the early morning bite at Democrat Point has been producing schoolies on small metals and soft plastics. The key is getting there before the wind builds — once it hits 15 knots from the southwest, you're fighting the push all day.

One thing to watch: DEC has been active on compliance checks. The party boat fleet got boarded this week, and they're tightening up on size limits. Make sure you know your measurements — sea bass need to be a solid 12.5 inches, and they're not giving any slack on borderline fish.

The water clarity in the bay has been exceptional. You can see bottom in 8 feet, which means the fluke are spooky in the shallows but aggressive in the deeper water where they feel secure. That's why the 25 to 35-foot zone has been so productive — deep enough for comfort, shallow enough for the bait to concentrate.

Looking ahead, this full moon Friday is going to be the game changer. Those spring tides will move serious water through the inlet, and I expect the bass bite to explode Friday night into Saturday morning. The outgoing tide at sunset should stack fish at the inlet mouth, and if you can get live bunker or fresh clam bellies in front of them, you're going to see some serious action.

The southwest wind pattern should hold through the weekend, which keeps the thermal gradient intact and the bait pushed against the north shore structure. That's where I'll be focusing — anywhere the current hits a hard edge and creates an ambush point.

Weather looks stable with light winds early in the week, perfect for getting out to the deeper water where the bigger fluke are holding. If you're planning a trip, target the first two hours of the outgoing tide and work the 30 to 40-foot zone between the inlet and the Robert Moses bridge. That's where the magic's been happening.
