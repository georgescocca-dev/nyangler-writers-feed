# 180 on the gravel, 210 on the mud: Jeffrey's split personality this week
*Cod are holding tight to the hard bottom while haddock work the mud basins, and the bluefin we flagged last week are starting to show.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 17** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

180 feet on the north end of Jeffrey's Ledge, hard gravel and scattered boulder — that's been my first mark every trip out of Rye this week, and it's still holding cod. Not a slaughter, but steady. Three to six keeper cod a drift in that 170-190 foot band, mostly 8 to 15 pounds, a few pushing 20 on the bigger jigs. The pattern hasn't changed much from last month: find the hard edge where gravel meets a little rubble, drop a diamond jig or a chunk of clam on a high-low rig, and work it slow off the bottom. The cod are sitting right on that structure, not roaming, so precision on the drift matters more than speed.

Slide east and a little deeper — 200 to 220 feet where the bottom softens to mud and clay — and that's where the haddock have been stacking. This is the mud-bottom program I run every August, and it's been paying off. Haddock to 3 and 4 pounds on standard cod rigs tipped with clam, sometimes doubling up on the hi-lo when the school's thick under the boat. The sounder tells the story fast on these drifts — if you're marking that soft, spread-out return low to bottom, that's haddock water, and you fish it different than the gravel. Lighter jigs, slower lift, let the bait sit.

Platts Bank has been the wildcard. I made two trips out there this week and it split — one day loaded with pollock in the 90 to 110 foot range over structure, schoolie-sized but a few pushing 12 pounds, hitting jigs worked with a fast erratic retrieve up off bottom. The next day it was quiet, just hake scattered on the mud edges, which tells me the bait hadn't settled in yet. Pollock chase bait around more than cod or haddock do, so Platts is going to be hit or miss until whatever's pushing that bait through decides to hold still for a few tide cycles.

Tillies Bank and Scantum Basin I fished lighter this week, mostly scouting. Tillies still has cod on the gravel humps in the 150-170 foot range, similar story to the north end of Jeffrey's — small but steady numbers, nothing to write home about but enough to fill a cooler if you're patient and work the edges right. Scantum's been mostly hake and a few pollock over the softer bottom, nothing that changes my plan for the week.

Now the part everybody's been asking about at the dock — the bluefin. We flagged this coming a week ago, saying the bait was building on the ledge and the tuna trend would likely strengthen, and that's exactly what's playing out. I've had giants show on the meter working bait balls over the deeper flats off Jeffrey's, and I know of fish being hooked within sight of the ledge itself this week. It's not the daily circus you'll hear about further south or up in the Maine warm pockets — the water out here stays cooler at depth, typical for this stretch of the Gulf of Maine in mid-August, and that's kept the bite more occasional than reliable. But when the bait balls up tight over the humps, especially in that late-afternoon window when the tide starts to build, the giants are coming up on it. If you're rigging for tuna out here, keep a rod ready on the troll while you're working groundfish drifts — I've had guys hook up almost by accident chunking mackerel off the stern while waiting on a cod bite.

Mackerel themselves have been thick around Cape Ann and pushing out toward Thatcher Island, which is a good early tell — where the mackerel schools go, the bigger predators follow. I'd keep an eye on that corridor between Thatcher and the ledge over the next week as bait continues to concentrate.

Tide's been building toward the August 28 full moon, and that's going to matter more as we get into the back half of this week. Stronger tidal push means better current on the gravel edges — cod tend to feed harder when there's some water moving, so I'd expect the bite on the north end of Jeffrey's to sharpen up rather than fade as we approach the full moon. Haddock on the mud are less tide-dependent; they'll keep producing on the softer bottom regardless of how hard the current's running.

My read for the coming week: keep cod effort on the gravel structure at Jeffrey's in that 170-190 foot band, work haddock on the mud basins deeper, and treat Platts as a game-day decision based on what the morning marks show. If the bait stays put over the ledge humps, I'd expect the bluefin activity to keep building through the week rather than tail off — that's the single best offshore story going right now, even if it stays a bonus rather than the main event. Bring the jig rods for groundfish, but don't leave the trolling gear at the dock.
