# Gator blues crash the Sound as water hits 68 degrees
*Big stripers still prowling the rocks, but the bluefish blitz is stealing the show.*

**EXECUTION ROCKS, NY — June 24** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The Western Sound is firing on all cylinders right now, and I'm seeing the kind of action that makes 30 years on these waters worthwhile. Water temps finally cracked 68 degrees this week — that magic number that flips the switch from spring patterns to full summer mode. And brother, the fish got the memo.

The big story is the bluefish invasion. These aren't your typical cocktail blues either — we're talking gator blues in the 8 to 15-pound class that are absolutely demolishing everything in their path. I watched a school tear through a bunker pod off Matinecock Point yesterday morning like a pack of wolves. The surface looked like it was boiling, birds diving everywhere, and every cast with a 2-ounce Hopkins or Deadly Dick was coming back with a screaming blue attached.

But here's the thing about these big blues — they're not just randomly roaming. They're following the thermocline, and right now that's setting up perfectly along the 30 to 45-foot contours. The 68-degree surface water is sitting on top of that cooler 63-degree bottom water, creating a perfect ambush zone. Fish your metals fast and erratic in the top 20 feet of water, and hold on.

The striped bass fishing is still phenomenal, but it's getting more technical. The slot fish are still thick around Execution Rocks and the Stepping Stones, but the big cows have moved deeper. I'm marking them in 50 to 70 feet, suspended about 15 feet off the bottom. Live eels fished on a fishfinder rig are still the ticket for the 40-plus fish, but you need to get them down to where the bass are staging.

Trolling is producing some spectacular fish too. Large Mojos and bunker spoons pulled at 2.5 knots over the ledges are connecting with bass in the 35 to 45-inch range. The key is working the edges where that warm surface water meets the cooler depths. The fish are using these temperature breaks like highways.

Fluke fishing has really turned on in the back bays. Little Neck Bay and Manhasset Bay are holding some quality fish, with keepers to 6 pounds coming on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and chartreuse bucktails. The grass shrimp are thick in the shallows, and the fluke are gorging on them. Fish the channel edges on the outgoing tide, dragging your bucktail slow and steady.

Porgies are finally showing up in numbers around the rocky structure. The usual spots — around Hart Island and the bridge pilings — are producing limits of quality fish on clam and sandworm. Nothing fancy here, just a basic high-low rig with 2/0 hooks and enough weight to hold bottom.

The bait situation is interesting. We've got bunker schools moving through, but they're not the massive pods we usually see this time of year. The spearing are thick along the shorelines, and there's plenty of sand eels around the deeper structure. What's really got me excited is the squid showing up in good numbers — that's usually a sign that the weakfish aren't far behind.

Looking at the lunar calendar, we've got a new moon coming Friday, which means spring tides and big water movement. That's going to flush a lot of bait out of the back bays and stack it up along the channel edges and drop-offs. I'm expecting the bass bite to really explode over the weekend, especially on the outgoing tides.

The weather pattern looks stable for the next week, with light winds and calm seas. That's perfect for getting out to the deeper structure where the big fish are holding. Just remember — this time of year, it's all about finding the temperature breaks and working them methodically. The fish are there, but they're not going to make it easy for you.
