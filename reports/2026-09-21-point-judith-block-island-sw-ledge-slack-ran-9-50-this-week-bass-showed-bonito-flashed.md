# SW Ledge slack ran 9:50 this week — bass showed, bonito flashed off Sandy Point
*Bigger Harvest Moon tides are stacking bait against the Ledge and pushing the albie run hard toward the Block.*

**GALILEE, RI — September 21** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack at SW Ledge fell at 9:50 this week, and the twenty minutes bracketing it — ten before, ten after — produced better than the rest of the tide combined. That's the window when the current stalls just enough for the bass to stop chasing bait through the rip and start eating it, and this week that window paid off more days than not. The bigger picture: we're riding Harvest Moon tides right now, which means more water moving faster through Block Island Sound, more bait getting pushed around, and more predictable concentration points for anything with teeth. That's exactly the kind of setup that turns a decent week into a good one, and it's why the fleet out of Galilee has had more consistent decks the last several days than at any point since Labor Day.

We flagged this last week — said this zone would track right alongside Block Island's strong fluke and albie pattern, and that's exactly what's playing out. The fluke bite off the Block has been the best-of-season story for late September, and the false albacore and bonito push that started building around the island a few weeks back has now firmly established itself as the defining run of the fall so far. Both patterns held through this week's tide swing, and both look set to keep running into the weekend.

Start with the albies and bonito, because that's where the excitement is right now. Sandy Point and the rip off North Light have both been holding bait — bay anchovies mixed with juvenile menhaden — and when the current is moving right, the surface has been breaking wide open in the mid-morning and again in the last two hours of daylight. Small epoxy jigs in the half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce range, worked on a fast, erratic retrieve, have been the most consistent producer, with olive-over-white and pink-over-white both getting bit. Deceivers in the same color combos, stripped fast on an intermediate line, have worked when the fish get finicky and start refusing anything moving too uniformly. The albies have generally been the more cooperative of the two — bonito remain moodier, flashing through a school and vanishing, which is typical for this stage of the run. Clay Head has produced some fish too, but it's been more hit-or-miss than the Point and the North Light rip, likely because the bait hasn't set up as heavy there yet.

Fluke fishing off Block Island has genuinely been the story of the month. Boats working the drift in 40 to 60 feet off the southwest side of the island have been putting up numbers that are unusual for this late — keeper-sized fish mixed with a healthy amount of shorts, biting best on the back half of the outgoing tide when the drift speed settles into that sweet one-to-one-and-a-half-knot range. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on half-ounce to one-ounce bucktails have been the go-to, tipped with a squid strip for scent when the bite gets tentative. The warm water sitting offshore right now is the direct reason this bite has stretched into late September the way it has — Montauk's readings this week held around 69 degrees, and the water off the Block has been running in that same warm-for-the-calendar range, which keeps fluke shallower, more active, and more willing to chase than they'd normally be by now. That's also the exact condition that will end the bite fastest — the day a real cold front pushes through and knocks a few degrees off the surface temp, expect these fish to slide off the structure and deepen up in a hurry.

Striped bass are the wildcard piece right now, and they're just starting to show the fall pattern everyone's been waiting on. SW Ledge has given up bass to the mid-30-pound class on the slack window mentioned above, mostly on live eels drifted along the edge where the rip meets the flat sandy bottom to the south. A few boats working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs off the deeper water near the Ledge have connected with fish holding tight to the bottom on the bigger tides — that's the bunker-and-bass relationship building the way it does every fall, and with the Harvest Moon tides moving more bait through the Sound, expect that pattern to sharpen over the next week rather than fade. It's not a blitz yet. It's a building pattern, and the difference matters — anglers expecting wide-open surface chaos every trip are going to be disappointed, while anglers willing to work the tide-change windows at the Ledge are going to keep connecting.

Black sea bass have stayed steady and mostly overlooked with everything else going on. Structure around the Ledge and the reef pockets off the southeast corner of the island continue to hold fish in the 15- to 25-foot range, biting on Gulp and squid combos fished tight to bottom. Not glamorous, but a reliable Plan B when the surface stuff shuts down.

Looking ahead: if the current warm-water pattern holds through the weekend, expect the albie and bonito action to keep building around Sandy Point and North Light, and the fluke bite off the Block to hold its late-season strength for at least a few more days. The bigger question is the first real cold front — when it comes, and it's close on the calendar, expect the fluke to tighten up fast and the striper pattern at SW Ledge to accelerate as more bass commit to the bait pushing through on these bigger tides. For now, the smart play is working the tide-change windows precisely — that 20-minute slack at the Ledge, the last two hours of the outgoing off the Block — rather than fishing the whole tide and hoping.
