# SW Ledge slack at 5:52: bass still eating in the dark, bonito nowhere yet
*Neap tide is softening the rips around Block Island, and that's changing how — and when — you need to fish them.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 20** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack at SW Ledge fell at 5:52 this morning, and I had the boat sitting over 58 feet of water with the fishfinder marking bait stacked tight to bottom fifteen minutes before the tide even finished turning. That's the tell this time of year — the bass move onto the structure ahead of the last of the current, not after it, and if you're not positioned and ready when that happens you're watching it happen instead of fishing it. We worked eels on 3-ounce jig heads down through the water column as the tide started to build south, and by 6:10 we had two fish in the 20-pound class and a short one released clean. Not a blitz. A working bite, on structure, on the tide window that actually matters.

Here's the thing everybody needs to understand this week: we're sliding into the first-quarter neap on the 21st, and that changes the whole rhythm out at the Ledge and around Block generally. Weaker current means the rip doesn't stand up the way it does on a full or new moon push — less white water, less defined seam — but it also means your presentation has more time to work before the drift blows you through the zone. I've been fishing slower retrieves and heavier jig heads than I would on a strong tide, letting the eel sink and swim instead of just getting dragged. If you're used to bombing that rip on peak current, back off the pace this week. The fish are still there. The water's just behaving differently.

The striper pattern right now is squarely a low-light and after-dark program, and that's not going to change until nighttime water temps drop back down, which isn't happening in July. Daytime, the bigger fish are sitting deep on structure — SW Ledge, the rip off North Light, and the drop-off north of Sandy Point — and mostly not feeding hard until the sun's off the water. We've had solid results running bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs in 40 to 55 feet off Sandy Point right at last light, and the tube-and-worm crowd trolling the edges of the Ledge at dawn has been connecting on fish in the high-20s and low-30-pound range. There's still a genuinely big class of bass around Block Island this summer — I've heard of and personally seen fish well north of 35 pounds coming off these grounds in the last few weeks — but they're not stupid about it. Dark water, moving tide, quiet approach.

Fluke fishing has been the one thing I can point to and say with confidence: it's dependable. The reef grounds south of the Judith — the rock piles and mussel bottom in 25 to 40 feet — keep producing keeper fluke on white and chartreuse Gulp paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow on the last two hours of the ebb. We flagged the reef bite as a season-long producer a couple days back, and it's holding up exactly like that — nothing flashy, just steady numbers of fish in the 3-to-5-pound range with the occasional 6 mixed in. Sea bass are riding shotgun on the same drifts, and the quality has been noticeably better than earlier in the summer — squid strips or clam on top-and-bottom rigs dropped right to the structure is still the simplest way to put a limit in the cooler.

Bluefish have been showing in pockets off the beaches west of the Point, chasing bait pods that are pushing this direction from the bunker runs further up the coast. Nothing sustained yet on our side, but if that bunker migration keeps moving south the way it's been, I'd expect daylight blitzes to start showing off Charlestown Breachway and working east toward Galilee within the next week or two. Worth keeping a topwater plug rigged and ready if you're running the beach.

Now, the question I'm getting at the dock every single day: where are the bonito. Short answer — not here yet, not confirmed. Water off Montauk's been sitting around 71-72 degrees, and that's in the range where we typically start seeing the first scouts show around Block, but I haven't personally marked one or heard a credible report off North Light or the rips at Clay Head. We called this a few days ago as a two-week window, and we're still inside that window — I'm watching the temperature breaks off Southeast Light and the rip off Sandy Point closely, because that's usually where the first fish show before anybody's talking about them. If you're itching to run out and blind-cast for albies right now, I'd hold off one more week and save the gas.

Looking ahead: the neap tide through the 21st and into the 22nd is going to keep softening the rip structure, which I actually think helps the bass bite at SW Ledge — longer windows, less current to fight, better presentation control on eels and swimbaits. I'd fish the two hours around slack rather than chasing peak current this week. Fluke and sea bass stay the safe bet on the reef south of the Judith regardless of moon phase. And if you want a longer shot, the offshore tuna grounds out toward Block Island canyon are still very much in play for boats running from Galilee — stable weather's holding, and that door isn't closing yet.
