# Peanut Bunker Push Into the Norwalk Islands — Blues Are Waiting at the Door
*The bait told the story before the bite did — and the neap slowdown we flagged last week showed up right on schedule at Stratford Shoal.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — August 7** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

I spent Tuesday morning just watching. Not fishing — watching. There's a difference, and this time of year it matters. Off the mouth of Oyster Bay Harbor and again inside the Norwalk Islands, I found peanut bunker balled up so tight the surface looked dimpled, like rain falling on flat water. Terns were working them in short, nervous dives, not the big diving blitzes you get later in the month, but enough to tell me the peanuts have arrived in numbers worth paying attention to. That's the tell. When the bait shows up in the back bays like this in early August, the blues are never far behind, and this week they weren't.

We flagged this exact move last week — said the peanut bunker distribution would expand and back-bay cocktail blues would spike within two weeks. Didn't even take that long. Port Jeff Harbor, Northport Bay, and the pockets behind the Norwalk Islands are all holding schoolie-to-cocktail blues in the 1-3 pound range, hammering anything silver and small. Half-ounce Kastmasters and small Deadly Dicks worked on the top on the last two hours of the incoming have been the move, especially at first light before the boat traffic pushes the bait down.

The bigger story this week was the current, or the lack of it. We also called that the August 5-6 neap would take a full gear off the daytime shoal bite by Wednesday, and Stratford Shoal proved it out. I fished the rip there Wednesday afternoon on a tide that should have been ripping and instead just sort of shuffled through. Marks were there on the machine but they weren't feeding — stacked tight to bottom in 35 to 45 feet, not chasing anything. That's neap water for you on that shoal; without the push, the bait doesn't get pinned against the rocks and the bass and blues have no reason to commit. If you fished the shoal midweek and came home disappointed, it wasn't you.

The SW blow that rolled through mid-week hit the western approaches harder than it hit us here. Oyster Bay and the water off Larchmont and Hempstead Harbor churned up with some chop and a little discoloration, but the central basin — Stratford Shoal, Middle Ground Light, the deeper water off Charles Island — took it in stride. Cleaner water behind the front actually nudged the daytime bite back up briefly Thursday, right where we thought it would. If you had to pick one piece of water to fish through unsettled weather this week, the central basin was it.

Night fishing has been the more consistent bass program with surface temps sitting in the mid-70s — Kings Point read 74.8°F, and it's noticeably warmer working east, with New London pushing 77.4°F. That west-to-east gradient matters: the fish on our side of the Sound are a touch more comfortable than what the eastern boys are dealing with, but they're still daytime-shy. I've had my best luck anchoring up-current of the Stratford Shoal rip an hour before the tide turns, soaking chunk bunker on a fish-finder rig in 30 to 40 feet, letting the scent work while the tide builds. Eels drifted along the rocky edge at Charles Island after dark have also produced, mostly fish in the 24 to 32 inch range, with the occasional better one mixed in on the peak of the flood.

Fluke fishing has been the one truly dependable piece of this week's puzzle, and it's staying that way as the shallow flats warm past comfortable. The channel edges off Middle Ground Light and the deeper drop-off on the north side of Stratford Shoal, 45 to 60 feet, have been holding fish that will make a dinner. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift with the tide pushing you along, have out-produced everything else I've thrown. Doubles up to 4 pounds have come over the rail more than once this week, though you have to grind through a lot of shorts to find them — it's not a fast bite, it's a patient one.

Porgies are scattered but present on the rockier structure around the Norwalk Islands and the reef pockets off Charles Island, small hooks and sandworms still the simplest way to load a cooler if that's your speed. Blackfish season's still off in the distance, but a few incidental catches on the fluke grounds tell me the fish are already staged on structure, which is normal for this point in summer.

Haven't seen the first bonito of the season yet, and I've been looking — every bait school I glass gets a second look for that quick, torpedo-shaped shadow cutting through the peanuts. Not there yet. Given the water we're sitting in and the bait now stacked from Oyster Bay to the Norwalk Islands, I wouldn't be shocked if that shows up before the month is out, but this week it's still blues and bass doing the work.

Looking ahead, the current is building back out of this neap, and if that holds, I'd expect the Stratford Shoal daytime bite to firm back up by the weekend — that's usually how it goes once the tide gets its legs back under it. If you're picking a day, I'd fish the shoal on the building flood Saturday morning and have a bucktail rigged for fluke as a backup if the bass don't cooperate right away.
