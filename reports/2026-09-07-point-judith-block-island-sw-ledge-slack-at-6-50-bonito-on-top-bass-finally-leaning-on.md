# SW Ledge slack at 6:50: bonito on top, bass finally leaning on the bunker
*The full moon tide loaded SW Ledge and Block Island's rips with bait, and both bonito and the first real striper interest showed up to eat it.*

**GALILEE, RI — September 7** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

SW Ledge ran hard on the full moon tide, and the true slack window this week is landing right around 6:50 in the morning, with the evening turn following about twelve hours later. That's the number to build a trip around. Outside of that ten-to-fifteen-minute window on either side, the rip is moving too much current to hold a clean drift, but right at slack — and for the twenty minutes coming off it — the structure on the ledge is stacking bait and the bonito have been sitting right on top of it, flashing white in the chop before anyone even gets a jig in the water.

The full moon that peaked the night of the 6th did exactly what we said it would when we flagged it earlier in the week — it tightened the bait schools up and down the coast, and Narragansett Bay and the water around the Point are showing the first real striper interest of the fall instead of fish just picking at stragglers on the edges of the bunker pods. That's a meaningful shift. All summer the bass around Point Judith and the west side of the Block have been scattered and mostly disinterested unless bait was pushed right into their face. Now they're starting to commit.

Water conditions support the timing. New London is reading 69.4, Montauk 69.1 — both numbers that say the eastern end of the Sound and the ocean side of the Block are still comfortably in that early-fall sweet spot where bass, bonito, and albies can all be working the same stretch of water without one species crowding the others out. That won't last much longer. Once nights keep cooling into the second half of September, that window narrows and the albies push the bonito out.

For now, the bonito bite off Block Island is the most consistent thing going. They're showing from the North Rip down past Clay Head, working bait pushed by the tide along the drop where the bottom falls from 20 feet to better than 40. Small profile is the key — 1/2-ounce epoxy jigs in bone or pink-over-white, cast into the surface activity and worked with a fast, erratic retrieve. These fish have been keying on rain bait and tiny anchovies, so anything bulky gets refused. The window is short — usually the first hour of a moving tide before the fish scatter — but boats working the drop off Clay Head and out toward Southeast Light have been finding them reliably enough that it's worth building a morning around.

Striped bass are still a mixed bag, and it's worth being honest about that. The bunker schools tightening up in the bay and around the Point have plenty of bass shadowing them, but the bite is not blitz-quality yet — fish are following, not committing, outside of short windows tied to the tide change. The better producers this week have been working bunker schools on the last two hours of the outgoing at the Point, drifting live bunker on a fish-finder rig or working large soft plastics — 9-inch white or bunker-colored paddletails on a 2-ounce jighead — right through the edge of the school where the bass are stacked underneath. SW Ledge itself has held bass in the 25 to 35 pound class right on the slack, holding tight to the rock pile in 30 to 40 feet, but the window to connect is exactly the ten minutes on either side of that 6:50 turn — miss it and the current takes over and the fish stop showing interest in anything but holding position.

Fluke are winding down, as expected for this point in September, but there's still a mixed bite of keepers around the deeper water off Sandy Point and the reef structure toward Block Island Sound on bucktail and Gulp combinations — white 4-inch swimming mullets on 3/4-ounce jigheads have been the most consistent combination, worked slow along bottom in 40 to 60 feet on the last of the ebb. Sea bass are holding up their end as the steadier option on those same reef sites, with keepers coming on squid strips and clam over structure in that same depth range. That fishery isn't glamorous right now but it's dependable, and worth knowing about for days when the wind kills the ledge or the rip.

Bluefish remain the wildcard that shows up uninvited and welcome. They're actively working the same bunker schools the bass are shadowing, and anyone throwing metal into a surface commotion around the Point or off Galilee this week has a real shot at a solid blue in the mix with the stripers.

Looking ahead, the tide is building again toward the new moon push arriving mid-month, and that's the trigger to watch. If the bunker stay concentrated through this week the way they have been, expect the first legitimate blitz-style feeding to start showing along the Point and into the Bay within the next several days rather than weeks. The albie push out of Buzzards Bay and the eastern Sound is close enough that it should start showing off the east side of the Block any day now — when it does, that will be the report worth chasing. For this weekend, build around the SW Ledge slack windows and the bonito push off Clay Head, and keep a rod rigged for bass on the bunker edges just in case the moon does what it's supposed to do.
