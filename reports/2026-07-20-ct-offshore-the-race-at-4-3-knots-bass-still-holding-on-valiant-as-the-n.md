# The Race at 4.3 Knots: Bass Still Holding on Valiant as the Neap Slide Begins
*Wire line and white bucktails are still connecting on the max ebb, but the slack window is about to get a lot more forgiving.*

**THE RACE, NY/CT — July 20** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

Four point three knots. That's what The Race was running on Wednesday's max ebb, clocked off Valiant Rock with the current meter I keep zip-tied to the rail. Down from the 5.1 we saw on the full-moon spring tide two weeks back, and it's going to keep dropping as we slide into Tuesday's first-quarter neap. That drop matters more than anything else I can tell you this week, because it changes how long you get to fish the good water instead of just surviving it.

Here's the read: on a big spring tide, the window at Valiant Rock is brutal and short — you get maybe eight, ten minutes of true slack before the current builds back to a wire-line-snapping pace. This week, with the moon backing off, I'm seeing that window stretch to fifteen, sometimes eighteen minutes on both ends. High water slack at Valiant ran 10:52 this morning, low slack at 5:14 this afternoon, and both times I had rods bent within the first five minutes of the tide going quiet. That's the neap effect — less current speed, longer bites, easier drifts. If you've been getting your teeth kicked in trying to hold bottom on wire line during the spring tides, this is your week to get back out there.

The bass are still on Valiant and around Race Rock, but they've gone almost entirely nocturnal on us, same pattern we're seeing coastwide right now with water sitting warm — 70 at New London, pushing 72 at Kings Point on the west end of the Sound. Daytime bites are there but they're short and current-dependent, right at the top and bottom of the tide change. After dark is where the real fish are showing. I've had my best drifts starting an hour before the last light dies, running 20-ounce wire line rigs with bunker spoons and tube-and-worm combos down through the rip at Race Rock, working the seam where the fast water meets the eddy on the northeast corner. Bucktails — 3 and 4 ounce, white and chartreuse — have been the better call up on top of Valiant itself where the rock pile sits shallower, 20 to 30 feet depending on where you're sitting relative to the pinnacle. Slow-roll them right through the slack, don't rush it.

Plum Gut has been the more consistent producer for numbers this week, even if it's not throwing trophy bass. Sea bass fishing there has been genuinely strong — somebody pulled a 28-inch, right around five pounds, off the structure on the east side of the Gut, and that's not a fluke catch, that's the kind of quality fish that tells you the reef community out there is healthy this summer. Squid strips and sea bass rigs, working 40 to 60 feet of water on the last two hours of the ebb when the current's manageable enough to hold bottom without a pound of lead. Fluke are mixed in with them, and honestly the fluke bite through Plum Gut and out into the Sluiceway has been the most dependable thing on my calendar the past ten days — moving water over structure, that's the whole game. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on half-ounce bucktails, drifted right along the edges where the Gut narrows down near Little Gull and Great Gull Island. Don't anchor there. You want to be drifting with the current, working bottom, not fighting to stay put.

Bluefish have been thinner out here than what the ocean-side guys are seeing with those bunker blitzes running up the Jersey coast, but we've had scattered choppers mixed into the bass around Race Rock after dark, and I expect that to build if bait keeps pushing east along the Sound. Worth having a wire leader rigged and ready even when you're bass fishing — you don't want to lose a good bucktail to a blue you didn't see coming.

No albies, no bonito yet through the Gut or the Sluiceway, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Montauk's sitting at 71.6, which is in the range where they typically show, and the temperature breaks are starting to set up the way they do right before the false albacore scout in. I'd start watching diving birds and nervous bait around Great Gull Island over the next two to three weeks — that's usually the tell before the epoxy jigs come out of the rocket launchers.

We flagged the offshore situation last week, and it's holding up — the warm filament water pushing toward the CT grounds has tuna and shark within range of boats running south out of the Sound, mirroring what the canyon fleets are already into. If you've got the range and the weather window, that's worth the run right now.

Looking ahead, I want to see how the bass respond once we're fully into the neap by midweek. Softer current usually means the fish spread out a little more off the tightest structure, which can mean fewer fish concentrated on Valiant but a wider window to find them. If the low-light pattern holds — and with this water temp, it will — I'd plan trips around dusk and the two hours after, working wire and bucktails through Race Rock and Valiant on whichever slack lines up closest to dark. That's where I'll be.
