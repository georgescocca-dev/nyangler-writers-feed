# Bass stack on Execution Rocks as bunker schools finally push west into the Sound
*Slot fish hitting live peanuts and bucktails as water temps climb past the magic 66-degree mark.*

**OYSTER BAY, NY — June 12** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

After weeks of waiting for the bait to show, the western Sound finally came alive this week. Water temps hit 66 degrees in the bays — a solid four degrees warmer than the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do: stacking bait on the flood and holding bass on the ebb.

The bunker schools that have been teasing us all spring finally pushed west past Matinecock Point, and the bass followed like they had dinner reservations. I'm seeing good action from Execution Rocks all the way down to the Stepping Stones, with the best bite coming on the outgoing tide when that warmer bay water drains out and creates a feeding highway.

Live peanut bunker is the ticket right now — hook them through the back, fish them on a fishfinder rig tight to structure, and hang on. The bass are averaging 28 to 35 inches, with a few overs mixed in. One guy I talked to at the dock pulled a 40-incher off the rocks Tuesday morning, released it clean. These fish are loaded with eggs still, which tells me the spawn isn't completely finished despite what the calendar says.

If you can't get live bait, white bucktails with chartreuse teasers are producing. Three-quarter ounce heads seem to be the sweet spot — heavy enough to get down in the current but light enough to work with some action. Drag them slow along the bottom on the drift, especially around the channel edges where the current breaks.

The Throgs Neck Bridge pilings are holding fish too, but you need to time it right. Hit them on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current really starts to rip. The bass are using those pilings as ambush points, waiting for bait to get swept past. Live eels work great here if you can get them, but bucktails will do the job.

Fluke fishing has been spotty at best. The water's still a bit cool for them to really turn on, and frankly, there's not much bait in the traditional fluke spots yet. I'm marking some fish in 25 to 30 feet off Lloyd Neck, but they're mostly shorts. The few keepers coming up are hitting white Gulp on the drift, but it's a grind.

Porgies are thick around any structure in 15 to 25 feet. Clam strips on high-low rigs will fill the cooler fast. Nothing fancy needed — just find the rocks and drop down. The Stepping Stones area has been particularly good for keeper-sized porgies.

The moon's been working in our favor too. We just came off new moon spring tides, which really got the water moving and flushed a lot of bait out of the back bays. That's what finally triggered this bunker movement. Now we're building toward the full moon on the 19th, which should keep the current strong and the fish active.

Looking ahead, I'm watching for the water to hit 68 degrees consistently. That's when the fluke really wake up and start feeding aggressively. The way the temps are climbing, we should be there by next week. The bass bite should stay strong as long as the bunker stick around — and with water this warm in the bays, I don't see them leaving anytime soon.

One thing to watch: the weekend boat traffic is getting heavy. If you're going to fish the popular spots like Execution Rocks or the bridge, get there early or stay late. The fish don't like the parade of boats any more than we do.
