# Stellwagen Basin in 110 feet: haddock stacked on the mud, whales pointing the way to bluefin
*Whale activity thick along the Edge this week is the tell — bait's piled up, and the bluefin are starting to work it.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — August 17** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

One-ten feet on the sounder along the Stellwagen Edge Tuesday morning, and the bottom paint was so thick with marks I had to run the gain down twice. That's haddock water — soft mud basin, the kind of bottom that holds sand eels and worms this time of year — and the haddock have been sitting right on it, not migrating off it, not spooking off it. That's the pattern I want to see in mid-August.

We flagged this last week — said the bluefin activity around Stellwagen would keep building as bait stacked up on the bank, with whale activity on top a good tell for what's underneath. That's exactly what's playing out. The humpbacks have been thick along the Edge and working north toward Tillies Bank, and where the whales are lunge-feeding on sand eels, the bluefin aren't far behind. I had two boats radio me Thursday describing fish busting right through bait balls the whales had pushed to the surface — that's about as textbook as it gets for a Stellwagen tuna show. Doesn't mean every fish out there is eating a bar jig, but it means the forage base is doing exactly what it needs to do to hold fish on the bank instead of pushing them through.

Groundfish-wise, it's been a two-bottom program all week. Cod are still on the gravel — the harder patches along the Stellwagen Edge and out toward Tillies Bank where the bottom kicks up rougher, 90 to 130 feet depending on where you find the hard stuff on the plotter. Jigging diamond jigs tipped with a chunk of clam has been out-producing bait-only rigs for me, mostly because the cod are eating aggressively right now — they want something moving, not just soaking. Fish have been running average-to-decent size, nothing to write home about in terms of giants, but a solid box of keepers if you work the edges where the gravel breaks up and put in the drift time.

Haddock are the more consistent story. Basin bottom — soft mud, 100 to 120 feet — has been holding fish tight, and a standard two-hook rig with clam bait or a small teaser jig has been getting bit steady on the drift. This is bread-and-butter Stellwagen haddock fishing, the kind that doesn't make headlines but fills coolers. Party boats working the deeper structure on the bank have leaned on this all week because it's dependable when the offshore stuff is more of a gamble.

And it is a bit of a gamble right now, if I'm honest. The bluefin activity is building, not established. I wouldn't tell a reader to burn a tank of fuel out to Carter Canyon or Cape Cod Tails chasing giants unless you've got eyes on breaking fish or a whale show worth following — those grounds are producing on temperature breaks, but the breaks have been moving day to day, and I've had guys find nothing where there was a blitz the day before. The smarter offshore money this week has been staying closer to the Stellwagen Edge itself, working the whale activity as it develops rather than running blind out to the canyon approaches on a hope.

Mackerel have been spotty as bait go-to's — some schools tight to the bank, some not, so if you're jigging your own bait before you set up on tuna, don't assume it'll be there. Check early, stock up, and don't burn daylight hunting mackerel once you've got fish showing topside.

Pollock have been mixed in with the cod on some of the harder structure, nothing to specifically target but a nice bonus fish on the jig when they show. Hake have been around the deeper mud edges of the basin in smaller numbers — not a program I'd build a trip around, but worth a drop if you're already anchored up on haddock and want variety in the box.

Moon's building toward the August 28 full, which should firm up the tide push over the next week and generally tighten up bait concentration on the bank — that's usually good news for both the groundfish and whatever's chasing the sand eels topside. If the whale activity keeps building the way it has, I'd expect the bluefin story to keep developing right along with it rather than fade. The wildcard is whether that offshore warm water holding position near the canyon approaches shifts — if it slides, the temperature break that's been organizing the tuna could move with it, and that changes where you want to be looking.

My plan for the coming week: mornings on the Basin mud for haddock while it's calm, working the gravel edges for cod on the back half of the tide, and keeping one eye on the horizon for whale activity every time we're running between spots. If you see the surface disturbed and birds working tight over it, that's worth a five-minute detour with a bar jig rigged and ready. It's not a guaranteed hookup, but it's the best tell we've got on this bank right now, and it's been paying off often enough to chase.
