# Bonito Hit The Race — Bass Still Sitting on Bait
*The Block Island push finally crossed into New York water this week, but the striper bite stays staged and stubborn until the bunker schools tighten up.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — September 4** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The bonito showed. We flagged this last week — said the Block Island push would ride the current through The Race and Plum Gut within days, and that's exactly what happened. Small, fast, silver fish are working the rip edges off Fishers Island and the eastern face of The Race on the tail of the ebb, and the fleet that got the timing right this week connected. That's the headline. The rest of the zone is still working through late-summer inertia, and honesty matters more than hype right now: the striper bite is present but staged, not committed.

Water temps explain both stories. New London is reading 69.4, Montauk's holding at 69.1, and Kings Point over in the western Sound is up at 70.9 — a real gradient running west to east. That cooler eastern water is exactly what pulled the bonito through The Race this week, and it's also why squid are building around Rhode Island and Nantucket Sound as that water eases into the high 50s and low 60s in the protected coves. That squid show is the next domino. When it thickens up around Fishers Island and pushes bait into the rip, that's when the Race turns from a bonito run into a genuine mixed bag with big bass mixed in.

For now, striped bass in the eastern Sound are doing what they do in early September before the trigger flips — sitting tight on bunker schools instead of running them down on top. Bunker are building fast in Raritan Bay, the LI south shore, and Narragansett Bay, and once that bait tightens under the building moon toward full on the 6th and 7th, the tidal flow through The Race and Plum Gut will do the rest. Increased current means bait gets pinned against structure, and that's when bass commit instead of shadowing. We're not there yet. Expect that within the next week to ten days, not this weekend.

What's actually working right now: bluefish are the most dependable thing in the water. Fresh peanut bunker showing thick off Orient Point and along the Plum Gut rip line is drawing consistent blitz activity, particularly on the last two hours of the ebb when bait gets squeezed through the gut and blues pin it against the surface. Poppers and 2-ounce metal — Deadly Dicks, Hopkins, anything that throws a long cast into moving current — are getting hit on nearly every retrieve when the birds are working. This lines up with what's happening basin-wide; the bluefish bite from here through Montauk and up into Nantucket Sound has been the most consistent story of the whole late-summer stretch, and there's no reason to expect that to slow down through Labor Day week.

Striper presentation needs to shift with the conditions. Fish are staged deep on bait rather than surface-feeding, so the move is dropping a 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktail — white or chartreuse — off the edges at Valiant Rock and Race Rock, letting it sink clean off the drop before working a slow, rhythmic bottom retrieve through the strongest push of current. That's the pattern producing slot and overslot fish right now, not blind-casting the surface hoping for a blitz that isn't here yet. Boats working the deep water on the moving tide at Plum Gut, particularly on the last of the incoming when current stacks bait against the eastern wall, are picking off better-grade bass mixed in with the bluefish.

Bonito tactics are simple and unforgiving — small profile, fast retrieve, right in the seam. Half-ounce to one-ounce metal, Deadly Dicks or Albie Snax in bone or silver, cast tight to the rip edge on the southwest side of Fishers Island and burned back through the moving water. These fish don't sit still and they don't forgive a slow presentation, so match the speed of the current or don't bother. The window has been tightest on the last two hours of the ebb when the rip is running hardest and bait gets funneled through the gut.

Fluke are winding down the way they always do this time of year — doormats abandoning the shallower structure for deeper ocean-side water ahead of the fall run. If you're still targeting them, focus deeper drops around the Gut on the moving tide rather than the flats, but don't expect the numbers of July.

Looking ahead: the next 72 hours should keep the bonito pattern building as more fish push west out of Block Island, and the building moon into the 6th and 7th is the number to watch for stripers. If overnight temps start dropping even a few degrees as we move through the second week of September, expect the first real blitz activity to break loose — most likely starting further west in Raritan Bay or the LI south shore before it works its way east into the Sound. Until then, the smart play in the eastern Sound is working the current edges at first light and the last of the ebb, staying mobile between Plum Gut and The Race, and treating the bonito as the bonus fish they are while the real striper trigger keeps building underneath.
