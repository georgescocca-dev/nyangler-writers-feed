# Cockenoe Over the Cows: Working Around the Weekend Fleet for Bass, Sea Bass, and Scup
*With tautog closed for the summer and the Cows jammed with weekend traffic, the smart play this week is Cockenoe Reef on the early ebb — and it paid off.*

**NORWALK, CT — July 17** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

I made a call Tuesday morning that I want to walk you through, because it's the kind of decision that separates a decent trip from a wasted one this time of year. I had two options sitting in the driveway with the boat hitched: run down to the Cows off Stamford where the porgies have been thick, or tuck into Cockenoe Reef and work the rockpiles on the last two hours of the ebb. The Cows on a Tuesday in mid-July still draws a fleet — day charters, weekend warriors who took a vacation day, paddleboarders drifting through the chum slick. I've fished that pressure before and it's not worth it unless you're first boat on the spot at first light. Cockenoe, by contrast, clears out fast once the sun's up, and the reef structure there holds fish tight to bottom regardless of who's buzzing overhead. I ran to Cockenoe. Good call.

Here's the why behind it. Kings Point is reading 71.2 degrees, which tells you the western end of the Sound has fully turned over into summer pattern — warm, stratified, with bait pushed up shallow and predators using structure and current edges to ambush rather than roam open water. That kind of water doesn't reward covering ground. It rewards picking a piece of structure, understanding which tide stage concentrates bait against it, and parking there. Cockenoe's boulder fields on the northwest corner fire hardest on the back half of the ebb, when the current funnels bait off the flats and stacks it against the rock in six to fourteen feet. That's where I found black sea bass to 17 inches stacked tight, taking squid strips fished on a simple two-hook bottom rig with just enough weight to hold in the current. Nothing fancy — this bite doesn't need it. The fish are there because the structure and the tide put the food there.

On blackfish, I'll be straight with you: it's a non-starter right now. Tautog season is closed through the end of August in the Sound, so if you're marking fish on the reef edges that look like tog, leave them be and shift your bottom rig over to the sea bass and porgy program instead. That's exactly what the Cockenoe trip turned into once I moved off the boulders into the sandier pockets between rock piles — porgies in the 9 to 12 inch range, steady action on bloodworm-tipped hi-lo rigs, best in the first hour of the incoming when the tide starts pushing warm water back over the flat. The Cows are still holding good porgy numbers too, from what I'm seeing and hearing off the dock, but if you want to fish it, get there before six, because by mid-morning the anchor lines and prop wash turn a good bite mediocer than it should be.

Striped bass have been the trickier read. The bait's here — bunker schools pushing through the Norwalk Islands, working the channels between Sheffield Island and the outer islands on the tail of the flood. I've seen bass working under those schools at first light, blowing up on peanut bunker pushed to the surface, but it's a short window. By 6:30, 7 o'clock, the sun gets up and the fish drop back down to the channel edges in 15 to 20 feet. That's when I switch from topwater and swimbaits to a slow-trolled bunker chunk or a bucktail dragged along the drop where the channel meets the flat off Sheffield. Slot fish, mostly — nothing over the mid-30s, but consistent enough that two or three fish an outing isn't a stretch if you're on the water at the right time. Bluefish are mixed into those same bunker schools, and they've been the more reliable player of the two — hit the surface blitzes hard with a topwater popper or a diamond jig ripped through the school, and you won't wait long.

Fluke have been a grind, honestly. I worked the sandy stretch off Penfield Reef on the outgoing tide with white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifting the deeper cuts in 25 to 30 feet, and came away with fish, but they were short more often than not — a lot of throwbacks for every keeper. The water's warm enough that the fluke are there, they're just scattered across a wider area than they were in June, which means more drift time and less certainty per drift. If fluke is your priority this week, I'd put in the time on Penfield's outer edge rather than the shallower flats — the deeper water's holding the better-grade fish.

Looking ahead, the pattern holds if this heat sticks around. Bunker schools should keep working the islands, which means bass and blues will keep showing in short windows at dawn and dusk. Weekend traffic is only going to get heavier through the rest of July, so my advice is simple: fish early, fish the reef structure that clears out fast once the crowds arrive, and don't fight for space on the Cows when Cockenoe or Sheffield's channel edges will give you the same fish with half the aggravation.
