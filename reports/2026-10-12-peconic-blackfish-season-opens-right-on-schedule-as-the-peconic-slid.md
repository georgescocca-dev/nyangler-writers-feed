That's the signature fall transition for this bay: fluke and summer weakfish losing ground, porgies thinning out as they stage for their move offshore, and tautog waking up right on time.

That's precisely what's shaping up. Tog season opened and the structure bite is already building, while bass continue to filter through the bay as incidental fish rather than the main event.

On the tautog, the pattern is classic cold-water Peconic: green crabs and Asian crabs fished tight to hard structure — the rock piles off Shelter Island's ferry docks, the rip-adjacent boulder fields around Plum Gut and Orient Point, and the jetty rocks at Greenport and Sag Harbor. A 3/4 to 1-ounce jig head with a half crab, dropped straight down and held just off bottom through the current, is doing the work. These fish aren't moving far to eat right now — drop it in the wrong spot by ten feet and you'll miss them entirely, which is normal for early-season tog before the bite really spreads out across the structure. Expect this to keep improving through the rest of October as the water keeps sliding into that ideal 50s-to-low-60s range tautog prefer.

Striped bass are still around, but this isn't blitz water anymore — that action has shifted to the ocean-side beaches and the classic fall funnel points further east and south. What's left in the Peconic is a push-through pattern: fish using the bay as a travel lane on their way out toward Montauk and the open Atlantic. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked along current seams near Orient Point and the Gut, are picking up incidental bass for anglers who are really there for tog or porgies. Bunker chunks fished on the bottom near Plum Gut on the stronger stages of the tide have also produced a few better fish mixed in with the smaller stuff. Nobody should plan a trip around targeting stripers inside the bay right now — treat them as a bonus, not the primary target.

Bluefish are fading in step with the bass, which tracks what we expected — bluefish tend to lose interest first as water temperatures drop, since they're less tolerant of cold than striped bass. Anyone still finding choppers mixed into the bunker schools near Orient or Gardiners Bay should expect that window to keep closing over the next week or two.

Fluke are winding down hard. The channels off Three Mile Harbor and Noyack were still worth a drift with white Gulp paired with a 3/4-ounce bucktail into last week, but as the water keeps dropping, that bite is going to thin out fast. If fluke is the goal, this weekend is probably the last realistic shot before effort needs to shift entirely to bottom species. Porgies are in a similar spot — still catchable on sandworm or clam over structure in Gardiners Bay and around Shelter Island, but numbers are declining as the bulk of the school stages up to leave the bay for deeper water.

Looking ahead, expect the cooling trend to continue through the week, which only helps the tautog pattern — the colder it gets within reason, the more consistently those fish set up tight to structure and feed. Tide stages matter more than ever right now; the stronger moves around the new and full moon phases are pushing the best current through Plum Gut and the Shelter Island cuts, and that's where both tog and the lingering bass are concentrating. If there's only one day to fish this week, I'd put it on a stronger tide stage around structure — crabs down for tog, a bucktail in reserve for whatever bass happen to be riding the current through. The bay is shifting seasons in real time, and right now it rewards patience on the bottom more than it rewards chasing open water.
