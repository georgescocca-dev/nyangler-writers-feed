# 120 feet on the Stellwagen edge: bluefin on the bait balls, cod still tight to the gravel
*Whales, mackerel, and tuna are stacked on the northern edge while the groundfish stay honest on the bottom.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — July 31** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

Ninety-five to one-twenty feet, that's where I've had the marks stacked up along the northern edge of Stellwagen all week, right where the gravel humps roll down into the basin. That's the number I keep coming back to. Below the thermocline, water's still cool enough to hold groundfish tight, and up top the surface has stayed warm enough to keep bait pinned in tight balls that the whales and the tuna are both working over.

We flagged this last week — said the bait and whale activity on the Bank would keep building and pull bluefin with it — and that's exactly how it's played out. Mackerel showed up thick on the edge starting Tuesday, packed into balls the size of a truck, and every time we found one there were finbacks or humpbacks slashing through it with birds working the surface. Where you've got that combination, the bluefin aren't far behind. We've had fish from 60 to well over 200 pounds coming up on greenstick and on bait, working the drift along the edge in 100 to 130 feet, mostly on the northeast corner where the current runs harder against the structure. Trolling spreads with green machines and small bird rigs have been drawing strikes, but the better fish came on chunked mackerel fished on a slow drift once we found the ball and shut the engine down. Jigging Shimano Butterflies through the bait itself has worked for the smaller schoolies mixed in underneath.

That's the reliable program right now, and I'd call it one of the more consistent things going on the whole coast this week — not lights-out every set, but steady enough that if you find the bait, you'll get a shot. Two out of three trips out there this week we connected, and the miss was more about sea state than fish; we had a stiff southwest breeze push in Thursday afternoon and it broke up the ball we'd been working before we could get gear in the water.

Down on bottom, the cod bite has been more of a grind, which is normal for late July. The fish are there, but they're sitting tight to the gravel patches in that 100 to 140 foot range on the western side of the Bank, and you need to work them with jigs rather than expect them to come up and chase. Diamond jigs tipped with a piece of squid, worked slow off bottom with short hops, have been the ticket — the fish aren't aggressive in this warm-water stretch, so a subtle presentation beats ripping it hard. We've had keeper cod to 15 pounds doing that, but you'll mark fish and have to work through some short ones before you connect with a slot fish.

Haddock have been the better bet for a full cooler. They're sitting on the mud patches in Stellwagen Basin, 130 to 160 feet, and they've been much more willing than the cod to come up for a bait. Standard high-low rigs with clam or squid strips, fished right on bottom, have been steady — not a banner week, but consistent enough that if you're patient and stay on the mud rather than chasing structure, you'll fill out a limit without too much trouble. Pollock have shown up mixed in on some of the deeper gravel edges near Tillies Bank, and those fish have actually been more aggressive than the cod, hitting diamond jigs worked with more speed and a longer sweep.

Hake have been scattered through the same deep basin water as the haddock, mostly picked up incidentally on bottom rigs rather than targeted, though a few boats working after dark near Stellwagen Basin have done well specifically on hake using cut bait fished right on bottom.

The wind's been the wildcard more than the water this week. We've had a run of light to moderate southwest breeze most days, which keeps the surface warm and bait balled up nicely for the tuna game, but it also means afternoon seas build enough on the Bank that you want to be planning your groundfish drops for the morning and shifting to the tuna hunt once the sun's higher and the wind fills in. Tide's swinging through the back side of the recent moon, current's still got some push to it, and that's helping keep bait concentrated rather than scattered — which is exactly why the whale and tuna activity has held together as well as it has.

Looking ahead, if the bait stays balled up on that northern edge and the whales keep working it, I'd expect the bluefin pattern to hold through the coming week — that's been the single most dependable program out there right now. Worth being at the dock early and idling out to the edge before the wind fills in, because the calmer the morning, the easier it is to spot the birds and the whale blows from a distance and get on the ball before it scatters. For groundfish, I'd stick with the plan that's been working: mud for haddock, gravel for cod, and don't expect the cod to be easy — work them slow and be patient. If a front pushes through midweek like these August systems tend to do, expect a day or two of chop that'll shuffle the bait balls around; when that happens, give it a day to resettle before you go looking for the tuna again.
