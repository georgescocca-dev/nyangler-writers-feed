# Double Creek Fluke Are There If You Work For Them — Blowfish and Kingfish Carry the Bay
*Weakfish have slid off the sedges for deeper water, but blowfish, kingfish, and a patient fluke drift are keeping rods bent through the July heat.*

**FORKED RIVER, NJ — July 13** — _by Gus Papadakis, Zone Writer_

I remember mating on one of the Point Pleasant boats back when I was maybe sixteen, a July morning thick as this one, and an old captain named Sal telling me, "Gus, this time of year the bay gets lazy on you. You gotta go find the fish, they won't come find you." I've thought about that line every July since, usually right about now, when the sedges go quiet and the fluke stop cooperating on the first drift and you have to actually work the boat instead of just soaking a line and hoping. That's exactly where we are this week on Barnegat Bay — not a bad bite, but a bite that makes you earn it.

Start with the fluke, because that's what most of the calls to the shop are about. Double Creek Channel is still the play, but it's not the wide-open drift you had in June. The keepers are there, we're just seeing them stacked tighter around the bends near the range markers and the deeper cuts on the edges of the channel, 12 to 18 feet, and you need to hit the tide right — the first two hours of the outgoing has been the difference between a decent box and a long, quiet afternoon. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, dragged slow right on the bottom, has been outproducing bare bucktails two to one. I had a father-son pair out of Forked River last Tuesday who ground out four keepers to 4 pounds on that combo after two hours of shorts — patience paid, but it wasn't handed to them.

Weakfish are the story of the season winding down, not winding up. May and early June had the sedge banks with tide-run weakies, but by mid-July most of that school has slid off into deeper water, probably staged somewhere off the inlet or holding in the channel humps where it's cooler. You can still scratch a few keeper weakfish working the last hour of the incoming at Tices Shoal, bucktail tipped with a shrimp or squid strip fished slow along the drop, but I wouldn't build a whole trip around it right now. If you connect, treat it as a bonus, not the plan.

Bluefish have been the more dependable fireworks. Barnegat Inlet, particularly the rocks on the north side, has had real morning blitzes on the incoming — chopper blues in the 3 to 6-pound class crashing bunker pods right at first light. Metal — Ava jigs, Kastmasters in silver or chartreuse — worked fast through the melee has been money, and if you want to have some fun with it, a topwater popper thrown right into the boil will get eaten just as quick. That bite tends to shut off hard once the sun gets up over the dunes, so if you're going for blues, go early.

Stripers in the bay proper have gone to their July pattern, which is to say they've gone nocturnal on us. Water in the shallow coves and up around Oyster Creek has been running warm enough that the bass just don't want to work during daylight — I'm seeing better results after dark around the bridge pilings and the deeper holes near the inlet rip, live bunker or eels fished on a fish-finder rig right on bottom. If you're a surf guy, Island Beach State Park at first light on the last of the falling tide has been quietly productive — the ocean side is still cooler than the bay, running in the low 70s off the coast, and that temperature break has been enough to hold some slot fish tight to the bar. It's not a numbers game out there right now, more a one-or-two-good-fish kind of morning, but those fish have had some shoulders on them.

The real bread and butter this week has been blowfish and kingfish, and I say that with real affection because this is the fishing that keeps kids and grandparents happy on a hot afternoon. Blowfish are thick around the Oyster Creek docks and the marina edges at Tices Shoal — small hooks, bloodworm or squid strips, doesn't take much skill, just patience and a bucket. Kingfish have been working the sod banks along Double Creek and out toward the Barnegat Light flats on the incoming tide, sandworm or bloodworm strips fished light on the bottom. Neither of these fisheries will make the cover of anything, but they've been steady when the marquee species have made you work.

Looking ahead, if the wind stays out of the southwest and lays down through the weekend, I'd expect the fluke bite in Double Creek to tighten up around that same outgoing window — moving water is doing the work right now, not the bait itself. The bluefish should keep showing at the inlet as long as bunker pods keep pushing through, and I'd bet the striper bite stays a dawn-and-dusk affair until we get a real cool-down. If you've only got one shot this week, I'd take the early flood at Barnegat Inlet for blues and finish the tide out with a fluke drift down through Double Creek — cover your bases, and don't be surprised if you have to move around to find them.
