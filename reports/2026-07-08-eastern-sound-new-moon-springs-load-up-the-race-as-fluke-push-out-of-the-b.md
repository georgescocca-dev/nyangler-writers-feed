# New moon springs load up the Race as fluke push out of the bays and into the rip
*Building current through Plum Gut and Orient Point is stacking bass in the eddies while bonito make their last-call push into the Sound's eastern mouth.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 8** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Last week the Race was still riding out the slack cycle off the June full moon — current strong but not violent, topping maybe 3.5 knots on the big pushes, and that gave the bass room to spread out instead of stack. Surface water's been sitting warm, upper 60s pushing 70 in the harbors, and a run of southwest wind kept the afternoons choppy and green through Plum Gut. Fishable, but sloppy enough that I was picking my windows — dawn and the last two hours of daylight, when the wind laid down and the rip cleaned up.

That's changing fast. We're building toward a new moon around the 13th, and that means spring tides loading in hard through the weekend. Watch what that does to the Race and Plum Gut — current speed ramps back up toward that classic 5-knot scream, and when it does, the bass quit roaming and get pinned into the eddies behind the rock piles and drop-offs where they don't have to fight the full flow to eat. The window I'm circling hardest is Friday through Monday, July 10–13. High stand at Orient is lining up close to sunset those evenings, which means the top of the tide — when the current eases just enough to let fish feed instead of just hold — hits right as the light goes gold. That's the first real PM flood window we've had since before the full moon, and it's worth building your evening around.

Fluke have been the story of the last two weeks, and it's not really about the Sound itself — it's about the bay water pushing them out. Peconic and the back harbors have gotten warm enough that the fluke are abandoning the flats and sliding toward anything with moving water and a temperature break. That means the mouths near Orient, the deeper edges of Plum Gut, and the rip lines off Fishers Island are holding better fish than the bays right now. I've been dragging 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails through 25 to 40 feet on the last of the outgoing at the Gut, letting the bait bounce bottom on the drift instead of ripping it — the fluke here are eating slow presentations, not aggressive hops. Best fish this week went about 5 pounds off the Orient rip on a chartreuse bucktail tipped with squid strip, but it's not a slam-dunk bite — you're covering water and culling through short fish to find the keepers.

Stripers are still very much a night-and-gray-light game right now. The bigger fish — and there have been some genuine 30-plus-pound reports scattered between here, Block Island, and out toward the Cape — are holding deep in the eddies at the Race in 60 to 90 feet, and they're coming up to feed hard right at the tide change. I've been running big soft plastics and 2-ounce bucktails down into the current seam, letting them swing and stall in the slack pocket behind the boulder fields rather than fishing them straight through the flow. Surface plugs — bone Danny-style swimmers and pencil poppers — have drawn a handful of violent strikes at first light off Orient Point when bait's been pushed up shallow, but that bite has been more feast-or-famine than steady; some mornings it's on for twenty minutes and dead the rest of the tide.

Bluefish are the more dependable player right now. Solid choppers, 4 to 8 pounds, are showing consistently in the rip current itself, especially where the Race dumps into deeper water on the ebb. Metal — Kastmasters and Hopkins, half-ounce to two-ounce depending on how hard the current's running — worked with a fast retrieve through the moving water has been drawing consistent hookups, and topwater has worked when there's bird activity marking bait on the surface. If you want a guaranteed bend in the rod this week, this is closer to it than the bass.

Porgies are stacked deep and reliable on the humps and rock piles inside Plum Gut and around Fishers Island — 25 to 45 feet, sandworms or clam on a high-low rig, bounced right on bottom during the slower current stages when the current's too strong for anything else to hold station. It's not glamorous but it's a near-guaranteed box of fish if the bigger stuff isn't cooperating.

Worth watching: this is the tail end of the pelagic window for the eastern end. First bonito and Spanish mackerel sign has started showing on the ocean side toward Moriches and Shinnecock, and with the bonito corridor widening east past Block Island, there's a real chance the first ones push into the mouth of the Sound around Orient and Fishers on clean incoming water before this window shuts down after the weekend. I wouldn't build a whole trip around it, but if you're already out there working the rip, keep a light spinning rod rigged with a small metal ready to fire.

Weakfish, for the record — still nothing. Water's plenty warm enough, but that species has ghosted this stretch of Sound for weeks now and I'm not counting on it changing. If I've got one day to fish this weekend, it's the Friday or Saturday evening flood at Plum Gut, working the eddy seams as the tide tops out into sunset. Plan B if the bass don't show is simple — drop down on the fluke drift at the Gut mouth and let the bucktails do the talking.
