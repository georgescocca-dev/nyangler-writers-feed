# Marine Parkway Bridge on the Outgoing: Bass Start Working the Bunker
*Bunker pods are tightening at the inlet mouth and the first real striper interest of the fall is showing on the falling tide — here's where to be for it.*

**ROCKAWAY, NY — September 7** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one shot at this bay in the next few days, the play is shore access under the Marine Parkway Bridge, fishing the Fort Tilden side as the tide dumps out toward the inlet. That's not a random call — bunker pods have been building tight against the inlet mouth all week, and with the full moon pulling extra water through on the outgoing, that bait has nowhere to go but past the bridge pilings. That's exactly where the first legitimate striper interest of the fall is starting to show, not just picking at stragglers but working the edges of those pods with some intent. We flagged this pattern a few days back — said warm water and building bunker were going to draw early striper attention while the blitz proper was still a week out — and that's precisely how it's playing out on the water right now.

The full moon that just passed on the 6th and 7th tightened those bait schools up, and the next real trigger is the new moon coming in on the 10th. That's the one to circle. Between now and then, expect incremental improvement rather than a sudden switch flipping — bass showing more consistently on the bunker, more fish willing to commit, but not yet the kind of sustained surface blowup that turns Rockaway Beach into a spectacle. If the pattern holds the way it usually does this time of year, the days around the new moon tide should be when that changes. Worth keeping an eye on Rockaway Beach itself for exactly that — once the bait gets pushed hard against the surf line on a building tide, that's historically been the spot where the season's first real blitz shows itself.

For access right now, the bridge is the smart bet because it gives you options depending on conditions. Shore anglers can work the rocks on the Fort Tilden side or hit it from the Breezy Point side depending on wind direction — with any west or southwest wind, tuck in on the bay side out of the chop. Boat guys are staging just off the pilings on the last two hours of the outgoing, working bunker-imitation swimbaits and bunker spoons along the current seams where the tide rips past the structure. Live-lining a snagged bunker on a fish-finder rig off a slow drift has also been producing when the artificials get looked at but not committed to — bass will often follow a swimbait and refuse it, then eat the real thing without hesitation.

Inside the bay itself, snapper blues are thick and that's the more reliable action if you want steady bites over big fish. Canarsie Pier remains the easiest access for that — small blues in close on the pier lights in the evening, taking tiny Kastmasters, Deadly Dicks, or just a bare gold hook under a bobber if you're introducing a kid to the sport. It's not glamorous, but it's the most dependable bite in the entire zone right now, and it tells you the same story the bunker does — bait is stacking up in the bay ahead of the fall push.

Fluke fishing continues its expected fade. Cross Bay Bridge and the channel edges nearby are still worth a drift if fluke is specifically what you're after, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails or a strip of squid on a bucktail teaser, but the numbers are down from where they were in July and August, and that's normal for early September as those fish start sliding toward deeper water ahead of their offshore run. If fluke is the goal, morning outgoing tide is still the better window before the afternoon heat and boat traffic push through the channel.

Bluefish remain active and aggressive wherever the bunker are holding, which right now means the inlet mouth and the water around the bridge. These aren't the biggest blues of the year but they're plentiful, and anyone throwing metal into a working bunker pod near the bridge should expect company almost immediately. Poppers and darters worked fast across the surface have been drawing the more aggressive fish, while a slower bucktail retrieve near bottom has been picking off bass that are shadowing the same bait but feeding with less urgency.

Looking at the next several days, the setup favors patience over urgency. The bass are here and interested, but this is still the build-up phase, not the peak. If you can only fish once this week, target the last two hours of the outgoing at the Marine Parkway Bridge, or the same tide stage along the inlet-facing rocks at Breezy Point, and focus on where bunker are visibly balled up rather than blind-casting open water. As the new moon tide strengthens toward the 10th, that same pattern should intensify — tighter bait, more aggressive feeding, and a real shot at the season's first blitz showing on Rockaway Beach or right at the inlet mouth. Kayak anglers should keep Dead Horse Bay and the shallower flats inside Canarsie in mind as a Plan B if wind kills the bridge and inlet access — those protected pockets tend to hold bass and blues working smaller bait even when the open water gets uncomfortable. Keep gear simple this week: a bucktail, a bunker spoon, a popper, and a bag of Gulp are covering nearly everything being caught right now.
