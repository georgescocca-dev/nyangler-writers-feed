# Great South Bay bakes past 78°, doormat fluke bail out to the inlet
*Hot bay water is pushing fluke, bunker, and bass toward Fire Island Inlet just as new-moon springs build the current — here's where to fish it.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 10** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The bay finally got summer-hot, and not in a good way if you've been drifting the Nicoll Bay flats hoping for fluke. We were reading 78.8 on the surface last week in the shallow guts, and that's a wall for a flatfish — they don't like water that warm any more than you'd like sitting in a bathtub somebody forgot to drain. What happened is exactly what happens every July: the fluke packed up and moved. Not far, just smart. They slid into the deeper channels, the bridge pilings at Robert Moses, and out through the inlet itself, where upwelled ocean water sitting around 71 degrees is cooler, cleaner, and loaded with the kind of current they want to sit behind and ambush bait in. If you're still working the same bay spots you fished in June and getting nothing, that's why — the fish didn't disappear, they relocated to where the thermometer makes sense.

This week the moon's the story on top of the temperature story. We're building into new moon springs, peaking right around July 12-13, which means bigger tidal swings than we've had since before the last full moon. Bigger swings mean harder-pulling water at the inlet — Democrat Point rip, the Robert Moses bridge pilings, the whole stretch of the Fire Island Inlet channel is going to be moving with more authority on both the outgoing and the incoming. For fluke that's exactly what you want: more current pushing bait past structure, more reason for a doormat to hold tight and eat. For bass, the bigger tide means bunker and peanut bunker get flushed harder out of the back bay on the ebb, which is when the stripers show up to eat them at the mouth of the inlet and along the ocean beach at Democrat Point. That flood-to-ebb transition around sunset is going to be the best two hours of your day through the weekend — write that down.

On the fluke, the bite at the Robert Moses bridges has been solid but not a slam dunk — you've got to work for it. Best fish have come in 25 to 35 feet on the outgoing, drifting slow with 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet, bounced right off bottom where the current stacks against the pilings. I had a 6-pounder come off the third span on the outgoing two mornings ago, and there've been a scattering of 4s and 5s mixed with a lot of shorts, so bring the net but don't expect every drift to be a keeper drift. The inlet itself, right where it meets the ocean off Democrat Point, has also been giving up fish on chartreuse bucktails and squid strips fished slow along the edge of the drop where the sand gives way to the deeper cut. That's classic July water — cooler, moving, full of bait getting funneled through a bottleneck.

Stripers have been a mixed bag, which is honest reporting, not a complaint. Dawn patrol at Democrat Point surf has produced schoolies and a few keeper-class fish in the 28 to 32-inch range on bucktails and topwater plugs worked in that gray light before the sun's up, but it's not every morning — some days it's quiet and you're just watching gulls. Nighttime has been more consistent for size: guys drifting live eels on the outgoing tide tight to the north jetty rocks at the inlet have been connecting with better fish, 34 to 38 inches, working the seam where the moving water meets the slower back-eddy. With the new moon building bigger tides this weekend, I'd put my money on that outgoing-tide eel bite at the jetty being the best shot at a real fish this week — the extra current should push more bait through and hold the bigger bass in that ambush lane longer.

Bluefish have been cooperative and, frankly, a little chaotic, which is how I like them. Cocktail blues in the 2 to 4-pound range are chopping up bunker pods right at the inlet mouth and along the beach off Robert Moses field 5, and if you see the birds working, don't overthink it — a diamond jig or a Hopkins worked fast through the melee will get bit almost every cast. Bring pliers, bring patience for retying leaders, and don't put your fingers anywhere near the mouth.

Porgies have quietly been the most reliable bay fish going right now, since the fluke moved out and the bass are more of a dawn/dusk proposition. The rubble and mussel bottom off Patchogue and around the Nicoll Bay reef structure has been giving up steady porgies on sandworm and clam bait, high-low rigs, standard stuff, but it's meat-in-the-cooler consistent, which counts for something on a hot July day when the kids want to catch something every ten minutes.

Weakfish, I'll be straight with you — nothing. Water's been above 70 for over two weeks now in the bay and I haven't heard of a single report, not one, despite everybody assuming the warm water would trigger something. That old rule about weakfish and 70-degree water isn't holding up this year; the temperature might be necessary but it clearly isn't enough on its own. If you want to burn an hour on channel edges at dusk with a bucktail and a chartreuse worm rig, I won't stop you, but I wouldn't build a trip around it right now.

Looking ahead, the new moon spring tides peaking Sunday into Monday are the thing to plan around. Bigger water movement through the inlet should sharpen up both the fluke bite on the outgoing and the bass bite at the jetty on that same stage. If I had one trip to make this week, I'd be at Democrat Point at first light Sunday working the surf, then sliding over to the bridge pilings on the back half of the outgoing for fluke once the sun's up and the bass window closes. Plan B if the stripers don't show is always the fluke drift — that fishery's been steady enough to lean on.
