# Skip the Fluke Boats — The Real Meat's at the Rocks After Dark
*While the center console crowd drifts Sloop Channel at 9 a.m., the bass are stacking bunker schools on the outgoing tide once the sun's down.*

**WANTAGH, NY — August 7** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Every morning this week I watch the same parade leave the ramps at Cedar Creek and Norman J. Levy — twenty boats running west to drift Sloop Channel for fluke before the heat gets bad. Good for them. I'm not knocking a fluke dinner. But if that's all you're doing in August, you're leaving the best fish of the week on the table, and it's not even close.

The bass have gone nocturnal on us, same as they do every summer once the surface water sits in the mid-70s. Buoy readings off the Bight are showing 75.9 degrees with some leftover swell — 3-4 footers still rolling in behind that southerly blow we caught over the weekend. I told you that blow was going to leave the beach ugly for a day or two, and it did — Field 2 and Field 4 stayed a washing machine through Tuesday. But the water's cleaning up now, and that matters because the bass don't want the surf loaded with sand and foam. They want it flat and dark, with bait pinned against structure.

That's exactly what's setting up at Jones Inlet right now. I've been working the jetty rocks off Short Beach and the east end of Jones Beach on the last two hours of the outgoing, right into the start of the low. Bunker schools are stacking in the deeper cuts along the inlet edge, and where you've got bunker in August, you've got bass working them from underneath after dark. Nothing complicated about the presentation — a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig fished tight to the rocks, or if you want to work it, a 2-ounce bucktail with a white teaser dropped down the current seam where the jetty meets the channel. I had three fish to 28 inches Thursday night doing exactly that, one over the slot that went back, and lost something bigger that just leaned into the current and didn't budge — probably wrapped me around the base of the rocks.

Field 1 is worth mentioning here because the state finally got the night fishing window right this year — dusk to 6 a.m., Friday through Sunday, all the way through November. If you've got your Night Sport Fishing Permit and your DEC registry squared away, that east end stretch is money right now because the current runs hard west into the inlet mouth and stacks bait against that first sandbar cut. I'd rather fish that three nights a week than fight the boat traffic on Sloop Channel any day.

Now — we called the peanut bunker move last week, and it's starting to show. I've had reports of small pods pushing into the back bay creeks off Freeport and up toward the Wantagh Bridge flats, nothing thick yet, but enough that the snapper blues have found them. If you've got kids or you just want steady light-tackle action, small Kastmasters or Deadly Dicks off the bridge on the last of the incoming are getting hit every third or fourth cast. Give it another week to ten days and I expect those peanuts to spread through the whole bay system, and when that happens the cocktail blues and the bass both flip a switch. That's when the real back-bay blitz shows up — bunker pushed into the shallows, birds working, everything happening at once. Not there yet. Close.

Fluke are still around if you want them, but the pattern's shifted with the heat — they've slid off the shallow flats onto the deeper channel edges, 18 to 25 feet, in Sloop Channel and the West Channel drop-offs near the inlet. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the outgoing, are still the ticket, and there are keepers in that mix, but you're working for them now instead of them being everywhere like June. Porgies are holding steady on the ocean-side reefs and the rock piles off Point Lookout — bring sandworms and clam, fish it on the bottom, and you'll eat.

Weakfish talk keeps coming up at the dock, and I'll say this — I've seen more short weakfish mixed in with the fluke catches in the bay than I have in years. Nothing keeper-sized that I've heard of, but the fact they're showing at all in numbers is worth paying attention to. If that trend holds another season or two, we might actually have a real weakfish fishery back in this bay, not just a memory guys tell stories about.

Looking ahead, I'd keep my night gear rigged and ready. If the ocean stays calm behind this pattern the way it looks now, I expect the bunker schools to hold tight to the inlet structure through the week, and the bass bite should stay steady on that outgoing tide — best window right around the last two hours before low, into the first hour of the new flood. If the peanuts flood the bay like I think they will inside ten days, that's when this whole thing gets loud. Until then, I'm at the rocks after dark, and I'm not apologizing for skipping the fluke drift to do it.
