# The Rip Went Green Before It Went Right — Fluke and Albies Holding at the Ledge
*Harvest Moon current is stirring the Southwest Ledge, and both the fluke and the false albacore are answering it.*

Here's what that synthesis says happened. Water temps across the region have stayed stubbornly warm for late September — Montauk holding at 67.3°F, the Sound reading similarly warm from Kings Point through New London — and that warmth is exactly why Block Island's fluke bite has stretched later into the season than it usually does. Warm water keeps flatfish shallow and feeding instead of sliding off into deeper, cooler holding patterns. Layered on top of that, the false albacore and bonito push that's been building out of Rhode Island waters all month has fully established itself here, with the island sitting close to the center of that warm-water intrusion working up the coast. We flagged this exact pairing last week — said the fluke and albie bites would hold together through stable tidal flow — and that's precisely what's playing out now, with the Harvest Moon adding current energy rather than knocking it down.

The fluke bite at Block Island has been the standout story of the back half of September, and it's not subtle. Boats working the drift along the Southwest Ledge and the deeper edges off the island's south side are reporting keeper-quality fish, some pushing into better-than-average size for this time of year, on the standard late-season combo — 4-inch white or chartreuse Gulp paired with a bucktail or bare jig head in the ¾- to 1-ounce range depending on how hard the rip is running. The fish are sitting tight to structure where the current breaks over the ledge, so the drag-and-pause presentation — long drifts with occasional bottom bounces rather than a fast retrieve — has been outproducing anything worked quickly through the water column.

The albie and bonito story is the other half of this week, and it's genuinely strong, not hype. Both species have been showing consistently around the rips and along baitfish schools pushed up by the moving water, with small metal jigs — quarter- to half-ounce epoxy jigs and slim metal like Deadly Dicks — doing the damage on fast, erratic retrieves worked through visible surface activity. The key word there is visible — this remains a sight-fishing game. When the birds pin bait against the surface and the water starts dimpling, that's the window, and it can close as fast as it opens. Boats working out of the Great Salt Pond area have described short, explosive windows rather than sustained action, which tracks with how albies and bonito typically behave this deep into the season — fast, then gone, then back again twenty minutes later somewhere else.

Striped bass are the quieter thread in this week's report, but they're building. Bunker schools moving through the rips are starting to draw bass attention, and with the moon adding current strength, expect that pattern to sharpen rather than fade over the next several days. Bluefish are riding along wherever the bunker concentrate, which is normal fall behavior — they're not the headline, but they're not absent either.

Looking ahead, the next 48 to 72 hours should keep this pattern intact as long as the warm-water setup holds. The bigger tides from the Harvest Moon are still building toward their peak push, which should keep both the fluke and the albie bites active around the Southwest Ledge, particularly on the stronger stages of the tide when current speed does the work of concentrating bait. The real question mark sitting out past this weekend is temperature. Late September is exactly when the first real cold front can move through and start tightening things up — fluke fisheries from Block Island down through the Sound tend to be the first to feel it, since flatfish respond quickly to even a few degrees of drop. If that front holds off, this stretch of warm, current-driven fishing has a real shot at extending into next week. If it doesn't, the smart move is treating the next few days as the window rather than waiting for a better one.
