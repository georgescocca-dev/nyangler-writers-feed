# Bay water hits 74 degrees as fluke fishing explodes in Moriches shallows
*Six-degree thermal gradient stacking bait inshore while southwest winds set up perfect drift conditions.*

**MORICHES BAY, NY — June 19** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

The thermal engine is firing on all cylinders in Moriches Bay this week, and the fluke are responding exactly as the physics predict. Bay water spiked to 74.2 degrees while the ocean side holds at 67.7 — that six-and-a-half-degree gradient is creating a bait concentration zone that's drawing quality summer flounder into the skinny water like a magnet.

I've been working the 3-to-8-foot zones from Tuthill Point to the East Moriches channel, and the pattern is textbook thermal stratification fishing. The warmest water is holding tight to the north shore flats, but the sweet spot is that transition edge where 72-degree bay water meets the cooler 68-degree flow from the inlet. That's where the 4-to-6-pound fish are stacked.

The rig that's been absolutely deadly is a 1/4-ounce chartreuse bucktail with a 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet, fished on 15-pound braid with a 20-pound fluorocarbon leader. The key is the presentation — you need to work it slow and low, almost dragging bottom on the drift. These fish are hugging the sand in the thermal layer, and they want that bait crawling through their zone.

Southwest winds at 13 knots have been setting up perfect drift conditions across the main basin. The wind direction is pushing surface water toward the inlet, which creates a subtle undertow that concentrates bait along the channel edges. I've been timing my drifts to catch the last two hours of the outgoing tide, when that thermal gradient is most pronounced and the current is pulling cooler water from the inlet across the warming flats.

The inlet itself has been producing some surprise striped bass action on the evening tides. Water temperature differential between bay and ocean is creating a mixing zone right at the mouth that's holding bait. I've been working live peanut bunker on fishfinder rigs around the jetty structure, focusing on the 15-to-20-foot holes where the current creates back-eddies. Nothing huge — mostly schoolies in the 24-to-28-inch range — but they're aggressive and fun on light tackle.

Cupsogue Beach has been inconsistent from the surf, mainly because the southwest wind pattern is creating an onshore flow that's muddying the water close to shore. The cleaner fishing has been from small boats working the outer edge of the surf zone, where you can stay in the clear water but still access the structure that holds fish.

Weakfish are starting to show in the deeper cuts around Smith Point. The cooler water from Block Island — running 62.4 degrees — is creating temperature breaks as it mixes with our warmer bay water. I've been finding them in the 12-to-18-foot range on small bucktails tipped with squid strips, particularly on the moving water around the new moon.

Looking ahead, this thermal pattern should hold through the weekend as long as the southwest wind flow continues. The pressure is steady around 1003 millibars, indicating stable weather, and the wind direction is keeping the bay water warm while maintaining that crucial temperature gradient at the inlet. Full moon is Friday, which means bigger tides and more water movement — exactly what we need to keep this bite going.

The key for the next week is going to be timing your fishing around the thermal cycles. Early morning, when the bay water is coolest, focus on the deeper channels and inlet areas. As the sun heats the shallows through the day, move to those transition zones where the temperature breaks are most defined. That's where the bait concentrates, and that's where the fish will be.
