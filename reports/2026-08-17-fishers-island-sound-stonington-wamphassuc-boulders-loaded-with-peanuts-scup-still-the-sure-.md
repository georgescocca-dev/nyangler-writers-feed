# Wamphassuc Boulders Loaded With Peanuts, Scup Still the Sure Bet
*Bait is thick on the rocks from Ram Island to Watch Hill, but the albie run hasn't crossed into the Sound yet — here's where to spend your slack tide.*

**STONINGTON, CT — August 17** — _by Manny Pereira, Zone Writer_

I poled the skiff off Wamphassuc Point Tuesday morning on the last of the ebb and the water over the boulders was nervous — not blitzing, just nervous. Peanut bunker balled up tight against the rock faces, maybe eight to twelve feet down, moving like they didn't trust the open water. That's the tell. When bait hugs structure that close instead of running the flats, something's pushing on it from below or it's just found the cool pocket and doesn't want to leave. Either way, it's exactly where you want to be poling a fly rod or flipping a small soft plastic.

Water's telling two different stories depending on which end of my beat you're fishing. Kings Point up toward the western Sound is reading close to 71, New London's holding around 69.4. That gradient matters here — Fishers Island Sound sits on the cool side of that line, and the bait knows it. It's why the boulder fields from Ram Island out to Wamphassuc and down to Esker Point have stayed loaded all month while water on the Connecticut side gets soft and lazy in the afternoon sun.

The porgy bite has been the one thing I could set a watch by. We flagged this over a week ago and it's held — scup fishing on the rock piles around Fishers Island and both Stonington breakwaters has been about as good as I've seen it in years. Small hooks, size 6 or 8, green crab or clam strip, just enough weight to hold bottom in the current — twelve to twenty feet on the east breakwater, a little deeper, twenty-five to thirty, on the rock pile off the Fishers side. Slack water and the first hour of either tide is when they really turn on. Fish to two pounds mixed in steady, and enough of them that a party of three can fill a cooler in a couple hours without much drama. It's not exciting fishing. It's dependable fishing, which this time of year is worth something.

Black sea bass are riding shotgun on the same structure, deeper rock out past the breakwater mouth and around the edges of Fishers Island itself, twenty-five to thirty-five feet. Bucktail-and-Gulp combo or a simple high-low rig with squid strips gets it done. Nothing huge, but a mixed bag with the scup makes for a good morning without needing to run far.

Striped bass have been the trickier read. They're working the Race hard on the tide changes — that's the one spot in my zone where current does something violent enough to hold fish through the middle of the day, not just dawn and dusk. Live eels or a big paddle-tail swimbait worked through the rip on the start of the ebb has produced some quality fish, mid-20s to high-30s, but you need to hit the window right. Twenty minutes early or late and it's dead water. Inside the Sound proper, along the boulder fields where that peanut bunker is stacked, the bass bite has been more of a dawn-and-dusk affair — low light, small profile flies, sparse Deceivers or Clousers in olive over white, worked slow right off the rock edges at Ram Island and down toward Watch Hill. Daytime, with that bait pinned tight to structure and the sun high, the bass mostly sit under it and sip rather than blow up. You've got to go down and slow to get bit.

Now the question everyone at the dock keeps asking me — where are the albies? Truth is, they're not here yet, not in numbers I'd write home about. I had one report of a single fish chasing bait off the east end of Fishers Island midweek, and I believe it, because the peanut bunker blitz pattern running hard just to our east in Rhode Island water has been building all week on this current cycle. Tidal flow is strengthening again as we build toward the August 28 full moon, and that's usually the trigger that pushes albies and bonito west out of the Vineyard and Block Island corridor into our rips. It hasn't crossed the line into Fishers Island Sound proper yet. When it does, Watch Hill Reef and the outside edge of the breakwater will be the first places it shows, because that's where the bait funnels hardest on a dropping tide.

Fluke have been a disappointment if I'm honest. Little Narragansett Bay and the channel edges near the Mystic River mouth have given up mostly shorts, occasional keeper on a bigger bait — a whole squid strip or a 5-inch Gulp in white worked slow along the channel drop. Warm, stagnant water in the upper bay has them lazy rather than hunting, same pattern we're seeing region-wide. If you want fluke, fish the moving water near the inlet mouths, not the flats.

Looking ahead — if that current keeps building into the full moon, I expect the albie window to crack open here within the next week to ten days, probably starting at Watch Hill and the outer edge of Fishers Island before it ever shows inside the Sound. I'd have a light spinning rod rigged with a small epoxy jig ready on the console just in case, but I wouldn't build a whole trip around it yet. The scup and sea bass on the breakwaters are the sure thing right now, and the striper bite at the Race on the tide change is worth planning a whole morning around if you can hit the window.
