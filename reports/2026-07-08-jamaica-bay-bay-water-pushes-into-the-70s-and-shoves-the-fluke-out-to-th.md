# Bay water pushes into the 70s and shoves the fluke out to the inlet
*Marine Parkway Bridge sets up for a real outgoing bite as new-moon springs build toward the weekend.*

**BROAD CHANNEL, NY — July 8** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay finally got hot — I mean really warmed up, the kind of warm where you can feel it through your waders standing on the flats at Big Egg Marsh at low tide. Surface temps in the back bay pushed into the high 70s, and that's enough to shove fluke off the flats and out toward the channels and the inlet, where there's still some exchange and cooler water moving underneath. That's exactly what I saw fishing the Cross Bay Bridge drift on Sunday — marked bait and marked fish on the meter, but they weren't biting until the current picked up and pulled cooler water through the pilings. The bay's basically told us where it wants us to fish this month: the channels, not the flats.

This week the moon's sliding toward new on the 11th, and that means building spring tides right through the weekend — bigger swings, stronger current at Rockaway Inlet and under Marine Parkway Bridge, and more water moving through the whole system on the outgoing. Bigger tides usually mean a better bite window because bait gets pushed hard and predators know it. I'd circle Friday through Monday, the 10th through the 13th, as the best stretch to be out there right at the top of the tide into the first two hours of the drop. Offshore there's a nice cool upwelled pocket sitting around 71 degrees that's been keeping the ocean side more comfortable than the bay, which is part of why the inlet mouth by Breezy Point has been holding better than the inside water lately.

Fluke are the story right now, but it's not the easy pick-'em-up bite from a few weeks back — you have to find the current seams. The best fish have come from the deeper edge of the main channel off Cross Bay Bridge, 15 to 22 feet, dragging 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails right on the outgoing, slow enough that you feel the bottom bump every few seconds. A few boats working the inlet itself, right off the Breezy Point side in 20 to 25 feet, have been doing better with chartreuse bucktails tipped with squid strips — something about that extra scent has made the difference on the slower bite days. Nobody's filling coolers, but a mixed bag of keepers and shorts with a shot at something better mixed in has been realistic if you put in a few drifts and move when you're not marking bait.

Stripers have been the more consistent story after dark. Marine Parkway Bridge is doing what it always does this time of year — staging bass in the shadow lines and current breaks on the outgoing, especially the first couple hours after the tide turns. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig right up against the pilings have been drawing bites from fish in the 24 to 30-inch class, with the occasional better fish mixed in for guys willing to sit through the slow stretches. If you'd rather work artificials, eels swum slow along the current seams at night have out-produced everything else I've heard about — nothing flashy, just a slow swim right along the edge where the light meets the shadow. Dawn has been decent too, casting topwater — a Doc or a needlefish — off the rocks at Breezy Point when there's bait flipping on the surface, though that bite's been short-lived, maybe 30 to 40 minutes right around first light before the sun pushes them down.

Bluefish have started showing inside the bay again, which tracks with what I'd expect this time of year once the bunker schools push in behind the warming water. Snapper blues are all over the shallows off Canarsie Pier and the flats near JFK, small but aggressive, great action if you've got kids who want bent rods on light spinning gear with tiny Kastmasters or Gotcha plugs. The bigger blues, 3 to 5 pounds, have been mixing in with the striper bite at the inlet and hitting diamond jigs worked with a fast retrieve through the same current seams where the bass are holding. It's not a blitz yet, but the bait's building, and that's usually the setup before things get more aggressive later in the month.

Porgies are steady but nothing to write home about — Cross Bay Bridge pilings and the drop-offs near Canarsie Pier have given up decent numbers on high-low rigs with bloodworms or clam, mostly hand-sized fish with the occasional bigger one to reward patience. Weakfish I have to be honest about — they're basically a non-event again this year. Water's been warm enough for a while now, but the reports just aren't there, a fish here or there at dusk on a soft plastic worked slow along the bulkheads, nothing like the run we used to see. I've stopped counting on them and I'd suggest you do the same for now.

For the week ahead, I'm circling that new-moon spring tide stretch, Friday into Monday, as the best window we've had in a while. Bigger current means bait gets moved harder through the inlet and under the bridge, and that usually translates into a better bass and blue bite on the outgoing, especially right around sunset when the light drops and the fish get bold. If the bass don't cooperate at the bridge, my backup plan is working the fluke drift along the channel edges on the same tide — the current that pushes bait past the pilings is the same current that's been holding fluke tight to the deeper structure. Either way, fish the moving water this week, not the slack. The bay's making that pretty clear.
