# Bay water hits 78 and pushes the doormats to the inlet — new moon springs are next
*Great South Bay is running hot, the fluke bailed for cooler water at the bridges, and building spring tides could open a real window at Fire Island Inlet this weekend.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 10** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Let's talk about the bay first, because it told us everything we needed to know last week. Great South Bay pushed up to 78.8 degrees on the flats — that's bathwater, and fluke don't like bathwater. They pulled off the shallow structure in Nicoll Bay and off Sayville and Patchogue and slid into the channels and, more than anywhere, right into the inlet itself, where the ocean's still feeding in cooler water off an upwelling event. We're seeing 71-degree water push through Fire Island Inlet on the incoming, and that temperature break right at the mouth is exactly where the fluke are stacking now. If you fished the bay flats last week and struck out, that's why — the fish didn't disappear, they just went and found the AC.

Looking ahead, we're building toward a new moon this weekend, and that means spring tides — bigger swings, stronger current, more water moving through the inlet on both the flood and the drain. That matters a lot right now because the fluke bite at Robert Moses and the inlet drift lives and dies by current speed. Slack water in this heat is dead water. But a strong outgoing on a spring tide pulls bait and cooler water out through the cuts and gets everything moving — bass, blues, and fluke all key off that flush. I'd circle Saturday and Sunday morning outgoing as the best water of the week, right as that new moon peaks Monday-Tuesday. Wind's been manageable, nothing blowing us off the water, which is a small blessing in July.

Fluke fishing has actually been solid where the current's working. Guys drifting the Robert Moses bridges — both the east and west sides — are finding fish to 6 pounds in 25 to 35 feet on white Gulp Swimming Mullets teamed with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along bottom on the outgoing. Chartreuse is producing too, especially in that greener water right off Democrat Point where the inlet current mixes with the surf. The keeper ratio isn't lights-out — you're culling through a lot of short fish to find the ones that count — but there are enough quality fish mixed in to make the drift worth running. If you're fishing bait, squid strips on a fluke rig, or a whole spearing/squid combo, tipped bucktails, still gets bit. Depth and current speed matter more than exact spot right now — find where the water's moving hardest through the cut and you'll find the fish sitting on the edges of it.

Striped bass have been a grind in the bay heat, which is normal for mid-July, but the surf at Democrat Point and the beach stretch toward Kismet has still produced at first light, before the sun gets up and pushes water temps around. Live eels on the outgoing, worked slow along the bar, have taken a few fish in that 28 to 34 inch range. Bunker are pushing into the bay in decent numbers right now, and where you find pods of peanut bunker getting nervous near the inlet mouth or along the Fire Island beaches, there's usually a bass or two shadowing underneath. A bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig, fished tight to the bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing, has been the more consistent play than blind-casting plugs in the heat. Bluefish have been mixed in with those bunker schools too — nothing giant, mostly 3 to 5 pounds, but they'll hit poppers and Deadly Dicks worked through the rips off Democrat Point in the early morning before the sun gets high.

Weakfish, I'll be honest with you, are still a no-show. The water's been warm enough for weeks now — well above that 70-degree mark people used to point to — and we're just not seeing them show up in the bay in any real numbers. That old rule of thumb about temperature triggering the weakfish run isn't holding up the way it used to. I wouldn't plan a trip around them right now. If you stumble into a few working a chunk bait drift near the Fire Island Inlet channel edges on a slow tide, count yourself lucky, but I'm not sending anybody out specifically chasing them this week.

Porgies have been steady and reliable, which feels good to say in a week where everything else has an asterisk on it. The rock piles off Captree and the rip current edges near the inlet buoys are holding good numbers of porgies in the 12 to 14 inch class. Small hooks, size 6 or 8, baited with bloodworms or clam strips, fished on the bottom during the last hour of the outgoing and into the start of the flood — that's been the pattern. Not glamorous, but it's a bend in the rod when other things are being stubborn.

One more thing worth watching — there's early bonito and Spanish mackerel activity building on the ocean side farther east, Moriches and Shinnecock way, on that clean incoming water. It hasn't shown up hard at Fire Island Inlet yet, but that warm-water pelagic push tends to walk west along the beach as the week goes. If you've got light tackle rigged for false albacore or bonito, it wouldn't hurt to have it in the boat when you're running the ocean side near Democrat Point — just don't build a trip around it yet.

My plan for the week: I'm fishing the Robert Moses bridge drift on the Saturday morning outgoing when that spring tide really starts moving water, working bucktails first for fluke, then sliding out toward the inlet mouth with bunker chunks as the tide turns for a shot at bass and blues working the bait. If the current's weak or the fluke are scattered, I'll shift into the bay channels near Patchogue where deeper water's holding the last of the warm-bay fish. Either way, I'm fishing the moving water and letting the tide do the work — that's the whole game right now.
