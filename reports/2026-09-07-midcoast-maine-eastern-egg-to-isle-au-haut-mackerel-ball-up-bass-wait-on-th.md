# Eastern Egg to Isle au Haut: Mackerel Ball Up, Bass Wait on the Bunker
*The full moon tightened the bait right on schedule — now it's a matter of which channel holds the fish when the water finally cools.*

**ROCKLAND, ME — September 7** — _by Rusty Pelletier, Zone Writer_

There's an old hand who used to work the Boothbay co-op — retired now, but he'd tell you the mackerel don't lie. When they ball up tight along the ledges and won't scatter even when you run the boat right through them, that's the signal the season's turning, whether the calendar agrees with you or not. He was right more often than the marine forecast. This week the mackerel did exactly that — tight schools stacked off Ram Island and out toward The Cuckolds, refusing to break up even under boat traffic. That's a September tell, and it's been reliable for forty years of watching this water.

The full moon on the 6th and 7th did what we said it would last week — it tightened the bait. Bunker schools that had been loose and scattered through the bay for most of August are pulling into tighter, nervier balls up the tidal rivers and along the Rockland breakwater, and the stripers know it. What hasn't happened yet, at least not in force, is the blitz behavior. The water's still holding summer warmth in the upper bay — surface temps in the mid-60s inside Rockland and Belfast harbors — and until a real run of cool nights knocks that down, the bass are staging more than they're feeding wide open. Dock talk from Stonington to Boothbay this week has been consistent: good numbers of schoolie-to-mid-30-inch bass working the edges of bait, but the sustained, top-of-the-water chaos everyone wants is still a few cool nights away.

The better bass action has been on the last two hours of the ebb around river mouths — the Sheepscot, the mouth of the Medomak, and the causeway at Eggemoggin Reach — where the outgoing tide pulls warm surface water off the flats and concentrates bait against current seams. Bucktails in the half-ounce to one-ounce range, white or olive, worked with a slow lift-and-drop along the bottom of the channel edge, have been outproducing top-water plugs, which tells you the fish are still holding a little deeper than they will once the water breaks into the 50s. Live mackerel under a slip float, drifted along the Owls Head ledges and off Ram Island, has also been picking up better-grade fish, 28 to 34 inches, on the last of the flood into the first hour of ebb.

Offshore of the bay proper, Monhegan is fishing the way it usually does in early September — pollock stacked and feeding right on the surface over the ledges south and east of the island, especially on the last two hours of the ebb when bait gets pushed off the rock and funnels into the current. Diamond jigs worked fast through the top ten feet of water have been the easiest way to connect, and there's been enough surface commotion out there that you don't need much finesse — mackerel-pattern jigs or even a bare hook dressed with tube have worked when the pollock are actively slashing bait. Cod and haddock are holding deeper and scattered, as they've been most of the summer — the deeper channels of outer Penobscot Bay, the water off Matinicus, and the drop-offs east of Vinalhaven have given up a fish here and there on bottom rigs with clam or herring, but nobody's calling it consistent. That fishery in this bay has been thin for years and this week didn't change that pattern — worth a drop if you're passing through on the way to something else, not worth building a whole trip around.

Eastern Egg Rock remains a good bet for both puffins and bass through the rest of September — the tern and puffin colony draws bait activity that in turn holds stripers along the edges of the rock, particularly on the last of an outgoing tide when current runs hard past the ledges. Small profile soft plastics — 4-inch paddle tails in bone or chartreuse — fished on light jig heads have worked better than bucktails out there this week, likely because the bait balling up around the rock has been mostly smaller peanut bunker and sand eels rather than adult mackerel.

Bluefish have started showing in modest numbers mixed with the bass around the bay mouths, nothing like the bigger blitzes further south but enough that a few boats working bunker schools near Rockport and Camden have picked up fish in the 3-to-5-pound class on the same bucktails intended for stripers.

Looking at the next several days, the pattern to watch is straightforward: nights are starting to run cooler, and if that trend holds through the coming week, expect the bass to shift from staging behavior into more aggressive feeding on the tightening bait schools — the kind of activity that turns a slow morning into a fast one in the space of twenty minutes. The tide sequence favors early morning and the last two hours of the ebb for the next several days. If there's only one window to fish, that ebb tide at a river mouth with bait visibly nervous on the surface is the one worth the trip. Keep an eye on water temp — once the upper bay drops out of the mid-60s, that's when this turns from a good week into the real fall run.
