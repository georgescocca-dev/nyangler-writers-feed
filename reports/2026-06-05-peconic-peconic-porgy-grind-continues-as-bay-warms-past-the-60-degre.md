# Peconic porgy grind continues as bay warms past the 60-degree threshold
*Estuary thermal stratification between inside bay water and Plum Gut keeps scup pinned to chum lines while weakfish numbers slowly build.*

**GREENPORT, NY — June 5** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The analyst desk came in thin this morning — no live buoy pull from the Peconic stations, and the SST package on file dates back to June 3 rather than today. That puts us reasoning from the last known thermal picture and the trajectory of the past two weeks. As of the most recent published imagery, inside-bay water from Noyack through Little Peconic and up into Southold Bay was running solidly into the low-to-mid 60s, with Gardiners and the approaches to Plum Gut and Orient lagging behind by six to ten degrees — the same inside/outside split that has defined this corner of the world since the bass first showed. Wind has been manageable, the bay glassed off through much of last week, and there is no indication the thermocline between the shallow bay basins and the deeper, tide-flushed water off Orient has closed yet.

What that means for the beat is straightforward. The porgies are locked into the warmer inside water — Jessup's, the Greenlawns, the Middle Grounds, the drops off the south side of Shelter Island — and they are responding to chum and tide stage more than to location alone. The bass that are in the system are still relating to the cold edge: Plum Gut, the rips off Orient, the deeper water where bay outflow meets Block Island Sound water in the upper 50s. Weakfish, which historically key on a 60-degree threshold in this estuary, are just beginning to register in respectable numbers, and that is consistent with the bay finally settling into their preferred range over the last ten days. Fluke are filling into the channels but the class is still mixed-heavy on shorts.

The porgy bite this spring has been a notch off the historic May standard — fewer of the easy, anchor-anywhere-and-load-the-box days that this bay is famous for. The fish are present in good size, with plenty of 16- to 19-inch jumbos pushing three pounds in the mix, but they are demanding the right ingredients: a working chum slick of clam, a clean drift or anchor set on a defined edge, and the flood tide doing the work. Drop on the wrong piece of bottom or get there during slack and you will sit. Spots near Shelter Island and the Jessup's complex have been the most reliable producers, while the Greenlawns and Middle Grounds have been streaky — boats sitting on top of marks without converting.

Weakfish have moved from a single-stray-per-trip oddity in mid-May to a legitimate secondary target by month's end, with keeper-class fish to four pounds turning up on porgy drifts and on dedicated drifts over the traditional weakfish bottom inside the bay. The pattern matches the thermal arc — these fish do not commit to the Peconics until the bottom water settles above 60, and we are there. On the bass side, the Plum Gut and Orient story has been a grind-it-out proposition, with slot-class fish available to those willing to work the tide and accept that the inside bay water is still six to ten degrees warmer than the Gut itself. As that gap closes through June, the bass will redistribute.

Looking ahead three to five days, the controlling variable is whether the inside bay pushes through 65 degrees and whether the Gut catches up into the upper 50s. If the warming trend holds and we get the light southwest pattern that has been dominating, the weakfish bite should consolidate into something a reasonable angler can plan a trip around rather than stumble into. The porgies will continue to demand chum discipline but should hold their size class through the moon. Fluke drifts in the deeper channels between Shelter Island and Noyack will start to convert better as the bait — spearing and small bunker — finishes pushing into the bay. Bass in the Gut become a window play: first and last light, hard outgoing, and likely shifting from the cold edge toward the bay proper as the thermal gradient relaxes.

The bay is doing what the bay does in early June — running a touch behind a classic year on the porgy front, building toward a real weakfish window, and holding bass on the cold edge until the thermal door opens. I would be fishing Jessup's on the flood with clam chum, prospecting weakfish on the tide changes, and saving the Orient and Plum Gut bass mission for a dawn outgoing rather than a midday run. The fish are here. The water is telling you which ones and where.
