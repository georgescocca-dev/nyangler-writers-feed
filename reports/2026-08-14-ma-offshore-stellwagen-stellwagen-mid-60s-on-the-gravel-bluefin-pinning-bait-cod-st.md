# Stellwagen mid-60s on the gravel: bluefin pinning bait, cod still deep and stubborn
*Whales working the Basin, mackerel balled up thick, and the tuna bite we flagged a week ago is finally paying off boats that stuck with it.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — August 14** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

128 feet on the gravel edge, water in the low-to-mid 60s at depth — that's the number that matters this week on Stellwagen. Surface is warmer, pushing summer temps, but drop a probe down through the thermocline and it's the same cold, green, nutrient-rich water mass we've had since the wind swung north-northeast a couple weeks back. That cold layer is why the bank's still fishing like the bank, even in the middle of August.

We flagged this last week — said the bluefin show would keep building as long as that cold water pattern held, and here it is. Whales are up and working the surface out over Stellwagen Basin most mornings, which is about the best tell you'll get that the bait's stacked underneath them. Mackerel schools are dense and tight, balled up defensively, and where you find the whales you're finding bluefin working the same bait from below. We've had boats jigging green machines and diamond jigs through the bait balls picking up school fish in the 60-to-100 pound class, and a few giants have shown up chasing the same pods along the Stellwagen Edge where the bank drops off into deeper water. It's not a fish-every-cast bite — you're reading birds, reading whales, and putting the boat in the right spot at the right time, but the ingredients are all there right now in a way they haven't been most of the summer.

Groundfish has been the tougher story, if I'm honest with you. Cod on the gravel patches out toward Tillies Bank have been there, but they're deep and they're not eager — we're marking fish on the sounder in that 100-to-130 foot range but you've got to put bait right on their nose. Clam and squid combo rigs on a standard cod rig, fished tight to bottom on the slower part of the tide, has been the difference between a couple keepers and a skunk. Jigging has been spotty; the fish are sitting heavy on structure and not chasing much this time of year, which tells me they're already keying in on the same bait the tuna are working, just staying low and lazy about it.

Haddock on the mud has been the more consistent of the two. Standard drift-and-jig over the softer bottom areas off the western side of the bank has produced decent numbers, fish running average size with a few chunky ones mixed in. Circle hooks and cut bait on a two-hook bottom rig, fished on the slower drift days, has out-produced straight jigging for us — haddock this time of year seem to want something sitting still more than something bouncing.

Carter Canyon has been more of a transition zone this week than a destination — we've marked bait moving through there but haven't had the kind of concentrated whale activity or consistent tuna sign that would pull me off the bank proper. Same with Cape Cod Tails — some scattered mackerel sign, nothing that's translated into a reliable bite yet. I wouldn't burn a day dedicated to either right now, but I'd keep half an eye on them as the bait shifts around over the next week or two, because that's usually how it goes out here — the bait moves, and a week later the fish follow.

Tide's been mixed this week, working through a post-new-moon cycle, and the stronger pushes have generally produced better drift speed for both the groundfish guys and the tuna guys running lines through bait schools. Slack water mornings have been quieter across the board — cod go tight to bottom, haddock stop moving, and the tuna scatter out until the current picks back up and reorganizes the bait. If you're planning a trip, I'd build it around the stronger half of the tide rather than fighting slack water hoping something happens.

Looking ahead, if that cold water mass holds — and there's no sign right now that it's breaking down — I'd expect the bluefin pattern on Stellwagen to keep building through the back half of August. Bait density is the whole story here, and right now it's about as good as we've seen it this summer. Cod's going to stay a grind on the gravel until we get some cooler nights working the water column, but haddock on the mud should stay steady if the tide cooperates. Best case, the whales keep working the Basin and the tuna sign holds through the week — worth putting a trip together around that if you can get the weather. If the wind shifts and knocks that cold layer around, I'd expect the tuna to scatter wider and get harder to pin down, so I wouldn't wait too long to get out there while it's holding together.
