# Jeffrey's in 180 feet: haddock working the mud, cod tight to the gravel edge
*The bottom is doing exactly what it should for early September — the question this week is whether the tuna show early on the ledge or wait for the usual October push.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — September 4** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

One-eighty feet of water on the northern end of Jeffrey's Ledge, soft mud bottom, and the haddock have been sitting right where they're supposed to be for this time of year. That's the read from the fleet this week, and it's holding steady. Move a half mile east onto the gravel patches where the bottom hardens up and the depth comes up into the 130-to-150 range, and that's where the cod have been stacking — not thick, but consistent, which is the more honest way to describe most of this beat's groundfish action right now.

The pattern is textbook late-summer Gulf of Maine. Haddock don't want the hard stuff. They sit on mud and clay bottom where the worms and small crustaceans live, and they'll hold in loose pods that move with the tide rather than camping on one piece of structure. Cod are the opposite — they want the transition zones, gravel-to-boulder edges where bait gets pinned against structure on the drift. Both patterns have been repeating themselves day after day on Jeffrey's, which tells you the water column hasn't been kicked around by much weather lately. Stable conditions mean stable fish behavior, and that's exactly what's been showing up trip after trip.

Platts Bank has been the more interesting stop for boats willing to run the extra distance. The pollock have been sitting up off bottom on the sonar in 20-to-40 feet off the peak, which usually means they're chasing bait suspended mid-water rather than glued to the structure itself. Diamond jigs worked with a fast, erratic retrieat through that suspended layer have been outproducing straight bottom bouncing this week — when pollock come up off the rock, you have to meet them where they are instead of fishing the bottom like it's cod.

Tillies Bank has been quieter than Platts, and that's not unusual for early September. The hake bite there tends to wait for evening and the first hour after dark, when they slide up off the mud flats to feed. Boats staying out late with cut bait or squid strips on a two-hook bottom rig in 150-to-180 feet have picked at decent whiting-class hake, but it's not a daytime program right now.

Scantum Basin continues to be the deep-water wild card. The mud there runs deeper than most of the ledge, and the haddock that hold in that basin tend to run a little larger on average — probably because fewer boats bother making the run. Anyone fishing clam or squid on a standard high-low rig in that basin has reported solid average-size fish, though the numbers haven't been eye-popping. It's a quality-over-quantity spot this week.

On the tuna side, there's chatter worth flagging without overselling it. A few boats working the deeper edges off Jeffrey's toward Stellwagen Bank's northern approaches have reported some scattered bluefin interest — nothing like the giant class fish this ledge is known for in October, but enough surface activity and bait balling to suggest warmer water is nosing further into the Gulf of Maine than typical for early September. That lines up with what's been reported from deep-drop swordfish activity pushing further north than usual this year. Whether that warm filament holds through Labor Day week and starts pulling real numbers of giants up onto the ledge early, or fades back south with the first real cool front, is the single biggest question for the next two weeks. Historically the giants don't show in consistent numbers until October, when the mackerel and herring bunch up tight over the ledge and hold there through the fall. Right now it's mackerel schools, not giant bluefin, doing most of the work — solid jigging on small diamond jigs and Sabiki rigs in 60-to-100 feet along the western edge of Jeffrey's has kept bait tanks full for anyone bottom fishing gravel for cod.

Weather-wise, it's been a clean stretch — light winds, moderate swell, water color holding clear enough on the ledge that sight-casting to surface activity has actually been possible on calm mornings. That's a gift this time of year. The moon is building toward full over the next several days, which should tighten up tidal flow on the ledge and could concentrate both bait and groundfish along the sharper edges — worth watching Jeffrey's gravel-to-mud transition zones closer than usual as that current strengthens.

Looking at the next few days, if the wind stays out of the south and southwest like it has been, expect the haddock-on-mud, cod-on-gravel pattern on Jeffrey's to hold without much change. The better bet for anyone chasing something different is the run out to Platts for pollock working that suspended bait layer, or a late trip to Tillies for hake after dark. On the tuna question, there's no reason yet to change the game plan — keep working bait and structure for groundfish, but keep an eye on the ledge's deeper edges toward Stellwagen. If that warm water holds through the week, this could be one of the earlier starts to giant season this beat has seen in a few years. If it breaks down with the first real cool front, it'll be back to the usual October timeline, and there's no sense burning gas chasing a bite that hasn't committed yet.
