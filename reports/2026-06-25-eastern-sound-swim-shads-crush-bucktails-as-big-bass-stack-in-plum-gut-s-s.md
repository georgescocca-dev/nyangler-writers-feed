# Swim shads crush bucktails as big bass stack in Plum Gut's screaming rip
*Seven-inch Tsunami shads producing overslot stripers while traditional bucktails get schoolies.*

**PLUM GUT, NY — June 25** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The rip is running like a freight train and the big bass are finally here. After weeks of schoolies and slot fish, the overslot stripers have moved into Plum Gut in numbers, and they're showing a clear preference for swim shads over bucktails.

I've been running head-to-head tests between seven-inch Tsunami swim shads and traditional Smiling Bill bucktails on the same three-way rigs, and the results are staggering. The shads are producing multiple overslot bass per tide while the bucktails are getting nothing but schoolies. Same drift, same depth, same current — the big fish want that soft plastic action.

The setup is critical in this screaming water. I'm running an 8 to 10-ounce cannonball on a three-way swivel, one foot below the main line, with a 5 to 6-foot leader of 80-pound test to the lure. That heavy leader isn't just for abrasion — the stiffness helps stand the bait off the bottom when you're drifting at 2.5 to 3 mph through the rip.

The bite window is tight. You've got maybe 90 minutes of fishable current before it gets too evil to present properly. I'm hitting the 80-foot mark on the east side and riding the contour as it rises to 40 feet across the main rip. The key is making preemptive cranks as the depth shallows — drag that rig on the rocks and you're done.

Double-drop technique is saving rigs out there. Drop to bottom, crank twice, then intentionally drop again to remove the belly that forms on the initial fall. The current's so strong it puts a bow in your line before you even feel bottom. Without that second drop, you're fishing 10 feet off the deck.

The Race is producing too, but it's a different game. Topwater action at first light with pencil poppers, then switching to three-way rigs when the sun gets up. The butterfish are thick, and that's what's drawing the Hudson River fish east. I'm seeing bass to 37 inches mixing with gorilla blues that'll wreck your expensive plugs in a heartbeat. Run wire leaders or stick to bucktails.

Water temperature hit 66 degrees this week in the eastern Sound — four degrees warmer than the ocean side. That thermal barrier is stacking bait on the flood tide and holding bass on the ebb. The gradient is most pronounced around Orient Point, where the warmer bay water meets the cooler ocean flow.

Bird life is the tell. When you see terns working over the rip, there's bait in the water column. That's when I switch from bottom rigs to 6-inch Tsunami spoons, letting the current suspend them mid-column before they hit bottom. The bigger bass are often feeding higher when there's active bait.

Fishers Island drops are holding blackfish for guys willing to bounce around in the chop. The 55-foot zone west of the island has been consistent for keepers to 19 inches, but you're dealing with wind against tide most days. Spot-lock technology is earning its keep out there.

Full moon Friday means spring tides and serious current. I'm watching for the first real push of weakfish on the big water moves. The Race and Plum Gut will be unfishable during peak flow, but the edges and eddies should stack fish. Plan your drifts for the hour before and after slack — that's when the big ones feed.
