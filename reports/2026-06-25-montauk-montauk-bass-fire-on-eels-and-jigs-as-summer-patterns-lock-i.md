# Montauk bass fire on eels and jigs as summer patterns lock in
*Forty-pound stripers after dark, steady fluke on the south side, and cocktail blues signal prime time.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 25** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is fishing like the Point should fish in late June — bass stacked on the rips, fluke spread across the south side drops, and enough bait in the water to keep everything honest. This is what we wait all spring for, and it's finally here.

The striper bite has been nothing short of spectacular. Diamond jigs are producing dozens of bass per trip in the rips, with fish running from schoolies to solid 20-pounders during daylight hours. But the real magic happens after sunset when the big girls move shallow. Live eels fished on fishfinder rigs are connecting with bass pushing 40 pounds — fish that make your drag scream and your arms ache. The key is getting those eels down to the boulder fields on the incoming tide, right where the current sweeps around the Point.

Bucktails tipped with Uncle Josh pork rind are still money for the surf crowd working the rocks under the lighthouse. One-ounce heads in white or chartreuse, bounced along the bottom on the last two hours of incoming, are picking up bass in the 5 to 15-pound range. The water's clean, running in the low 60s, and the fish are aggressive — classic late June conditions.

What's got me really excited are the cocktail blues showing up. These 1 to 2-pound snappers are a sure sign that the bait cycle is firing on all cylinders. When you see cocktails, you know the big bass aren't far behind. They're mixed in with the stripers on the diamond jig bite, and they're absolutely crushing small metals worked fast through the current.

The fluke fishing on the south side has been steady and productive. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are the go-to rig, dragged slow along the 30 to 50-foot drops. The bite has been best on the outgoing tide when the current pushes bait off the structure. Sand eels are thick in the water column, and the fluke are gorging themselves. Keepers to 5 pounds are coming over the rail with regularity, though you'll sort through plenty of shorts to find them.

Porgies are clustering up in their usual summer spots, but they're not everywhere like they used to be. When you find them, though, it's game on. Small hooks, light tackle, and pieces of clam or squid will fill the cooler quickly. The clusters seem to be holding in 40 to 60 feet of water, particularly around the rocky bottom east of the harbor.

The bait situation tells the whole story. Sand eels are everywhere, thick clouds of them moving with the tide. Squid made a push through the area recently, and there are still some around for those targeting fluke. What's missing is bunker — they're scarce compared to normal years, which might explain why the bass are so willing to hit artificials.

Water temperatures are sitting in the low 60s, perfect for this time of year. The fish have settled into their summer patterns, with bass using the rips and boulder fields as highways, fluke spread across the sandy bottom south of the Point, and baitfish stacked wherever the current creates an edge.

The new moon we just had dispersed the fish for a few days, but as the current picked back up, everything tightened up again. That's classic Montauk — the fish follow the tide, and when the water moves, they move with it.

Looking ahead, we're building toward the full moon next week, which should create some serious spring tides. Those big tides will flush even more bait through the area and could trigger some epic fishing. I'm watching for the first real push of weakfish — they usually show up when the water hits the mid-60s and the moon gets big. The conditions are lining up perfectly for what could be a memorable week of fishing at the Point.
