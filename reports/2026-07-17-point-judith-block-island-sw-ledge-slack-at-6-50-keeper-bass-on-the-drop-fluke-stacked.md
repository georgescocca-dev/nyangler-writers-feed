# SW Ledge slack at 6:50: keeper bass on the drop, fluke stacked on the flood
*No bonito yet off the Block, but the structure bite at SW Ledge is paying rent for anyone who fishes the tide right.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 17** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

6:50 a.m. is the number I've been working off SW Ledge all week — that's when the morning slack sets up on the bigger tides, and it's a ten-minute window on either side where the current goes soft enough to hold bottom without ripping your jig sideways. Miss it by twenty minutes and you're fighting three knots of green water for nothing. Hit it right and you're marking fish stacked on the drop from 55 to 80 feet, right where the ledge falls off the hard sand into that rubble bottom south of the tower.

This isn't the October class of bass yet — I'm not going to sell anyone on giants this week. What's down there right now is a healthy population of 26 to 34-inch fish holding tight to structure, feeding hard on the last twenty minutes of the ebb and picking back up as the flood starts to build. I've had the best luck dropping 4-ounce diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid straight down on the upcurrent side of the ledge, letting it fall to the bottom, and working it up in long sweeps rather than the short jigging stroke you'd use in the fall. These fish are eating slow right now — the water's warm enough that they're not chasing hard, they're ambushing. A bucktail in white or chartreuse, 2 to 3 ounces depending on the drift speed, dragged just off bottom on the last of the ebb has worked just as well as the jig, and it's a lot less arm work for a morning charter.

Fluke have been the more consistent story the last ten days. The south side of Block, working the drift from the Coast Guard Channel out toward the deeper water off Old Harbor, has been giving up decent numbers of keepers in 30 to 45 feet on the incoming tide. Gulp swimming mullets in pink shad or white, threaded on a 3/4-ounce bucktail with a squid strip trailer, dragged slow along the sand-to-mud transitions — that's been the winning combination. Nothing giant, mostly 17 to 20 inches with the occasional 4 or 5-pounder mixed in, but enough action to keep rods bent through a tide change, which is more than I can say for some weeks out here.

Sea bass are doing what sea bass do in July — sitting tight on every piece of structure around the Ledge and out toward the humps southwest of the island, and they'll eat just about anything you drop if you're patient enough to find the right depth. Clam on a bucktail teaser rig, bounced right on bottom in 50 to 70 feet, has been steady. Not a bonanza, but a reliable fill-the-cooler bite if the bass and fluke go quiet on you.

Now, the question I'm getting at the dock every single day this week: where are the bonito? Short answer — not here yet, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise. Water off the Block has been running in that 68 to 71-degree range, which is right on the edge of what gets bonito interested, but I haven't had a confirmed sighting or a hookup to report. What I have seen is bait starting to show heavier around Sandy Point and North Light — peanut bunker pushing through on the last of the ebb, and some silversides working the beach at Clay Head in the evenings. That's the setup. Bonito follow that bait, and historically for this stretch of coast, late July into early August is when the first scouts show off the Block, usually right around Sandy Point where the current sweeps bait around the north end. I'd keep a bag of Deadly Dicks or small metal in the boat starting this week, because when they show, they show fast and they don't stick around long enough for you to run home and get gear.

Bluefish have been filling in the gaps nicely, and I mean that as a compliment this time of year — chopper blues in the 4 to 8-pound class are hammering bunker chunks fished on the bottom around the Ledge on the tide change, and they've saved more than one slow morning when the bass wanted nothing to do with anything moving. If you're marking bait on the sounder and getting refusals from stripers, there's a good chance blues are sitting under that same bait and will eat what the bass won't.

Looking at the week ahead, I'd keep working that 6:50 slack window at SW Ledge as long as the tide charts hold that timing, and I'd start checking Sandy Point in the evenings for surface activity — birds working, bait nervous, anything that looks like the first bonito showing. If the water pushes past 72 off the north end like it's been trending, I wouldn't be surprised to get a confirmed report by the first days of August. Until then, the honest move is fluke on the drift and bass on the structure — steady work, not spectacular, but the kind of fishing that fills a cooler if you respect the tide.
