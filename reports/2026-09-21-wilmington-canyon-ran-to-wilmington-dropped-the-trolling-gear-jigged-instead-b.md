# Ran to Wilmington, dropped the trolling gear, jigged instead — bluefin and yellowfin paid for the fuel
*The warm filament is still holding at the canyon, and this week the metal outfished the spread two to one.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 21** — _by Capt. Dana Cooper, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one shot this week, the call is simple: run straight to Wilmington and rig for jigging before you touch the troll gear. That's not the old playbook, but it's what's paying off right now. The warm filament pushing off the Gulf Stream is still sitting over the canyon, and it's held together longer than a lot of the fleet expected going into the back half of September. We flagged this last week — said the bite would hold as long as that warm tongue didn't break down — and here it is, still stacking fish along the same edges.

The pattern this week has been jigged metals outproducing the troll spread, and it's not close. Boats working Ronzi-style speed jigs — the long, narrow butterfly-style metals in the 150-200 gram range — dropped down to the thermocline and worked them on a fast vertical retrieve are connecting on bluefin in the 40-80 pound class, with a mix of yellowfin mixed in on the same drops. The fish are sitting tight to structure, not spread out and roaming, which is exactly why jigging is winning right now — you can put the bait right on their head instead of hoping they intercept a spread running past at six knots. That doesn't mean troll is dead. Black-and-purple and green-machine skirted spreads are still drawing strikes on the troll, especially early and late in the day when the fish push up shallower to feed, but the ratio has clearly tilted toward the vertical program this week.

Wahoo have been part of the mix too, mostly as incidental strikes on the high-speed troll lines when boats run a spread with a couple of wahoo bombs or Ilanders mixed in behind the tuna spread. Nothing to build a whole trip around yet, but worth keeping a rigged rod out if you're already running lines. Mahi have been scattered — a few fish coming up under floating structure and weed lines on the way out and back, small numbers, but a nice bonus if you're marking bait balls or grass mats on the ride. White marlin reports have been thin this week; a couple of shots reported but nothing consistent enough to plan around. If billfish are the target, keep the naked ballyhoo or a small green machine dredge in the water but don't expect it to be the main event right now.

The why here matters. That warm filament sitting over Wilmington is creating a hard temperature break, and that break is doing what it always does — it concentrates bait, and the tuna stack up right on the edge of it instead of spreading across open water. When that edge stays put day after day like it has this week, the fish learn the terrain and hold tight to it, which is exactly why the jig program is working — the fish aren't chasing, they're ambushing from a fixed position, and a jig dropped right through the strike zone gets bit a lot more reliably than a trolled bait covering water where the fish aren't.

On timing: mornings have been the more consistent window, with the bite softening midday and picking back up again in the last two hours of light. That's typical late-September behavior once the sun gets up and pushes the bait deeper — the fish follow it down, and the jig program keeps producing while the troll spread running up top goes quiet. If you're planning a run, build the day around dawn and dusk and treat midday as a scouting window — mark bait, watch the temp break on the plotter, and reposition if the color or the machine tells you the edge has shifted overnight, because these filaments do move.

Looking at the next 72 hours, the setup still favors Wilmington over running further afield. The filament has been stable enough that there's no strong signal it's about to collapse or slide off the canyon edge, and as long as that holds, the jig-over-troll pattern should keep paying. The real risk heading into month's end is a cold-water intrusion pinching that warm water off — if that happens, expect the bite to tighten hard and push the fish deeper onto the true canyon structure rather than spread across the edges like they are now. Nothing in the water right now says that's imminent, but it's late September, and that's exactly the kind of week these breaks can fall apart fast. For now, load the jig rods, keep a troll spread rigged as backup for the bite windows at first and last light, and treat any drop in water clarity or a sudden cool reading on the way out as your signal to reassess before burning fuel deeper into the canyon.
