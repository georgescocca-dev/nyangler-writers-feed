# Holgate's New Slough Holds Fluke, Bunker Pods Pull Blues Through Barnegat Light
*A fresh cut behind the Rip is stacking fluke on the slack while bunker schools drag blues past both inlets on the tide change.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — July 20** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

I flew the drone over the Holgate cuts Tuesday morning before the wind picked up, and there's a new slough that wasn't there three weeks ago — carved out behind the second bar, running parallel to the beach for maybe 150 yards before it dumps into a bowl at the north end. That kind of structure doesn't happen by accident. It's a sign the sandbars down there are still shuffling from whatever swell rolled through in June, and it's exactly the kind of skinny-water trough that holds fluke on the last two hours of the outgoing before it goes dead flat.

We flagged this last week — said the bunker-driven blitz activity would intensify as bait kept pushing south with the tide, and that's precisely what's happening at Barnegat Light. Pods of bunker have been stacking up tight against the north jetty rocks on the last of the flood, and the blues have found them. Nothing like the Belmar-style daylight blitzes yet, but Wednesday morning I watched three separate boils inside the inlet mouth on the changeover, blues in the 3-5 pound class slashing through, gulls working hard overhead. If you're throwing metal — a 2-ounce Deadly Dick or a Hopkins — get it past the boil and let it sink a beat before you rip it back through. Don't cast right into the froth, feather the edges. That's where the bigger fish sit, picking off the cripples that drop out of the pod.

Fluke fishing is the one thing on this whole stretch I can call genuinely steady right now. Little Egg Inlet channel, the deep bend off Graveling Point, and the back side of Holgate are all producing on moving water — doesn't seem to matter which stage as long as there's current pushing. I've had my best drifts working 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets, bounced slow along the channel edges in 12 to 18 feet. Keeper-class fish, some pushing 22-23 inches, mixed in with a lot of shorts that make you work for your five. The back bay behind Beach Haven has been kicking out similar numbers on the same rig — smaller boat, same presentation, just shallower, 8 to 10 feet over the grass edges on the outgoing.

Stripers are the tougher story, and I'm not going to sell you a bill of goods here. Daytime bass fishing on this stretch has gone quiet — the water's too warm on the surface for keeper-class fish to want to feed hard in the middle of the day, and that's holding true from here up through the whole Jersey coast. The play right now is after dark, working the same bunker schools that are pulling the blues. I've had better luck live-lining a bunker on a fish-finder rig off the Barnegat Light rocks starting an hour before sunset and working through the first few hours of dark, or chunking bunker on a fish-finder rig anchored up-current of the pods at Little Egg. It's a slower game than daytime jigging, but the fish that come are worth the wait — I heard of a mid-30s fish taken off a chunk bait near the inlet mouth Thursday night, which tells me there's still some real size mixed in with the schoolies.

Weakfish have been quietly rebuilding around the Mullica mouth and up into Graveling Point on the calm, warm evenings we've had. Nothing like the old days, but if you're throwing a small swimming plug or a light bucktail-teaser combo on the last of the incoming right at dusk, you've got a real shot at a couple of weakfish mixed in with your fluke. Keep the retrieve slow — these fish don't chase hard in July heat, they want it presented right in front of their nose.

Kingfish showed up on the ocean side sandbars off Ship Bottom and Beach Haven this week, working the trough behind the first bar on high tide. Bloodworm or Fishbites on a simple high-low rig with a sandflea teaser has been the ticket — nothing exciting, but it's a fun beach-chair bite for the kids while dad works a bucktail in the same wash for a stray fluke.

Looking ahead: we're rolling into the first-quarter neap on the 21st, which is going to soften the current through both inlets over the next several days. That usually widens my bite windows rather than shrinking them — less water moving means the fluke and bass have more time to set up on structure instead of getting flushed through on a hard rip. I'd expect the bluefish pattern to keep pushing south along the beach as long as this bunker migration holds together and the weather stays calm — watch Barnegat Light and Little Egg both for daylight action if the pods keep funneling through on the tide changes. If I only had one window this week, I'd fish the last two hours of Saturday's outgoing at Holgate for fluke, then swing north to the Light for the evening bunker show before dark settles in for the bass.
