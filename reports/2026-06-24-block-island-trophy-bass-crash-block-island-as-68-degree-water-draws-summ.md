# Trophy bass crash Block Island as 68-degree water draws summer migration
*Southwest Ledge producing 40-inch fish on bucktails while bonito arrive early at the rips.*

**BLOCK ISLAND, RI — June 24** — _by Jack Sullivan, Zone Writer_

The summer migration is officially on. Water temps hit 68 degrees around the island this week, and the bass responded exactly like they're supposed to — big fish moving in from deep water to feed in the cool, oxygenated currents that make Block Island the East Coast's premier striper destination.

The Southwest Ledge has been the star of the show. I'm talking legitimate trophy fish — bass pushing 40 inches coming up from the 60-foot contour on bucktails and flutter spoons. The key has been working the drop-off where the shallow ledge meets deep water. Fish are holding in that 45 to 60-foot zone, right where the thermocline sets up. White bucktails with pork rind trailers have been deadly, especially on the drift when you can keep contact with bottom but still work the lure through the water column.

What's making this bite special is the bait situation. The sand eels are thick and they're the right size — not the tiny stuff we see early season, but proper 4 to 5-inch baits that big bass can't ignore. I've been watching birds work over schools of them, and where you see that activity, you'll find fish underneath.

The North Rip has been producing too, but it's been more technical. The current has been ripping with these new moon tides, so you need to time it right. Hit it on the slack or just as the tide starts to move, and work flutter spoons vertically. I've seen fish to the mid-40-inch class come up from 80 feet of water when you drop a spoon right on their heads.

Here's what's interesting — we're seeing the first bonito of the season already. That's early, even for Block Island. Small schools are showing up at the rips, hitting anything that moves. If you're targeting them specifically, small metals and tiny poppers are the ticket, but honestly, they're so aggressive right now that they'll eat a bucktail meant for bass.

The fluke fishing has been solid but not spectacular. The 30 to 40-foot zone around the island is holding fish, with keepers coming on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and chartreuse bucktails. The bigger fish seem to prefer the outgoing tide when bait gets flushed off the shallow areas.

Porgies are everywhere if you want to fill the cooler. Any structure in 40 to 60 feet is loaded with them. Clam strips on small hooks, nothing fancy required.

What's driving all this activity is that perfect Block Island setup — 68-degree water that's clean and loaded with oxygen, strong tidal flow that concentrates bait, and structure that gives fish ambush points. The temperature gradient between the island's cool water and the warmer stuff offshore is creating an edge that's stacking everything from bait to bass.

Looking ahead, we're moving toward the full moon Friday, which means bigger tides and more current. That should fire up the rips even more and potentially draw more fish up from deep water. The bonito showing up this early tells me we might be in for an exceptional summer season. When the water conditions are this good this early, it usually means the migration is going to be strong and sustained.

If you're planning a trip, focus on the Southwest Ledge and North Rip. Work the tide changes, keep your bucktails in contact with bottom, and don't be surprised if you hook into the fish of a lifetime. This is what Block Island is all about.
