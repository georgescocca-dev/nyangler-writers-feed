# Fort Trumbull Holds the Line: Thames Bass Still Eating in Daylight
*A steep temperature gradient at the river mouth is keeping stripers feeding through the day while the rest of the Sound goes nocturnal.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — July 18** — _by Marcus Webb, Zone Writer_

There's a seam off Fort Trumbull right now that you can practically see with the naked eye on the back half of the ebb — darker, cooler water sliding out of the Thames and shouldering into the warmer green stuff pushing up from the Sound. I've watched that line form for twenty years and it never gets old. This week it's doing more work than usual. New London's reading 67.6 degrees at the mouth, and Kings Point over in the western Sound is sitting at 71.6. That four-degree gap might not sound like much on paper, but on the water it's the difference between fish that eat all day and fish that only eat after dark.

Western Sound bass have gone nocturnal — that's a heat-avoidance thing, not a disappearing act, and I've seen guys down that way frustrated because they're fishing the wrong hours. Up here, the Thames is still flushing cool enough water on every ebb that stripers are holding right through the current seams at Fort Trumbull and staying willing to eat in full daylight. I had a message from a buddy who fishes out of the state pier who boated three keepers between 26 and 31 inches Tuesday morning, ten o'clock, sun straight up, working a chartreuse-and-white bucktail, 1 to 1.5 ounce depending on the push, right along that temperature line where the current stacks bait. That's not a fluke — that's the gradient doing what it does.

Gold Star Bridge is fishing the way it always fishes this time of year — inconsistent if you don't respect the pilings, very consistent if you do. The eddies on the downstream side of the center piers, especially the second and third from the New London side, are holding bass and some decent bluefish on the last two hours of the outgoing. Live eels drifted right into the slack pocket behind the pilings have been the ticket for the bigger fish, but if you don't want to mess with eels, a swimming plug like a Doc-style stickbait worked slow through the same seam has been drawing strikes too. Depth there runs 18 to 24 feet depending on where you are relative to the channel, and the fish are holding tight to the structure, not out in open water.

Bluefish have shown up with some teeth behind them the last week and a half, and we flagged this coming — bunker schools moving up the coast were going to pull blues with them, and that's exactly what's happening in the harbor now. Pods of peanut and small adult bunker have been working their way up past Pleasure Beach and into the lower Thames, and the blues are riding right underneath them. Metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Deadly Dicks and Kastmasters — has been the fastest way to connect when you see them busting on top, which has been happening in the early morning off Ocean Beach and again right at last light. Nothing blitz-scale, but enough consistent surface activity that if you see the gulls working, it's worth a dozen casts before you move on.

Fluke fishing has held up nicely on the structure adjacent to the sub base, which doesn't surprise me — that stretch of bottom stays cooler and better oxygenated than open Sound water thanks to the current running through there all day. Drifting 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullet in the 15 to 22 foot range along that edge has produced a mixed bag, most fish in the 16 to 19 inch range with the occasional keeper mixed in. Slow drift, bounce it right off bottom, and don't rush the retrieve — these fish haven't needed to chase anything hard in this water.

Porgies are still going at Ocean Beach and around the rockier stretches off Pleasure Beach, and that new moon tide cycle from last weekend is still carrying some extra push through here, which the porgies like. Small hooks, squid strips or clam, right on bottom in 10 to 20 feet — simple, reliable, good for anyone who wants steady action without overthinking the presentation.

Tide-wise, we're easing off that July 13 new moon push and heading toward the first quarter on the 21st, so the current through the Fort Trumbull rip and under the Gold Star pilings should soften up over the next several days. That's actually good news for presentation — slower water lets you work a bucktail or an eel more deliberately instead of just holding on for the drift. If the thermal gradient at the river mouth holds — and there's no reason right now to think it won't, with the Thames running cool and steady — I'd expect the daylight bass bite here to keep outperforming the rest of the Sound through the week. My plan is the last two hours of the outgoing at Fort Trumbull early, then sliding over to the bridge pilings as the tide bottoms out. If the bluefish keep riding those bunker pods into the harbor, keep a rod rigged with metal ready — you won't get much warning before they show on top.
