# Ram Island Boulders on the Slack: Albies Stacked, Bass Sliding in Behind the Bunker
*Fishers Island Sound is running its best albie week of the fall, and the first real bass are starting to show tight to the rock.*

**STONINGTON, CT — September 21** — _by Manny Pereira, Zone Writer_

Peanut bunker are pouring off the flats around Ram Island and Wamphassuc Point on every dropping tide right now, and where that bait funnels through the boulder fields, the water's been breaking white with albies from first light until the sun gets high. That's the story of the week out here — not one big event, but a pattern that's held for days: small, tight-schooled bait getting squeezed against structure, and everything with fins showing up to eat it.

We flagged this last week — said albie numbers would keep building through Fishers Island Sound — and that's exactly what's happened. The push out of Rhode Island never slowed down. It's settled in hard on this stretch from Watch Hill down through the Stonington breakwater and out to the Fishers Island rips, and for the moment this is as good an albie zone as there is in the Sound system. Kings Point is still reading close to 71 degrees and New London's holding right around 69, which tells you the western end of this stretch is a couple degrees warmer than the water coming off the Rhode Island line — enough of a gradient to keep bait and fish stacked right here rather than pushing straight through.

The albies are eating small. Epoxy jigs in the 3/4-ounce range — pink-and-white, olive-over-white — worked on a fast, erratic retrieve through the rips off Ram Island and along the outer face of the Stonington breakwater have been the most consistent producer. Fly guys working peanut bunker patterns on intermediate lines are doing just as well when they can get in front of a school without spooking it — these are close-quarters fish right now, feeding thirty and forty yards off the rock, not way out in open water. Bonito are mixed in with them on the better pushes, especially on the last two hours of the ebb when the current really starts ripping through the gut between Ram Island and the mainland. Stripe your leader down to 20-pound fluorocarbon if the bonito show — they're leader-shy in this clean, cooling water.

Striped bass are the other half of this story, and they're just starting to turn on. The bunker schools moving through Little Narragansett Bay and stacking off Esker Point have started drawing bass up off the bottom on the last of the ebb and first hour of the flood — nothing blitz-scale yet, more a slow build as more bait funnels in. Boats working bunker spoons and umbrella rigs around the edges of the pods off Esker and out toward the Wicopesset side have been picking up schoolies to the mid-20s with the occasional better fish mixed in. The Harvest Moon's bigger tides this week are pushing more water and more bait through the system faster than normal, and that usually means the bass window tightens up right around the tide changes rather than staying open all day. If you're picking one tide to fish for bass right now, make it the last two hours of the ebb into dead low around the breakwater and Ram Island — that's when the bait gets pinned against the rock and the bass stop cruising and start committing.

Blackfish have started showing more interest around the deeper boulder piles off Fishers Island and the rockier stretches inside the Sound as the water eases down out of summer temperatures, but it's early yet — more a sign of what's coming over the next few weeks than a bite worth building a trip around today. Green crabs and Asian crabs fished tight to structure in fifteen to twenty-five feet have picked at a few, nothing to write home about.

Fluke are holding up better than they usually do this late in September, which tracks with what's happening up and down the coast this fall — the water simply hasn't cooled off enough yet to push them out. The sandy stretches inside Little Narragansett Bay and the flats off Mystic River have still been giving up keeper fluke on Gulp swimming mullets and bucktail-and-bait combos fished slow along the bottom on the last of the incoming. That bite is living on borrowed time, though. The first real cold front that pushes through will start moving these fish toward deeper water fast, so if fluke's on the list, this coming week is a better bet than next.

Looking ahead, the pattern holding through the next several days is straightforward: keep the albie and bonito effort concentrated on the boulder fields and current seams from Watch Hill through Stonington and out to Fishers Island, working the tide changes hardest, and start paying closer attention to the bass building behind the bunker at Esker Point and Little Narragansett Bay. If the warm-water push out of Rhode Island holds — and there's no sign yet that it's breaking down — expect the albie window to stay open into early October. The bigger variable is the first real cold snap. When that comes, it'll tighten the fluke bite fast and start pulling bass numbers up in a hurry as more bait gets shoved out of the back bays. Until then, fish the rock, fish the tide change, and don't leave the epoxy jig at home.
