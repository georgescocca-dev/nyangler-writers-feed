# Full-Moon Ebb Tops 5 Knots: Stripers Load West End, First Albies Hit East Rip
*Big tides are stacking bait against the current lines, and the first real fall predators showed up right on schedule.*

**BOURNE, MA — September 7** — _by Mike Devlin, Zone Writer_

Max ebb at the Sagamore end topped five knots again this week, and that's the number that matters right now, not the air temperature. Full-moon tides do that to this ditch — they wind the current up tighter, they push bait harder against the rip lines, and they force everything with fins to make a decision. This week the decision was easy. Bass, blues, and now the first albies and bonito of the season all showed up to answer it.

We flagged this last week — said the full moon would tighten the bait schools and the first legit blitz activity was likely within the week, and that's exactly what's playing out at the east end right now. Bunker have been funneling through the Canal on the tide changes for about ten days, and this week they finally got pinned tight enough against the current seams for the predators to commit instead of just picking at stragglers.

The pattern right now is a tale of two ends. At the west end, off Mashnee Island, Hog Island, and the Wings Neck side toward Phinneys Harbor, stripers are working the current seams on the back half of the ebb, when the moving water pulls bait off the flats and lines it up along the drop where the fast current meets the slower water tight to the rocks. That seam has been holding fish from mid-twenties up to a few over 40 inches this week, and the better bite has been the last two hours of ebb into dead slack, when the current slows enough for a bucktail to sit in the strike zone instead of getting swept past it. A 1.5 to 2-ounce white or chartreuse bucktail, dragged slow along bottom right on that seam line, has been the go-to. Remember it's lures only in the Canal — single hook, no bait — so soft plastics like a 6-inch RonZ on a jighead or a swimming plug worked slow through the seam are the other options when the bucktail bite goes quiet.

The east end is where the real story is building. Bluefish have been thick and aggressive off the Herring Run and down toward the Sandwich side, crashing bunker on both tide changes, and they're not picky — Kastmasters, poppers, anything that moves fast through a breaking pod gets hit. But the intel everybody's chasing right now is the first wave of false albacore and bonito showing at the east end rip near the mouth, where the Canal current dumps into Cape Cod Bay. That's classic early-September behavior — the same push that's been building through Rhode Island and Buzzards Bay all week finally reached the Canal's doorstep. These fish are keying on small bait, bay anchovies and tiny silversides mixed in with the bunker, so downsizing matters. Small epoxy jigs and 2-inch Deadly Dicks in silver or pink have been out-producing the bigger metal that works for the bass and blues. The window has been tight — dawn, right on the last hour of the ebb before the rip lays down at the mouth — but the fish that have connected have been solid albies, not just a stray fish mixed in with the blues.

Scusset Beach has been a mixed bag. Some bass in the surf off the jetty on the early flood, mostly schoolies with the occasional keeper, but the better fishing there has been secondary — casters working the rip after the boats and shore regulars at the east end proper have already worked it over. If you're set up at Scusset, fish the first hour of the incoming before the crowd shows and before the current gets too fast to hold a lure in the zone.

What's setting up next is the piece everybody in the shop is talking about. The moon is building toward new phase on September 10, and in this Canal, new moon tides after a full moon week mean the current keeps running strong instead of easing off — back-to-back big water. That's the kind of tidal stacking that turns a good week into a real blitz week. If the bunker stay pinned the way they have been, expect the west end bass bite to get more consistent through the mid-tide stages, not just the tail end of the ebb, and expect the albie and bonito window at the east end to widen from a one-hour dawn shot into something you can fish through more of the morning tide.

The short version for anyone with limited time this weekend: fish the west end seams off Mashnee and Wings Neck on the last two hours of ebb for bass, and be at the east end rip right at first light on the tail of the ebb for a shot at albies and bonito mixed with blues. Watch the moon-driven current — if it's running harder than usual for the stage you're used to, that's not a bad sign, that's the Canal doing exactly what it does this time of year, pulling everything tight to the lines where the fish are waiting.
