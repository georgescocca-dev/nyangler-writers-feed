# Raritan Bay bass blow up as 67-degree water stacks bunker on the flood
*Spring tides and warming bay temps trigger the classic Jersey June bite from Sandy Hook to Manasquan.*

**RARITAN BAY, NJ — June 19** — _by Joey Napolitano, Zone Writer_

The water hit 67 degrees this week and everything changed. After weeks of picking at schoolies, the Raritan Bay exploded with keeper bass as warming temps pulled bunker tight to the channels and the new moon spring tides started flushing bait like a fire hose.

I've been working the Raritan hard since Monday, and the pattern is textbook June fishing. The bay is running 74 degrees — a full 6-degree premium over the ocean side — and that thermal barrier is stacking adult bunker on every flood tide. Find the bait pods in 12 to 18 feet and you'll find bass from 24 to 38 inches crushing everything that moves.

The bite window is tight but predictable. Start of the flood through the first two hours of incoming water, the bass are feeding hard on the channel edges where the current sweeps bait against the structure. I'm throwing 6-inch white Hogy soft baits on 3/4-ounce bucktails, working them slow along the bottom on the drift. The key is staying in contact — these fish are eating on the drop, not the retrieve.

Live bunker is absolutely deadly if you can net them. Fish a 6/0 circle hook through the nose on a fishfinder rig, let it drift with the current about 3 feet off bottom. The bass are so locked on bunker right now they'll ignore everything else when the live stuff is in the water.

Sandy Hook has been producing consistent action on the ocean side, especially the north jetty on the outgoing. The rip forms up perfect about two hours into the ebb, concentrating bait in that 20-foot hole just outside the rocks. Diamond jigs in 2 to 3 ounces are working, but you need to match the current speed — too light and you're fishing in the middle of nowhere.

Manasquan Inlet is fishing well on the turn of the tide, both directions. The inlet mouth has been holding fluke to 22 inches mixed with short bass, and the back bay is loaded with bunker. I watched birds working over massive bait schools Tuesday morning — acres of peanut bunker getting hammered by blues and bass from sunrise until the wind picked up.

The ocean side is still a bit rough — 5-foot seas at Barnegat with a southwest wind that's been blowing 15 knots all week. But if you can handle the ride, the structure is holding fish. The Shark River reef area has been producing sea bass limits for boats willing to make the run, and there are reports of early weakfish showing up in the deeper holes.

What's driving this bite is the classic June setup. Water temps hit that magic 67-degree mark where bass start feeding aggressively, the spring tides are moving massive amounts of bait, and the thermal gradient between bay and ocean is creating a feeding funnel that concentrates everything in the back waters.

The moon goes full Friday, which means even bigger tides and more bait movement. I'm watching for the evening outgoing tides to really fire up — that's when the big bass typically show up to ambush bait getting swept out of the tributaries. The Shrewsbury and Navesink rivers have been holding fish, and with these spring tides pulling everything seaward, the inlet mouths should be prime time.

Looking ahead, this pattern should hold through the weekend as long as the southwest wind stays manageable. The 67-degree water is the key — once we're locked into summer temps, the bass spread out and the easy fishing ends. But right now, with bait concentrated and fish feeding hard, this is as good as Jersey bass fishing gets in June.
