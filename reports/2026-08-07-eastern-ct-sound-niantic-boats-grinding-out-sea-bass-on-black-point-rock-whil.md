# Niantic Boats Grinding Out Sea Bass on Black Point Rock While Fluke Play Coy in the Heat
*The bottom bite is steady if you know where the rock piles are, but the fluke drift has turned into a game of patience with New London water pushing near 78 degrees.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 7** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Talked to a couple of the Waterford skippers this week and the story's the same up and down the dock — sea bass are saving the trip sheets right now, and fluke are making guys work for every keeper. That's not a complaint, it's just where we are in early August. New London's reading 77.4 degrees, which is warm enough to push some of that flatfish activity into deeper water and off the classic sand flats where they've been sitting since June.

Start with the good news. Black sea bass are stacked on the rock structure off Black Point and around Hole-in-the-Wall, holding in that 35 to 55 foot range where the bottom breaks up into boulder and rubble. The drift boats working those ledges are putting good numbers of keepers in the box on standard two-hook rigs — squid strips or Gulp Sea Bass Bites on the bottom, jigging heads bounced right off the rock rather than dragged across it. These fish don't move far this time of year, they just sit tight to structure waiting for the tide to push bait past them, so precision matters more than covering ground. Slack water kills this bite fast — you want moving current, either stage of the tide, to get them feeding up off the bottom instead of buried in the rocks.

Porgies are the other steady producer, and this is where the family charters and the walk-on trips out of Niantic have been finding their fun. Bloodworms and clam strips on standard porgy rigs are working over the gravel and mussel bottom off McCook Point and around the mouth of the Niantic River. Nothing complicated here — light tackle, small hooks, patience for the bite, and you'll fill a cooler. It's not glamorous fishing but it's honest fishing, and right now it's about as reliable as anything in the Sound.

Fluke is where things get more interesting, and I'll be straight with you — it's a mixed bag out there. Twotree Island Channel and the deeper edges of Niantic Bay have been giving up fluke to 5 and 6 pounds, but the counts are down from where they were a few weeks back, and the fish have slid deeper as that surface warmth built through late July. Drifts in 30 to 40 feet with white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4 to 1-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, are still the go-to setup. The difference now is timing — the early morning outgoing tide before the sun really cooks the water is producing better than the midday drifts. If you're only getting out once, make it that first light window on the ebb.

Striped bass and bluefish have been showing up together, which tells you the bunker are still thick in Niantic Bay and around Black Point. When those pods bunch up tight near the surface, especially in that low-light window at dawn and again toward dusk, it can turn into some genuinely explosive topwater action — poppers and Doc pencils worked fast across the school, or a livelined bunker dropped just under the surface if the fish are holding deeper. We flagged this pattern a few weeks back — said wherever those bunker schools stayed thick, the topwater blitzes would keep coming as long as the weather held stable, and that's exactly what the fleet's been running into. The bass mixed in are mostly schoolies with the occasional slot fish, nothing to write home about size-wise, but it's fun fishing and it's putting bent rods on the boats that find the right pod at the right time.

Blackfish guys, I know you're out there — tautog season's closed through the summer in Connecticut waters, so that bite's on pause. Worth spending some of this downtime re-rigging jigs and crab hooks, because once that fall opener hits, Black Point and the rock structure around Hole-in-the-Wall turn into some of the better blackfish water in the eastern Sound.

On the tide side, we called the softening current from the last neap a couple weeks back, and that's exactly what played out — current speed eased up through late July and the bite got a little more forgiving, especially for the bottom guys working the rock piles. We're building back out of that now toward stronger tides, which should tighten up the drift speed at Twotree Channel and put some more urgency back into the fluke bite. If that current does pick up like it should heading into the next week, I'd expect the fluke numbers to firm up some, particularly on the stronger stages of the tide rather than the slack in between.

Bottom line for anyone planning a trip out of Niantic or Waterford this week — sea bass and porgies are your bankable options right now, fluke needs an early start and some patience, and the bunker schools are worth chasing at first light if you like your striper fishing loud and visual. Watch the wind, too — anything out of the southwest for a few days straight tends to push warm surface water and grass into the bay, so if it's been blowing that direction, expect some dirty water on the inside flats and better clarity out toward Black Point.
