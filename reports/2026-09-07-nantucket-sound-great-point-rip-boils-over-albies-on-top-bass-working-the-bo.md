# Great Point Rip Boils Over: Albies On Top, Bass Working the Bottom
*The first real albie showing of the fall hit the Point on the full moon tide, and the bluefish and bass haven't left the party either.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — September 7** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

Stood on the beach at Great Point Wednesday evening and watched a hundred terns fold up and drop into a patch of nervous water off the rip, and under them the surface just erupted — not the splashy, disorganized bluefish kind of erupt, but that tight, torpedo-shaped albie explosion where the fish are moving as one animal. That's the visual we've been waiting two weeks for out here, and it finally showed up right on the full moon.

We flagged this at the end of August — said the albie bite would firm up as more fish pushed in and settled into a rhythm, and that's exactly what's happening now. They're not thick everywhere, and they're not easy, but there's a real push of false albacore working Great Point, the rip off Sankaty, and pockets off Monomoy and Chatham that weren't holding fish ten days ago. The full moon tide this week put some serious current through the rips, and that's tightened up the bait — mostly bay anchovies and small silversides right now, which explains why the smaller profile stuff is outfishing everything else. Half-ounce epoxy jigs, Deadly Dicks, and Albie Snax in the smaller sizes are getting hit; the bigger 3-inch metal that worked in years past is getting refused more than it's getting bit.

The pattern right now is classic early-fall Nantucket Sound: bait gets pushed and compressed by the tide through the rip, predators stack on the down-current edge, and the whole thing can turn on and off with the push of water. Best window has been the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the flood at Great Point, when the rip is really working and the bait has nowhere to hide. Same story off Monomoy Point and the rips coming off the elbow toward Chatham — southwest wind lays the water down enough to see the nervous water, and that's when you want to be idling the edges instead of running the middle.

Bluefish are mixed right into the albie schools almost everywhere, and honestly for a lot of guys that's been the more consistent bite of the week. They're not picky the way the albies are — bucktails, poppers, small metal, doesn't matter much, they're eating everything in the wash. If the albies go quiet on you for a stretch, don't leave the area; work the same water with something bigger and the blues will usually keep you bent.

Bonito are the one we're still waiting on locally. The push out of Rhode Island and Buzzards Bay has been strong this week, and that push has been working west and north, but as of now the fleet out here hasn't reported a real showing inside the Sound. Worth watching the next few days — if the pattern holds the way it usually does this time of year, bonito should start showing on the western end of the Sound and around Horseshoe Shoal before too long, especially if this wind stays out of the southwest and keeps pushing warm water up against the shoreline.

Striped bass are doing the thing they do in early September — staging, not blitzing yet, but showing more interest than they have all summer. Bunker schools are tightening up nicely on both sides of the Sound, and there's been decent bass activity underneath the albie and blue schools at Great Point on bigger swimming plugs and bucktails worked slow along the bottom of the rip. Nobody's calling it a blitz yet, but the ingredients are there — tightening bait, building tide, cooling nights. The moon's going to keep working in our favor through the week, and if the bunker schools keep getting squeezed the way they have been, the first real blitz behavior of the fall wouldn't be a surprise inside the next week to ten days. That's the window to watch, not this weekend necessarily, but soon.

Fluke are winding down out here the way they always do this time of year, but there's still a respectable bite left on the flats around Monomoy and in the deeper cuts off Nantucket Shoals for anyone willing to put in bucktail-and-Gulp combination time. Four-inch white or chartreuse Gulp swimming mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the sand-to-structure transitions, are still picking up keeper fish, though the counts are noticeably thinner than they were in July. This is closing-window fishing now — worth a stop on the way to or from the rips, not a dedicated trip anymore.

Looking ahead: the wind and tide setup the next few days favors the rips. If the southwest breeze holds without blowing too hard, Great Point and Sankaty should keep producing on the ebb, and Monomoy's rips should fish well on that same wind direction pushing against the current — that's when the structure really turns the water over and stacks bait against it. Keep an eye on Horseshoe Shoal and the western Sound for the first bonito sign; when that push finally arrives it tends to come in fast once the water's right. And with the bass pattern building the way it is under this moon, the smart play the next week or two is working the same rip water at first and last light — albies and blues on top early, bass underneath as the light comes up and the bait gets pinned tight against the current edge.
