# Wamphassuc's rock piles are stacked with peanut bunker — albies and bass both know it
*The boulder fields between Ram Island and the breakwater are holding bait tight to the surge, and both albies and stripers are working the seams.*

Peanut bunker have pushed into the pockets between the boulders off Wamphassuc Point and Ram Island this week, riding the surge in and out of the cracks like they're trying to disappear into the stone itself. They can't. Not with albies working the outside edges and stripers holding underneath the wash line, picking off stragglers on every push of current. That's the read off this stretch of rock right now, and it's a good one.

We flagged this ten days back — said albie numbers would keep building through the structure zones here, and that's exactly what's happened. Fishers Island Sound has been running a Rhode Island-style false albacore fishery for weeks now, and this week it didn't let up. The warm-water push out of Block Island and Narragansett Bay hasn't broken yet, and with New London still holding mid-60s water, there's no reason it should in the next few days either. Bonito are mixed in with the albies along the same current lines, particularly where the breakwater's boulder field drops into deeper water on the outside face — that transition from rock to sand is where the bait gets pinned and the speedsters come through to work it.

On the albie side, small profile epoxy jigs in the half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce range are doing the damage — pink-and-white or bone colored, worked with a fast, erratic retrieve right off the rock edges where the rip forms on the ebb. Fly guys are matching bay anchovies and silversides with small Clouser minnows and epoxy baitfish patterns on intermediate lines, 9-weights standard, and getting takes right in the wash where the boulders break the current. The fish aren't holding in one spot — they're moving with the bait pods, so the game is reading where the bunker balls are getting pushed against structure, not staking out a single rock and waiting.

Striped bass are running the same bunker pattern that's lighting up the whole coast right now, just with a Stonington accent. The Mystic River mouth and the deeper water off Esker Point are holding adult bunker schools, and bass are shadowing them tight, using the boulder fields as ambush points on the drop of the tide. Snag-and-drop with a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig is the straightforward play at the river mouth; along the rockier stretches, a 1.5-ounce white or chartreuse bucktail worked slow off the edges as the tide falls is getting bit by fish sitting just off the base of the rocks, waiting for bait to get swept off the structure. The Harvest Moon's bigger swings this week should keep that bait moving harder and pin fish more predictably to these edges over the next several days — bigger tides mean more current, and more current means the ambush windows at the structure get sharper, not softer.

Fluke are still around in the sandy pockets between the boulder fields, though the bite's more of a bonus than a target right now. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on light bucktails, dragged slow through the sand seams near Ram Island, are picking up a mix of keepers and shorts — nothing to build a whole trip around, but worth a few drifts if you're already out there for the albies. Warmer-than-normal water is the reason they're still shallow and active this late in September; the first real cold front will start pushing them toward deeper water, and that clock is ticking.

Black sea bass are holding steady on the deeper structure off Fishers Island, no real change there — squid strips and clam on a high-low rig near the rock piles will still put a mixed bag in the cooler.

Looking at the next few days, the pattern holds as long as the warm-water filament sitting off Rhode Island doesn't break down. If it holds — and there's no strong signal yet that it won't — expect the albie and bonito activity to keep building right through the boulder fields here, with the bass bite intensifying under the bunker schools as the moon tides keep bait moving hard. The bigger question, the one worth watching into next week, is whether the first real September cooldown arrives before or after the fluke clear out. Until then, the rocks between Ram Island and the breakwater are the address, and the tide change is still the time to be standing at it.
