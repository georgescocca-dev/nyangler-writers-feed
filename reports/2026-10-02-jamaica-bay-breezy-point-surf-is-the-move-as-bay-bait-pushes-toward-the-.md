What I'm seeing says the bait has made its decision, and it's walking out the back door of Jamaica Bay toward the inlet, which means your access point should follow it.

Here's the logic. Snapper blues and blowfish, which have kept the inside of the bay busy all summer, are winding down fast as water temps slide — not gone, but thinning out as the bunker pods that held them there get pushed toward open water by cooling surface temps and shrinking daylight. That bait is funneling through Rockaway Inlet and piling up along Breezy Point, and striped bass and bluefish are riding right behind it. A boat gives you the most flexibility to work the inlet rips on the tide change, but if you're shore-bound, Breezy Point puts you in direct casting range of the same bait funnel without needing a hull. Canarsie Pier and the Cross Bay Bridge area are still worth a stop for stragglers working the bay-side flats, but they're playing second fiddle to the inlet right now. Kayak access works best early, before boat traffic picks up on the inlet rips, if you can time a launch around the tide change and stay off the main channel.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the mullet run would keep building as tidal flow stayed elevated, and that's exactly what's happening. The moon is building toward full on October 6, which means tidal range is increasing day over day, and that extra push of water is exactly what's dragging bait out of the back bay faster. Ocean buoy data offshore is showing water in the mid-60s with some leftover wave energy in the 2-to-3-foot range — not glassy, but not blown out either. Inside the bay and along the beach, that translates to decent water clarity with enough chop to give bass confidence without shutting down the bite.

On the tactics side, this is still a bait-and-timing game more than a lure-color game. At Breezy Point, the better reports are coming on bucktails in the 1-to-1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, cast into the wash and worked with a slow bottom hop as the current sweeps it along the bar. Swimming plugs — SP Minnows and similar long, slow-sinking swimmers — are drawing better strikes right at first light and again at dusk, when bass push up tight to the suds chasing disoriented peanut bunker and mullet. If you're fishing bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig off the inlet rocks or from a boat working the rip, keep it simple and let it sit in the current seam rather than retrieving it — bluefish especially are hitting hard on dead-stick presentations right in that moving water.

Bluefish are mixing heavily into the same schools right now, and they're not shy about it — expect some leaders to get shredded if you're not running wire or at least a heavier mono leader. That's a good problem to have. The bite itself has been more pulse than constant — strong around the tide changes, quieter in the middle of the tide — so don't expect wall-to-wall action if you show up at noon on a slack tide. Show up on the push, and the window opens up fast.

Fluke are basically off the table here now that state waters closed October 1, so if that's been your bread and butter, it's time to pivot. Porgies are still around structure near the Marine Parkway Bridge pilings on bloodworms or clam, and blackfish season prep is worth thinking about as water continues to cool — that bite usually starts to build in earnest once the bass run thins out later in the month.

Looking at the next 72 hours, the building tides into the full moon should keep intensifying the bait push at the inlet, which is good news if you're working that rip on the tide change. The bigger wildcard is weather — any northeast wind event that rolls through in this window could be the trigger for the season's first real beachfront blitz, mixing the water column and pinning bait hard against the sand at Breezy Point and the Rockaway beachfront. If that front shows up, I'd shift from the inlet rocks to the open beach and work the wash line hard on the first light tide. If it stays quiet, stick with the inlet rip pattern that's already working. Either way, the bait's moving in one direction right now, and your access point should move with it.
