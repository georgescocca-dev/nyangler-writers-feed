# Rough Monday, Redemption Thursday: Poor Man's Turns It On After the Squid Show Up
*A tough start to the week gave way to a solid yellowfin bite once the Illex squid packed onto the edges of Poor Man's and Spencer.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 13** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

If I had to grade this week, I'd call it a C-minus through Wednesday and a solid B by the time we tied up Friday. That's the canyon game in mid-July — you don't get to pick your week, you pick your day, and this week the day was Thursday.

Monday and Tuesday we ran out of Ocean City toward Poor Man's expecting the yellowfin bite we'd heard about, and instead found green, sloppy water and not much life. No birds, no showers, sparse squid on the meter. We trolled a full spread — small ballyhoo behind Green Machines, a naked bird up top, a couple cedar plugs in the shotgun — and scratched out two fish in six hours off the northeast edge of Poor Man's, working the 100-fathom curve where it kicks toward Baltimore Canyon. That's not a day I'm proud of, but it's an honest one. Water was warm on top, but there wasn't a clean break to hold anything.

Wednesday night is when it turned. We had a boat working Spencer report squid stacking up thick on the drop-off after dark, and that's the signal I've been waiting on all July. Squid show up, tuna show up behind them — that's about as reliable a rule as I've got in this business. By Thursday morning the color had cleaned up nicer along the Spencer/Lindenkohl stretch, and we found yellowfin working bait balls right on the surface starting around 8 a.m., roughly along the eastern lip of Lindenkohl where it bends toward the Wilmington side.

That day was the kind of morning that makes the whole week worth it. We put six yellowfin in the box, sizes running from a couple of "rats" in the 15-20 pound class up to two better fish in the 60s, all on the troll — same spread as before, but this time we slowed the boat to 6.5 knots and let the ballyhoo swim instead of skip. When the school got finicky mid-morning, we switched over to jigging, dropping Shimano Butterfly jigs — the 150-gram in blue/silver — down through 250 to 300 feet where we were marking suspended fish on the sounder. That's when the bigger fish showed up. Jigging produced our two best yellowfin of the day, both hooked on the pause after a long upward stroke.

Friday we pushed south to Norfolk, which is a commitment out of Cape May — figure six hours each way if the seas cooperate — but it's worth the run this time of year because Norfolk tends to hold the bigger class fish and it's where we've seen white marlin activity the last two seasons in mid-July. We didn't connect on a marlin Friday, but we did mark several fish following the spread and had one bill up on a naked ballyhoo we couldn't convert. The yellowfin bite down there was more scattered than Spencer — smaller pods, less consistent — but we still managed three fish trolling the canyon edge in about 700 feet of water, plus one respectable mahi, about 20 pounds, that came up on a weed line drifting just inside the canyon mouth.

Bluefin have thinned out compared to June. We're seeing occasional schoolies mixed in on the troll at Lindenkohl, but nothing like the numbers reported off central Jersey in the 20-25 fathom midshore grounds. If you're set on bluefin specifically, that shallower water might be a better bet right now than running all the way to the canyons.

Here's the pattern I'm watching heading into next week: this heat dome sitting over the mid-Atlantic means calm mornings and building southwest wind in the afternoons, which is actually good news for canyon runners — you can get out early, fish the bite window before the seas build, and be back inside before it turns ugly. The squid concentration that turned Thursday around at Spencer needs to hold or spread toward Poor Man's for that pattern to repeat. If it slides north toward Wilmington instead, I'd expect Lindenkohl to stay hot and Poor Man's to lag behind again.

My plan for the next few days: hit Spencer and Lindenkohl first thing, working the edge where the squid showed up, and keep the jigs rigged and ready because that's where the quality fish came from Thursday. If that dries up, Norfolk's still holding fish and white marlin sign, even if the numbers aren't overwhelming — worth the long run if you've got the fuel range and a decent weather window. Poor Man's I'd treat as a wildcard until we get another squid report out of there; no sense burning fuel on hope alone.

Wahoo have been quiet for us this stretch — a little surprising given the warm surface temps, but they'll usually show once the water color firms up blue instead of this transitional green we've had most of the week. Keep a high-speed lure or two rigged in the spread just in case, especially trolling the drop-offs at Norfolk where the bottom falls off fast.

Bottom line: rough start, real finish. The squid are the tell this time of year — find them stacked on a temp break and the yellowfin generally aren't far behind. That's where I'm putting my time this week, starting at Spencer and working the chain south if it doesn't produce.
