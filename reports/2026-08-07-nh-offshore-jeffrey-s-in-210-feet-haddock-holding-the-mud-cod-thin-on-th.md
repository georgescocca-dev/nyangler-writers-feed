# Jeffrey's in 210 feet: haddock holding the mud, cod thin on the gravel edge
*Steady green water and building bait have the ledge fishing true to form for early August, but the cod bite still needs work to find keepers.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 8** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Two-ten on the sounder this morning, soft mud color coming back on the eastern flat of Jeffrey's Ledge, and that's exactly the water I wanted to see. Haddock like that bottom — soft, gray-green, a little give to it — and this week they were sitting right where the chart says they should be, holding tight to the transition where the mud flattens out east of the main ledge before it drops toward Scantum Basin.

We've had four trips out this week, three of them solid, one a grind. The pattern's been consistent: haddock from 190 to 230 feet on the mud, working a slow drift with clam bait on standard two-hook rigs, 6-ounce bank sinkers to hold bottom in the current. Fish ran 18 to 24 inches, a few pushing bigger, and when we found a patch of them the bite came in flurries — five, six fish in ten minutes, then dead again until the drift carried us back over the same stretch. That's typical August haddock behavior out here. They're not spread evenly across the mud, they're bunched in pockets, and you earn your limit by working the GPS track back and forth until you find where the pocket sits this particular tide.

Cod have been the tougher story, and I'll be straight about it — the gravel bite on the western edge of the ledge, the classic 120 to 160 foot structure where you'd normally expect keeper cod stacked up on the hard bottom, has been thin. We picked at a few fish jigging diamond jigs and 8-ounce Norwegian jigs tipped with squid strips, but for every keeper we saw two or three shorts come over the rail. Water's still cold and green down there, which is the right color, but the cod just haven't packed onto the gravel in numbers yet this year. I talked to a couple other boats out of Rye and they're seeing the same thing — scattered, not stacked. If you're going after cod specifically right now, I'd treat it as a bonus fish while you're haddocking rather than build the whole trip around it.

Platts Bank has been worth the extra run for guys willing to push north. Similar mud pattern to Jeffrey's, maybe a touch deeper, and the haddock there have been running a little bigger on average — I heard of a few pushing past 26 inches off the deeper mud there this week. Tillies Bank I haven't fished personally in the last ten days, so I won't speak to it beyond saying the bottom type is right for the same mud pattern if the Jeffrey's fish get pressured off.

We flagged last week that conditions looked steady with no major shift coming, and that's exactly what played out — the water held its color, the thermocline didn't move much, and the fish stayed put in the same general zones instead of sliding around on us. That kind of stability is a gift out here. It means the numbers you write down Monday are still good Thursday.

Mackerel have been showing in patches on the ledge itself, schools flickering on the sounder in that 60 to 90 foot range on top of the structure, and that's worth paying attention to even if you're not targeting them — it's bait, and bait brings everything else with it. We haven't seen bluefin sign yet, no boils, no birds working hard on the surface, no whale activity that would tip you off to a real bait concentration building. That's normal for early August. The giants don't usually show heavy on Jeffrey's until the water turns over into fall — October is when this ledge earns its reputation for big fish on the surface, and I'm not going to pretend that clock has moved up just because the mackerel are around a little early. Worth watching, not worth planning a trip around yet.

Tide-wise, we're running toward the new moon on the 12th, and I expect the current to pick up some strength on the ledge edges over the next several days. That usually sharpens the haddock bite on the mud — a little more push moves more bait past the fish and gets them feeding in shorter, more predictable windows instead of the long lazy pickings we had this week. If I had to guess where I'll put my time over the next several days, it's the same eastern mud flat, working the drift a little tighter to the tide change, morning outgoing especially. The cod I'll keep checking on the gravel, but I'm not counting on a turnaround there until the water cools another notch or two.

Bottom line for anyone planning a trip out of Rye: bring the clam bait, work the mud in the 190 to 230 range, and don't be surprised if the gravel cod fishing stays stingy a while longer. It's a steady week, not a spectacular one, and out here on Jeffrey's that's usually a fair trade — steady means you can plan around it.
