# August 3: Washington's Already Fishing Like Mid-Month
*The canyon's running two weeks ahead of the calendar again, and the yellowfin/mahi combo we called last week is exactly what showed up on the color change.*

**OCEAN CITY, MD — August 3** — _by Capt. Chris "Wash" Morrison, Zone Writer_

Here's the thing about Washington that guys running out of the other canyons still haven't figured out after all these years — we don't wait for August to fish like August. By the first week of the month, this canyon's usually already running the pattern the Norfolk and Baltimore boys won't see until the 15th or later, and this year's no different. Gulf Stream's been tight to our edge since late June, and that head start compounds. June was slow feeling, July built fast, and now on August 3rd we're sitting in what I'd call a full mid-August bite three weeks early. I don't say that to brag. I say it because it changes how you should fish this week versus how your buddy fishing Poor Man's Canyon is fishing his.

The west wall — that stretch running down from around 38°48'N, 74°42'W where the 100-fathom line breaks hard into the canyon proper — has held the most consistent water all week. You're dropping off that ledge from 600 feet down into 1,400, 1,800 feet fast, and that kind of vertical relief is exactly what stacks bait against structure when there's any current push at all. We've had a modest eddy sitting off the northeast corner of the canyon, spinning slow but steady, and it's been pulling a nice color change right across the top third of the wall — that green-to-blue seam is where I've spent most of my trolling hours this week, and it's paid.

Yellowfin have been the backbone of it, 50 to 75 pounds, coming on the troll early and then switching over to the chunk once the sun gets up and the fish go soft. Ballyhoo behind Sea Witches in dark colors — black/purple, green machine — worked the troll along that seam in the morning, then butterflied bunker on a slow chunk once we anchored up on the color break itself. Mahi have been riding right on top of that same edge, some nice ones in the 12 to 20 pound range mixed with the usual schoolies under any bit of floating grass. We flagged this exact combination last week — said boats working the color change would see a mix of yellowfin and mahi — and that's precisely what's been in the box.

White marlin have started showing on the temp break itself, which usually runs somewhere in the 76 to 78 degree range this time of year along that eddy edge. Nothing stacked up yet, but we've pulled two or three a day dredging naked ballyhoo and small green machines behind spreader bars, mostly right where that warm tongue pinches against the cooler canyon water. If you're rigged for it, don't ignore the marlin bite just because the tuna are easier — those fish are here and they'll be here more as that eddy tightens up, which I expect it will over the next week or so as the moon builds toward new.

Wahoo have been a bonus, not a target — a couple caught high-speed trolling right along the lip of the wall in that 600 to 800 foot zone, planers down deep pulling Ilanders in blue/white. If you want a wahoo specifically, work that ledge itself rather than the open color change; they're holding tighter to structure than the tuna are right now.

Bigeye have shown at night on the chunk for boats willing to sit out there past dark, mostly southeast of the main eddy in the deeper water past 1,500 feet. Nothing consistent enough to call a pattern yet — one or two a trip for guys putting in the hours — but that's normal for early August here. That fishery tends to build through the month as the water settles and the deep scattering layer gets more predictable.

I won't oversell the week. It's been good, not great — a solid tuna and mahi bite with marlin and wahoo mixed in, but you still have to find the right piece of the color change and sit on it. I had one trip where we worked three miles of edge before we found fish holding tight, and another where they were right where we expected them inside twenty minutes. That's the difference between reading the eddy correctly and just running to numbers you fished last month.

Looking ahead, if that eddy keeps tightening the way it's been the last few days, I'd expect the marlin bite to firm up first — they like a cleaner, sharper break than the tuna do. Wind's been cooperative, nothing over 15 knots out of the southwest, and if that holds through the weekend I'd put my money on the west wall color change again, working the troll early and settling into the chunk once the sun's high. If a front pushes through and knocks that eddy apart, I'd shift east toward the deeper structure and fish it as a scattered bite rather than a concentrated one. Either way, Washington's still running ahead of the pack — that's not going to change just because the calendar says early August.
