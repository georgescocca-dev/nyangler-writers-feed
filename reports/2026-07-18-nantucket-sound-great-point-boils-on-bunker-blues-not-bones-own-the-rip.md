# Great Point Boils on Bunker — Blues, Not Bones, Own the Rip
*Bluefish are crashing bait at Great Point and Monomoy while stripers go nocturnal, and I'm still staring at every swirl hoping for a bonito flash.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — July 18** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning, half-light, I'm idling off Great Point watching a knot of terns work a bait pod that's been pushed tight against the rip line on the last of the ebb. Something boils under them — heavy, chunky, no green flash, no torpedo shape cutting sideways. Bluefish. A dozen of them, maybe more, blowing bunker three feet in the air. I've been out here every clear morning for two weeks half-expecting that first bonito flash to cut through a bait ball like a blade, and it hasn't happened yet. Not at Great Point, not off Sankaty, not on the Monomoy rips. Soon, though — I can feel it building.

Here's the thing about this week: the blues have taken over the show that stripers usually run this time of year, and they're not being shy about it. Bunker schools are stacking against the rip at Great Point on the tail end of the ebb, and the blues underneath them are feeding hard, surface-crashing, the kind of visible chaos you can run to from half a mile out just by watching the birds. Same story working down through the Monomoy rips — Butler Hole side especially — where the current runs harder and the blues are using it to pin bait against the shoal edge. Poppers and metal — Hab's or a 2-ounce Kastmaster — thrown right into the wash have been getting hit on nearly every cast when the blitz is up. It doesn't last all morning. It's a window, usually 45 minutes to an hour around the tide change, and then the bait scatters and the blues go with it.

Stripers are still around, but they've gone quiet in the daylight, and that's not a mystery — the Sound's running warm enough now that bass are doing what warm-water bass do: sliding off the flats and structure during the heat of the day and feeding mostly after dark or in that gray window right at first light. Kings Point over on the Sound side is reading well up into the low 70s, and while Nantucket Sound proper stays a touch cooler thanks to better flush with the open Atlantic, it's warm enough that I'm not finding daytime bass on the shallow humps like I would in June. If you want stripers right now, fish the deeper cuts off Monomoy Point after dark, or hit the rips at first light before the sun gets up over the dunes — swimming plugs and darters worked slow through the current seam have been the ticket, and a couple of solid 30-inch fish came off Great Point last week on bunker chunks fished on the bottom during the night tide.

Fluke are the one thing I can tell you with real confidence is producing well right now, and it's been that way for a few weeks. Nantucket Shoals and Horseshoe Shoal both have been giving up keeper fluke consistently, mostly in that 20 to 40 foot range where there's current sweeping over structure — sand-to-mud transitions, the edges where the shoal drops. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift, have been outfishing bait for me the last two trips, though squid strips tipped on the hook never hurt. Fish are running mostly 18 to 22 inches with enough keepers mixed in to make a limit worth chasing if you're willing to grind a few drifts. The bite holds up regardless of the surface warmth that's pushing the bass around — fluke don't seem to care, they're locked onto the bottom structure and the bait riding the current over it.

Now, about that bonito question, because I know it's the one everybody's asking at the dock. Nothing confirmed yet in the Sound — no fish in the boat, no reliable flash reports from Great Point or the Monomoy side. But the water's been trending the right direction. We flagged last week that Block Island had crossed into comfortable bonito range, and the expectation was a spread east through the Sound over the following week or two. That's exactly the kind of signal that should be showing up here first — at Great Point where warm surface water funnels around the rip, and off Monomoy where the bait gets squeezed against structure. I'd bet the first Sound bonito of the season shows in one of those two spots before it shows anywhere else out here. Doesn't mean this week, but I wouldn't be surprised by next week.

Looking ahead: the moon's sliding toward first quarter on the 21st, which means the current's easing off its peak push. That usually means slower presentations start working better in the rips — less need to horse a bucktail through, more room for a natural drift. If the calm, stable weather we've had holds, I'd expect the bluefish blitzes to keep firing at Great Point and Monomoy on the tide changes, fluke to stay steady on the Shoals, and stripers to keep their heads down until after dark. And if I had one morning to burn this week, I'd be sitting on the Great Point rip at first light with binoculars up, watching every boil a little closer than I did last week — because when that first bonito does show, it's going to happen fast, and I don't want to be looking the other way.
