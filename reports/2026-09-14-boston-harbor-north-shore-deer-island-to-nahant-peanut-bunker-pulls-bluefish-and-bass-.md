# Deer Island to Nahant: Peanut Bunker Pulls Bluefish and Bass Into the Wash
*The harbor islands are holding the bait right now, but the North Shore rocks are where the bigger fish are starting to show behind it.*

**BOSTON, MA — September 14** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one tide window this week, work the harbor islands first thing and save the North Shore rocks for the evening push — that's the call right now, and here's why. Peanut bunker have been pouring out of the Mystic and Chelsea Creek systems all week, stacking up thick around Deer Island, Spectacle Island, and the President Roads channel edge. That's pulled bluefish in tight, and where the blues go, the bass follow a tide or two behind, sitting deeper on the structure and picking off what the blues miss.

We flagged this building last week — said the bait push off the new moon would drag striper and bluefish activity up with it, and that's exactly what's happening now. The tidal range has grown since the new moon, and that extra current is doing the work: it's concentrating peanut bunker and mackerel against structure instead of letting it spread out over open water. Wind's been manageable, mostly light out of the northwest in the mornings, which has kept the harbor and the Nahant side clean enough to read bait on top. That clarity matters — captains working Deer Island Light and the flats off Winthrop this week have been able to see bunker showering ahead of feeding fish instead of guessing.

Inside the harbor, the pattern has been consistent on the last two hours of the ebb. Current stacks bait against the Deer Island outfall structure and along the drop where the channel falls off toward President Roads, and bluefish in the 3- to 6-pound class have been chopping through it hard, with occasional bigger cows mixed in. Small metal — Deadly Dicks, half-ounce Kastmasters, or a 3-inch epoxy jig in bunker color — worked on a fast, erratic retrieve has been the go-to when the blues are up top. When they push down, switching to a bucktail in the 1- to 1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, bounced along that channel edge, has been picking up both bluefish and better-grade bass in the 28- to 34-inch range. Spectacle Island's north side has also been holding fish on the last of the ebb, particularly where the current wraps the point — that's a spot worth a drift with live mackerel under a slip float if you can get bait, since the bigger stripers there have been showing a preference for something with more presence than plastic.

Up the North Shore, Nahant has been the more consistent bite for stripers specifically. The rocks off East Point and the ledges running out toward Bass Point have been holding fish on the incoming tide, especially in that hour around first light when the mackerel schools push in tight to feed. Working a 3/4-ounce bucktail with a swim shad trailer, or a topwater popper early before the sun gets on the water, has been drawing strikes from bass in the mid-20s to low-30s range, with the occasional better fish mixed in on the rougher structure closer to the point. Gloucester and Cape Ann have been slower to turn on this week — the bait hasn't pushed that far north in numbers yet — but the rock piles off Brace Cove and the outer edges near Eastern Point are worth checking as the week goes on, since that's typically where the North Shore bite catches fire once the migration gets rolling in earnest.

South toward Plymouth, Duxbury Bay, and Scituate, the story has been quieter, and it's worth being honest about that. Water there hasn't seen the same bait concentration yet, and reports out of that stretch have been mixed — some flounder still coming off the mud flats in Duxbury Bay on sandworms and clam strips fished on the bottom during the last of the outgoing, but striper action has been spotty at best. That's likely to change as more bait gets pushed south and the fall pattern deepens, but right now it's not the priority stop.

Looking at the next few days, the setup favors more of the same with a chance to build. Tidal range keeps growing into the weekend as we move away from the new moon, which should tighten up the bait even further against structure in the harbor and along the Nahant rocks. If the wind stays out of the northwest and holds under 15 knots, expect the blitz-style feeding on peanut bunker to become more frequent and more visible, particularly on the outgoing tide in the harbor and the incoming along the North Shore beaches. Bonito have been showing at the outer harbor edge in small numbers, and that's worth keeping an eye on for anyone running light spinning gear with small metal — it hasn't turned into a real pattern yet, but the ingredients are there. The bigger story over the next week is bass committing harder to the bait as water continues easing, so anyone with flexibility should lean toward the harbor islands early and the Nahant ledges on the evening tide, with Gloucester next in line once that bait wave reaches Cape Ann.
