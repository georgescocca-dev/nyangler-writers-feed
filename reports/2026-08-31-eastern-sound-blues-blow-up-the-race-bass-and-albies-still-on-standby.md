# Blues Blow Up The Race — Bass and Albies Still On Standby
*Bunker-fed bluefish are running the rip hard while the fleet waits on the first bonito sign and the bass hold their cards.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 31** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning at The Race looked like a war zone. Gulls stacked three deep over a bunker school getting hammered from below, blues to 12 pounds busting bait on top for the better part of an hour on the last of the ebb, with a handful of slot bass mixed in tight to the current seam where the rip folds back on itself. That's the read on Eastern Sound right now — bluefish are the engine, bass are riding shotgun, and everybody's watching for the first sign of false albies or bonito that hasn't landed yet.

The why is straightforward. Kings Point is reading 70.9 and New London's sitting at 69.4, which puts a real thermal gradient across the Sound west to east — warmer water pushing bait and predators toward structure and current edges where the temperature break holds. The moon's building toward full, tidal flow is picking up day over day, and that combination is exactly what stacks bait in the pinch points — The Race, Plum Gut, the Orient Point rip, the reefs off Fishers Island. Bunker schools are getting squeezed into those funnels and the blues are all over them. We flagged bluefish tracking bunker density last week and that's exactly what's playing out — where the bunker sit, the blues show up in numbers, full stop.

Bluefish are the most dependable thing going right now, and they're not subtle about it. Poppers and 2-ounce Deadly Dicks worked on the up-current side of the rip at Plum Gut are getting hit on nearly every retrieve when the bunker are balled up. Ava 27s and 17s bounced through the lower third of the water column at The Race on the max of the ebb are producing better than most people expect this late in August — fish to 14 pounds reported off Fishers Island Sound on bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig in 25 to 40 feet. If you want quality over quantity, work the edges of the blitz rather than the middle — the bigger fish tend to sit on the margins picking off cripples instead of fighting the pack.

Bass are in a genuine holding pattern. There's fish around — the surf has been giving up slot and overslot stripers on hard-running current edges from Orient Point down through the Fishers Island rips, mostly on bucktails worked slow off the bottom right where the flat drops into the channel. But this isn't blitz behavior yet. The bunker haven't concentrated enough to trigger the kind of feeding that turns individual fish into a wolf pack, and until that bait density builds — which the building moon and stronger tides over the next week or two should force — expect the bass bite to stay a game of right spot, right tide stage rather than a guaranteed thing. Best window has been the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the new flood, working eels or big soft plastics along the rock piles at Fishers Island and the drop-off at the east end of Plum Gut where the current stacks bait against structure.

Fluke have done exactly what late August fluke do — they've softened. This is the seasonal shift, not a crash, and we called that shift last week: fluke sliding into deeper channel structure and ocean-front edges as the shallow bay water loses its edge. The producing pattern right now is a slow drag with white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails along the deeper edges of Plum Gut and the channel drop-offs around Orient Point, fished tight to bottom on the last of the incoming when the current eases enough to hold bottom contact. Keeper-sized fish are still coming, but it's a pick, not a pile — expect two or three quality fish for an afternoon's work rather than a fast bag limit.

Porgies are still holding on the deeper rock and rubble bottom around Fishers Island and off Plum Gut in 30 to 50 feet, biting steady on bloodworms and squid strips fished on high-low rigs — a reliable fallback bite while everyone's attention is on the bigger stuff up top.

The thing everybody in the shops and on the radio keeps circling back to is the albie and bonito question. The Rhode Island push out of Block Island and Point Judith is close, and the current setup — building moon, stronger tidal flow, bait concentrating in the exact rip structure that mimics what's holding fish in Rhode Island right now — makes The Race and Plum Gut the logical next stop. It hasn't shown yet on this side of the line, so don't burn gas assuming it's happening; but if you're rigging up for the blitz anyway, keep a light spinning rod with small Deadly Dicks or Albie Snax rigged and ready on deck. When it turns on here it usually turns on fast and it doesn't wait around.

Look at the next several days through that lens. Wind stays manageable and the current keeps building toward the full moon, expect the bluefish bite to hold or improve at The Race and Plum Gut, the bass bite to stay a tide-specific pick along the rock structure at Fishers Island and Orient Point, and the albie watch to intensify — this is the window where it's most likely to break. If there's only one day to fish this week, work the last two hours of the ebb at Plum Gut with poppers ready for blues and a bucktail rigged for whatever else shows up in the wash. That's where the action's concentrated right now, and that's where it's most likely to change first.
