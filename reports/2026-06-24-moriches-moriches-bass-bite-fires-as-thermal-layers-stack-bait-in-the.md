# Moriches bass bite fires as thermal layers stack bait in the inlet
*Trophy stripers hit live spot at night while fluke action heats up in skinny bay water.*

**MORICHES, NY — June 24** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

The inlet's been absolutely electric this past week, and I can tell you exactly why. Water temps hit 68.2 degrees on the ocean side while the bay's running a solid 4 degrees warmer at 72 — that thermal gradient is creating a conveyor belt of bait through Moriches Inlet that's got the bass stacked like cordwood.

Night fishing has been the ticket for trophy stripers. I'm talking fish in the 35-inch class hitting live spot on fishfinder rigs, worked tight to the inlet's north jetty on the outgoing tide. The key is getting your bait down into that 8-to-12-foot channel where the cooler ocean water meets the warmer bay flow. That thermocline is holding massive schools of peanut bunker, and the bass are gorging themselves in the dark.

The bathymetry here tells the whole story. When that bay water dumps out on the ebb, it's carrying everything with it — grass shrimp, juvenile weakfish, sand eels — and funneling it right through the inlet's throat. I've been positioning at the inlet mouth just as the tide turns, watching my sonar light up with bait clouds at 15 feet. Drop a live spot into that chaos on a 6/0 circle hook with a 2-ounce egg sinker, and you're in business.

Fluke fishing has turned the corner in the bay's skinny water. We're seeing consistent action in 4 to 8 feet over the sand flats between Tuthill Point and the East Moriches channel. The secret is downsizing everything — 1/4-ounce bucktails with 3-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets, dragged painfully slow on the drift. These bay fluke are spooky in the shallow water, but when you dial in the presentation, you're looking at fish to 6 pounds.

The water clarity has been exceptional after last week's northwest winds cleaned out the bay. I'm seeing bottom in 6 feet, which means the fluke can see your rig from a mile away. Switch to 15-pound fluorocarbon leaders and keep your jig heads light. The fish are relating to the subtle depth changes — those 2-foot drops off the channel edges that most guys blow right over.

Cupsogue Beach has been producing solid bluefish action on the evening tide. The blues are running 3 to 8 pounds, crashing bait in the wash as the sun drops. Metal lips and small poppers are getting crushed in that last hour of light. The key is reading the water — look for the nervous bait dimpling the surface about 100 yards out, then work your way into casting range.

Weakfish are starting to show in better numbers around the inlet. I picked up three fish to 4 pounds this week on small bucktails tipped with Gulp, working the deeper holes on the bay side during slack water. The fish are holding in 12 to 15 feet, relating to the channel edges where the current creates those subtle eddies.

The lunar cycle is setting up perfectly for the coming week. We've got a new moon approaching, which means spring tides and serious current flow through the inlet. That's going to flush even more bait out of the back bay and concentrate the feeding fish in predictable spots. I'm planning to hit the inlet hard on the big outgoing tides, especially that 2-hour window before slack water when everything gets pushed through the channel.

Looking ahead, these northwest winds should keep the water clean and the temps stable. The bass bite should only get better as we move deeper into summer, and I'm expecting the first real push of weakfish to show up with the stronger tides. Keep your live wells stocked with spot and your light tackle rigged — this is shaping up to be one of those weeks where everything comes together.
