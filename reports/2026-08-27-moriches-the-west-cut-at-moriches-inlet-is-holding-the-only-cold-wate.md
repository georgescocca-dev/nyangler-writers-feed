# The West Cut at Moriches Inlet Is Holding the Only Cold Water Left
*Warm-water fluke scattered across the bay flats, but the channel edge by the west jetty is still stacking bait — and bass — on the last of the ebb.*

**MORICHES, NY — August 27** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

The bottom of Moriches Inlet has been doing something worth watching this week. The scour hole on the ocean side of the west jetty — the one that deepened out after the last real nor'easter reworked the cut — is still holding a noticeably cooler bottom layer than the rest of the system, and it's become the only reliable current edge in the zone right now. Everything else, from the flats off Tuthill Point to the back stretches toward East Moriches, has gone soft and warm under this stretch of high pressure and light southwest wind. That thermal split is dictating almost everything happening out there this week.

Water in the open bay has been sitting warm and mostly slack on the tide changes, which is exactly the kind of regime that spreads fluke out instead of concentrating them. That's been the story for two weeks now — fish scattered thin across flats that would normally hold a tight pack, softer catch rates everywhere except where current keeps the bottom stirred and a touch cooler. The inlet channel, the west cut, and the deeper edge along Cupsogue are the exceptions, and they've been fishing accordingly better than the open bay. We flagged this pattern last week — said tide-driven windows would be the best bet through the stretch — and that's held up almost exactly. The bite hasn't gotten easier, it's gotten narrower, concentrated into the last two hours of the ebb and the first hour of the flood when the current is actually moving water across structure.

Striped bass are in their expected late-August holding pattern — not absent, just not aggressive in daylight. Warm surface temps push them tight to structure and into low-light programs. The better bass action has been coming off bunker schools working the ocean side off Cupsogue and Westhampton, where boats trolling bunker spoons and umbrella rigs in 15 to 25 feet have picked up cocktail-to-mid-20-pound fish mixed in with bluefish on the same schools. Inside the inlet itself, bucktails in the 1- to 1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked along the deep edge of the west cut on the last two hours of the outgoing, have been the more consistent play for anyone working from a boat or the jetty rocks. Let it settle into that scour hole, hop it slow along bottom — that's where the cooler water is holding both bait and fish.

Bluefish have been the one constant nobody's complaining about. Snapper blues are thick through the bay — Tuthill Point, the flats off Mastic, even up into the creeks — small enough that bait shops are selling out of tiny bucktails and Kastmasters just for that action. Cocktail-sized blues in the 2- to 4-pound class are mixing into both the fluke drift and the troll off the inlet, hitting anything moving with flash. If there's a guaranteed bend in the rod this week, it's a snapper blue off a bay dock on a strip of spearing.

Fluke are the honest disappointment of the week, but not a surprise given the water. The better fish — some to 4 and 5 pounds — are still coming from the channel edges near Moriches Inlet and the deeper cuts off Cupsogue, where boats drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, are working 12 to 18 feet of water on the last of the ebb. Away from that current-swept structure, it's mostly a shorts show. Weakfish have shown up sporadically in the same deeper holes near the inlet, mostly incidental fish on bucktails intended for fluke, nothing worth planning a trip around. Porgies remain a dependable bay fish over structure and shell bottom off Westhampton and the inlet rocks, taking clam and squid on light tackle in 10 to 20 feet — a solid backup when the fluke bite goes quiet.

Cupsogue Beach continues to be the most productive surf stretch in the zone, and the reason is bathymetric, not just seasonal — the sandbar troughs there hold bait against a moving current in a way the rest of the ocean beach doesn't right now, and that's been enough to draw both bass and blues within casting range at dawn and dusk. Anyone working the surf should focus on the cuts between bars rather than the open beach face.

Looking ahead, the moon is building toward full, and that alone should sharpen these bite windows rather than blow them open. Bigger tidal swings mean more water moving through the inlet channel and the west cut on each change, which should tighten up both the bass and fluke bite there even as the open bay stays soft. Watch for the first hints of the fall shift starting to nudge things — cooler nights are still a couple weeks off from meaningfully dropping bay temps, so this holding pattern likely persists through the week. If there's only one window to fish, it's the last two hours of the ebb at the inlet, working the deep edge where the cold water is still hiding.
