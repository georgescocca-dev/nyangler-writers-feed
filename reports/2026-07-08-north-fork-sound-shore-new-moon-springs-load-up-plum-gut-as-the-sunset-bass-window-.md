# New moon springs load up Plum Gut as the sunset bass window reopens
*Bigger tides start pushing bait through the Gut this weekend, and the bass are answering the dinner bell at last light.*

**MATTITUCK, NY — July 8** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Last week was one of those stretches where you had to work for it. Wind sat out of the southwest most days, laid the Sound down flat by afternoon but stirred just enough chop overnight to keep the bait from settling anywhere predictable. We were coming off the June full moon, so tides were already easing toward neap — slack water lingered longer at Horton Point and Rocky Point, and the current through Plum Gut never really got up on its legs. Fish were around, but scattered, feeding in short windows and shutting off fast. Surface water's been holding warm right through the heat, the kind of warm where the bass want to be down deep during the day and only come up to hunt when the light drops off.

That's changing this week. We're building toward a new moon around the 13th, which means the tides are loading up again — bigger swings, stronger push through the Gut, more water moving past Orient Point on the ebb. The prediction I like most for our stretch is that PM flood window lining up with sunset from Friday through Monday, peaking the 11th through 13th. That's the first evening high-tide bite we've had since before the full moon, and it's worth building your week around. When the flood stacks up against last light out at the Gut approach, bass that have been sulking deep all day get pushed up onto the structure to intercept bait getting squeezed through the current. That's when you want to be standing on the rocks, not sitting in the truck.

Bass fishing off Horton Point has been a game of patience more than volume. Guys working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs into the deeper water off the point have been picking up fish in the 28 to 34-inch range, mostly on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current slows enough for a bait to sit still. Bucktails haven't been doing much there in daylight — too bright, water's too settled — but at Plum Gut itself, the story's different. Guys drifting white or chartreuse 1 1/2-ounce bucktails with a curly-tail trailer through the rip on the ebb have connected with better fish, some pushing 20 pounds, holding right where the Gut current meets the slower water off Orient. That's classic Plum Gut structure fishing — find the seam, not the middle of the rip, and let the bait swing through it instead of retrieving fast.

Bluefish have been the more reliable show. Bunker schools have been working their way through the Gut on the tide changes, and where you find pods of bunker getting nervous on the surface, there are choppers underneath cutting them up. Topwater poppers and metal — Kastmasters, Deadly Dicks — have been drawing fast strikes off Rocky Point and out toward Orient. Nothing giant, mostly 3 to 5 pounds, but they hit hard and they don't make you wait around.

Fluke have shifted the way you'd expect with the heat we've had. Inshore water's been warm enough that the fluke that were sitting shallow have pushed out toward the deeper channel at Mattituck Inlet and the drop-off just outside it. Guys drifting white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails through that inlet channel on the outgoing have found keeper-class fish, four and five pounds, mixed with a lot of short ones you have to sort through. It's not a bang-bang bite — more like one or two solid fish for every dozen drifts — but the fish are there if you put your time in on the moving current rather than the slack.

Porgies have been steady and unspectacular off the reef structure around Rocky Point, small hooks and sandworms or clam on high-low rigs in 20 to 30 feet. Nothing to write home about size-wise but a good bend-your-rod option when the bigger stuff isn't cooperating, and a fine way to fill an hour waiting on the tide to turn for bass.

Blackfish have been quiet, which isn't unusual for July — tautog don't love the warm water and tend to hold tight to structure in slightly deeper holes this time of year. If you're looking for them, fish green crab on jig heads tucked right against the rock piles off Horton Point in 25 to 35 feet, but don't expect fast action.

Weakfish, I'll be straight with you — still nothing. The water's been plenty warm enough for them by any number I've seen, but warm water alone doesn't bring weakfish back if the fish aren't in the system. I haven't heard a credible weakfish report out of this stretch all season, and I'm not going to pretend otherwise just because the temperature checks a box.

Looking ahead, the new moon springs building into the weekend are the story. If the wind stays reasonable — nothing hard out of the southwest to muddy up the Gut — I'd be at Plum Gut or the Orient Point approach Friday through Monday evening, working that PM flood right into sunset. That's the first real evening window we've had since the last full moon, and the bigger tidal push should wake up fish that have been lying low in the heat. Plan B if the bass don't show: work the Mattituck Inlet channel on the outgoing for fluke, because that bite's been consistent even when the bass have been stubborn.
