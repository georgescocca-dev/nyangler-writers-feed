# Eel Down, 38 Pounds Up — The Race Turns On at the Turn
*A big bass off Fishers Island confirms what the current's been telegraphing all week: the rip is loading up ahead of the fall push.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 27** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

A 38-pound striper came over the rail at the top of the ebb off the western end of Fishers Island this week, taken on a live eel drifted tight against the rock on the down-current side of the rip. That's the fish the docks from Orient to New London are talking about right now, and it's not an outlier — it's a signal. The bait is stacking hard against structure in the Race and Plum Gut, and the current is doing exactly what it does this time of year: compressing everything into a tight window at the turn of the tide.

Water's still warm for the calendar. New London is reading 69.4°F, Montauk's holding at 69.1°F, and that's actually the cooler side of the Sound — Kings Point up west is pushing 70.9°F. That gradient matters. It means the eastern stretch, from Mount Sinai out through Wading River and into Orient, is running a few degrees cooler than the central basin, and cooler water out here means the bass and blues stay more active through the daylight hours instead of shutting down and waiting for dark like they're doing to the west. We flagged this exact stretch last week as an early fall-staging zone, and it's playing out — bait is tightening up along that shoreline, and both bluefish and stripers are following it in tighter to the beach than they were two weeks ago.

The moon's building toward full over the next few days, which means bigger tidal swings and sharper current through the Race and Plum Gut. That's the single biggest factor to plan around this weekend. When that current runs 4 to 5 knots at max ebb, the fish don't chase — they hold in the eddies and let the bait wash to them. The window that matters is the hour before and after the turn, when the water slows enough for a bass to commit without burning energy fighting the rip. Show up mid-tide expecting a bite and you'll be fishing dead water.

Striped bass are the story right now, and they're behaving like late-August bass should — daytime activity pushed onto structure and into the deeper eddies, with the real window opening up at the night bite. The boulder fields off Orient Point and the north side of Fishers Island are holding fish after dark on big pencil poppers and Danny-style swimmers worked slow across the surface at the first hour of the incoming. If you want numbers over size, live eels drifted through the western Race on the last two hours of the ebb are still the most consistent producer the fleet's reporting — start the drift up-current of the visible boil line and let it swing naturally into the slack pocket behind the rock.

Bluefish are the dependable piece of this puzzle. Jumbo cocktail blues to 12 pounds are mixing right into that same striper program on the rips, hitting everything from bunker chunks to metal-lip swimmers worked through the froth at Plum Gut. Snapper blues are thick in every harbor from Port Jefferson to Orient — small poppers and Kastmasters off the docks at dusk will keep a rod bent for anyone who wants an easy hour with a kid.

Fluke are the soft spot in the report, and there's no dressing it up. The warm surface layer has spread fish out across the flats instead of concentrating them, and catch rates have thinned out everywhere except where there's real depth and current to hold a cooler bottom layer. That means Plum Gut's deep channel and the drop-off on the New London side of the Race are still worth a drift with a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a bucktail — 3/4 to 1 ounce depending on the stage of tide — but the days of stacking doubles on the flats are done for now. Fish deep, fish the seam, and don't expect volume.

Porgies are quietly one of the better bets in the zone right now. The rock piles and reef structure around Fishers Island and the north side of Plum Gut are holding good numbers in 20 to 40 feet, and sandworms or clam on a high-low rig will fill a cooler fast on the slower stages of the tide when the current's not ripping too hard to hold bottom.

Looking a few days out: expect the pattern to hold, maybe sharpen. As the moon fills, tidal exchange through the Race and Plum Gut should get more aggressive, which tightens the bite windows but also concentrates the fish harder against structure — that's good news if you can time it. There's also real reason to keep an eye on Fishers Island Sound and the water toward Block Island. Squid have started showing in numbers up in Rhode Island and Nantucket Sound, which is usually the first domino before bonito and albies push west along this stretch. Nothing confirmed on the dock yet, but the ingredients are lining up. If there's only one trip in you this week, make it the last two hours of the ebb at the Race with an eel in hand, or first light at Orient with a popper — those are the two windows with the best odds of a real fish on the end of the line.
