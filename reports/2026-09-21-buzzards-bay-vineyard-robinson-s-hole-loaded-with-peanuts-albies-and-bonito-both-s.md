# Robinson's Hole loaded with peanuts — albies and bonito both stacked on the rip
*Harvest Moon tides are ripping bait through the Elizabeths, and both albies and bonito are camped on it.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — September 21** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Peanut bunker are getting squeezed through every gut in the Elizabeth Islands right now, and that's the whole story this week. Robinson's Hole and Quick's Hole are both loaded with bait on the last two hours of the ebb, silversides mixed in with the peanuts, and the current is moving hard enough on these Harvest Moon tides to pin that bait against the rock and hold it there. That's exactly the setup false albacore and bonito want, and both species have been all over it.

We flagged this two weeks ago — said the albie and bonito run building across southern New England was fully established in this zone, and it's held right through. If anything it's tightened up. The bonito showed first at Robinson's Hole in the last week of August, and now the albies have caught up and, in spots, are pushing the bonito out of the way on the same bait balls. That's a good problem to have.

Water's still carrying summer heat into fall, which is why this bite has legs it doesn't always have by late September. The warm filament that's been parked over the Northeast all month hasn't broken yet, and until a real cold front comes through and knocks temps down hard, there's no reason for these fish to leave. The tradeoff is the fishing has stayed a little temperamental — strong for twenty, thirty minutes around the tide change, then it shuts off completely until the next stage. Anyone who fished it expecting an all-day blitz got frustrated. Anyone who showed up forty minutes before the tide turned did fine.

Menemsha has been the most consistent read all week. The rip off the jetty and out toward the western edge of Vineyard Sound has been holding bait on the last of the incoming and first two hours of the outgoing, and that's when the boats working out of there have found albies busting steady on top — not huge fish, mostly 8 to 12 pounds, but plenty of them once you find the right push of current. Half-ounce Deadly Dick and Albie Snax in bone or pink/white have been the call, cast just ahead of a breaking fish and worked with a fast, erratic retrieve. Epoxy jigs in 1/2 to 3/4 ounce have also been producing when the fish get finicky and start refusing straight retrieves — a stop-and-go with the jig gets more takes than a steady burn.

Bonito have been thicker at Robinson's Hole itself, right in the throat of the cut where the current squeezes down and dumps into Buzzards Bay side. Small profile is the key there — 00 or 0 size Deceivers, size 1 Albie Snax, anything that matches those peanut bunker rather than the bigger stuff. Bonito want it close to the boat and want it moving fast; albies will chase further and tolerate a slower retrieve. If you're marking both on the same push of bait, downsize first for the bonito, then upsize slightly once the albies take over, which tends to happen as the tide builds toward max flow.

Cuttyhunk has been quieter for albies this week but that's turned into a bass and bluefish story instead. Bunker schools are moving through Buzzards Bay on these bigger tides, and stripers have been shadowing them along the rip off the west end of the island and out toward Sow and Pigs. Bluefish are riding the same bait, and they've been the more reliable of the two — snapper-sized fish mixed with a few in the 3-4 pound class blitzing on top when the bunker get pinned against the rocks on the last of the ebb. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5 ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked along bottom on the drift, have taken stripers up to the mid-30s off Cuttyhunk's west side. Live-lined bunker on a stinger rig has also worked for anyone anchoring up tight to the rip.

Fluke have hung around longer than usual this fall, which tracks with how warm everything's stayed. Vineyard Sound structure — the drop-offs around Middle Ground and the deeper edges off Oak Bluffs — is still giving up keeper fluke to 4 and 5 pounds on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and bucktail-Gulp combos dragged slow along bottom in 25 to 35 feet, best on the last two hours of the outgoing when there's still enough current to work a jig properly without getting swept sideways. That bite is living on borrowed time, though — the first real cold front that pushes water temps down hard will tighten it up fast, so worth hitting now if it's on your list.

Scup have been the dependable fallback all week, holding around the Woods Hole drawbridge pilings and the rock piles off Naushon on both sides of the tide, taking squid strips and clam on light tackle in 15 to 25 feet. Nothing exciting, but steady enough that it's a good option when the albie window closes and there's still daylight left.

Looking ahead, the pattern that's been working should hold through the weekend as long as this warm stretch doesn't break. The Harvest Moon tides are still building toward their peak pull, which means stronger current and tighter bait concentrations at Robinson's Hole, Quick's Hole, and the Menemsha rip through the next several days — that's where the time belongs if albies or bonito are the target. If there's only one window to fish, make it the two hours around the tide change at Robinson's Hole on an outgoing tide; that's been the most consistent combination of bait, current, and fish all week. Keep an eye on any cooling trend moving in behind the moon — when the water starts dropping, this whole pattern can shift east toward the Vineyard's south side fast, and the smart move is staying mobile rather than parking on one spot out of habit.
