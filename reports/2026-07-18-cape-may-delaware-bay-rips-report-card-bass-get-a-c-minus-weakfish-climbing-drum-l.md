# Rips Report Card: Bass Get a C-Minus, Weakfish Climbing, Drum Long Gone
*Warm water pushed the stripers into the dark and pulled the weakfish back into the bay channels right on schedule.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 18** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Grading the rips this week: C-minus on bass, B-plus on bluefish, incomplete on weakfish because they're just now walking back into the room. That's the honest read, and I'm not going to dress it up past that.

The ocean side has been running warm and a little sloppy — 73, 74 degrees out at the edge of the shipping lanes, with a low swell out of the south keeping a light chop on the rip line most mornings. That's not a wall, it's just enough push to make the drift a little sloppier than I'd like on the last two hours of the incoming. Striped bass haven't left the rips, but they've gone strictly nocturnal on us, which is a thermal thing, not a fish-count thing. We're seeing them stack up over the humps off the Point on the sounder after dark and go quiet as soon as the sun gets any angle on the water. If you want a bass out there right now, you're fishing the last two hours before first light or right at last light on the ebb, working bucktails or eels down deep — 30 to 50 feet — where that water's still holding some coolness off the bottom. Daytime, forget it. I had two charters out there this week on the morning flood and we managed shorts and one 29-incher on a white bucktail tipped with a Fin-S strip, worked slow along the bottom edge of the rip. That's a C-minus day and I'm not going to tell you different.

Hereford Inlet's been the better story. Bunker pods are stacked up along the beachfront from the inlet down toward Wildwood, and the bluefish have been under them daily — not blitz-every-hour stuff, but reliable, especially on the last of the outgoing when that bait gets funneled and squeezed through the inlet mouth. Diamond jigs, an ounce and a half, worked with a hard snap-and-drop, have been getting bit steady, and if you've got poppers, throw them right into the surface nervousness when the gulls start working — those blues are eating up top more than they're eating deep right now. Fluke inside the inlet have been a grind on the smaller stuff, but we flagged this a week ago — said the back-bay fluke would start sliding out to the inlet mouths and ocean structure as the water kept climbing, and that's exactly what's happening. The keeper-class fish are showing on the reef sites — Wildwood Reef and the stone piles off Cape May Point — in 25 to 35 feet, drifting Gulp Swimming Mullets in white and chartreuse on 3/4-ounce bucktails. Back bay's still holding shorts by the bucket, but if you want a keeper for the cooler, you're running out to the reefs now.

Delaware Bay's a different animal this time of year. The drum run wrapped up back in late May like it does every spring — that fishery's closed out for the season, and anybody still soaking bunker chunks off Fortescue hoping for a straggler drum is wasting bait. What's interesting is the weakfish. Bay water's pushed into the mid-70s, and that's the exact trigger — we called this last week, said the warm bay water would bring them back into the channel drop-offs on calm evenings, and sure enough they're staging where the warm surface water meets that cooler current coming through the channel edges. Ship John, the deeper cuts off Egg Island, and the drop-off in front of Fortescue have all given up weakfish in the 2 to 4-pound range on the early morning outgoing, mostly on bucktails tipped with a strip of squid or worked slow with a swimming plug over the channel lip. It's not a stacked bite yet — more like three or four fish on a good tide instead of none — but it's building, and if the pattern holds on these calm, settled evenings we've had, I'd expect that grade to climb into next week.

Water's been clean out at the rips, cleaner than I'd expect for mid-July, which tells me the wind's been cooperating more than the tide charts have. We're running toward the first quarter moon on the 21st, which means the current's easing off the bigger push we had around the full moon — that usually favors a slower presentation in the rips, less about horsing a big bucktail through and more about working it methodically along bottom structure. If I had one morning to fish this week, I'd take the last hour of the outgoing at Hereford before first light, throw bucktails at the bluefish under the bait, then run out to the reef structure once the sun's up for fluke. If I had one evening, I'd be on the bay at Ship John working that channel edge for weakfish before the light goes flat.

Look, it's mid-July — the ocean side isn't going to hand you an easy bass day right now, and anybody telling you different is selling something. But the bluefish are reliable, the fluke moved right where we said they would, and the weakfish are starting to show up on schedule. That's enough to build a trip around if you pick your tide and don't waste time waiting on a daytime striper that isn't coming.
