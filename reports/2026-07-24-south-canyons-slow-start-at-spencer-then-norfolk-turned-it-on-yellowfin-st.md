# Slow start at Spencer, then Norfolk turned it on: yellowfin steady through the week
*A shaky Monday at Spencer gave way to a Norfolk chunk bite that never quit, with mahi riding shotgun the whole way.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 24** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Monday I ran the numbers to Spencer expecting the same warm push we'd been riding, and it just wasn't there. Marks were soft, water looked right on the temp gauge but the fish weren't stacked on it the way they'd been the week before. Pulled the spread for four hours along the 100-fathom curve, one flat-fall knockdown, no hookup. That's canyon fishing — you go where the intel says and sometimes it's stale by the time your spread hits the water. I picked up and ran south.

By Wednesday I had Norfolk dialed and it was a different world. Clean blue water, a hard edge sitting right along the 300-fathom line on the north wall, and yellowfin stacked under it from 60 to 90 feet down. We anchored up on the edge with the chunk — butterflied bunker, a slow bag going out, and it turned into as steady a bite as I've had all month. Fish from 25 to low 40s, a couple pushing 50, all on 6/0 circle hooks buried in the butterfly, 40-pound fluoro leader, no wire. The current was moving nice, maybe a knot and a half, which kept the chunks sliding at the right pace instead of piling up under the boat. That's the difference between a good chunk bite and a dead one — current speed dictates everything.

We flagged this last week — said the canyon bite would hold as long as the offshore water stayed put, and it did exactly that. High pressure never budged, the shelf break stayed loaded with warm, clean blue water pushing into the high 70s at the surface, and Norfolk cashed in on it while Spencer took a breather.

Thursday we ran Poor Man's on a tip about a temp break showing warm water wrapping the western edge, and found mahi before we found tuna. Weed line stretched almost two miles, holding gaffer-sized mahi in the 8 to 15 pound range, plus a pile of schoolies that kept the rods bent on spinning gear with skirted ballyhoo. Green machines and pink Ilanders on the troll picked off a few yellowfin working the same structure, 30 to 35 pounds, but the real show was under that weed. If you've got light spinning tackle rigged with a small popper or a chunk on a jig head, that's money on floating structure like that — don't just run past it on the way to the drop.



Lindenkohl was the one I skipped this week. Had two separate calls saying the water there had gone soft and green along the edge, temp break not holding shape, and I've learned not to burn fuel chasing a broken edge when three other canyons are producing. That's the whole game running four canyons out of Cape May and Ocean City — you're not married to one piece of water, you're reading which one's holding structure day to day and moving the boat accordingly.

Bluefin showed up mixed in at Norfolk too, slot-class fish in the 50 to 70 inch range crashing the chunk slick a few times, quick fights, quick releases since we're still working inside the post-July 3 slot rules. Nothing trophy-sized showed, which tracks with what's been happening fleet-wide since that closure — steady grinding on the smaller class fish rather than the giants.

No billfish action to report this week out of any of the four. Had one bill swipe at a naked ballyhoo trolling the temp break at Poor Man's Thursday morning but no eat, no hookup. Water's right for white marlin, temp's holding in that window, but the reports just aren't backing it up yet. Wahoo were quiet too — one solitary fish, mid-40s, caught on a high-speed diving plug trolled at 14 knots along the Norfolk edge Wednesday afternoon, but that was the only wahoo intel I've got worth passing along.

Looking at the week ahead, I like Norfolk to keep producing if that north wall edge holds its shape — the chunk bite there has the kind of rhythm you want, steady current, steady marks, fish committing to the slick instead of just flashing under it. I'd run there first if I only had one day. Second choice is Poor Man's for the weed line mahi if it's still floating out there — those lines move with the current so it might not be exactly where we found it, but the pattern of warm water holding structure along that western edge should persist as long as high pressure doesn't break down. Spencer I'd give another look by midweek — sometimes these edges just need a few days to reload after they go quiet, and the same thermal pattern that's fueling Norfolk and Poor Man's should eventually wrap back around. Lindenkohl I'm leaving alone until somebody calls me with a better report than 'soft and green.'

Bring extra leader material regardless of which canyon you pick — the fish have been leader-shy on the troll, biting better on fluoro than mono, and losing a couple rigs to bluefin teeth is just part of the cost of doing business out there right now.
