# Matinecock Bunker Balls Pull Blitzing Blues While Bass Sit on the Edges
*Bluefish are tearing up bait from Hart Island to Execution Rocks, but the bass are still staged and picky in water that hasn't quite turned the corner.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — September 4** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The talk at the counter this week wasn't about stripers, it was about blues. With the Surfside Hotel derby lines full of guys chasing tournament fish, the question everybody kept asking was the same one I've been asking myself since August: when does all this bunker turn into a bass blitz instead of a bluefish blitz? Right now the answer is patience. We flagged this last week — said the bluefish would stay elevated through the tournament window and that any bunker holding past Labor Day could start pulling stripers in behind them — and that's exactly the shape of things out there right now. The blues showed up. The bass are still circling.

Water's still carrying summer heat. Kings Point is reading just under 71 degrees, and even out toward New London it's holding in the high 60s, so you've got almost no thermal gradient pushing fish to do anything in a hurry. That's the story of the whole week — warm, stable, bait-rich, and stripers that are staged but not committed. They're sitting under and around the bunker schools rather than exploding through them. When the water's in the low-to-mid 70s like this, bass get lazy about it. They'll eat, but you have to put it right on their nose instead of waiting for the boil.

The bluefish don't have that problem. Bunker schools have thickened up nicely off Matinecock Point and along the flats toward Hempstead Harbor, and the blues have been running through them hard on the last two hours of the ebb, right as that bait gets funneled and squeezed by the current. Boats working bunker spoons and 2-ounce Deadly Dicks off the schools have had doubles and triples most mornings, fish in the 4-to-8-pound range with the occasional bigger one mixed in. If you'd rather chunk, anchoring up-current of a bunker ball with a chunk on a fish-finder rig and letting it settle has been just as effective, maybe more so on the days when the surface action goes quiet but the fish are still stacked underneath.

Stripers are there too, just working the margins. Execution Rocks has been the most consistent spot for them, particularly right as the tide turns to outgoing — that rip line off the rocks concentrates whatever bait gets pulled through the Stepping Stones corridor, and bass have been sliding up onto it to pick off stragglers rather than crash the main school. Slow-rolled swim shads in the 5- to 7-inch range, white or bunker pattern, fished just off bottom in that 20-to-30-foot stretch, have out-produced anything fast or flashy. Same story around Hart Island — fish are there on the drop-off, but you need to slow down and work it like it's October, not July.

Fluke are winding down, right on schedule for early September. Numbers are thinning as the bigger fish start sliding out toward deeper structure ahead of the fall run, but there are still keepers to be had on the last of the tide around the Throgs Neck Bridge pilings and out toward the mouth of Manhasset Bay, mostly on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with squid strips, fished on a light bucktail so you can feel bottom in that 25-to-35-foot depth. Don't expect the numbers you had in July, but there's still a fish or two worth keeping if you put in the drift time.

Porgies are steady and reliable, which this time of year is worth something. Stepping Stones reef and the rockpiles off Little Neck Bay have been giving up decent porgies on sandworms and clam on a simple high-low rig, best on the last of the incoming when the current slows enough for bottom fish to feed comfortably. Weakfish have been scattered and inconsistent — a few reports of fish mixed in with the fluke around the deeper edges of Hempstead Harbor, but nothing you'd plan a trip around.

Looking ahead, the moon builds toward full on the 6th and 7th, and that's the piece of the puzzle everybody's watching. More moon means more current, and more current is what tightens up loosely scattered bait into the kind of concentrated schools that get bass to commit instead of just shadow. If the bunker stays put through this lunar push — and there's no reason yet to think it won't — the next real shift in this pattern is a legitimate striper blitz breaking open somewhere between Matinecock and Execution Rocks within the next week to ten days. It won't happen on a calendar, it'll happen the first cool night that knocks a couple degrees off the surface water and gets those bass aggressive instead of lazy.

For now, if you've only got one tide to fish this weekend, make it the last two hours of the ebb at Execution Rocks or off Matinecock Point — that's where the bait is thickest and where both the blues and the occasional committed bass are showing up together. Keep the bluefish gear rigged heavy in case the blitz swings your way, but don't be surprised if the striper bite stays a finesse game a little while longer. The ingredients are all there. It's just waiting on that first real push of cooler air to light the fuse.
