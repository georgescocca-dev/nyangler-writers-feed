# Slow Monday, stacked Thursday: Spencer's bigeye chunk bailed out a rough start
*The front half of the week had us running four different numbers looking for bait — the back half we didn't have to leave the slick.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 20** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Mixed grades this week, and I mean that literally spot by spot. Norfolk gets a C, Lindenkohl a solid B, Poor Man's a B-plus with an asterisk for the marlin work, and Spencer — Spencer earns the A. If you fished all four like we did, you saw the whole spectrum of what late July canyon fishing looks like right now: patchy in spots, loaded in others, and the difference came down to who found the temp break and who didn't.

Here's the why. High pressure sat on top of the Mid-Atlantic most of the week and didn't budge, and that mattered more than any single tactic. We said last week the bite would hold as long as the offshore water stayed put, and it did — that eddy of warm Gulf Stream water that's been nosing up against the canyon edges never slid off like it sometimes does when a front comes through. That kept bait pinned to the structure instead of scattering it, and it's the same reason Hudson, Wilmington, and Washington to the north have been quietly building all month. We're just getting our version of it down here.

Spencer was the standout. We worked the 100-fathom curl around 39°34'N/71°56'W starting before first light, running a standard spread — green-and-yellow machines down the shotgun, naked ballyhoo on the short riggers, a small spreader bar in the shotgun slot. Yellowfin from 25 to about 45 pounds came steady through the morning push, best window right at gray light through about 9 a.m. before the bite went quiet under the sun. That's when we switched over — anchored up on the edge in around 900 feet, butterfish chunks fanned out slow off the stern, and let it soak into the evening. Bigeye showed up reliably on that slick, biggest one we boated pushing 180. Night chunking is still the money tactic for bigeye down here, and it hasn't changed — small chunks, thin leader, patience, and staying off the spreader lights until the bite's established.

Lindenkohl fished a notch cooler and a notch slower on the tuna side, but mahi more than made up for it. We found a good weed line stacked with sargassum working the 60-fathom curve, and it was stop-and-go dolphin the whole drift — schoolies in the 5 to 10 pound range hammering green-and-white Ilanders and dink ballyhoo skipped off flat lines, with a couple better fish to 15 mixed in that wanted a pitch bait once we got baits in the water near the structure. If you're burning gas out there anyway, don't run past floating debris or a clean grass line without a look. That bite doesn't need perfect water temp, it just needs cover.

Poor Man's is where the billfish work is picking up, and it's exactly the pattern we flagged coming — white marlin migration building through July, and this week it showed. We had shots on teasers most days we ran that canyon, dink ballyhoo behind small spreader bars pulling fish up, and we put a handful of whites in the 38 to 42 inch class in the water on pitch baits before release. Nothing stacked wall to wall, but consistent enough that if marlin are your thing, Poor Man's is where I'd point the bow right now over Norfolk or Lindenkohl.

Norfolk was the disappointment, honestly. We ran down there twice hoping the deeper, more southern water would hold better numbers, and both times we found clean water but thin bait — the temp break wasn't as sharp, sitting closer to 74 to 75 degrees instead of the harder 76-plus edge we found up at Spencer. Marked some fish on the machine but couldn't buy a real bite until late in the day on the last trip, when a few smaller yellowfin came through on the troll right at the canyon's northern lip. If Norfolk's on your list this week, I'd treat it as a scouting run rather than the primary play until that edge tightens back up.

Looking ahead, the moon's rolling into the first-quarter neap on the 21st, which should ease current across the board — less water moving means chunk slicks hold shape longer and anchor fishing gets a little more forgiving, which favors the Spencer bigeye program specifically. Best case, that stable high pressure holds one more week and the eddy stays glued to the shelf edge the way it has been — if that happens, I'd keep running Spencer for the double-header troll-then-chunk day and lean on Poor Man's for marlin shots. If the water slides east like it sometimes does this time of year, Lindenkohl's weed lines become the fallback and Norfolk's the one I'd skip until it resets. Either way, check the temp break before you commit fuel — that's been the single biggest difference between a great trip and a mediocre one all week.
