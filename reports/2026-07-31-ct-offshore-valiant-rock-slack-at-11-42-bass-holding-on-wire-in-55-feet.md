# Valiant Rock Slack at 11:42: Bass Holding on Wire in 55 Feet
*The Race is easing toward neap but the bass never left Valiant — you just have to hit the window right.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — July 31** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

Four-point-one knots. That's what I clocked on the last max ebb through The Race on Wednesday, GPS-tracked drifting off Valiant Rock with 400 feet of wire out the transom. A week ago that same tide was running closer to five — we're sliding off the full moon push toward neap as the tide cycle winds down before it builds back up for the August 12 new moon. Less water moving through the gut means the bite windows stretch a little longer, which is good news if you've ever tried to get a bucktail down through five knots of white water and felt like you were fishing a fire hose.

Here's what that means on the water: slack at Valiant this week is running close to 11:40 in the late morning and shifting into the early evening around 6:15, give or take ten minutes day to day — check your local corrected tide before you commit, because a five-minute miss on either side of true slack there is the difference between a controlled drift and getting your wire line swept sideways into the rocks. I've been starting my drifts about twenty minutes before predicted slack, working from the 55-foot trough on the northeast side of the rock down into the 70-foot hole, wire line straight down with a 2-ounce bucktail tipped with a chunk of fresh bunker. Bass are there — not stacked, but present, mostly mid-20s to low 30s with a few pushing 20 pounds mixed in when I hit the tide change right on the nose. Anything after slack, once the current starts building again, the fish drop tight to the bottom of the rock pile and you need the wire just to hold depth against the drift.

Race Rock itself has been quieter than Valiant — I think the bait's thinner there right now, and with the Sound running warmer than the fish like (Kings Point read 70.9 this week, New London closer to 69.4), the stripers are picking their structure carefully, holding wherever the current stacks bait against hard bottom rather than spreading out over open rock. That thermal split between the western Sound and out here at the eastern end matters. New London's cooler water is exactly why we're still seeing quality fish on structure instead of pushed strictly nocturnal like they are further west and down the Jersey shore, where Hooper's crew and everybody else is reporting bass going deep and dark because the surface is pushing near 80.

Plum Gut has been the more consistent read the last several days. Current there tops out lower than The Race — I saw 3.6 knots on the last good ebb — but it concentrates bait hard against the east side drop-off toward Great Gull Island. Bluefish have moved in thick on that structure, choppers in the 8-to-12-pound class hammering diamond jigs and bucktails fished just off bottom in 35 to 50 feet on the last two hours of the outgoing. If you're throwing wire for bass and getting your leader bit off every other drift, that's your answer — the blues are mixed right in with the stripers right now, and honestly some days they're outnumbering them two to one. Not a bad problem to have if you just want bent rods, but if you're targeting bass specifically I'd fish bunker-tipped bucktails on the bottom third of the water column and let the smaller profile jigs stay up top for the blues.

Fluke have been steady rather than spectacular on the sand pockets flanking the Gut and along the edges of the Sluiceway, drifting Gulp swimming mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails through 25 to 40 feet on the last of the incoming. Keepers mixed with a lot of shorts — a decent box fish if you cover water, not a limit-in-an-hour bite. Sea bass remain the most dependable thing going right now. The wrecks and reef structure north of Plum Gut and around Little Gull are holding fish all day regardless of tide stage, and that's been the honest bread-and-butter program while the bigger species sort themselves out.

On the albie and bonito front — nothing confirmed yet in the Gut or off Valiant, and I'm not going to tell you otherwise. We flagged two weeks back that water temps at Montauk and Block Island were crossing the threshold and that building current should trigger the first real showing, and Montauk's still sitting right at 69.1, which is squarely in that window. I've had my eyes on the rips off Great Gull and the Sluiceway edge every trip, epoxy jig rigged and ready, and I haven't seen a scout yet. That could change fast — these fish show up on a good temperature break with almost no warning, and the building tide cycle heading toward mid-August is exactly the kind of setup that's produced early runs in past years.

For the coming week: with tides easing toward neap, expect longer, more fishable slack windows at Valiant and the Gut, which favors the wire-line program over covering water fast. If you've got one trip in you, I'd fish the evening slack at Valiant Rock and work the drop into darkness — that's where the bigger fish have been showing. Keep a bucktail rigged light in the box just in case something with a torpedo body and no patience for a slow retrieve finally shows up in the Gut.
