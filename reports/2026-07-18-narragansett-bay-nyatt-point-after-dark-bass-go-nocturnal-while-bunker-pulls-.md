# Nyatt Point after dark: bass go nocturnal while bunker pulls blues into the Bay
*The daytime bass bite has gone quiet with the warm water, but the bluefish are running hot on bunker and the fluke are sliding out toward the reefs.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — July 18** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Ran into Sal down at the Fields Point bait shack Tuesday morning, and he's shaking his head about the same thing everybody's been telling me all week — nobody's touching a bass in daylight anymore. Water's sitting warm enough up in the Providence River and around Conimicut that the stripers have wised up and gone to bed during the day. That's not them leaving the Bay, that's just thermal common sense. I've fished this water my whole life and I know what it means when the day bite dies but the guys running eels off the Mount Hope Bridge abutments at eleven o'clock are still coming home with fish.

Buckeye Brook's herring run wrapped up weeks ago — that's ancient history now, a spring memory. What's driving the Bay this week is bunker. Big pods of peanut and adult bunker have pushed up into Greenwich Bay and around Rocky Point, and the bluefish are camped underneath them exactly like you'd expect this time of year. We said last week the blues would follow the bunker schools up the coast and settle into a consistent pattern, and that's precisely what's happening — Rocky Point ledge, the mouth of Greenwich Cove, and the flats off Nyatt Point have all had bluefish blowing up on bunker in the last hour of the outgoing tide. Snag-and-drop with a treble on a wire leader is still the simplest way to connect, but if you want to have some fun with 'em, a chrome Kastmaster or a Hopkins fished fast through the melee will get bit just as quick. Fish are running three to six pounds, plenty of them, and they don't seem to be going anywhere as long as that bait stays thick.

Stripers themselves — I'm not going to sugarcoat it, the day bite off the bridges and points has been tough. You want bass right now, you fish the tide change after dark. Mount Hope Bridge, the Jamestown-Verrazzano pilings, and the rip off Conimicut Light have all given up fish on live eels and bunker chunks fished on the bottom during the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the flood. That current break where the East Passage funnels past Conimicut is money at night — bass are stacking on the edge waiting for baitfish to get swept off the flat. Nothing huge, mostly schoolies to low-20-inch fish, with the occasional slot fish mixed in for guys willing to put the hours in after dark. The moon's building toward first quarter on the 21st, so the tide swings are still moderate — once we get closer to full, expect the current to pick up and that night bite to sharpen up around the bridge structure.

Fluke are the one thing you can still count on in daylight, and true to what we expected, that bite is starting to slide out of the back coves and toward the reefs and channel edges. Guys working the drop-offs around Prudence Island and the deeper cuts off Nyatt Point are finding keepers in 20 to 35 feet on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and chartreuse bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom on the tail end of the incoming. The shallow warm coves that were holding fluke back in June have gotten too warm to be productive during the day — those fish have pushed to the cooler, deeper structure, which is exactly the shift you want to anticipate this time of summer. If you're marking bait on the meter around the Prudence flats, slow your drift down and work it — that's where the keepers are hiding.

Scup fishing has been the most reliable thing going on the rock piles. Rocky Point, the ledges off Nyatt Point, and the rip line running along Poppasquash Point have all been stacked with scup in the 10-14 inch range. Small pieces of squid or a chunk of green crab on a high-low rig, fished right on the bottom in 15 to 25 feet, is about as close to a sure thing as this Bay offers right now. Kids on the pier at Rocky Point have been filling five-gallon buckets on bloodworms too, if you can find them — but squid's cheaper and works just as well.

Weakfish have started trickling back into the mix on the calmer evenings, which lines up with what we figured would happen once the water settled and the wind laid down. Nothing consistent yet — a fish here, a fish there around the mouth of Greenwich Bay on soft plastics fished slow near bottom during the last light — but it's worth keeping an eye on if these stable, warm evenings keep holding through the week.

Looking ahead, I'd focus effort on the night tide around the bridges for bass, keep hammering the bunker schools for blues wherever they show, and shift your fluke game out toward Prudence and the deeper reef edges rather than the shallow coves. If the wind stays out of the south and the water keeps creeping up, expect the bass pattern to stay nocturnal and the fluke to keep pushing deeper. Nothing wrong with any of that — just means you fish smarter, not longer.
