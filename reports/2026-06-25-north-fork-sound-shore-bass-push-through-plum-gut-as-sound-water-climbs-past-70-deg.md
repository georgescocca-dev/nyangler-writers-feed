# Bass push through Plum Gut as Sound water climbs past 70 degrees
*Incoming tides stack bait against the North Fork rocks while weakfish show in deeper water.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — June 25** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Water's finally acting like summer out here. Sound temperature climbed past 70 degrees this week, and that's got everything moving. The stripers are pushing through Plum Gut in good numbers, following the bait that's been stacking up against our rocky shoreline on the incoming tides.

Best action's been from Horton Point east to Orient, working the deeper water where the channel swings close to shore. Bass to 35 pounds are taking live bunker on fishfinder rigs, but you need to get the bait down to them — they're holding 25 to 30 feet in this warm water. The key is positioning yourself where the incoming tide pushes against the rocks and creates that upwelling. Fish are sitting right in that seam where the current breaks.

Eels have been deadly after dark, especially around the oyster cages east of Rocky Point. Rig them on a fish-finder with just enough weight to hold bottom — maybe an ounce and a half in the current. Let the eel work in that 15-foot zone where the cages create structure. Had a regular pull three keeper bass there Tuesday night, biggest going 28 pounds.

Porgies are thick in 40 to 50 feet off Mattituck Inlet, and they're good-sized fish — plenty of keepers mixed in with the shorts. Clam strips on high-low rigs are producing steady, but the better fish want whole clams or green crabs. Work the incoming tide when the current's not ripping too hard.

Weakfish showed up this week for the first time since early May. Nothing huge, but fish to 4 pounds are taking small bucktails tipped with Gulp in 35 to 45 feet. Pink and chartreuse are working best, bounced slow along the bottom on the drift. They're scattered, not schooled up tight, so you need to cover water to find them.

Bluefish are around but not in the numbers I'd expect for late June. Mostly cocktail blues in the 2 to 4 pound range, hitting anything that moves. They're mixed in with the bass schools, so if you're live-lining bunker and getting chopped off, switch to wire or heavy fluorocarbon.

Bait situation's solid. Bunker are thick in the shallows, especially early morning and late afternoon. Spearing are showing in good numbers around the docks and pilings. Sand eels are in the deeper water, and that's what's drawing the bigger bass through the Gut.

Current's been running hard through Plum Gut with these spring tides — we're coming off the new moon, so the water's really moving. That's good for stirring up bait but makes it tough to fish light tackle. Plan your drifts accordingly, and don't be afraid to bump up your sinker weight.

Weather's been cooperative — light winds most days, seas under two feet. That's let the smaller boats get out and work the productive water without getting beat up.

Looking ahead, we're heading into the full moon cycle, which should keep these strong tides running another week. Water temperature's still climbing, and if it hits 72 degrees, expect the bass to push deeper or move to the rips where there's more current and cooler water. The weakfish bite should improve as they settle into their summer pattern. Keep an eye on Plum Gut — when the tide's running hard and the bait gets pushed through, that's when the big bass show up to feed.
