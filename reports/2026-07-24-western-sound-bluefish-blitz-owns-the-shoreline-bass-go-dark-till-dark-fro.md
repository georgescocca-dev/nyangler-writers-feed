# Bluefish blitz owns the shoreline, bass go dark till dark from Execution to the Throgs Neck
*Bunker schools have blues chewing along the beach in broad daylight, but the bass have punched out until the sun does too.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — July 24** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Guy at the bait shop in City Island asked me Tuesday if the stripers "packed up and left the Sound." I told him no, they didn't leave — they just got smart. Thirty years watching this water and every mid-July it's the same story: the bass go nocturnal on us, and the guys who don't adjust go home skunked wondering where all the fish went.

Here's what's actually happening. Kings Point is reading just under 71 degrees on the surface, and that's plenty warm enough to push mature stripers off the flats and down onto structure they can sit tight to during daylight — the deep water off Execution Rocks, the basin edges around Hart Island, the channel drop-off inside the Stepping Stones. During the day those fish are hunkered low, barely feeding, waiting for the sun to lose its grip. Come dusk, through the night, and into that gray hour before dawn, they climb back up onto the rockpiles and rip lines and they feed hard. If you're fishing this water at 10am wondering why your bucktail isn't getting bit, that's why.

I had a guy out Sunday night — we anchored up-current of Execution on the last two hours of the outgoing, right where that rock pile breaks up the tide, and we picked at bass to 28 inches on live eels drifted back with just enough weight to keep them down near bottom. Nothing crazy, but steady enough to keep the rods bent every twenty minutes or so from about 9pm until the tide went dead around midnight. That outgoing push off Execution has been money for as long as I've fished it, and it doesn't stop being money just because it's July — it just means you fish it after the tiki torches go out on the beach houses instead of during the day.

Now the real story on the Western Sound this week, and I mean the story everybody's talking about at every dock from Throgs Neck to Oyster Bay, is the bluefish. We flagged this pattern building last week and said it would keep spreading as long as the bunker held tight to the beach — and that's exactly what's happened. Big pods of bunker are stacked shoulder to shoulder off Matinecock Point, off the Whitestone side of the Throgs Neck, and scattered right along the Hempstead Harbor mouth, and the blues are absolutely tearing through them in broad daylight. This isn't a subtle bite — you'll see the surface erupt, bunker skying out of the water, gulls diving, and you idle over and start firing poppers or metal into the chaos.

Best producer this week has been a 2-ounce Kastmaster or a bunker-colored Hopkins worked with a fast, erratic retrieve right through the edge of the melee — don't throw into the thickest part, work the perimeter where the bigger blues are hanging back picking off cripples. Guys are also doing well snagging a bunker off the school with a weighted treble and drifting it back on a stinger rig; that's producing the bigger cocktail blues, some pushing 8 to 10 pounds, right off the rocks at Matinecock and along the Sands Point shoreline. The blitzes have been showing mid-morning and again in that late afternoon window when the sun angle pushes the bait tight against the beach — keep your eyes on the birds, because when they go from circling to diving, you've got maybe five minutes before the school sounds and moves.

Fluke fishing is doing what it always does this time of year — background noise, dependable but unspectacular. The reef structure off Hart Island and the drop-offs around the Stepping Stones are holding fish, but you're picking through a lot of shorts to find keepers. Best move has been dragging a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a bucktail, 3/4-ounce, tipped with a strip of squid, working the deeper edges — 25 to 35 feet — on the tail end of the incoming when there's still enough current to keep your bait moving without getting swept sideways. Porgies remain steady on the same structure; a simple high-low rig with sandworms or clams over any of the rockpiles from Execution down through Manhasset Bay will keep you busy, even when nothing else wants to cooperate.

We're coming off the first-quarter neap now, which means current everywhere in the Sound has been on the soft side this week — that's part of why the structure bite has actually held up better than the open-water trolling program. Weaker current means fish sit tighter to rockpiles and channel edges instead of spreading out, which is exactly why Execution and the Stepping Stones have kept producing even with the tide moving at half speed. Expect that current to start rebuilding over the next several days as we move away from the neap, and when it does, I'd look for the bass bite at Execution and off Hart Island to sharpen up, especially on the night tides.

Looking ahead — if the bunker stay pinned along the beach and the warm surface water holds, I don't see any reason the bluefish blitz backs off through the weekend. That's the play right now: chase the birds, work the edges of the bait schools, and don't be shy about running from Matinecock to Whitestone if one spot goes quiet. For bass, I'd be planning trips around the two hours before and after the tide change, after dark, at Execution or the Stepping Stones — bring eels if you can get them, or a big paddletail swum slow along bottom. Daytime bass fishing is going to stay tough until we get a real cool-down, and with this weather pattern, I wouldn't hold my breath for that anytime soon.
