# Bunker-fueled bass bite holds the Western Sound as offshore reference data thins
*With 44025 offline and 44097 intermittent, the working signal is inside water — slot-class stripers stacked on structure from Execution Rocks east to Matinecock.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — June 12** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The Dr. Fish analyst desk is running short on hard offshore numbers this morning. NDBC 44025 south of Islip is dark, and Block Island's 44097 water-temperature string has been blinking in and out for most of the week. The last clean SST package in the catalog is dated June 10. That leaves the Western Sound reading itself from the inside out — and what the inside is telling us is consistent with where mid-June normally puts us: a stratifying basin with a warm surface skin running through the mid-60s across the western pocket, cooler and saltier water still seated below the pycnocline, and a tide-driven mixing zone scouring the hard structure from the Throgs east to Matinecock. Wind has been workable, the chop manageable on the early tides, and bunker schools are still anchoring the western basin rather than pushing out toward the middle grounds.

What that means for this beat is straightforward. The thermal engine inside Little Neck, Manhasset, and Hempstead Harbor is now running several degrees warmer than the main-stem Sound, and that delta is doing exactly what it does every June — pulling adult bunker into the harbor mouths on the flood and flushing them across structure on the drop. Execution Rocks on the outgoing remains the cleanest play for slot-and-better fish, and Stepping Stones is back into its early-summer rhythm where the last two hours of the ebb stack bait against the light. Matinecock Point is the eastern anchor of the pattern, and the rip off the point has been producing the larger class of fish for boats willing to work live bunker rather than troll past it. Hart Island is the wildcard — it fishes best on the back side of the tide and tends to hold the bigger resident fish that won't chase a spread.

The on-the-water intelligence over the last week reinforces what the structure-and-bait picture would predict. Drift fishing live bunker on the western rockpiles has been giving up multiple fish in the high-30-inch class with quick releases, and the bite has been compressing into the final hour of moving water before slack — a classic sign that the fish are pinned to structure rather than roaming open water. Trolling mojos and bunker spoons across the basin is still putting fish in the boat, predominantly slot-class with a sprinkling of overs, but the catch rate has tipped clearly toward boats that switched to live bait or soft plastics worked deep on the edges. Topwater has been a sunrise-only proposition and has cooled noticeably as the surface layer warmed past the mid-60s.

Fluke remains the frustration. Drifts from Oyster Bay east to Lloyd Neck and over toward the Northport stacks have been producing volume but very few keepers — a lot of 10- to 14-inch fish and the occasional 17- to 19-incher that barely scrapes the slot. Bait signatures on the sand have been thin, and without a steady push of sand eels or spearing on the flats, the bigger fluke aren't committing to the western drifts. Porgy fishing on the hard bottom around Hart Island and the City Island reefs is building toward its normal late-June stride, and blackfish season is closed, so the rockpile crowd is all bass right now.

Looking three to five days out, the controlling variable is going to be wind direction and how it interacts with the bunker schools. A southwest flow holds the bait pinned into the western harbors and keeps Execution and Stepping Stones lit. Any sustained northeast push will scatter the schools and force a reset — typically the fish recover within a tide cycle, but the bite gets choppy in the interim. The new moon late in the week will sharpen the tides and should concentrate the bite into tighter windows on either side of the ebb. If the surface layer climbs past 68 degrees, expect the larger fish to slide off the shallowest rockpiles and set up on the deeper edges in 25 to 35 feet — the Matinecock rip and the back side of Execution become the priority reads.

With the offshore buoy network running degraded, the read this week has to come from the bait, the birds, and the structure. The Western Sound is doing what mid-June asks it to do, and the fish are where the water says they should be. Work the moving water, fish the edges of the bunker schools rather than the middle of them, and respect that the bite window is getting shorter as the surface warms.
