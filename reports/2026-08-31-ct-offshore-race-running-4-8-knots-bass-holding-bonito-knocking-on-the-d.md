# Race Running 4.8 Knots: Bass Holding, Bonito Knocking on the Door
*Spring tides are loading Plum Gut with bait while the Block Island bonito push creeps toward the Sluiceway.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — August 31** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

The Race is starting to run hard again — pushing into the 4.5 to 4.8 knot range on the max flood as the moon builds toward full over the next week. That's spring-tide strength, and it changes everything out there. Slack at Valiant Rock is tightening into a narrower window, maybe 20 minutes either side of the turn, and if you're not set up on the rock before the water goes still, you're fishing the wrong tide. That's the technical read for the week: bigger current, shorter slack, and a thermal gradient across the Sound that's still steering where the bait — and the bass — want to sit.

Kings Point is holding at 70.9 degrees, New London's at 69.4, and that three-quarters of a degree spread across the Sound matters more than it sounds like it should. Warmer water to the west is keeping bait mobile and spread out; the cooler eastern end around the Race and Plum Gut is where forage tends to stack up against structure once the tide starts to squeeze it. That's exactly what's happening at Valiant Rock and Race Rock right now — not a blitz, but a consistent grind of resident stripers working the eddies on the down-tide side once the current lays over.

Striped bass are in a holding pattern, and it's worth saying that plainly instead of dressing it up. The fish are here, they're catchable, but the true fall push — the one that turns the Race into a washing machine of birds and breaking bass — hasn't started. That trigger is bait density, and bunker schools from Raritan Bay to the LI south shore to Narragansett Bay need another week or two of tidal flow to concentrate before that switch flips. In the meantime, wire line trolled tight along the rock piles at Valiant Rock and Race Rock, or bucktails bounced through the seams off Little Gull Island on the last hour of the ebb, are producing keeper-class fish in the 28 to 34 inch range. Chartreuse and white 2-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of mackerel belly are getting bit better than plain jigs right now — something about that extra scent line in current this strong.

Bluefish remain the most dependable predator in the zone. They're chopping bait through the Sluiceway and along the Plum Gut rip on both stages of the tide, not just around the turn, which tells you they're not current-dependent the way the bass are — they're just following bait wherever it drifts. Diamond jigs in the 3 to 4 ounce range worked with a fast, erratic retrieve are the go-to, and there's no need to finesse it. Warm water has kept them aggressive and spread wide, and with the Surfside Hotel tournament crowd working the western Sound hard this week, that pressure is pushing some of that bluefish biomass east toward Gull Island and the Gut mouth. Expect this to hold through Labor Day.

The number everyone in the shop is talking about is the bonito. They crashed into Block Island and Point Judith in real numbers this week, right on the seasonal schedule we've been watching build. False albacore are showing in Nantucket Sound too, though they're feeding on tiny bait and staying finicky — small profile epoxy jigs and Albie Snax in the smallest sizes you own are the play if you find them. Neither species has committed to the Race or Plum Gut yet, but the historical pattern says this push spreads west into Fishers Island Sound and the eastern approaches within days to two weeks. Little Gull Island and the outer edge of the Sluiceway are the first places it'll show on our side — keep half an eye on the surface for nervous bait and diving terns anywhere along that stretch starting this weekend.

Fluke have softened into the typical late-August pattern — fair, not great, and thinning out as the bay water stays warm and the fish push toward cooler structure. Plum Gut's deep channel edges and the drop-offs along the Sluiceway are still worth a drift on the last of the incoming, working white Gulp paired with a 3/4-ounce bucktail slow along bottom. Don't expect numbers, but the fish that are there are decent size. Black sea bass remain the steady bycatch on the reef structure around Race Rock and Valiant Rock — jigs tipped with squid strips are filling out coolers even on days when everything else is quiet.

We flagged this last week — said conditions would stay favorable for pelagic species through the near term — and the bonito arrival at Block Island is exactly that call playing out on schedule. The next 72 hours should hold that pattern: canyon and coastal pelagic activity staying strong, the bluefish grind continuing straight through the holiday weekend, and the first real test of whether that bonito push reaches the Race before Labor Day traffic hits the water. If it does, Valiant Rock and the Gull Island edge are where to be on the morning slack. If the bass are your priority instead, keep working the rock piles on wire and bucktail through the tide changes — the fall blitz isn't here yet, but the bait's building, and the moon's doing exactly what it needs to do to make that happen inside the next two weeks.
