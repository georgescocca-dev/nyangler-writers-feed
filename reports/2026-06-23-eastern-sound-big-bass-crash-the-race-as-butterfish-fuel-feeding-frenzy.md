# Big bass crash The Race as butterfish fuel feeding frenzy
*Overslot stripers smashing topwater at first light while swim shads outproduce bucktails in the rips.*

**THE RACE, NY — June 23** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The eastern Sound exploded this week. I'm talking about the kind of action that makes you forget about sleep, forget about everything except the next drift through water that's absolutely loaded with fish.

The Race has been the epicenter of it all. Big migratory bass — the Hudson River fish that everyone waits for — finally showed up in force, and they're feeding like they haven't eaten in weeks. I've been working the rip at first light with topwater, throwing Yo-Zuri Hydro Pencils and X-Walks into the boils, and it's been absolutely insane. These aren't schoolies. I'm talking 35 to 45-inch fish that hit so hard they nearly rip the rod out of your hands.

The key has been the bait. Butterfish are everywhere — thick clouds of them stacked against the current breaks. It's unusual to see them this heavy this early, but the bass don't care about the calendar. They're gorging themselves, and when they're not hitting topwater, they're crushing flutter spoons dropped into the deeper water off the edges of the rip.

Plum Gut has been equally productive, but you need to adjust your tactics. The three-way rig is still king here — 8 to 10-ounce cannonball, one-foot drop from the main line to a swivel, then 5 to 6 feet of 80-pound leader to your lure. That heavy leader isn't just for abrasion resistance; the stiffness helps stand your presentation off the bottom in the screaming current.

Here's where it gets interesting: swim shads are absolutely destroying bucktails for size. I've been running head-to-head tests, alternating between 7-inch Tsunami swim shads and traditional bucktails on every drift. The shads are producing multiple overslot fish while the bucktails are getting mostly slot-sized bass. It's not even close. The bigger profile and different action of the shad is triggering the larger fish in a way that bucktails just aren't matching right now.

The technique is critical in these rips. You're drifting at 2 to 3.5 mph, covering serious ground on each pass. Drop to bottom, crank up a couple turns, then drop again to remove the belly that forms during the initial fall. Keep that rig tracking along the contour as you drift from 80 feet up to 40 feet across the rip, then back down. Take preemptive cranks as the bottom shallows to avoid dragging.

Bluefish have also arrived in numbers, and they're wrecking expensive lures. I lost three bucktails in one morning to gorilla blues that hit like freight trains. Use leaders — these aren't the small harbor blues. These are 15 to 20-pound fish that will cut through anything lighter than 80-pound test without even slowing down.

The squid bite that was so productive earlier in the month has shifted. There's still some squid activity around Fishers Island and in the deeper water near the Thames River, but the bass have moved on to the butterfish and other baitfish that are more abundant right now. If you're still fishing squid patterns, switch to something that mimics butterfish or the small herring that are also in the mix.

Timing has been everything. The best action is happening on the moving water — either the last two hours of the ebb or the first two hours of the flood. Slack tide has been dead, but as soon as that current starts pushing, the fish turn on like a switch.

Weather has been cooperating, but pay attention to the wind. When it's blowing against the tide, the rips get nasty fast. I've seen 3-foot standing waves in The Race when the wind opposes the current, and that makes presentation nearly impossible. Pick your tides and your wind windows carefully.

Looking ahead, we're coming off the new moon, so the tides are starting to build toward the full moon on July 1st. That means stronger currents and better bait movement. The butterfish should continue to be a major factor, and as the water continues to warm, I expect to see more variety in the bait schools. Keep an eye on the water temperature — we're sitting right around 68 degrees, which is perfect for this kind of feeding activity.

The eastern Sound is fishing as well as I've seen it in years. If you've been waiting for the right time to make the run out to The Race or Plum Gut, this is it. Just bring plenty of tackle — between the big bass and the gorilla blues, you're going to lose some gear. But trust me, it's worth every rig you donate to the bottom.
