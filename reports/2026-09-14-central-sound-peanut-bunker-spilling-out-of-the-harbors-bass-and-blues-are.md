# Peanut Bunker Spilling Out of the Harbors — Bass and Blues Are Waiting at the Mouth
*Bait movement out of Port Jeff, Northport, and the Norwalk coves is setting the table for the first real fall commitment from stripers and blues in the Central Sound.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — September 14** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

The tell this week wasn't a boat count or a radio call — it was the bait. Peanut bunker have been pouring out of the harbor mouths on the last two hours of the ebb, dense little clouds of them getting pinched through the necks at Port Jefferson, Northport, and the Norwalk Islands coves. Watch the surface long enough and you'll see it: nervous water, then a school of peanuts folding in on itself, then the push of something bigger underneath breaking the pattern. That's not random bait behavior. That's peanuts running the seasonal exit cue, and predators are starting to organize around the exits.

The temperature split across the Sound explains a lot of what's driving this. Kings Point is still holding at 70.9, New London has slipped to 69.4, and that west-to-east gradient means the Central Sound is sitting in the sweet spot — warm enough to keep bait comfortable and active, cool enough to trigger the fall shuffle. We flagged this exact setup last week, saying bunker schools would tighten up from the western bays through Narragansett under the building moon and that stripers would start showing real interest instead of just picking at stragglers. That's precisely what's happening at the harbor mouths right now. It's not a blitz yet, but the commitment is visibly increasing day over day.

Stratford Shoal is the first place to check that theory. The rip there has been holding bait on the last of the ebb and first of the flood, and reports out of that stretch have blues in the 3-to-6-pound class working through the peanut schools with real aggression, alongside stripers in the mid-20s to low 30-inch range shadowing the same bait a little deeper on the drop. Bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked with a slow bottom sweep through the rip, have been the most consistent producer — match the sink rate to the current or you're fishing over the fish, not through them. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig off the shoal edge have also been picking up bigger blues on slower tides when the jig bite goes quiet.

The Norwalk Islands circuit has been a mixed bag but worth the run if you can work the tide right. Peanut schools have been thick in the back coves around Sheffield and Copps Islands on the outgoing water, and small stripers have been on them consistently, but the bigger fish have been more selective — showing for maybe an hour around the bottom third of the ebb and then going quiet. If you're only getting one window there, fish it on the last two hours of the outgoing tide with a peanut bunker imitation like a 4-inch RonZ or a small Game Changer, swimming it just under the surface where the bait is nervous. That's also where the early false albacore activity has been concentrated — we called for that bite to firm up as water eased slightly, and the albies working the edges of those bait schools near the Norwalk Islands and off Charles Island back that up. Small profile epoxy jigs and Deadly Dicks in the half-ounce to one-ounce range, cast into the edge of the commotion rather than the middle of it, have been the better play. They're moving fast and picky about presentation, but they're there.

Fluke fishing is doing exactly what it does this time of year — thinning out. Middle Ground Light and the deeper flats off Port Jefferson have still produced keeper-class fish on bucktail and Gulp combinations worked slow along the bottom, but the numbers are down from a month ago and the better fish are starting to slide toward deeper water as the seasonal exodus gets underway. If fluke is the priority, the next two weeks are the window before that pattern accelerates further, and afternoons on the last of an incoming tide have been the more productive stretch.

Blackfish are the other story starting to build. As the water eases off summer peak, tog have begun showing up on the rockpiles off Charles Island and around the reefs near Stratford Shoal. It's early — this isn't a full-blown blackfish bite yet — but green crabs and hermit crab fished tight to structure on a stand-up jig have picked at fish in the 14-to-18-inch range on slower tide stages when the current isn't ripping. Porgies remain the steady backbone bite across the same structure, still filling coolers on squid strips and small pieces of clam without much drama.

Looking at the next few days, the tidal range is building toward the next moon phase, and that's the piece that should push this from a building pattern into something more consistent. More water movement means more bait getting pinched through the harbor mouths and funneled past the rips, and that typically tightens up the predator response rather than spreading it out. If the wind stays out of the north and northwest and doesn't stir the Sound into chop, the harbor-mouth pattern at Port Jefferson and Northport, plus the Stratford Shoal rip on the moving tide, are the two bets worth prioritizing this weekend. The Norwalk Islands albie window is a longer shot but worth a scouting run if you're already rigged light — that bite can turn on fast once the bait schools compress further. Keep an eye on the bird activity over the next few tide changes; where the gulls start working tight instead of scattered, that's where the bunker exodus is concentrating, and that's where the fall run starts to show its hand for real.
