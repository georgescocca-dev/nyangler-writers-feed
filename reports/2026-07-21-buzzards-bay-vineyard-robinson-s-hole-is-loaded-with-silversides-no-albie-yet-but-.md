# Robinson's Hole is loaded with silversides — no albie yet, but the table's set
*Bait is stacking in every rip from Woods Hole to Menemsha; fluke and scup are paying the bills while we wait on the first bonito flash.*

**WOODS HOLE, MA — July 21** — _by Maria Fonseca, Zone Writer_

Stand on the Robinson's Hole bridge an hour before the tide turns and you'll see it — silversides balled up so thick the water looks like it's got a pulse, terns working the edges, and every so often that little nervous ripple that makes your heart jump because for a half second you think it's a bonito push. It isn't. Not yet. But the bait is exactly where it needs to be, and that's the story of this week: the table is set, and we're just waiting on the guest of honor.

I've been running the rips — Robinson's, Quick's Hole, the backside of Nashawena — every day this week on the last two hours of the ebb, and the bait pattern is textbook late July. Peanut bunker are working down from Buzzards Bay proper, silversides are thick in the current seams, and there are enough sand eels showing on the sonar around the Sow and Pigs reef off Cuttyhunk to keep the stripers interested after dark. Water in the bay is sitting in the low 70s, which is warm enough that the bass have gone almost entirely nocturnal — I'm not seeing much daytime interest anywhere inside Buzzards Bay unless there's a hard tide and low light to go with it.

We flagged this pattern last week — said this stretch of water, Buzzards Bay through the Vineyard, is one of the first zones to watch for the albie and bonito push, typically showing in early-to-mid August as temps peak. Nothing confirmed yet on my end, no flashes, no reports off Menemsha or the Middle Ground, but Block Island's now sitting in that 68-degree comfort range that usually means bonito are close behind. I'd put money on the first scout fish showing at Robinson's or off Squibnocket within the next two to three weeks. Keep a bag of Deadly Dick or a small epoxy jig rigged and ready — when they show, they show fast, and you don't want to be re-spooling when the blitz starts.

In the meantime, fluke are doing the heavy lifting. The channel edges around Woods Hole and the sandy stretch off Quick's Hole have been holding fish all week, and the bite's been genuinely good — not spectacular, but consistent, which this time of year I'll take over a one-day miracle. Best drift has been over 20 to 35 feet of water on the last of the incoming into the first hour of the outgoing, when the current's still got some push but isn't ripping your bucktail sideways. White Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail, tipped with a squid strip, dragged slow along bottom — that combo has out-produced everything else I've thrown. Had a mixed bag Tuesday with three fluke over 20 inches and a pile of scup mixed in around the same structure, fishing small strips of squid on light jigs right on bottom. Scup have been thick around any rock pile or mussel bed from Tarpaulin Cove down to Cuttyhunk — easy action if you want to fill a cooler while you wait on bigger things.

Striped bass are around, but you have to work for them right now. The bay water's warm enough that daytime fishing inside is mostly a waste of gas — I tried the flats off Naushon midday Wednesday and didn't get a sniff. Dawn and dusk at the Cuttyhunk rip, and especially after dark working eels or a swimming plug over the Sow and Pigs, that's where the keeper-class fish are showing. Nothing huge — mostly 26 to 32 inches — but a legit bite if you're willing to fish the hours nobody wants to fish. Menemsha basin at first light has also been worth a look, bass working the edge of the current where the bite starts to slack.

Bluefish have been the wildcard, showing up in the middle of the day when you least expect them, usually right on top of the same bait balls the terns are working. Had a solid 20 minutes off Tarpaulin Cove where blues in the 3-to-5-pound range tore through a school of peanut bunker like they were angry about something — poppers and small metal got bit fast and often before they moved off. Worth having a rod rigged for that specifically, because it comes and goes in a hurry.

We're heading into the July 21 neap, which is going to soften the current through these rips for the next several days — Robinson's and Quick's won't rip as hard, which actually widens the window for working bucktails and eels without getting swept off structure. If I had one day this week, I'd fish the Cuttyhunk rip at first light for bass, then slide into Robinson's Hole midday to watch bait and be ready if that first bonito flash finally shows. It's close. The water says so. We just need it to commit.
