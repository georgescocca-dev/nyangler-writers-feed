# Captree dock talk: bunker got the blues going wild, bass gone to bed
*Fluke are steady if you find the deep humps, but the real show at Fire Island right now is happening on the surface.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 24** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Guy pulls up to the fuel dock Tuesday morning, still shaking, telling me he ran out of the inlet at six and thought he'd hit a school of bass busting bait off Democrat Point. Wasn't bass. Wasn't even close. Blues, and I mean stacked — bunker getting shredded on the surface so hard the water looked like it was boiling from underneath. He said he threw a Deadly Dick into the mess and had three fish in the boat before he even got his motor properly shut off. That's been the story all week out here, and I told him straight: we called this one. Said last week the bunker-bluefish thing would keep spreading as long as the bait held and the weather stayed calm, and here we are — it's still going, maybe even better.

Let's talk about that first because it's the loudest thing happening on this beach right now. Bunker schools are pinned tight along the barrier beach from Democrat Point stretching east past Robert Moses, and the blues are working them like they've got something to prove. You don't need finesse for this — a bucktail with no trailer, a Hopkins, even a bare umbrella rig pulled through the edge of a blitz will get bit. The birds tell you where to look before your eyes do — wheeling, diving, sometimes just sitting on the water waiting for scraps. If you see gulls stacked up and working low over a patch of nervous water off the beach, point the bow there and don't overthink it. Blues in the 4 to 8 pound range, some bigger, and they're not shy about it in the morning or the evening. Midday sun seems to push the action tighter to the beach or drives it deeper for a stretch, but it comes right back with the tide change.

Striped bass, though — that's a different conversation, and I've had it about ten times this week at the dock. Ocean water's pushed into the mid-70s along the beach, and the bass have done what they do this time of year: gone deep, gone nocturnal, and stopped playing during daylight unless you're fishing structure that holds cooler water. If you want bass right now, you're getting up stupid early or staying out stupid late. Inside the inlet, working the seam off Democrat Point on the last of the outgoing into the start of the incoming, guys are still picking off fish on bunker chunks fished on the bottom, or bucktails with a swim shad trailer worked slow along the drop where the channel meets the flat. Best window has been that gray hour before real light, when the water's still holding the night's cooler temp. Once the sun gets up and starts baking the flats, the bite shuts like someone flipped a switch.

Fluke are the honest, unglamorous producer right now, and I'll take honest over flashy most weeks. The bay's warmed up enough that the shallow stuff is mostly short fish and bait-stealers — if you want keepers you've got to go find the deeper humps and channel edges where it stays a few degrees cooler. I've had good drifts in 25 to 35 feet off the Robert Moses bridges, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid for insurance. Slow drift, bounce it right off bottom, let the current do some of the work for you. The bite's better on the stronger stages of the tide — dead slack kills it fast out there this time of year. Same story working the deeper edges toward the inlet itself, where the current runs harder and keeps things stirred up and cool. Nothing crazy, but a solid keeper or two mixed into a pile of shorts is a fair day right now.

Weakfish and porgies have been quieter than I'd like to admit — porgies still around the reef structure and rubble patches inside the bay, biting on bloodworms and clam if you drop right on them, but it's not the kind of numbers game it can be in a good year. Blackfish, obviously, way off — that's an autumn conversation, not a July one.

Here's what I'd do if I had one shot this week. I'd be at Democrat Point before first light, throwing metal into whatever's busting on the surface — that bite's been reliable enough that it's worth building your whole morning around it. If the blitz hasn't started or has moved off, I'd shift into the inlet channel and work bunker chunks on the bottom through the last of the outgoing, hoping to connect with a bass working that current seam before the sun climbs too high. Afternoon, I'd go find fluke on the deeper humps off the bridges and grind out a limit the honest way.

Wind's been mild, seas offshore have had a little bump to them but nothing that's kept the inside water dirty, and as long as that bunker stays pinned along the beach I don't see any reason for the blues to slow down. If a front comes through and scatters that bait, this whole picture changes fast — so if you've got a window this week, I wouldn't wait on it.
