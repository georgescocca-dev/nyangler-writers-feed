# Cockenoe on the Last Ebb: Bunker-Fed Bass, Blackfish Waking Up on the Norwalk Rockpiles
*With bait pinned tight to the reefs and the tide building toward the new moon, the smart play is picking your structure by the hour, not the day.*

**NORWALK, CT — September 14** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Right now the call on the Western Sound isn't which reef — it's which two hours of which tide. Cockenoe Reef on the last of the ebb, working the western tongue of rock in 10 to 14 feet, is outproducing everything else in the fleet this week, and it's not close. The reason is simple: bunker pods that have been holding off Sheffield Island and the Norwalk Islands all summer are finally getting pushed by current instead of just sitting there, and every predator in the system knows exactly where that current squeezes them into a chokepoint.

Kings Point is reading 70.9 and New London's already down to 69.4 — that gradient matters more than people think. The Western Sound is running a few degrees warmer than the water forty miles east, which means the bait isn't in any hurry to leave here yet. Bunker schools are stacking thick off Greenwich Point and into the mouth of the Norwalk River, and that warmer pocket is buying this zone an extra couple weeks of quality fall structure fishing before the exodus really accelerates. We flagged this last week — said bass activity would build as the tide strengthened toward the new moon — and that's exactly what showed up on the reefs the past several days. The moon's pulling harder water through Cockenoe, Penfield and the gaps between the Norwalk Islands, and the fish are using it.

Penfield Reef is still in play but it's a tide-specific spot right now, not an all-day spot. The blackfish bite there wants the first two hours of the incoming, working tight to the rockpile edges in 15 to 20 feet with green crab on a standard blackfish rig — not a lot of finesse needed, just get the bait down and hold bottom. Numbers are still modest, mostly fish in the 2 to 4 pound range with the occasional 5, but the pattern is building week over week as more fish push onto the structure ahead of the real cold-water shift. Give it into October and this becomes the backbone bite for the whole zone.

Porgies are the more reliable meat-in-the-box option right now, and the Cows off Stamford are still thick with them. Hi-lo rigs with sandworm or clam, fished in 20 to 30 feet on the slower half of either tide, are producing steady action — not trophy fish, but a full cooler if you're patient and willing to move around the rockier patches rather than anchoring on the first hit. Best window has been the two hours around the tide change, before the current picks up enough to drag bait off bottom.

Bluefish are the loudest story on the board. Bunker-driven blitzes have been showing at the mouth of Sheffield Island and off Greenwich Point on the tail end of the outgoing, and the fish working those pods are not small — snag-and-drop rigs with bunker chunks are getting hit hard, and topwater poppers thrown right into the surface disturbance are drawing violent strikes when the blitz stays up long enough to cast into it. The tricky part is timing: these blitzes are short, tide-triggered windows, sometimes twenty minutes, sometimes an hour, and they're moving with the bait rather than holding station. If you're running from the Norwalk side, keep the boat rigged and ready rather than committing to one spot and waiting.

Striped bass are shadowing those same bunker schools but staying disciplined about it — mostly sitting on the down-current edge of the reef structure rather than committing to the top of the blitz. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5 ounce range, white or bunker-colored, dragged slow along the drop-offs at Cockenoe and the western Norwalk Islands, have been the better producer than trying to match the surface chaos with poppers. Slot fish have been the majority, with a scattering of overslots mixed in on the days the tide's been running hardest.

Fluke are still around but this is the tail of that story, not the middle of it. The bite has been tapering through September as expected, and what's left is scattered rather than concentrated — a few keepers still coming off the sandy stretches between Cockenoe and Sheffield Island on Gulp and bucktail combinations, but it's not worth building a whole trip around anymore.

On boat traffic: weekends are getting crowded on Penfield and the inner Norwalk Islands as the moorings empty out for the season, so if you've got a weekday to work with, take it — you'll get clean drifts on the reef edges without three other boats swinging on the same structure. If Saturday or Sunday is all you've got, get to Cockenoe or the Cows before 8 AM and work the tide change before the recreational traffic picks up.

Looking ahead, the tide range keeps building into the new moon, and that should tighten the bunker pods further and intensify the blitz pattern at the harbor mouths over the next several days. Expect the blackfish numbers at Penfield to keep climbing steadily as more fish stage onto the structure, and expect the bass to stay committed to the reef edges rather than the open water as long as the bait stays pinned. If the wind stays out of the north and keeps the water clean, this is shaping up to be one of the better stretches of the fall so far.
