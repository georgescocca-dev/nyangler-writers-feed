# Sound bass chase bunker deep as new moon stirs the middle grounds
*Spring tides push bait off the ledges while fluke move shallow on the warming flats.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — June 24** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

The new moon hit Friday and it's doing exactly what I expected — stirring up the middle Sound like a cocktail shaker. Water temps are sitting pretty at 68 degrees across most of my beat, but here's what's got my attention: there's a solid thermal break setting up between the shallow Connecticut flats and the deeper channels.

Striped bass are stacked along the 30 to 45-foot contours from the Stratford Shoal east to Middle Ground Light. The spring tides are flushing bunker out of the harbors and these fish are positioned perfectly to ambush them. I'm seeing consistent action on white bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, worked slow along the bottom with the current. The key is reading the tide — fish are hitting hardest during the first two hours of the ebb when that bait gets swept off the ledges.

What's interesting is how the fish are behaving differently depending on depth. In 25 to 35 feet, they're aggressive on topwater at dawn — pencil poppers and SP Minnows are producing solid fish in the 28 to 34-inch range. But once the sun gets up, they're dropping to the thermocline and you need to get down to them with bucktails or live eels on fishfinder rigs.

The Norwalk Islands have been particularly productive, especially the rocky drops on the back sides where current creates natural ambush points. I'm finding the best action on the outgoing tide when bait gets pushed through the channels between the islands. A 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce jighead has been deadly, especially when you let it sweep naturally with the flow.

Fluke fishing has taken an interesting turn with the warming water. They're moving up onto the flats in 15 to 20 feet, particularly around Port Jefferson Harbor and the shoals off Charles Island. The warmer water — we're seeing 72 degrees in the shallows versus 68 in the deeper water — is concentrating bait and the fluke are following. Pink and white Gulp on light bucktails are outproducing everything else, but you need to match your weight to the current. In the lighter flows, a 1/2-ounce head keeps you in the strike zone without hanging bottom.

The current situation is textbook for this lunar phase. We're seeing 3 to 4-knot flows during peak tide changes, which is perfect for moving bait but challenging for presentation. The trick is positioning yourself up-current from structure and letting your offering drift naturally through the strike zone. Don't fight the tide — work with it.

Porgies are thick around any hard bottom in 25 to 40 feet, and they're running large. These aren't the usual dinner-plate fish — I'm seeing plenty of 12 to 14-inchers that are worth keeping. Small pieces of clam or squid on a high-low rig will fill the cooler quickly if you need backup dinner plans.

Looking ahead, this new moon cycle should keep the action strong through the weekend. The big tides are going to continue moving bait, and with water temps holding steady in the upper 60s, the fish should stay active. I'm particularly watching for the first real push of weakfish — the conditions are setting up perfectly for them to move through the western Sound in the next week or two.

The key to success right now is mobility. Don't sit on a dead spot hoping fish will show up. Use your electronics to find the bait, watch for bird activity, and be ready to move when the action shifts. The fish are here — you just need to think like they do and position yourself where the current is doing the work for you.
