# New moon springs push GSB fluke into Jones Inlet as bay water tips 78 degrees
*The bay's too warm for summer flounder to sit still — they're stacking in the channels and inlet mouth on the upwelled water, and the building tides this week should only help.*

**JONES INLET, NY — July 8** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what it does every July — it cooked. Great South Bay pushed up to 78.8 degrees in the shallows, and that's too warm for a fluke that wants oxygen and moving water, not a bathtub. What that did was shove fish out of the flats and into the channels and the inlet itself, where the tide keeps things turning over. The ocean side of Jones Inlet has been running cooler — upwelled water sitting around 71 degrees at the mouth — and that temperature break right where bay meets ocean is exactly the kind of edge fluke set up on. I've been watching this pattern build for two weeks now and it's not subtle. If you were still grinding sod flats in South Oyster Bay last week wondering where the fish went, this is why.

This week the moon's the story. We're running into new moon on the 11th-13th, which means spring tides — bigger swings, stronger current through the inlet in both directions. That's good news for anybody drifting Jones Inlet or Sloop Channel because the extra push moves more bait and pulls fluke up out of the mud to feed. It also means the ebb out of Jones Inlet is going to be flushing serious volume late in the week, which is the tide I want to be fishing. Wind's been manageable, nothing that's blown the water to milk, and I don't see a front lined up to change that through the weekend. Water temp trend is stable to slightly warming in the bay, stable to cooling at the inlet mouth on the incoming — that seam is where I'm putting my time.

Fluke fishing has actually been solid, not spectacular, but solid — which this time of year I'll take. Best marks have been the deeper bend of Jones Inlet on the channel side toward Short Beach, 18 to 24 feet, drifting the last two hours of the outgoing into dead low. Three-quarter ounce white bucktails with a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet trailer have been the money combo, dragged slow along bottom with just enough hop to keep the tail kicking. Guys working Sloop Channel with the same setup are getting a mixed bag — keeper fluke to 4 pounds mixed with a lot of teen-inch fish you're throwing back. It's not a five-fish-limit-by-nine-AM bite, but if you put in a couple hours on the right stage of tide you're bringing something home. The West End 2 grounds and the deeper hole off the Jones Beach Coast Guard station have both produced better-grade fish on the outgoing — that's where the current concentrates bait against structure.

Stripers have been a night game right now, which is normal for mid-July once the sun's been baking the surf all day. Resident bass are holding tight to the jetty rocks at Short Beach and the inlet breakwater, and the better fish are coming after dark on the incoming tide, eels drifted right up against the rocks on a fish-finder rig with just enough weight to hold bottom without snagging. Nothing huge — mostly 20 to 28 inches, with the occasional mid-20-pound fish mixed in for guys who stick it out past midnight. This isn't fall-blitz bass fishing yet. That's two months out. But it's a real bite if you're willing to lose sleep for it, and the new moon current this weekend should sharpen it up — more water movement means more bait pushed past those rocks.

Bluefish have shown up as the usual July nuisance and I mean that as a compliment — snapper-sized blues and some harbor cocktail blues in the 2 to 4 pound class are chopping bait up and down the bay side, especially around the Wantagh Bridge pilings and the Freeport channel markers on the last of the incoming. Small metal — half-ounce Deadly Dicks or Kastmasters — thrown into the surface activity will get bit fast. Good action for kids or anybody who wants bent rods without overthinking the presentation.

Weakfish I want to be straight with you about, because there's been a lot of talk about a comeback and I'm not going to oversell it. Water's been above 70 degrees in the bay for over two weeks now, which is supposed to be the trigger, and I'm just not seeing the reports to back it up yet. A few marks on the sonar drifting Cedar Creek at dusk, one report of a decent fish caught on a bucktail meant for fluke — that's about it. The temperature is right, but temperature alone clearly isn't enough. I'm still checking the marina lights at night on light tackle because that's historically where they show first, but I wouldn't plan a trip around weakfish this week. Treat it as a bonus, not a target.

Porgies are keeping rods bent on the structure spots while everybody waits on bigger things — the reef markers off Jones Beach and the rock piles inside the inlet are stacked, and a simple high-low rig with clam will keep you busy between fluke drifts.

Looking ahead: I want the outgoing tide Thursday through Saturday at Jones Inlet, right as the new moon springs build the current to its strongest push of the month. If that current stays clean and doesn't get blown apart by wind, I expect the fluke bite to tighten up and the bass to feed a little more aggressively at the jetty after dark. Plan B if the fluke get finicky on the strong current — slide into Sloop Channel where the flow's a touch softer and the bucktail stays in the strike zone longer.
