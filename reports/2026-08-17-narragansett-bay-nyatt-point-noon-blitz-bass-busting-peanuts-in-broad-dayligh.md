# Nyatt Point noon blitz: bass busting peanuts in broad daylight
*Guys at Sabin Point are texting pictures of stripers exploding on bunker schools at lunchtime — the bay hasn't fished like this in years.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — August 17** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

I was down at Bold Point Tuesday morning grabbing coffee before I put the skiff in, and there's a guy leaning on his tailgate telling anybody who'll listen that he watched bass blow up on peanut bunker off Sabin Point at eleven in the morning — full sun, dead calm, birds screaming overhead. Normally I'd say the guy's exaggerating, but I've seen it myself twice this week off Nyatt Point, so I believe him. This bay is drowning in peanut bunker right now, little pogies stacked so thick they're darkening the water off Conimicut and pushing up into Greenwich Bay, and the bass have decided they don't care what time it is anymore. When the bait's that dense, the fish stop being nocturnal. That's exactly the pattern we flagged last week — said the blitz would hold as long as the bait stayed thick, and here it is, still going, right through the middle of the day.

The money move has been running the shoreline from Conimicut Point down past Nyatt and watching for diving terns and nervous water. When you find the peanuts balled up tight, cut the engine and drift a small white or pearl soft plastic — 3-inch paddletails on light jigheads, no weight if you can help it — right through the edge of the bait. Topwater walks a lot too; a small popper or a Zara Spook-style plug worked slow gets crushed the second it lands near a busting school. Most of what's coming over the rail is schoolie to mid-20-inch class, with the occasional slot fish mixed in when the blitz sets up over deeper water off Prudence Island's west side. It's not a guarantee — I burned an hour Wednesday afternoon running from Rocky Point to Warwick Neck with nothing showing, then found the whole thing happening off Nyatt twenty minutes later. You've got to keep moving and trust the birds.

Bluefish are riding shotgun in these same schools, and they're not shy about it — anything from a pound and a half up to four or five pounds is slashing through the bunker right alongside the bass. If you want bluefish specifically, a wire leader ahead of that same soft plastic keeps you from losing gear to their teeth. Down in the back coves and river mouths — Bullock's Cove, the mouth of the Providence River, up into the Barrington River — the snapper blues are absolutely thick, exactly the kind of building block we called for two weeks back when it comes to bigger blues later in the season. Kids on the dock with bobbers and bits of squid are having a field day with those, and it's a good sign for October if this keeps up.

Fluke have been the honest bite between the blitz windows. I've been working the channel edges off Nyatt Point and the drop-off along the west side of Prudence in 15 to 25 feet, dragging a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a half-ounce bucktail tipped with a squid strip. It's not a banner year for size — most fish are 16 to 19 inches, plenty of shorts mixed in — but on a good outgoing tide you can put together a few keepers without much trouble. The mouth of Greenwich Bay has also been giving up a few better fish for guys drifting live killies on a fish-finder rig.

Scup are doing what scup do — stacking on every ledge and piece of hard bottom from Rocky Point down to Rose Island and Gould Island. Squid strips and clam on a simple hi-lo bottom rig, fished tight to the rocks on the last two hours of the incoming, will fill a bucket fast. Black sea bass are mixed in on the same structure, especially the rockier stretches around Prudence's north end — nothing huge, but steady action for anybody who wants a mess of fish for the table. At night, squid have started showing under the lights at the Henderson Bridge and around the docks in Bristol Harbor — small glow jigs or bare hooks under a light will connect if you're patient, and it's worth a look since that squid is part of what's holding the whole bait chain together right now.

Tide-wise we're building back out of the new moon slack toward the full moon on the 28th, and that extra push should tighten up the current lines and concentrate bait even more over the next week. Water's sitting warm through the upper and mid-bay, which is exactly why the fish are willing to feed on top in daylight instead of waiting for dawn and dusk. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd be running the stretch from Conimicut to Nyatt starting mid-morning, watching for birds, and I wouldn't leave the rod rigged with anything heavier than what it takes to work a peanut bunker imitation clean. Just don't expect every blitz to be a slam dunk — some pop for five minutes and vanish, others hold for an hour. The bay's giving you the window. You still have to be there when it opens.
