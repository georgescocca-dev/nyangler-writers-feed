# Penfield on the last ebb: bunker blitzes building, blackfish setting up on the turn
*Harvest Moon tides are stacking bait against the reefs from Greenwich to Bridgeport, and the bass are starting to show up where they should.*

**NORWALK, CT — September 21** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

The call this week is Penfield Reef, and it's a call built on tide, not luck. With the Harvest Moon pushing bigger swings through the Sound, the last two hours of the ebb off Penfield are moving more water than they have in a month, and that current is doing exactly what you want it to do — pulling bunker off the flats at Fairfield Beach and dragging them across the rockpile in a moving buffet line. That's where the bass and blues are setting up, not on top of the reef where the leisure fleet parks on a Saturday, but on the down-current edge where the structure breaks the flow and bait gets disoriented. If you can only fish one window this week, that's it: last two hours of ebb into the bottom of the tide, working the drop-off on the northeast corner.

The bigger picture backs this up. Kings Point is reading close to 71 degrees, New London's down near 69, and that gradient means the warmer water — and the bait it's holding — is stacked hard on our side of the Sound. We flagged this pattern last week, saying the bluefish and bass blitzes building around bunker pushed out of the western bays were a standard fall setup, and that's exactly what's showing up now from Greenwich Point down through the Norwalk Islands. Bunker schools are getting squeezed out of the back bays and river mouths on the bigger Harvest Moon tides, and every reef system in this zone is turning into a chokepoint.

Greenwich Point and the Cows off Stamford have been the most reliable blitz water the last several days. Boats working the outer edge of the Cows at first light are finding bass in the mid-20s to low 30-inch class and bluefish running 3 to 6 pounds, both hammering bunker schools that get pinned against the rock structure on the incoming tide. Bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, white or bunker-colored, cast into the boils and let sink before a slow retrieve, are outproducing everything else right now — the fish are eating disabled bunker, not fleeing baitfish, so a lure that looks hurt beats one that looks fast. Topwater walkers work too when the blitz is surface-visible, but that's a short window right around dawn before the boat traffic pushes the schools down.

Cockenoe Reef and the water off Sheffield Island are holding a similar pattern but with more bluefish in the mix than bass, especially on the last of the incoming when bait gets funneled through the channel between the islands. That's tighter water with more moving boats on a weekend, so weekday mornings or the tail end of a Sunday evening tide are the smart play if you want room to work a spread without getting run over.

On the bottom, the blackfish bite is starting to wake up as water temps ease off the summer peak. Penfield Reef and the rockpile off Cockenoe are both worth a green crab or hermit crab soaked tight to structure on the slack before the tide turns — blackfish don't want the ripping current, they want the calm window right at the top or bottom. Porgies are still thick on the Cows, and that bite hasn't slowed at all; a high-low rig with squid strips in 15 to 25 feet of water is still filling coolers there, especially on the last of the ebb when the current softens enough to hold bottom without a half-pound sinker.

Fluke are fading but not gone. The channels off Norwalk Islands and the drop-offs around Sheffield Island are still giving up keepers on the incoming, mostly on Gulp in white or chartreuse behind a bucktail, but the bite window has shrunk to that first hour of moving water. Warmer-than-normal water has kept them around longer than usual this fall, and that's a gift that won't last much past the first real cold front.

As for albies — the push moving west along the Sound hasn't put fish on our reefs yet, and it's not fair to readers to say otherwise. If it holds the pace it's been running, the outer edge of Cockenoe and the rips off the Norwalk Islands are the first places worth a look, but that's a watch-and-see, not a plan-the-weekend-around-it.

Looking at the next few days, the Harvest Moon tides keep building through midweek, which should keep intensifying the bunker-driven blitzes at every reef mouth in the zone — expect bass and blues to stay concentrated hardest right at the tide changes. The bigger question is the first real cooling front behind it. If that comes through as expected in the next 72 hours, look for the fluke bite to tighten up fast and the bass to start pushing bigger and moving with more urgency, which is good news for surf and jetty guys at Greenwich Point and bad news for anyone hoping this pattern holds indefinitely. For now, work the reefs on the moving water, respect the boat traffic on weekends by fishing the off-hours, and don't overlook the bottom bite — blackfish and porgies are the sure thing while the blitz pattern sorts itself out.
