# Sound shore bass hit the shallows as water climbs past 68 degrees
*Schoolies thick from Mattituck to Orient Point as bait pushes tight to shore.*

**MATTITUCK, NY — June 24** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Water's finally acting right out here on the Sound shore. Hit 68 degrees this week and stayed there, warmest I've seen it since early May. That's the magic number that gets the bass moving shallow, and they're responding exactly like they should.

Been working the rocks from Horton Point east to Orient every morning this week, and the schoolies are stacked thick in 8 to 15 feet of water. Nothing huge — mostly 20 to 28 inchers — but they're feeding hard on the incoming tide. Best action's been first light to about 9 AM, then again around sunset when the bait gets nervous.

White bucktails with 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets are doing the damage. Three-quarter ounce heads in the deeper pockets, half-ounce when you're working the shallower structure. Key is keeping it slow and low — these fish aren't chasing anything that moves too fast in this warm water. Let it bounce bottom, then lift and drop. The hit comes on the fall nine times out of ten.

Seen some decent bluefish mixed in too, especially off Rocky Point where the current runs harder. They're taking the same rig but you better have wire or they'll cut you off quick. Nothing massive — three to five pounders mostly — but they're fat and fighting hard in this warm water.

Porgy bite's been steady in the deeper holes between the rocks. Twenty to 30 feet of water, high-low rigs with small hooks and squid strips. Nothing fancy, but you can fill a cooler if you find the right spot and the tide's moving. Been seeing some keeper-sized fish mixed in with the shorts, which is encouraging.

What's really got my attention is the bait situation. Seeing more peanut bunker than I've seen in years, thick schools pushing right up against the shoreline on the flood tide. That's what's drawing these bass shallow, and it's setting up what could be a real good summer bite if the water stays stable.

Plum Gut's been running clean and green, about 4 knots on the spring tides we had earlier this week. That current's been pushing bait out of the Race and spreading it along the Sound shore, which explains why the fishing's been so consistent from Mattituck all the way east. When you get that kind of current flow, it moves everything — bait, bass, blues, the whole food chain.

Weakfish have been showing up in small numbers, mostly at night around the inlet mouths. Nothing you can count on, but I've heard of a few being taken on soft plastics after dark. Worth a shot if you're out there anyway.

Water's been clean and green most of the week, visibility good enough to sight-fish in the shallows when the sun's right. Light northwest winds have kept it calm, which makes reading the structure easier and lets you work lures properly without fighting a chop.

Looking ahead, we're coming up on the new moon this weekend, which means bigger tides and more current. That should push even more bait around and get the bass feeding harder. I'm expecting the bite to pick up, especially in the deeper rips where the current runs strongest.

If this weather pattern holds — light winds, stable temperatures, clean water — we could be looking at some of the best Sound shore fishing we've had in years. The bass are here, the bait's here, and the water's right. Just need to be there when the tide's moving and work your lures like you mean it.
