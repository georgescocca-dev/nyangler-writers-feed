# Breezy Point Jetty Is the Play: Bass Committing to Bunker on the New-Moon Push
*Shore access at Fort Tilden and the Breezy Point rocks is outfishing the boat crowd this week as bait tightens against the beach on the outgoing tide.*

**ROCKAWAY BEACH, NY — September 11** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one way into this water over the next few days, make it your feet. The Breezy Point jetty and the Fort Tilden rocks are the access point of the week, and it's not close. Bunker have been pushing tight against the beach from the tip of Breezy east toward the inlet mouth, and the September 10 new moon gave this system the hardest tidal pull we've seen since spring. That kind of push squeezes bait against structure — jetty rocks, the inlet edges, the beachfront itself — and bass have been committing to it, not just picking at the stragglers the way they were two weeks ago. We flagged this last week — said bass interest in those bunker schools would intensify as the tide built toward the new moon — and that's exactly what's setting up now.

Boat guys working out of Cross Bay Bridge and the Rockaway side have a shorter run to the inlet edges, and that's still a legitimate option if you want to work bunker spoons or troll mackerel through the deeper cuts. But the surf game right now doesn't need a boat, and honestly it's producing the more consistent numbers. Kayak paddlers launching out of Canarsie Pier or the Motts Basin ramp have another angle entirely — working the channel edges and marsh drop-offs inside the bay where bunker pods are still holding before they get pushed out through the inlet on the bigger tides.

Striped bass are the story, but be honest about where they are in the size range — this is early fall staging, not the wide-open blitz phase yet. The better fish are working bunker on the outgoing at Breezy Point and off the Fort Tilden rocks, especially in the last three hours of ebb when bait gets pinned against the beach with nowhere left to go. Bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, white or bunker-colored, worked with a slow bottom hop along the drop right off the rocks, have been the most consistent producer. If bass are visibly working bait on top, a bunker spoon or a large white popper thrown into the boil will get bit fast — don't overthink it when you see fish crashing bait, just get something in front of them before the school moves. Marine Parkway Bridge is still holding bass on the outgoing as well, staged in the current seams off the pilings, though the size class there has been more mixed — some slot fish, plenty of shorts working the eddies.

Snapper blues are thick throughout the back bay right now, exactly like the pattern we called out last week, and that's usually the tell that adult bluefish aren't far behind as the water keeps trending cooler. If you're fishing Canarsie Pier or Cross Bay Bridge with the kids, small Kastmasters or Deadly Dicks under a bobber will keep rods bent all afternoon on snappers. The bigger blues haven't shown in real numbers inside the bay yet, but with bait stacking near the inlet mouth the way it is, that could change fast over the next tide cycle or two — worth keeping a wire leader in the bag just in case.

Fluke are winding down and it's worth being straight about that. The keeper-grade fish that were around earlier in the season have largely pushed out toward deeper water ahead of the fall exit, and most of what's left inside the bay on the flats and channel edges is undersized. If you want a shot at a legal fish, work the deeper cuts near the Rockaway Inlet channel with white Gulp on a bucktail, dragged slow on the last of the outgoing — that's where the better-conditioned fish are staging before they leave. Don't expect the numbers from June, but a mixed bag with a few keepers mixed into the shorts is realistic through the next week or so.

Porgies are still holding around the pilings at Cross Bay Bridge and off Canarsie Pier structure, and that's an easy, low-drama bite if the wind kicks up and makes the ocean side unfishable — small hooks, sandworms or clam, right on bottom. Blackfish season isn't quite in gear yet for this zone, so hold off there.

Looking ahead: the tidal energy from this new moon should keep building through the next 24 to 48 hours before it starts to ease off its peak. That window is when the first real blitz-style activity should show along the beachfront and inlet edges, as bunker get squeezed to their tightest concentration of the season so far. If the wind stays manageable, the outgoing tide at Breezy Point and Fort Tilden in the next two to three days is where I'd put my time — early morning and the last two hours of daylight both have shown fish. By the weekend, as the moon moves off new phase and the tidal push softens, expect the frenzy to settle into a more typical fall pattern — still productive, but you'll need to work the tide stages more precisely rather than just showing up and finding fish waiting.
