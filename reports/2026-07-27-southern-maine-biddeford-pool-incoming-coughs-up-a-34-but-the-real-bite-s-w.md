# Biddeford Pool Incoming Coughs Up a 34, But the Real Bite's Waiting for Dark
*Bass have gone nocturnal on the warm surface water, bluefish are running Higgins in daylight, and the mackerel are still stacked on the Cape Elizabeth ledges.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — July 27** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

Tuesday night, an hour into the incoming at the Pool, a guy I've fished next to for fifteen years — never says much, doesn't need to — hooked into something on a black Gibbs Danny that bent his 9-footer into a horseshoe and didn't quit for ten minutes. Thirty-four inches, thick through the shoulders, came off the outer bar where the channel dumps into the basin. That's the fish that tells you the story right now: they're in there, they're eating, but you're going to work for it and you're going to do most of that work after the sun goes down.

Surface water along this stretch has been sitting in the low-to-mid 70s, which for Kittery to Cape Elizabeth is warm enough to shut the daytime bite down but not so warm it's chasing the bass out of the system entirely. That's the trade we've got right now compared to everything south of us — Massachusetts and Rhode Island are dealing with stripers that won't touch anything before midnight, full stop. Up here the river mouths and the cooler ledge water still give you a shot in daylight, but it's thinner than it was in June, and the real windows are opening at dusk, at dawn, and through the dead of night.

Biddeford Pool is still the best hour in Maine surf fishing and it hasn't lost that title. The incoming tide pushes bait — mackerel, pogies, the odd school of peanut bunker — through the cut and into the basin, and the bass stack up on that current edge waiting for it. Right now the pattern that's working is fishing the last two hours of dark into first light, working the outer bar and the drop just inside it with big swimming plugs — Danny-style plugs in black or dark green, and needlefish for guys who want to cover more water with less splash. Slow retrieve, feel for the current seam, and don't be afraid to let the plug sit and swim on a semi-slack presentation right where the channel bends. Daytime, you're mostly getting shorts and the occasional keeper on the bottom of the tide when there's some cloud cover, so if you can only fish daylight, pick an overcast morning and don't expect a repeat of that 34.

Higgins Beach has been the bluefish show all week, and it's a legitimate one. Bunker schools are riding the beachfront in daylight, tight to the surf line, and the blues have been on them hard on the push of the tide — mid-morning into early afternoon has been the window two days running. These aren't snappers. Guys are into 3- to 6-pound blues on Kastmasters, pencil poppers, and darter-style swimmers worked fast across the surface. When the bunker get pinned against the bar and the water goes nervous with bait skipping, that's your signal — cast into the boil, not past it, and keep the retrieve moving. It won't last all day. Once the tide turns and the bait scatters, the blues follow it back out and the window closes until the next push.

Pine Point and Prouts Neck have been more of a grind. With the wind sitting out of the southwest most days this week, Prouts has actually fished better than you'd expect — that wind pushes bait tight against the rocks on the south side and gives the stripers a current edge to work without beating up the surf too bad. Best window there has been the last of the outgoing into the start of the incoming, fishing bucktails and soft-plastic jerkbaits tight to the ledge rock in 10 to 15 feet. It's not a blitz. It's two or three fish for a couple hours of casting, which for late July around Prouts is honest work, not a bad night.

Mackerel are still holding on the open ledges off Cape Elizabeth and out toward the Wells and Ogunquit rockpiles, and that's not nothing — it's bait for the guys chunking at night, and it's also a solid hour of its own fun if you want to load a cooler on Sabiki rigs before the sun's fully up. Cod have been quiet, which is normal for this water and this month — nothing worth chasing specifically right now.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the warm, stable stretch would keep pushing the bass toward nocturnal feeding, and that's exactly what's playing out from the Pool to Prouts.

Moon's building toward full over the next several nights, which means more current push through places like the Pool cut and around Prouts Neck's ledges — that's going to sharpen the bite in current-dependent spots and probably extend the good window at Biddeford by another twenty or thirty minutes on either side of the tide change. If the southwest wind holds into the weekend, I'd fish Prouts on the last of the outgoing and be at Biddeford Pool for the two hours around the incoming after dark — that's where I'm putting my time. Higgins in daylight if the bunker are still stacked, because that bite's been reliable enough to build a morning around. Bring dark plugs, bring patience, and don't expect the daytime bass bite to hand you anything easy until this heat breaks.
