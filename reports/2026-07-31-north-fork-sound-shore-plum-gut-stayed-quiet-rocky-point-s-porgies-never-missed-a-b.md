# Plum Gut Stayed Quiet — Rocky Point's Porgies Never Missed a Beat
*Warm water is keeping the bass honest after dark, but the scup bite on the reefs is the one thing you can plan a whole day around.*

**MATTITUCK, NY — July 31** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Kid pulled up to the Mattituck ramp Tuesday morning, maybe twenty-five years old, rod already rigged with a topwater plug, asking me if the bass were "going off" out front. I told him what I tell everybody who asks me that this time of year — go home, come back at nine o'clock tonight. He looked at me like I'd told him to go fish the parking lot. Didn't listen, of course. Fished two hours in bright sun, caught nothing but a sunburn, and I saw him back at the ramp at dusk rigging up a bunker chunk like he'd had the idea himself all along. That's how it goes out here in late July. Nobody believes the water temperature until it costs them a morning.

And the water is warm — Kings Point's reading just under 71, New London's sitting at 69 and change, and out my way in between, it's about the same story. That's above where a striped bass wants to live comfortably, and they know it better than we do. We said last week the mid-70s water would keep the bass a low-light game on this stretch, and that's exactly the hand we've been dealt. Daytime, they're down deep on the rock piles off Horton Point and Rocky Point, sulking, not feeding much. Come dark, they come up onto the current edges to work.

Best bass action right now is right at the mouth of the Gut and around Orient Point, fished on the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the flood, full dark or close to it. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig, or a whole eel drifted back through the rip if you've got the current for it — that's what's putting fish in the boat. Nothing huge, mostly schoolies and a few solid teens, but a couple guys have found slot fish working the seam where the Gut current meets the flat water off Orient. Don't waste your time bombing casts out into open water — these fish are sitting tight to the edges and the boils, feeding on the push, not chasing bait across open sound. Feather your cast right into the seam and let it sink.

Now the porgies — that's the bite I'd send a stranger to right now with confidence, and I don't say that about much this year. Rocky Point reef and the rock piles off Horton Point are stacked with scup, and they haven't slowed a step in this warm water, unlike the bass. Hi-lo rigs, size 4 or 6 hooks, clam or squid strips, right on the bottom in twenty to thirty-five feet. You'll go through shorts, but there are enough eight-and-nine-inch fish mixed in to make a nice pan of them. This is the bite you take a kid or a first-timer to. Steady, no drama, but it fills a cooler.

Blackfish are still mostly a memory for now — water's too warm for them to be doing much of anything, and I wouldn't waste bait on them till the water starts to slip back down in September. Fluke are around but scattered — I've heard of decent drifts along the channel edge at the Mattituck Inlet mouth, white Gulp swimming mullets on a half-ounce bucktail, dragged slow on the last of the outgoing. You'll pick at shorts most of the drift and then find a stretch where two or three keepers come over the rail in twenty minutes. That's fluke fishing out here right now — not a blitz, just patience rewarded in patches.

Bluefish are the ones taking full advantage of this warm water nobody else wants. They don't care that it's pushing 71 degrees — if anything they like it. Peanut bunker schools have been getting worked over off Rocky Point and out toward Orient in the last light of the evening, blues busting bait on the surface hard enough to hear it from shore. Poppers or a diamond jig with a bit of surgical tubing will get hit fast if you can find the school, though they don't sit still long. If you see the birds working, don't wait — that window closes in ten minutes sometimes.

Weakfish I'll be honest with you — thin. A few reports of small fish mixed in with the bass at night around the Gut approach, nothing worth planning a trip around.

As for what's coming up on the Gut — I've had my eye out for the first albie scouting reports the way I mentioned last week, water's certainly warm enough for them to be curious, but nothing's showed yet that I'd call real. Full moon's put some extra push in the tides this week, which has helped stir bait around the structure, and if that current stays lively into next week while the surface holds warm, I wouldn't be shocked to hear the first albie or bonito rumor out of the Gut before the middle of August. Until then, I'd plan my week around the porgies for the guaranteed fun and save my night hours for the bass at Orient and the Gut mouth. If the wind stays out of the southwest and light like it's been, that pattern should hold — but keep an eye on any thunderstorm blowing through midweek, because a hard rain will dirty up the inlet mouth fast and shut the bite down for a tide or two until it clears.
