# Fort Tilden Surf Access: Blues Blitzing While Bass Stack Behind Bunker
*The walk-in at Fort Tilden puts you on the bluefish blitz and the staging striper bite at the same time — here's why that's the smart play this week.*

**ROCKAWAY, NY — September 4** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one shot at this zone in the next few days, the Fort Tilden walk-in — the beach access west of the parking lot, toward Breezy Point — is the smarter play over the Rockaway Beach boulevards right now. Reason is simple: the bunker pods pushing along this stretch of beachfront are thick enough that you can see them from the dune line on a calm morning, and where the bunker sit, the bluefish and the first serious striper interest are sitting right behind them. That's not true everywhere on this beach yet. It is true there.

Here's the rundown. Water's still running warm — low-to-mid 70s inside the bay and along the beachfront — which is keeping striped bass in that scattered, non-committal mode instead of true blitz mode. They're staged on bait, not yet stacked under it. Bluefish don't have that hesitation. They've been the most reliable bite in this zone for two straight weeks, and with the moon building toward full on the 6th and 7th, the bunker schools running Raritan Bay through the LI south shore corridor — including this stretch off Rockaway — are getting squeezed tighter with every tide cycle. We flagged this exact pattern last week: warm water keeping the blues aggressive and widespread through Labor Day week, and that's exactly what the beach has delivered. No reason to expect that to change through the weekend.

What that means for stripers is patience for another week to ten days. The building moon and tighter bait schools are the trigger — once tidal flow ramps up around the full moon and overnight air temps start easing off, that's when the scattered fish start committing in numbers. Right now they're there, but you have to work for them.

On the outgoing tide at Marine Parkway Bridge, that staging pattern is showing up clearly. Boat and kayak guys working the pilings and the rip line off the Rockaway side on the last three hours of the ebb are picking at striped bass in the 24-to-32-inch range, mostly on bucktails — white or chartreuse, 1 to 1.5 ounce — bounced tight to the structure, and on live eels for the after-dark crowd fishing the deeper scour holes under the span. It's not a blitz. It's a fish here, a fish there, but the quality has been decent and the bite window is real if you're on the outgoing.

Surfside from Fort Tilden through Breezy Point, the bluefish have been the story — cocktail-size fish mostly, with some better ones mixed in, chopping bunker pods right in the wash. Bunker chunks fished on a simple fish-finder rig off the beach will get you in the game, but the more consistent action has been metal — half-ounce to one-ounce diamond jigs and Kastmasters worked through the peanut bunker showers when the birds start working. Early and late are still the windows; midday sun has been pushing the bait and the blues out a little deeper past the bar.

Inside the bay, fluke are doing exactly what they do this time of year — thinning out, not disappearing. The channels off Cross Bay Bridge and the deeper cuts around the Raunt and Ruffle Bar are still giving up keeper fish, mostly on the smaller side but with the occasional surprise. White or pink Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, drifted slow on the last of the incoming, have been the better producer over bare bait rigs this week. This is a normal seasonal transition — the doormats are sliding toward ocean-side structure ahead of the fall run — and there's no reason to expect fluke to shut off completely before late September, but the days of easy limits inside the bay are behind us for the season.

Canarsie Pier remains the most practical access for anyone without a boat or kayak who still wants a crack at porgies, and that bite has held up fine on bloodworms and clam fished on the bottom around the pier pilings and the drop-off just off the rocks. Blackfish season isn't open yet in these waters, so leave that structure alone for now and focus effort where it's legal. Weakfish have been quiet — a few reports of fish taken after dark on the bay flats near Canarsie and the Raunt on soft plastics, but nothing worth planning a trip around specifically.

Looking ahead, the next few days should track close to what's already happening: bluefish staying aggressive on bunker and peanut bunker through the beachfront, striped bass staging tighter behind the bait as the moon fills, and fluke continuing their slow slide out of the bay. If the first real cool front of the season shows up in the next week to ten days like the pattern suggests it should, that's the trigger that turns the staging bass into an actual blitz — and when it does, Rockaway Beach and the inlet mouth off Breezy Point are going to be the first places it shows. Until then, work the tides you know — outgoing at the bridge for bass, first and last light on the beach for blues — and keep a rod rigged for whatever shows up in the wash.
