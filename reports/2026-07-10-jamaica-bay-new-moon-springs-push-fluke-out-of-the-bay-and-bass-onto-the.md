# New moon springs push fluke out of the bay and bass onto the outgoing at Marine Parkway
*Warm bay water is emptying the flats while a fresh moon builds the tides — here's where the fish are actually sitting this week.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — July 10** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what it does every July when the sun really leans on it — the flats cooked. Grassy Bay and the shallows off Canarsie Pier were sitting warm, uncomfortably warm for fluke, and that pushed the doormats and the cocktail-size fish alike out of the skinny water and into anything with current and depth. That's not a guess — I watched it happen on my own drifts. Two weeks ago I was picking up keeper fluke in six feet of water off the flats near Ruffle Bar. Last week those same drifts were dead, and the fish showed up instead in the channels and at the mouth of the inlet, where moving water keeps things cooler and bait gets funneled through like a chute. Meanwhile the ocean side has actually been getting a gift — upwelling out of the northeast has been pulling cleaner, cooler water into the surf and up against Breezy Point, and that 71-degree water is a much nicer address for a fluke than the bathtub the back bay's become.

This week the moon is the story. We're building toward new moon, and that means spring tides — bigger swings, stronger current, more water moving through the Cross Bay and Marine Parkway bridges than we've seen in a few weeks. Peak alignment lands July 11–13, and that's exactly when the evening flood starts lining up with sunset. That combination — big water, low light, current ripping under structure — is when the striped bass at Marine Parkway wake up and actually feed instead of just holding. If you've been marking fish on the bridge pilings all week and getting nothing, this weekend is when that changes. Bigger tides also mean the inlet drains harder and faster on the outgoing, which is pushing more bait — and more fluke — out through Rockaway Inlet toward the ocean side. That's the pattern I'm fishing this week, and it's the one I'd tell you to fish too.

On the fluke side, forget the flats for now. I've had my best drifts working the edge of the main channel between the Cross Bay Bridge and the inlet, 15 to 22 feet, dragging a white or chartreuse 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail, slow, right along bottom, bumping structure where you can find it. The bite isn't wide open — I'm putting two or three keepers in the cooler on a good outgoing tide and culling through a lot of shorts to get there — but the fish that are there are quality. Heard of one push past 12 pounds weighed off a boat working the deeper water outside the inlet a couple weeks back, which tells you the big girls haven't left the area, they've just relocated to where the water makes sense. If you're working the ocean side of the inlet itself, try squid strips tipped on the bucktail when the current's ripping too hard for plastic alone to stay down — gives it a scent trail and a little extra weight to hold bottom.

Stripers at Marine Parkway have been a grind through the neap tide stretch we just came off — slower current, less urgency, fish sitting deep and picky. That should change with these bigger tides moving in. I'd be there on the outgoing, working live eels or bunker chunks tight to the pilings and along the rip line that forms off the bridge on a strong ebb. Evening into dark has been the better window, and with the flood-to-sunset alignment building through the weekend, I'd plan my trip around last light Saturday or Sunday specifically. Surfcasters walking Rockaway Beach have had a scattered mix — some schoolie bass in the wash early, especially the stretch from Beach 90th down toward Beach 116th, working white bucktails and small swim shads on the outgoing. Nothing that qualifies as a real blitz yet, but worth having a rod rigged if you're already out there for fluke.

Bluefish are starting to trickle into the back bay — nothing dramatic, but Canarsie Pier regulars have been picking off cocktail blues on diamond jigs and small bunker spoons on the last of the outgoing, usually mid-morning when the sun's up and the bait's moving. If that pattern holds and more bunker push into the bay behind these bigger spring tides, that bite should build through the month. Weakfish, I'll be honest with you — the water's been warm enough for a while now, and I keep waiting for a real showing at the inlet or off the pier, and it just hasn't materialized. I wouldn't plan a trip around them right now. Porgies are around the bridge structure and the reef pilings in modest numbers if you want a bucket of dinner fish on bloodworms or clam, but it's a fill-in bite, not a target.

Looking ahead, I'd put my time this weekend at Marine Parkway on the evening outgoing into the flood, right through the new moon peak July 11–13 — that's the best alignment of tide and light we've had in weeks for bass. If the bass don't show or the current's too ripping to fish comfortably, my backup is the inlet channel edge for fluke on the last two hours of the outgoing, when the water's still moving but manageable. Keep an eye on the bunker showing up in the bay — more bait usually means more blues and eventually a better bass bite behind them. That's the domino I'm watching this month.
