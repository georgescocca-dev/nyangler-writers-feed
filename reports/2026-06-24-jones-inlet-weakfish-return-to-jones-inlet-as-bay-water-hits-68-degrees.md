# Weakfish return to Jones Inlet as bay water hits 68 degrees
*First keeper weakies in years showing at the inlet mouth while fluke fishing picks up steam.*

**JONES INLET, NY — June 24** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The water's telling a story this week, and it's one I haven't heard in a long time. Bay temperatures hit 68 degrees — a solid four degrees warmer than the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do. It's stacking bait on the flood tide and holding predators on the ebb. More importantly, it's brought the first real weakfish bite to Jones Inlet in years.

I'm talking keeper weakfish, not the usual runts. Fish pushing 24 to 26 inches are showing at the inlet mouth on the last two hours of outgoing water. The pattern's simple: fresh bunker chunks on fishfinder rigs, fished tight to the jetty rocks where the current sweeps hard. These fish are feeding in the wash, right where the bay water dumps into the ocean and creates that perfect feeding lane.

The weakfish bite is early morning and late evening — classic low-light pattern. But here's what's interesting: they're not alone. I'm seeing stripers mixed in, good fish too, some pushing 40 inches. Both species are keyed on the same thing — peanut bunker getting flushed out of the bay system on the dropping water.

Fluke fishing's been solid throughout the inlet and back bay. The Meadowbrook Bridge area is holding steady with keeper-sized fish on the southeast side during incoming water. White bucktails with squid strips or fresh smelt are producing best, but you need to fish them right. Single hook on the bottom, teaser bucktail 18 inches up the leader. The key is keeping contact with bottom on the drift — these fish are hugging structure.

Wantagh Bridge has been consistent for smaller fluke, mostly shorts but good action for kids. The north side's been better than the south, especially on the first three hours of incoming. Gulp Swimming Mullets on quarter-ounce jigheads are deadly here — white and chartreuse are the colors.

The real surprise has been the back bay action. Hempstead Bay's been producing bluefish in the 18 to 20-inch range, perfect eating size. They're chasing spearing and sand eels in the shallows, especially around the channel edges near Wantagh Park. Small diamond jigs with white or green tails cast to breaking fish are connecting.

Bait's been the story all week. The bay's loaded with peanut bunker, and I mean loaded. Every outgoing tide brings clouds of them through the inlet. Sand eels are thick too, especially on the ocean side of the jetties where the current creates that perfect feeding zone. The birds have been working overtime — terns diving constantly, which tells you the bait's staying high in the water column.

Weather's been cooperative with light southwest winds most days, but that's changing. We've got a front moving through this weekend that should stir things up. The good news is it's bringing northwest winds behind it, which typically cleans up the water and gets the fish feeding aggressively.

Moon phase is working in our favor too. We're coming off new moon, so the tides are still running strong but starting to moderate. That's perfect for inlet fishing — enough current to move bait but not so much that you can't control your drift.

Looking ahead, I'm watching for the weakfish bite to expand. If this thermal gradient holds and we keep getting good bait flushes, we could see the best weakfish fishing in a decade. The key will be hitting the inlet on the last two hours of outgoing water with fresh bunker. Don't overthink the rig — simple fishfinder with a 6/0 circle hook.

For fluke, the action should only get better as water temperatures stabilize. Focus on structure — bridges, channel edges, anywhere the current creates an ambush point. And don't ignore the back bays. With all this bait around, the blues and stripers are going to follow it wherever it goes.
