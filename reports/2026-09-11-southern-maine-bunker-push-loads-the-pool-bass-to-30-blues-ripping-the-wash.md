# Bunker Push Loads the Pool: Bass to 30, Blues Ripping the Wash at Higgins
*The new moon tide jammed bait against every beach from Kittery to Prouts Neck, and the striper bite finally has some teeth to it.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — September 11** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

Somebody at the counter in Kennebunk described it as bunker "stacked three schools deep" off Gooch's Beach on Monday morning, with birds working so tight to the surface that a couple of surfcasters just stood on the sand and watched before they even wet a line. That's the kind of week it's been. The September 10 new moon put a real shoulder into the tides up and down this stretch, and the bait that's been loitering offshore all summer finally got squeezed hard against the beaches, the river mouths, and the back side of every point from Ogunquit to Cape Elizabeth. We flagged this coming — said the mackerel pushing through Kittery and York would drag stripers into the tidal rivers with them, and that's exactly the pattern showing up now in the Piscataqua and up into the Mousam and Kennebunk systems.

The short version: bait is thick, tides are strong, and the bass are finally committing instead of just picking at the edges. That's a real shift from the scattered, lazy feeding pattern we had through August. It won't stay this generous forever — once the moon eases off its new-phase pull over the next several days, the tidal push will soften and some of this concentrated bait will spread back out. But for right now, the window is open, and the fleet is using it.

Biddeford Pool remains the crown jewel of this whole beat on the incoming, and this week proved why. The bass are stacking in the deeper channel edges, roughly 8 to 12 feet, as the tide floods hard through the gut and pushes baitfish up against the sandbar. The last two hours of the incoming, right up through the top of the flood, has been the string to fish — bunker schools get trapped against the shallow water and the bass come up underneath them hard. A 7-inch Gibbs Danny swimmer in bone or mackerel pattern, worked with a slow wobble just under the surface, has been drawing the bigger fish, several reported in the 28 to 30-inch class with a few pushing past slot into the high 30s. Guys throwing bucktails tipped with a strip of fresh mackerel down in the deeper holes near the mouth have also connected, especially on the bottom third of the incoming before the surface bite turns on.

Pine Point and Higgins Beach have been the bluefish show this week, and it's been a legitimate one — not constant, but when it goes, it goes hard. Blues in the 3 to 6-pound range have been slashing through bait pods right in the wash, close enough that waders without a boat are getting shots. Al's Goldfish in chrome and bunker-colored Deadly Dicks worked fast through the froth have been the go-to. Best window has been first light through mid-morning on the outgoing, when the current pulls bait out of the river mouths and blues ambush it right at the seam. Don't sleep on the last two hours of daylight either — a second push has shown up some evenings as the tide turns again.

Prouts Neck is fishing the way it always does when the wind sits in the southwest — which it has for stretches this week. That wind pushes bait up against the rocks and gives the bass cover to work the boulder fields without getting sky-lit by low sun. Big surface swimmers and Atom Poppers worked slow along the ledges at dawn have taken fish in the 20 to 26-inch range, with the occasional better fish holding tighter to the deeper pockets off the point. Cape Elizabeth's rocky stretches are running the same pattern — darker water, slower retrieves, focus on the last two hours of the ebb when bait gets funneled off the rocks into the current.

Flounder fishing has been steady but unspectacular in Wells Harbor and the calmer stretches around the Kennebunk River mouth — bloodworms and sandworms on a simple two-hook bottom rig in 15 to 20 feet, working the last of the ebb into the start of the flood. Nothing to write home about, but a reliable way to fill a cooler while waiting on the tide to turn for stripers. Cod remain a background story this time of year on this stretch — most of that action is farther offshore than this surf-focused beat typically covers, and nobody's reporting anything worth chasing inshore right now.

Looking ahead, the next several days should hold this pattern together, maybe even sharpen it as cooler nights keep nudging water temperatures down and pushing bait tighter into the rivers and coves. If the wind stays out of the southwest into the weekend, Prouts Neck and Cape Elizabeth should keep producing on the early tide. If you've only got one shot this week, make it the incoming at Biddeford Pool — that's still the best hour of striper fishing this whole coast offers, and right now it's loaded. Just don't expect every tide to be a repeat of Monday's bunker show. The bite's real, but it's still tide-dependent and wind-dependent, and the fish are moving with the bait, not sitting still waiting on anybody.
