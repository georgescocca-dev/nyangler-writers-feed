# Weakfish crash Jones Inlet as bay water hits 68 degrees
*First real run of weakies in years coincides with perfect thermal gradient at the inlet mouth.*

**JONES INLET, NY — June 24** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The water temperature hit that magic number this week — 68 degrees in the bay, 4 degrees warmer than the ocean side — and the weakfish responded like they've been waiting all season for this moment. I'm talking about real weakfish, not the scattered singles we've been picking at for the past few years. Schools of 3 to 6-pound fish are stacked up on the inlet's flood tide, and they're hungry.

The best action is happening right at the inlet mouth on the incoming water, especially the last two hours of flood. These fish are sitting in 12 to 18 feet, right where that warm bay water meets the cooler ocean flow. I've been working 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 1/2-ounce bucktails, keeping them just off the bottom on a slow retrieve. The key is feeling that thermal break — when your lure hits that cooler water, you know you're in the zone.

Fresh bunker chunks are also producing, especially fished on a fishfinder rig tight to the jetty rocks on the outgoing tide. The weakies are feeding heavily on the small bunker that have been thick in the bay all month. I watched one angler pull a 26-inch fish on a 9-inch sand eel imitation — these fish are keyed on longer baits right now.

The fluke bite has been solid but not spectacular. The Meadowbrook Bridge area is holding decent numbers, with most fish running 15 to 17 inches. White bucktails with squid strips or fresh smelt are the ticket, worked on the southeast side of the bridge structure. The incoming tide has been more productive than the outgoing, probably because it's pushing that warmer bay water over the structure and activating the bait.

Striped bass are showing in decent numbers around the inlet, especially at sunset. The fish are holding on the small sandbar just before the trough, and they're feeding on sand eels in the wash. A 2-ounce diamond jig with a green tail, cast past the break and worked back through the incoming water, has been the most consistent producer. After dark, switching to an SP Minnow has extended the bite.

The thermal gradient we're seeing right now is textbook for late June — bay water warming faster than the ocean, creating that conveyor belt effect that concentrates bait and draws in predators. With the new moon approaching Friday, we're looking at spring tides that should really flush the system and potentially trigger the first major weakfish run we've seen in years.

Bait has been exceptional. Peanut bunker are thick throughout the bay system, sand eels are showing in good numbers along the beach, and the squid have been consistent around the deeper structure. The birds have been working overtime on the small baitfish, which tells me the food chain is firing on all cylinders.

Looking ahead, this thermal setup should hold through the weekend. I'm watching for the full moon tides to really move some water and potentially push more weakfish into the system. The bass bite should continue to improve as we get deeper into the summer pattern, and the fluke fishing should pick up as the water continues to warm. This is shaping up to be the kind of week that reminds you why Jones Inlet has always been special — when conditions align, this place can produce like nowhere else on the South Shore.
