# Neap Tide Squeezed the Rips — Execution Rocks Still Gave Up Bass After Dark
*A soft moon killed the daytime current, but fluke on the reefs and bunker-chasing blues kept rods bent from Throgs Neck to Matinecock.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — August 7** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Guy at the bait shop in City Island this week wanted to know why his Tuesday afternoon drift past Hart Island produced nothing but a single dogfish and a snapped leader. I didn't need to look at a tide chart to answer him — I could feel it in my knees walking the dock. That current just wasn't moving. We're three days off the first-quarter neap, and I said it last week and I'll say it again: this moon is softening every current-driven bite in the Sound. The bridge rips, the reef edges, Execution Rocks, Stepping Stones — they're all running on borrowed current right now, and the windows are shorter and weaker than they were two weeks ago. That's not bad luck. That's physics. Time it right and you can still eat. Show up at the wrong hour and you're the guy complaining at the bait shop.

Here's where the timing actually paid off. Execution Rocks on the last two hours of the outgoing, right around dusk, has been the most consistent thing I've fished all week. Water's warm — Kings Point is reading high 74s — so the stripers aren't exactly aggressive in bright sun, but once that light drops and the tide starts pulling hard off the rockpile, bass in the 26 to 34-inch class have been stacking on the down-current side. Live bunker on a fish-finder rig, or if you can find them, live mackerel, fished right on bottom in that 30 to 40-foot depression off the rocks. Bucktails work too if you're willing to work for it — 1.5-ounce white with a chunk of pork rind, cast up-current and let it swing dead on bottom through the sweet spot before you start cranking.

Stepping Stones has been quieter than a normal August, and I'll be straight with you — that's the neap talking. The light usually turns that stretch into a highway on the spring tides. Right now it's more like a back road. I still marked fish there on both tides Wednesday, but they weren't feeding hard until the tide really got moving in the last ninety minutes before the top. If you fish it, fish the clock, not the calendar.

Fluke are the one thing that hasn't cared one bit about the neap, and that's the best news I've got this week. The reef structure off Matinecock Point and the mixed bottom running out toward Hart Island have been holding steady numbers of keeper fluke in 25 to 35 feet. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom on the drift, have out-produced everything else I've tried. A few guys are still doing well on bigger fish — one came across the scale in Port Washington at just under 7 pounds, taken on a bucktail-and-Gulp combo tipped with a squid strip, fished in that deeper channel water off the Manhasset Bay entrance.

Bluefish are doing exactly what you'd expect with bunker schools spread the way they are. Adult blues are working the peanut and adult bunker pushing through the mouth of Manhasset Bay and off Matinecock, and once you find the school getting worked from above by gulls, it's not subtle — cast a metal-lip swimmer or a diamond jig into the boil and hang on. Snapper blues, meanwhile, have completely taken over Little Neck Bay and the inner reaches of Manhasset. If you've got a kid who wants nonstop action, small silver spoons or Kastmasters off the dock in the last two hours of daylight will keep a rod bent all evening. We flagged the bunker distribution spreading a couple weeks back and said the cocktail blues in the back bays would follow — and here they are, right on schedule, chewing through anything shiny in three feet of water.

Weakfish are the sleeper story right now. They've been trickling into the same bays holding the peanut bunker — Manhasset, Little Neck, the shallower flats off Whitestone. Nothing to build a whole trip around yet, a fish here and there mixed in with the fluke on light jigs and 4-inch swimbaits, but it's the kind of pattern that tends to build through the back half of August as more bunker push into those bays. Worth keeping a rod rigged light if you're working that water anyway.

Looking ahead, the moon starts building back toward full over the next week, and that means the current is only going to get stronger. If the pattern holds the way it usually does off a neap, expect the Execution Rocks and Stepping Stones bite to sharpen up fast by the weekend — more current means more bait getting pushed off the structure and more predictable feeding windows, instead of the short, moody ones we've had. I'd plan around the last two hours of the outgoing at Execution Rocks and the top of the incoming at Stepping Stones, and I'd keep fluke gear rigged regardless, because that bite hasn't needed the moon's permission to stay good. Watch the wind too — if we stay light out of the southwest the water should stay clean enough to keep sight-fishing those bluefish blitzes when they show.
