# Execution Rocks After Dark: Eels and Bunker Chunks Still Owning the Ebb
*Daytime bass have gone quiet in the mid-70s water, but the night program off Execution Rocks and Stepping Stones is holding up as the moon builds toward full.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — August 27** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

A regular at the bait shop on City Island asked me Tuesday why nobody's marking bass on the meter off Execution Rocks in the middle of the day anymore. Told him what I've been telling everybody who'll listen this month: the fish didn't leave, they just went to work at night. Kings Point is reading 70.9 degrees on the surface, and that's warm enough to shut down the daytime bite on anything bigger than a schoolie. The bass that are still around — and there are still plenty around — have pushed down onto structure and slid their feeding windows to dusk, dark, and dawn. That's been the pattern through most of August and it's not breaking yet.

The moon goes full tomorrow night, and we flagged this coming — said the tide swings would sharpen up and the current would start concentrating bait harder as we built toward it. That's exactly what's happening at Stepping Stones and Execution Rocks right now. The extra push on the ebb is stacking peanut bunker and silversides against the rocks, and the bass that have been holding deep and lazy are getting more aggressive in those tighter current windows. If you've been struggling to get bit in the middle of the tide, the last two hours of a big ebb around the new full moon phase is where the switch flips.

Execution Rocks is still the most consistent night spot on this end of the Sound. The outgoing tide pulls hard around that light, and the rockpile on the northeast side holds bass that are using the structure to ambush bait getting swept through. Eels fished on a fish-finder rig, or bunker chunks fresh cut and fished on the bottom with just enough weight to hold in the current, are doing the damage. Depths in the 15 to 25 foot range right around the rocks, working the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the flood, has been the most productive window. Fish are coming mixed — some short, some solidly in the slot, and a few reports of overslot fish pushing 30 inches that came on whole bunker fished tight to the base of the rocks after 10 p.m.

Stepping Stones is running the same program. That light has always been a spring highway for migrating stripers, but in late August it turns into a night ambush point instead. Live bunker, snagged fresh off the schools that are still stacked up around Matinecock Point, are the best bait when you can get them. If bunker aren't cooperating, chunked bunker on a standard fish-finder rig, fished on the bottom in 20 to 30 feet on the outgoing, has kept rods bending. Bluefish are mixed in with these bass most nights, and if you're getting your chunk baits shredded before the bass show up, that's your sign — the cocktail blues are working the same current seam.

Matinecock Point still has bunker stacked up thick, and that bait is the reason the western end of this zone keeps producing despite the warm water. Snapper blues are thick in Little Neck Bay and up into Manhasset Bay — small gold or silver Kastmasters and tiny bucktails on ultralight gear will keep kids busy for hours off any dock or bulkhead in those back bays, and it's about the most reliable action going right now if you want a guaranteed bend in the rod during daylight.

Fluke have been the tough one. With water this warm across the board, the fluke have spread out across the flats instead of holding tight, and that's softened catch rates almost everywhere except the channel edges and deeper structure where there's still a cooler bottom layer. The Throgs Neck Bridge pilings and the deeper cuts in Manhasset Bay channel are still worth a drift — white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along bottom in 25 to 35 feet on the last of the outgoing, have been picking at some keeper-class fish, but it's grind fishing right now, not a sure thing.

Porgies remain the bread-and-butter bite around Hart Island and the rockpiles off City Island. Sandworms and clam on a simple two-hook bottom rig in 15 to 25 feet of water is still filling coolers, and that fishery hasn't slowed a bit despite everything else going soft in the heat.

Blackfish are starting to trickle into the conversation, which is the first real sign the calendar's turning even if the thermometer hasn't caught up yet. It's early — most of what's being picked up is incidental on porgy trips over rocky structure — but expect that program to build through September as water temps start their seasonal slide.

Looking ahead, the next few nights around the full moon should be the best window this zone has seen in weeks for night-bite bass at Execution Rocks and Stepping Stones, with the stronger current doing the work of pinning bait against structure. Daytime fishing will likely stay soft on anything beyond snapper blues and porgies until nighttime air temps start knocking the surface water down, which the pattern suggests is still a couple weeks off. If you can only fish one tide this weekend, make it the last two hours of the ebb after dark on a rockpile, eel or bunker chunk in hand, and be patient — this bite rewards waiting on the right stage of the tide more than covering water.
