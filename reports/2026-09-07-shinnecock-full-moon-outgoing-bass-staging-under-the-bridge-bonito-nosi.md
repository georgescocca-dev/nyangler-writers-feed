# Full Moon Outgoing: Bass Staging Under the Bridge, Bonito Nosing Into the Inlet
*With bunker tightening up and the new moon four days off, the smart move right now is fishing the back half of the ebb after dark, not the daylight troll.*

**HAMPTON BAYS, NY — September 7** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one window this week, make it the last three hours of the outgoing, starting an hour before midnight, and fish the inlet instead of the ocean beach. That's the call right now, and here's why. The full moon hit last night, which means big tide swings and a lot of ambient light on the water — not always a bass angler's friend on open sand, but inside the inlet, where the current does the work instead of your eyes, that extra push is exactly what stacks bait against structure. The moon pulls harder, the outgoing runs harder, and the bass that have been picking at stragglers all August are starting to commit. We flagged this back on the 4th and 5th — said the building moon toward full would tighten bunker schools and that the first real blitz behavior of the fall was coming within the week. That's the pattern setting up in front of us right now, from Raritan Bay all the way out to this end of the Island.

Water's still warm for early September, which is keeping the bite honest but not desperate — the fish aren't starving, they're staging. That means precision matters more than volume this week. The rocks on the east jetty at Shinnecock Inlet, and the deep hole just inside the inlet mouth on the bay side, are holding bass that feed hard on the back half of the ebb when the current rips bait off the flats and funnels it past the rocks. Fresh bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig, or a 2-ounce bucktail with a white Fin-S trailer worked slow along bottom, have been the better producers after dark. Daytime action at the inlet has been spotty — a few fish on the last of the incoming, nothing consistent — which tells you the bulk of the population is still holding out until the light drops and the current turns.

Ponquogue Bridge is worth a stop on the way home from the inlet, or as a standalone night trip if the inlet's crowded. Weakfish have been showing there consistently on the outgoing, stacked around the pilings where the current breaks. Small bucktails tipped with squid strips, or a bare jig head with a 4-inch chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullet, bounced slow along the bottom in that 12 to 15 foot channel under the span, has been the winning combo. Sizes have been mixed — plenty of teen-inch fish mixed with a few keepers pushing 18 inches — so it's not a blitz, it's a pick, but it's a reliable one if you're patient and fish the tide change tight.

Out in the bay, fluke are winding down from their summer peak, which tracks with what's happening across the rest of the Island right now. The drift from the Tiana Beach flats out toward the canal mouth has still been giving up keeper-class fish in the 18 to 22 inch range on white Gulp paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, worked slow on the last two hours of the outgoing when the bay water clears up and pushes bait through the deeper guts. Live killifish or peanut bunker on a spinner rig has out-produced straight bucktail on the slower days. This bite has another couple weeks in it before the fish start staging for their run out through the inlet, so don't wait too long if fluke's the target — the taper is already underway.

Bluefish remain the most dependable thing going right now, and they're not subtle about it. Chunked bunker or bright metal — a Kastmaster or a Hopkins — worked through any bait concentration near the inlet or along Tiana has been drawing consistent strikes, day or night, and sizes have been solid, not derby fish, but plenty of 3 to 6 pounders keeping rods bent while the bass sort themselves out.

The bigger story building offshore of this stretch is the bonito and false albacore starting to show right at the inlet mouth, working the bay anchovies and spearing that concentrate there this time of year. We called this one too — said the westward push would build over the next few days, and the early signs are there now. It's not a full-blown blitz yet, more a scattered morning and evening presence, but small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dicks worked fast through the rips at first light have been connecting. Worth keeping a light spinning rod rigged and ready if you're already out there for bass.

Looking ahead, the new moon on September 10 is the pivot point everybody on the dock is circling. If the bunker schools keep tightening the way they have been from here up through Narragansett Bay, that tide should be the trigger for the season's first real blitz — bass exploding on bait in the surf and right at the inlet mouth, not just picking quietly after dark. Between now and then, the smart play is still nights, still the outgoing, still working the inlet rocks and the deep hole rather than burning gas looking for something showier. If the wind stays out of the south and the water holds clean, Wednesday and Thursday night's outgoing look like the better bets before the new moon tide takes over the conversation entirely.
