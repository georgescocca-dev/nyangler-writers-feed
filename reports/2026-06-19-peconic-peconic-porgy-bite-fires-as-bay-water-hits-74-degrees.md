# Peconic porgy bite fires as bay water hits 74 degrees
*Jumbo scup stacked around Shelter Island while early weakfish show in the channels.*

**GREENPORT, NY — June 19** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic is cooking at 74 degrees this week — a full 6 degrees warmer than the ocean side — and that temperature differential is concentrating bait like a funnel. The porgy bite around Shelter Island has been lights-out, with jumbo scup in the 2-3 pound class hitting clam chunks and squid strips on high-low rigs. The key is getting your chum slick established on the flood tide and letting the current carry scent down to those deep holes where the big ones stack up.

I've been working the 25-30 foot drops on the north side of the island, and once that chum line takes hold, it's been non-stop action. The fish are quality — real jumbos pushing 18-19 inches with shoulders on them. Circle hooks in the 4/0 range on 20-pound fluorocarbon leaders are handling these bruisers without any problem. The bite has been strongest on the first two hours of the flood, when that warmer bay water is really moving.

Weakfish are starting to show in decent numbers too, which always gets my attention this time of year. I'm picking them up in the deeper channels between Shelter Island and Greenport, mostly on the drift with bucktails tipped with squid or Gulp. The fish are running 18-22 inches — real quality weakfish that haven't been this consistent in the Peconic for years. They're hitting on the same tide stage as the porgies, which makes for some interesting mixed bags.

The striped bass bite has been more scattered, but there are fish around. I'm finding them early morning and late evening in the rips near Orient Point, where that 74-degree bay water meets the cooler Sound. Bunker spoons and white bucktails are producing fish in the 28-32 inch range. The key is working the edges where the temperature breaks — bass are using that thermal line like a highway.

Bait has been everywhere in the bay. Sand eels are thick in the shallows, and I'm seeing good numbers of peanut bunker getting pushed around by the tides. That bait concentration is what's driving this early season action. The warm water has everything active and feeding.

Looking at the conditions, we've got southwest winds settling into a more manageable 10-15 knot range after some breezy days. The new moon is behind us, so tides are moderating, which actually helps the porgy fishing — less current means better bottom contact and more time for the chum to work.

Next week looks promising with this temperature pattern holding. That 74-degree water in the bay versus 67 degrees on the ocean side is creating a perfect setup for bait and predators alike. I'm expecting the weakfish bite to get even better as we move deeper into June, and the porgy fishing should stay strong through the month. The fluke are starting to show up in the channels too — nothing spectacular yet, but a few keepers mixed in with the shorts on bucktails and Gulp.

For anyone planning to fish the Peconic this week, focus on the flood tide around Shelter Island for porgies, drift the deeper channels for weakfish, and hit the temperature breaks early and late for bass. The bay is fishing like mid-summer already, and that's exactly what we want to see in mid-June.
