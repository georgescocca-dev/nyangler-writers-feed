# Barnegat Ridge in 75 Feet: Sea Bass Stacking Up While the Tuna Wait Out the Warm Edge
*A month-old warm filament is still holding mid-range tuna in play as the reef wrecks turn on for fall sea bass ahead of the first real cool-down.*

**MANASQUAN, NJ — September 21** — _by Capt. Rey Santos, Zone Writer_

Seventy-five feet on Barnegat Ridge, bottom temp still soft enough to keep bait balled up tight on the high spots — that's the number that matters this week. Not a cold snap, not a clean-out, just a slow bleed of summer that's letting the fall pattern build in layers instead of flipping the switch all at once. The Harvest Moon tides pushed through hard the last several days, and that extra water movement did what big tides do out here — it scattered bait off the open bottom and pinned it against structure. Wrecks, reef balls, high relief — anything that breaks current has been holding fish tighter than it was two weeks ago.

The black sea bass bite is the backbone of the week and it's a solid one. Triple Wrecks and Axel Carlson Reef have both been giving up sea bass in the 1.5 to 3-pound class, with a scattering of better fish mixed in on the up-current side of the high spots. Green and white Gulp Sea Bass Bites tipped with squid strips on a bucktail-and-teaser dropper rig has been the go-to, fished on the bottom half of the drift when the current is still moving enough to keep the bait swinging but not so hard you're bouncing bucktails at a 45-degree angle. Slack water kills this bite fast — the fish shut down and sit on the structure instead of feeding off it, so timing the drift to the meat of the tide matters more right now than boat position.

Sea Girt Reef and Shrewsbury Rocks are still giving up a mixed bag, sea bass mostly, with blackfish starting to show as bycatch on the deeper wreck pieces. That's the early tell for the tog pattern setting up — crab baits are starting to draw interest on the rockier stretches of Shrewsbury, though the real conviction bite is still a couple weeks off. Boats working there now are mostly incidental tog while targeting sea bass, but it's worth keeping a few green crabs in the well if you're anchored up on structure with any hard bottom mixed in.

Mahi have thinned out but haven't disappeared. The pot lines and any floating structure holding weed have kept a few stray gaffer-class mahi around longer than usual for this point in September, a function of that lingering warmth still sitting on the surface layer. Small chunks of butterfish or a green machine trolled past anything floating is still worth a look on the way out or back, but don't build a trip around it — this is a bonus fish right now, not a target.

The bigger story offshore is that the warm filament sitting over the mid-range grounds has held for close to a month now, and that stability is exactly what's kept the bluefin bite alive on the troll and the jig for boats running past the Klondike toward deeper water. We flagged this a week and a half ago — said the filament's position was the swing variable for the whole mid-shore tuna picture, and it's proven out. Boats working that warm edge have done well on Ronzi-style metal jigs dropped and worked fast through the middle of the water column, and on small black-and-something skirted spreads pulled behind bird teasers. Live-lined peanut bunker has also been producing when the fish are up shallow and marking on bait balls rather than deep structure. Yellowfin have mixed in with the bluefin on the better days, though the bluefin have been the more consistent player this month.

That filament is the thing to watch heading into the weekend. It's held for close to thirty days now, which is a long run for this time of year, and every extra day it stays put is another day the tuna bite holds where it is instead of sliding back out toward the true canyon edges. If a cold front comes through and breaks that warm water up — and there's real reason to think the first meaningful cooling push of the fall shows up before the month is out — expect the tuna to pull off the mid-range grounds fast. That would mean a longer run for boats that have gotten used to finding fish closer than usual.

For the next few days, the smart play is sea bass on the reef wrecks in the morning while the tide is moving well, working Triple Wrecks or Axel Carlson Reef on the strength of the current rather than fighting slack water. If you've got the range and the weather window, checking the warm edge out past Barnegat Ridge for bluefin is still worth the fuel while that filament holds — but keep an eye on any forecast chatter about a front, because that's the one thing that flips this whole picture. Blackfish guys should start scouting Shrewsbury Rocks now, even if the real bite is still a couple weeks out, because when it turns on it tends to turn on fast once the water drops another few degrees. Bring crabs just in case, but don't be surprised if sea bass still outnumber tog on any given drift for now.
