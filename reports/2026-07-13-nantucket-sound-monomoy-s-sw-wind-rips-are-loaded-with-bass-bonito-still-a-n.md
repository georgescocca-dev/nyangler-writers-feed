# Monomoy's SW-Wind Rips Are Loaded With Bass — Bonito Still a No-Show
*Sand eels and squid are stacking the current lines from Great Point to Monomoy, but the first bonito of the season hasn't punched the clock yet.*

**NANTUCKET, MA — July 13** — _by Charlie St. Pierre, Zone Writer_

I was idling along the edge of the Monomoy rip Tuesday morning, just outside the second bar, when a school of sand eels came apart like someone dropped a grenade in the water — silver spray, then bass boiling right through the middle of it, tails up, gill plates flashing in that low morning light. No birds working it yet, just the fish themselves giving it away. That's the kind of thing that gets me out of bed at 4:45 even after fifteen years of doing this.

That's been the story on the south side all week. The southwest wind that's been blowing steady out of the Sound has been stacking bait against the Monomoy rips — Southeast Rip, the bar off the Powder Hole, the seam that runs off the Point — and the bass have moved up onto it hard. These aren't cruisers either. Fish in the 28 to 36-inch class, some bigger, working the current edges where the clean green water from the ocean side collides with the darker, warmer push out of the Sound. I've been running 1.5-ounce bucktails in white and olive, tipped with a strip of squid, cast up-current and let sink before working it back with that slow, stuttering retrieve along bottom. When they're really on the sand eels, a 5-inch Deadly Dick or a slim epoxy jig fished with a fast, twitchy retrieve up in the top few feet of the water column has been drawing the more aggressive fish off the bottom entirely. Best window has consistently been the last two hours of the ebb into dead low — that's when the rip really turns on itself and the bait gets pinned against the shoal.

Great Point has been a mixed bag, and I'll be straight about it. Some mornings the ebb rip off the point is stacked with bluefish — proper choppers, 8 to 12 pounds, blowing up on anything shiny — and the bass are underneath them, picking off the scraps on bucktails dropped through the melee. Other mornings it's dead calm and quiet, just current moving over sand with nothing showing. That's mid-July on the Point for you. The squid have been pushing into the Sound in bigger numbers than I've seen the last couple summers, which is exactly the kind of signal that usually precedes the bonito showing up — squid draw bait, bait draws speed. But I've made probably a dozen trips out to the Shoals and around Horseshoe Shoal specifically hunting that first green flash of a bonito breaking on top, and I haven't seen one yet. Neither has anyone else I've talked to at the co-op. It's coming — it's usually the last week of July into early August around here — but if you're rigging up small Deadly Dicks and Albie Snax hoping to connect this week, you're early. I'd rather tell you that now than have you burn a tank of gas chasing ghosts.

What is producing, and producing well, is the fluke fishing out on the Shoals. This has been the best fluke bite I've had in a few seasons. Drifting the edges around the Nantucket Shoals in 25 to 45 feet, using 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullet in pearl or white, and we've been putting fish in the boat steadily on every drift — a handful of shorts for every keeper, but the keepers have been running heavy, several pushing 6 and 7 pounds. The key has been staying right on the transition where the sand gives way to broken bottom and current speed picks up — that's where the bait gets funneled and the bigger fluke sit waiting. Slow drift, bounce that bucktail right on bottom, and don't be afraid to let it sit a beat longer than feels natural before the next hop.

Water's been running warm across the Sound — nothing dramatic, just a steady, healthy summer push, comfortable in the mid-to-upper 60s out toward the Shoals and warmer still in the shallower stretches inside toward Hyannis and the harbor mouths. That warmth combined with the squid and sand eel activity is exactly the recipe that usually kicks the whole system into gear for late summer, so I'm not worried about the pieces being in place. They are. It's just a matter of timing.

Looking ahead, I'd keep working the Monomoy rips on that SW wind while it holds — the bass bite there has been the most consistent thing going, and dead low into the first hour of the flood has been money. I'd keep half an eye on Great Point every morning you're out that way, because when the bonito do show, they tend to show fast and thick, and the guys who are already out there with small metal rigged and ready are the ones who connect first. And if you want a sure thing this week, point the boat at the Shoals and drift bucktails for fluke — that bite has been the most dependable thing in my logbook the last ten days. I'll be out chasing that first bonito flash regardless. Two weeks of watching and nothing yet, but that's exactly how it goes right before it doesn't.
