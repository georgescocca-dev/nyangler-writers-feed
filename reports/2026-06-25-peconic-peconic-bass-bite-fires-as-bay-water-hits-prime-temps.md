# Peconic bass bite fires as bay water hits prime temps
*Slot stripers crash bucktails on the flood while porgies stack thick around Shelter Island.*

**GREENPORT, NY — June 25** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic is singing that summer song now, and the bass are finally listening. Water temps climbed into the mid-60s this week, triggering the kind of consistent striper action we've been waiting for since the spring migration stalled out in May.

The flood tide has been the ticket, especially that sweet spot two hours after the turn when current really starts moving through the channels. I've been working the edges around Shelter Island where the deep water meets the flats, and the bass are stacked there like cordwood. Thirty-inch fish are the norm, with plenty pushing into the keeper zone. White and chartreuse bucktails in the 3/4 to 1-ounce range are doing the damage — keep them bouncing just off bottom on the drift and let the current do the work.

The early morning bite has been exceptional. First light through about 8 AM, when that flood really gets cranking, you can almost set your watch by it. The bass are feeding aggressively on the bait getting swept through the channels, and they're not being picky about presentation. I've seen guys scoring on everything from soft plastics to live eels, but those bucktails remain the most consistent producer.

Porgies continue to dominate the numbers game around the traditional spots near Jessup's Neck and the Middle Grounds. The bite has been steadier than earlier in the season, when it was hit-or-miss. Now you can count on solid action if you get your chum line established and fish the right stage of tide. The flood has been best for porgies too — seems like everything in the bay is keyed into that incoming water.

Sizes have been impressive, with plenty of fish pushing 17 to 18 inches and the occasional jumbo topping 3 pounds. High-low rigs with size 4 hooks and fresh clam have been the standard approach, but don't overlook green crabs if you can get them. The porgies have been absolutely crushing crabs, especially the softer ones.

Weakfish numbers are building nicely throughout the bay system. I'm seeing consistent catches of keeper-sized fish, mostly in the 18 to 20-inch range, with the occasional bruiser pushing 4 pounds. They're showing up mixed in with the porgy action, but you can also target them specifically by working the deeper channels during slack water periods. Bucktails tipped with Gulp work well, but live killies on a fishfinder rig will really light them up if you can get the bait.

The new moon we just passed stirred things up nicely, creating those strong tidal flows that really get bait moving through the system. Now we're heading into the waxing phase, which should maintain good current velocity without the extreme highs and lows that can make fishing challenging.

Looking ahead, I expect this pattern to hold through the weekend and into next week. Bay temperatures should continue climbing toward 70 degrees, which will only improve the bass bite. The key is going to be timing your trips with the tide — that flood stage from two hours after the turn through slack has been producing the most consistent action.

For the weekend warriors, Saturday's morning flood looks particularly promising. Get out there early, work those channel edges with bucktails, and don't be surprised if you find yourself in the middle of one of those magical Peconic mornings where every drift produces fish. This is what we wait for all spring — the bay is finally alive and the fishing is only getting better.
