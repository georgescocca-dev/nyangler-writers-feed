# Albies Crash Plum Gut, Bass Stack Behind the Bunker
*The first false albacore showed up in the Race this week right on schedule, and they walked in behind a bass bite that's finally getting serious.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — September 14** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The first false albacore of the fall showed up in Plum Gut this week, and the mates working that rip already have the pattern dialed. Small pods, moving fast, breaking on rain bait pushed through on the last two hours of the ebb — right where the current stacks against the eastern edge before it dumps into the Sound. We flagged this last week, said the albie push out of Rhode Island would keep working west into this zone, and here it is. Early, but real. Epoxy jigs and 000 Deadly Dicks got tested against fish that wouldn't commit twice, which is about right for week one of an albie run — the window opens and closes in about twenty minutes, and if you're not already in position when the birds pin, you're rebaiting instead of fighting fish.

That's the sideshow though. The real story this week is bunker, and it's exactly the story we called seven days ago. Bass are locking onto tightening bunker schools from Raritan Bay clear up into the Sound, and this zone is starting to show it. New London is reading 69.4°F, Kings Point out west is a touch warmer at 70.9, and that gradient is doing what it always does in early September — it's pushing bait east and holding it against structure instead of letting it spread thin. The Race and Plum Gut are both current-scoured enough to hold bunker pods tight against the rock, and where the bunker sit, the bass show up underneath them.

The pattern on bass right now is textbook staging behavior, not full commitment yet. Boats working the west side of the Race on the last of the ebb are marking bunker balled up tight over 40 to 60 feet, with bass and bigger blues holding low and picking off stragglers rather than blowing through the school. That means bunker-spoon trolling and snag-and-drop with a live bunker on a fish-finder rig are outproducing straight bucktail right now — you need to get down into that lower third of the water column where the commitment fish are sitting. Surf guys walking the Orient rocks at first light are doing better with big soft plastics — 9-inch swimbaits and paddle-tails in bunker colors — worked slow through the seams where the current eddies off the point. Nothing explosive yet, but enough quality fish in the 20s and low 30s to keep the 3 a.m. crowd coming back.

Bluefish are the more aggressive story right now, and the size class matters. Montauk put an 18.49-pound blue on the scale this week off thick bunker schools, and that's not a fluke fish — that's a signal that a real wave of big fall blues is working the bait. That same bunker-driven pattern is starting to show at the western end of this zone, especially around Fishers Island Sound where current edges concentrate the peanut schools funneling out of the harbors. If you want blues right now, look for diving birds working tight over bunker balls on the last two hours of the ebb at either the Race or the Gut, and throw big poppers or bunker-spoon imitations — these fish are eating with intent, not sipping.

Fluke are winding down, and that's not a surprise this time of year. The bite is still there if you know where to look — deeper water off Orient Point and around the east side of Fishers Island is still holding keeper-class fish on bucktail and Gulp combinations, five-inch white or pink, fished slow on the drift in 30 to 40 feet. But the schools are thinning fast as fish stage for the offshore push, and the next stretch of cooling nights is going to accelerate that exodus. If fluke is the priority, this weekend is a better bet than next.

Porgy fishing has been the quiet bright spot all week — steady, dependable, the kind of bite you can set a family trip around. Sandworms and clam on high-low rigs over the rock piles off Plum Gut and around Fishers Island are producing full coolers with minimal effort, and that bite should hold right through blackfish season opening in a few weeks.

The tidal range is building toward the next moon, and that's exactly the mechanism that's going to push this whole system forward. Bigger tides mean harder-running current at the Race and the Gut, which means more bait getting pinned against structure and more predator commitment on top of it. Over the next 48 to 72 hours, expect the albie push to solidify rather than fade — the water's in the right range and the bait's already here. Expect bass to keep building behind the bunker schools rather than blitzing outright; this is a staging pattern that typically takes another week or two of cooling nights to turn into the full-on fall run. If you've only got one window this weekend, fish the last two hours of the ebb at Plum Gut with bunker-spoon gear ready for blues and a bucktail rigged for whatever's sitting underneath. Keep an epoxy jig tied on a second rod — you won't get much notice when the albies show.
