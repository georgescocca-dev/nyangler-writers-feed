# Bunker Balled Up Off Mattituck, and the Blues Finally Came Back to Eat Them
*Peanut bunker packed the inlet mouths all week, and for the first time since June the bluefish showed up to work them properly.*

**SOUTHOLD, NY — August 14** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Ray Tuthill's been running charter out of Mattituck longer than I've had gray in this beard, and Tuesday morning he came by the bait shop with a look on him — not excited exactly, more like a man who'd just watched something he half-expected finally show up. Said he had bunker balled up so thick off the inlet mouth his depth finder looked like a bowl of stew. Told him that's no surprise to me. I've been watching the gulls work that same stretch for the better part of two weeks, and a gull tells the truth before the tackle shop chatter ever does.

We flagged this a week back — said the blues reappearing on the deep Western Sound reefs would push east along the North Fork as bait redistributed with the new moon tide, and that's exactly what's happened. The new moon came through on the 12th, tide's been building hard behind it, and that flow has done what it always does this time of year — pinned the peanut bunker tight against the beach from Mattituck clear out past Rocky Point. Where the bunker goes, the bass and blues follow, and this week they both showed up to the party.

Striper fishing off Horton Point has been the most honest work on my stretch. Guys working the rip on the last two hours of the ebb are doing well with bunker spoons and 1 to 2-ounce bucktails in white or chartreuse, bounced along bottom where the current stacks up against the rocks. Nothing enormous — most of what's coming over the rail is 24 to 30 inches, a mix of keepers and fish you're kissing goodbye — but it's steady, and on a slow retrieve through the seam you're getting bit more often than not on the right tide stage. Live-lining a snagged peanut bunker off a fish-finder rig has worked just as well for the guys who'd rather sit than cast, especially right at the Mattituck Inlet mouth where the bait funnels tight on the outgoing.

The bluefish are the real story of the week, though, and it's a welcome one. They'd been thin on this end of the Sound all July, but they've come back in numbers strong enough to matter, working the same bunker schools as the bass, sometimes right on top. Poppers and metal — Kastmasters, Deadly Dicks, anything that throws a wake — have been getting hit hard early and late, especially where you can see the bait getting nervous on the surface off Rocky Point. Snapper blues are thick in the coves too, which tells you the adult class fish coming through now are just the leading edge. Expect more of them as the month wears on.

Porgies remain the quiet, dependable worker on this beat — no drama, no blitzes, just steady action on the reefs and rockpiles off Orient and out toward the Plum Gut approach. Sandworms and clam on a simple high-low rig in 15 to 25 feet is still doing the job. Nothing's changed there in weeks and I don't expect it to.

Blackfish season's still a ways off, but I've had three different fellows stop me at the launch this week asking about green crab supply and tying up double-hook Asian crab rigs, scouting the same rockpiles they'll fish come October. That's normal for mid-August — a man likes to know where his rocks are before the water cools and the tautog move onto them. Nothing to report yet but the intent, which is its own kind of sign.

Fluke have been modest but not absent — a few keepers coming off the deeper water outside Mattituck Inlet on white Gulp swimming mullets and small bucktails, drifted slow on the last of the incoming. Nothing to build a trip around, but worth a drift if you're already out there working bass water. Weakfish, I'll be honest with you, have been scarce on my end this week. A few reports of small ones taken incidentally on bucktails meant for something else, but nobody's targeting them with any confidence right now, and I wouldn't either.

The water's holding warm enough to keep this pattern rolling — readings from Kings Point down around 71 on the west end of the Sound, cooling off toward the high 60s as you work east toward New London, which is about what I'd expect for mid-August and puts my stretch squarely in the productive middle of that gradient. That kind of spread usually means bait keeps moving through on the tide rather than settling in one place, which is exactly what we've seen.

Looking ahead, I'd keep working the last two hours of the ebb at Horton Point and the inlet mouths through this weekend — the tidal flow off this new moon should keep sharpening as it builds toward the next few days, and that's when the bass and blues tend to commit hardest to the rips. Keep an eye on the water out toward Plum Gut, too. There's warm water and bait moving through that corridor right now, and if the pattern holds the way it's been trending region-wide, this is about the time of year the first false albacore or bonito starts showing up in that current. Nobody's seen one on my end yet. But I've got a rod rigged for it just the same.
