# The Ledge Goes Quiet at Noon — Bass Feeding After Dark, Bonito Water Building
*Warm surface water has pushed the striper bite into the low-light hours around Block Island, while fluke keep the rods bent through the heat of the day.*

**BLOCK ISLAND, RI — July 21** — _by Jack Sullivan, Zone Writer_

I felt it before I saw it — walked down to the dock Tuesday morning and the air had that flat, heavy stillness that tells you the water's gone warm on top. No breeze pushing through the Great Salt Pond, glass calm out past the breakwater. That kind of stillness usually means the bass have gone smart on us, and sure enough, that's exactly what's happened.

The Southwest Ledge and the North Rip have both slowed to a crawl during daylight this week. Boats working the Ledge at high noon are marking bait and marking fish on the sounder but getting nothing to commit. It's not that the stripers left — it's that they've pushed their feeding window to the edges of the day, dawn and dusk and full dark, same pattern we're seeing coastwide right now with surface temps sitting in the high 60s to low 70s. I fished the North Rip at 5 AM Thursday, eel on a three-way rig, 20 feet down on the drop into the rip current, and connected on two fish in the mid-30s before the sun cleared the horizon. By 8 o'clock, dead. That's the rhythm right now — get there early or fish it after dark, don't waste your prime tide in the middle of the afternoon.

Night fishing off the Ledge has actually been the more dependable program. Bunker pods are holding tight to the structure after dark, and the bass are stacking underneath them in that 25-to-40-foot band where the current sweeps hard on the ebb. A live bunker on a fish-finder rig, freespooled back into the current seam, has been the ticket for guys willing to put the hours in after midnight. We're not talking blitz conditions — it's a fish here, a fish there, quality over quantity — but the ones coming over the rail have had some size to them, low 30s to high 30s, with a few reports of fish pushing 40 taken right off the rocks on the west side after dark.

Fluke have been the more honest bite this week, and I mean that as a compliment. While the bass have gone moody, the fluke haven't changed their habits one bit. Structure and moving water, same as always — the drift off Charlestown Breachway side of the island, the mussel beds south of the Great Salt Pond entrance, and the deeper edges off Southwest Ledge itself in 35 to 50 feet have all been holding fish. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, dragged slow on the last two hours of the outgoing — that's been the winning combination. Nothing giant, mostly 16 to 19 inches with a scattered keeper pushing 4 pounds, but steady enough that you can fill a cooler if you keep the boat moving and stay on structure.

Scup fishing has been a nice consolation prize for anyone who wants an easy bag limit without burning gas hunting bass. The reef structure around Block Island Sound and the rocky patches off Point Judith are loaded with porgies right now, and a simple high-low rig with squid strips or clam will keep the rod bouncing all afternoon. Good family fishing while the bigger targets are being stubborn.

Now for the one we've had our eye on. We flagged last week that water temps pushing into bonito comfort range around Montauk, the Vineyard, and Buzzards Bay meant we should start watching the temperature breaks closely — and this week that watch is paying attention. No confirmed bonito in Block Island Sound yet, but the water's sitting right in that window where they typically show, and I'd be surprised if we don't see the first scattered fish working the rips within the next week or two. Worth running the East Grounds and the tide rips off Sandy Point with small metal jigs — half-ounce Deadly Dicks, Kastmasters — just in case you're the one who finds them first.

We're sliding into a first-quarter neap right now, and that's going to soften up the current through the North Rip and off the Ledge over the next few days. That's not bad news — it actually widens the window for eel and swimbait presentations, since the water won't be ripping through so hard that you can't hold bottom. If I had one trip to make this weekend, I'd fish the last hour of the outgoing at first light off the Southwest Ledge with a live eel, then switch over to bucktails on the drift once the sun's up and the fluke start showing on structure. Keep an eye on that bait — where the bunker sit after dark is where the bass are going to be, neap tide or not.
