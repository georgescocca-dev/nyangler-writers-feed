# The Bunker Wall Hits the Rip: Bass Stacking Behind the Bait at North Bar and Shagwong
*Harvest Moon tides are pushing peanut and adult bunker straight into the Point, and the fall run is finally showing its teeth.*

**MONTAUK, NY — September 21** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

This is the week of the season everybody at the Point waits for. The calendar has turned toward the Harvest Moon, the tides are building, and the bunker schools that have been staging off the South Side beaches all month are finally getting pushed into range by the bigger water movement. Montauk doesn't run on a calendar — it runs on bait and tide — and right now both are lining up the way they're supposed to in the last week of September. Water off the Point is holding in the upper 60s, which is warm for this stretch of fall, and that warmth is exactly why the multi-species mix out here has been so wide open: fluke still biting, weakfish showing in numbers nobody expected this late, bluefish riding every bunker school like they own it, and striped bass building behind all of it with real purpose.

We flagged this coming — said the blitz pattern at the Point would hold and likely build as the tidal range grew toward this moon phase, and that's exactly what's setting up now. The Harvest Moon tides are moving more water through the Rip than we've seen in weeks, and that flow is doing what it always does at Montauk: concentrating bait against structure. North Bar, Shagwong Reef, the boulder field off the Lighthouse, Turtle Cove — all of it is holding bunker right now, and the stripers know it.

The surf has been the story. Reports off the ocean beaches have been consistent — slot and overslot bass mixed with genuinely large bluefish, staged tight against the current edges and boils where the rip meets the flat. Very few shorts in the mix, which tells you this is migratory fish moving through, not the resident summer stock. The presentation that's been working off the beach is simple and it's been working for fifty years for a reason: a 1.5-ounce bucktail cast across the seam, let it sink clean off the edge before you start the retrieve, then work it back slow and rhythmic along the bottom. When the bass are up in the wash chasing bunker, switch to a bunker-colored swimming plug or a big popper worked right through the boil — don't long-bomb it, feather the cast into the seam itself. That's where the fish are sitting, not out in open water.

From the boats, the pattern has been live bunker on wire — snag one out of the pod with a treble, drop it back on a fish-finder rig or free-line it right into the school, and hang on. The bigger migratory bass coming in behind the peanut bunker are hitting adult menhaden hard, and that's the fish to target if you want a shot at something over 40. North Bar has been the most consistent starting point on the building tide — the bar itself pushes the current up and forces bait to the surface, and that's when you'll see the birds working and the blitz go off. Shagwong Reef has been holding fish a little deeper, better for trolling umbrella rigs or bunker spoons if the surface action isn't showing.

Fluke have been a pleasant surprise for this late in September. Warmer-than-normal water is keeping them shallower and more active than you'd expect with fall already underway, and boats working the drift off the south side structure are still putting keepers in the box on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with squid strips, fished on 3/4-ounce bucktails in 25 to 35 feet. That bite typically shuts off hard with the first real cold front, so if fluke are on your list, this week and maybe next is the window — don't wait.

Weakfish have been mixing into the same current edges as the bass, which isn't something you see often this late in the year, but the warm water is holding them around longer than usual. They're coming on the same bucktail-and-bottom presentation as the stripers, just lighter gear and a softer hand on the retrieve. Bottom guys working porgies and blackfish around the harbor jetties and the rock piles off the Point have had steady if unspectacular action — not the headline, but a solid consolation prize on a day when the blitz doesn't show.

The next 48 to 72 hours look like the strongest window of the run so far. The moon keeps building, the tidal range keeps growing, and that means more bait getting pushed through the Rip on a more predictable schedule — which at Montauk is everything. If the wind stays manageable out of the south and west, expect the surface action at North Bar and off the Lighthouse to intensify rather than fade, especially in that first hour of the building tide before the sun gets high. If a cold front comes through and knocks the water temperature down even a few degrees, the fluke bite will be the first thing to tighten up, and the bigger bass could start pushing harder and faster on their way west — which would mean less patience and more urgency for anyone planning a trip this week. Best case, this holds together through the weekend and the Harvest Moon tides keep the bait stacked right where it is now. If you've only got one morning to give it, make it the building tide at first light, bucktail in hand, eyes on the birds.
