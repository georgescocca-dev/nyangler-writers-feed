# The Cut at Holgate Deepened Overnight — Fluke Moved In Behind It
*Bass have gone dark with the heat, bluefish are chewing through bunker on the beach, and the inlet structure is doing all the work now.*

**SHIP BOTTOM, NJ — July 18** — _by Tommy Nguyen, Zone Writer_

I walked the north end of Holgate at gray light Tuesday, low tide, and the bar that's held shape since Memorial Day had opened a new cut about 150 yards south of the old washout — a foot deeper than it was two weeks back, with a hard rip line still visible where the outgoing water was funneling through. That's the kind of change you only catch if you're out there at the right stage of tide, and it explains why the fluke bite has been so lopsided this week. The fish followed the deeper water in, and they've been stacked on the seam of that new cut ever since.

Fluke's been the honest bite on LBI right now, and it's a daytime program almost exclusively — the last two hours of the outgoing, working the jetty rocks at Barnegat Light and the channel edge off Beach Haven Inlet in 8 to 14 feet. Four-inch white Gulp on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow with the current rather than against it, is putting keepers in the cooler — nothing huge, mostly 18 to 20 inches, but steady counts if you work the drift right and don't rush the retrieve. We flagged this transition last week, that the back bay fluke would push out toward inlet mouths and ocean structure as the water warmed further, and that's exactly what's happened. The mud flats inside Manahawkin Bay have gone quiet for fluke — all the activity's moved to hard bottom now, Graveling Point's channel edge and the rock piles at the BL inlet jetty holding the better fish.

Striped bass are a different story, and I'll be straight with you — daytime bass fishing on LBI right now is mostly a waste of gas. Bay temps are sitting mid-to-upper 70s, and that's pushed the resident fish into a strict nocturnal pattern. This isn't a disappearance, it's thermal avoidance — the bass are still here, they're just feeding after dark when the water cools a few degrees and the current breaks near the Causeway bridge pilings and the deeper holes off Ship Bottom come alive. If you want a shot at a keeper, fish the two hours around the bottom of the tide, midnight to 3 a.m., swimming a Bomber Long A or a soft-bodied swimbait slow through the current seam. It's not comfortable fishing, but it's the only fishing that's working for bass right now.

Bluefish are the one species making noise in daylight, and it's the bunker that's driving it. Schools have been pushing south along the beachfront all week, the same pattern that's had the beaches north of us — Belmar on down — blitzing daily, and it's finally spreading into our stretch. I had reports of blues busting through bunker off Surf City and again off north Beach Haven on back-to-back mornings, blitz conditions right in the wash, fish in the 3-to-6-pound class hammering anything that moves. We called this one too — said the blues would follow the bunker down the coast, and here they are. If you see the bait balling up and the birds start working, don't overthink it — a chrome Kastmaster or a top water pencil popper thrown right into the whitewater will get bit almost every cast while it lasts. These blitzes are short-lived, twenty minutes and gone, so keep the plug bag light and be ready to move fast up the beach.

Weakfish have been the quiet surprise. Graveling Point and the mouth of the Mullica have both given up a handful of legal weakfish on calm evenings this week, taken on soft plastics — pearl white paddletails on light jigheads, fished slow along the channel drop where the river current meets the bay. We said this bite would rebuild on stable, warm evening conditions, and it's tracking that way, though I want to be honest — it's not a bite you can plan a trip around yet. A couple of guys got two or three fish in a session, nothing consistent, but the pattern's building and worth checking if you're already out there for stripers after dark.

Ocean conditions have kept most of the action pinned to structure. Swells have been running around 2 to 3 feet with a short period, sloppy enough that the open beachfront hasn't been fishing clean, so I've been steering people toward the inlet jetties and channel edges where the structure breaks up the energy. Water off the coast is holding in the low 70s, considerably warmer than what's typically pushing bass activity, which is another reason the daytime program has shifted so hard toward fluke and blues.

Looking ahead — the moon's building toward first quarter on the 21st, which should ease the tidal swing a touch by the weekend. Slacker current usually means the fluke bite gets a little tougher to time right, so I'd fish the last hour of the outgoing hard rather than spreading effort across the whole tide. If the bunker schools keep holding tight to the beach, I expect the bluefish blitz pattern to keep creeping south and get more consistent right through Ship Bottom and Beach Haven by the weekend. For bass, stick with the night program until we get a real cool-down — nothing on the horizon suggests that's coming soon, so plan your trips around the dark hours and the deeper current breaks, not the calendar.
