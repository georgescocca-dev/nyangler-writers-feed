# Race Point rips still giving up mackerel-fed stripers as the Bay tips toward fall
*Bait schools are thick off Provincetown and the bass are following them tight to structure — here's where the next week sets up.*

**PROVINCETOWN, MA — September 14** — _by Donny Costa, Zone Writer_

The dock talk this week out of P-town has been consistent: mackerel, mackerel, mackerel. Charter boats working Race Point and out toward Peaked Hill Bars have been running into thick schools of mackerel stacked from the surface down to 30 feet, and where that bait sits, the bigger stripers haven't been far behind. We flagged this pattern last week — said the mackerel schools were the tell, and that predator activity would follow close behind — and that's exactly how the last several days have played out. The bass aren't blitzing on top consistently, but they're working the bait pods hard on the change of tide, and that's given the fleet a workable window most mornings.

Water temps in the Bay are still holding in that comfortable low-to-mid 60s range for September, which is keeping both the mackerel and the bass in residence rather than pushing everything out toward the Canal early. Wind's been the bigger variable — a few days of brisk southwest breeze chopped up the surface over Billingsgate and Sandy Neck, but Race Point and the rips off the backside of the Cape have stayed fishable because that structure breaks up the swell. The moon is building toward full later this month, and tidal push has been noticeably stronger on the last few drops — that's moving more bait through the rips and giving the bass a reason to stage tighter to structure instead of scattering across open water.

The best producing pattern right now is live-lining mackerel on a slider rig over the rips at Race Point, fishing the top of the incoming through the first hour of the ebb. Boats anchoring up-current and drifting baits back into the rip line have been connecting with bass in the 28- to 38-inch class, with a few better fish pushing into the 40-pound range showing up on the wider drifts off Peaked Hill Bars in 25 to 40 feet of water. When the mackerel balls tighten up and birds start working, that's the cue to get baits down fast — the window when bass commit hard to the bait usually only lasts 20 to 30 minutes before the school scatters and regroups elsewhere along the rip.

Bluefish have been mixed into the same water, and they're not shy about it. Diamond jigs — the 2- to 4-ounce range in the chrome or bar finish — worked on a fast retrieve through the mackerel schools have been picking off blues in the 4- to 8-pound class, with the occasional bigger fish mixed in when the bait gets pressured hard and pushed to the surface. Anyone fishing bait for stripers off Race Point this week has had to deal with blues cutting baits off, which is as good a sign as any that there's real forage density out there right now.

Billingsgate has been quieter than the rips, but not dead. The flats and the deeper edges around Billingsgate Shoal have given up some fluke on the last of the outgoing tide, working white Gulp on light bucktails or a simple hi-lo rig with squid strips, drifted slow over the sand-to-mud transitions in 15 to 25 feet. It's not the kind of numbers game you get in July, but there are still keeper fish in that 18- to 22-inch range for boats willing to make a few drifts and cover water rather than sit on one spot.

Cod haven't shown in any real numbers yet on the deeper structure off Provincetown, and that's normal for mid-September — the better cod bite in the Bay typically doesn't turn on until the water cools further into October and November, when fish push up onto the deeper humps and ledges out past the rip. Worth keeping an eye on, but not worth planning a trip around just yet.

Looking at the next few days, the pattern holds if the wind stays out of the north and east rather than kicking up out of the southwest again — that's the difference between a clean drift over the rips and a chop that pushes the bait and the bass off structure. If the mackerel schools stay put off Race Point and Peaked Hill Bars, and early signs suggest they will as the water hasn't shown any signs of pushing out yet, the live-bait bite on the morning tide should keep producing through the weekend. Sandy Neck and the Barnstable side of the Bay are worth a look for anyone chasing bluefish on top when conditions are calm, since bait tends to get pushed into that corner on a building tide.

The bigger picture is that the fall push is starting to show its hand. Bait is concentrating, tides are building toward the full moon, and the bass are shadowing the mackerel tight to structure rather than roaming open water. That's the kind of setup that historically tightens up as September rolls into October in this Bay — worth paying attention to over the next couple of weeks as more forage gets squeezed through the system and the bigger bass start committing harder to the rips.
