# Biddeford Pool's Incoming Hour Still Owns July — Bass to 28, Blues Wrecking Higgins
*A dawn Danny plug bite at the Pool, a blitz that shut down half of Higgins Beach, and why the southwest wind is your friend at Prouts Neck right now.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — July 17** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

Five-forty in the morning, first pink light over the Pool, and a bass came up under a black Danny plug like it had somewhere to be. Twenty-eight pounds, maybe a hair over, hooked on the incoming right where the channel bends past the yacht club moorings. That fish didn't chase the plug — it ambushed it, one turn from the boulder edge, and I swear my elbow's still sore. That's the thing about Biddeford Pool on the incoming: it isn't a long bite, it's an hour, hour and a half tops, but it's the best hour in Southern Maine striper fishing and it has been since before I had gray in my beard. Water stacks up through that narrow mouth, bait gets pinned against the rocks and the sandbar, and big fish sit in the current seam waiting for the buffet line to start moving. This week that seam has been loaded with pogies pushed up from the south, and the bass know it.

Higgins Beach gave me one of those mornings you tell people about at the boat ramp for a month. Birds were already working when I pulled in around 7, diving hard off the second sandbar, and it wasn't subtle — blues busting bait on top, water churned white in patches the size of a driveway. Threw a chrome Kastmaster on 20-pound braid and didn't get two full cranks before getting hit. Fish ran 3 to 6 pounds, blitz lasted maybe forty minutes before it moved south toward the Spurwink River mouth and thinned out. If you're throwing metal or a Super Strike popper at Higgins right now, keep your eyes on the gulls more than the water — they'll tell you where to be faster than any app will.

Pine Point has been a grinder this week, not a blitz spot. The current runs hard there on the bigger tides and you need to work the drop-off where the flat falls into the channel — that's holding schoolie bass and the occasional slot fish, mostly on the last two hours of the outgoing when bait gets flushed out of the marsh. Soft plastics have outfished hard baits there — a 6-inch white Slug-Go on a light jighead, twitched slow along bottom, has been the ticket over bucktails. The water's warm enough now that fish don't want to burn calories chasing something fast.

Prouts Neck has fished exactly like it should with this southwest wind we've had most of the week. That wind pushes bait right up against the rocks on the ocean side, and stripers stack in tight to structure to intercept it. I like a darter or a needlefish plug there in the last light of the evening, worked slow and parallel to the rocks — not bombed out to open water. The fish holding there aren't big-water fish, they're ambush fish, and you need to put the plug where they're sitting, not where you think the school is.

Mackerel have shown up thick off Kittery and around Cape Neddick, schools stacked 20 to 40 feet down, and that's worth knowing even if you're not targeting them directly — live mackerel on a fish-finder rig has been the best bait I've fished all month for the bigger bass holding deeper off Prouts Neck and the outer edge of the Pool. If you can jig up a few macks in the morning on a small Sabiki rig, keep three or four in the livewell and drift them past structure on the last of the incoming. That's how the 28-pounder's cousin, a fish just under 30, got landed off a buddy's boat near Wood Island Light on Tuesday.

Flounder have been steady but not exciting in Wells Harbor and up the Kennebunk River — bloodworms and skimmer clam on a simple two-hook flounder rig, fished on the bottom during slack water, mostly keeper-sized fish in the 14 to 16 inch range. It's a good bite for anyone who wants dinner without fighting current or crowds, and it's about the most reliable thing going on this beat right now.

Cod fishing has gone quiet close to shore, which is normal for mid-July — the water up top has warmed enough that most of what's left has slid deeper. A few boats out of Cape Elizabeth are still finding shorts and the occasional keeper working structure in 60 to 80 feet, but nobody's calling it hot, and I wouldn't plan a trip around it right now.

Looking ahead, if this southwest wind holds through the weekend I'd be back at Prouts Neck at last light with a darter, and I'd be at Biddeford Pool at first light on the incoming with a Danny or a Doc — that combination has been the most consistent one-two punch on this whole stretch of coast this month. Higgins is a wildcard; when the blitz is on it's about as good as surf fishing gets in Maine, but it can vanish for two days at a time chasing bait south toward Spurwink or north past Cape Elizabeth. Watch the birds, not the calendar, and you'll find it.
