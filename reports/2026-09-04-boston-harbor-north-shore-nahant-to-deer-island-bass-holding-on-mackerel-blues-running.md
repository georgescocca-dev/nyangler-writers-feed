# Nahant to Deer Island: Bass Holding on Mackerel, Blues Running the Bait Off the Rocks
*The harbor islands are still the safer bet than the open rocks this week, but the first real cool nights are lining up to change that fast.*

**BOSTON HARBOR, MA — September 4** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one or two days this week, the call is the harbor islands first, the North Shore rocks second. That's not a knock on Nahant or Gloucester — it's a read on where the bait is holding tightest right now, and bait is still the whole story in early September. Boston Harbor's islands — Deer Island, Spectacle, the flats off President Roads — are stacking pogies and mackerel on the tide changes, and the bass are staged on them rather than actively blitzing. That's a pattern we flagged last week: the bunker and pogie schools needed to concentrate further before the bass would commit in numbers, and that's exactly the stage we're in now. Water's still sitting warm, low-to-mid 70s inshore, which keeps fish comfortable enough to spread out and feed on their own schedule instead of balling up and busting bait on the surface. The moon builds to full around September 6-7, and that's the mechanism that should start tightening things up — bigger tidal swings, stronger current, bait pinned harder against structure. Until then, this is a mixed, honest bite: good fish around, but you have to find the seam.

The harbor program right now centers on the last two hours of the flood into the top of the ebb around Deer Island and the Nahant flats. Live mackerel on a fish-finder rig, or a chunk of pogie fished on the bottom in 15 to 25 feet off the Deer Island drop, has been the most consistent producer for keeper-class bass, with a scattering of fish pushing over 30 inches on the deeper edges where the flat drops into the channel. Light-tackle guys working bucktails — 1 to 1.5 ounce white or chartreuse, worked slow along bottom on the last of the ebb — are picking at fish too, but it's not a wide-open bite. You're working for your fish, not covering water blind and expecting them to show up.

Bluefish are the more dependable target basin-wide right now, and that's held true here as well. The warm water that's kept them aggressive up and down the coast all summer hasn't broken yet, and the harbor and North Shore rocks are both giving up blues in the 3-to-6-pound range on any incoming bait push. Nahant's outer rocks and the ledges off Gloucester and Cape Ann have both been holding bluefish tight to structure, chopping up pogies and mackerel schools that get pinned against the rocks on the last of the flood. Poppers and metal — Deadly Dicks, Kastmasters, anything in the half-ounce to two-ounce range worked fast over the top of the school — have been the go-to when the blues are up and feeding visibly. When they're not showing on top, a bucktail bounced along the base of the ledge in 10 to 20 feet has been just as effective, sometimes better, because you're not competing with every other angler casting into the same boil.

Flounder are still around on the harbor flats — Deer Island and the flats off Winthrop have been giving up a few keepers on sandworms and clam bellies fished on a simple two-hook flounder rig, best on the slower stages of the tide when the current isn't ripping the bait off bottom. It's not a numbers game right now, more a bonus fish while you're anchored up working bass or bait, but worth a rod in the spread if you're set up on the flats anyway.

Cod and haddock are the harder call this week. The deeper ledges off Cape Ann and out toward Stellwagen have produced sporadically, but with water still warm and no major cooling trend through the water column yet, the bottom bite hasn't turned on in a serious way. If you're making the run out there, treat it as a bonus stop rather than the primary plan until nights start cooling consistently — that shift usually shows up in the codfish first once the thermocline starts to break down.

Looking at the next few days: wind and tide look manageable, nothing disruptive on the horizon, and that stability is actually working against a hard blitz trigger — it takes a real push, usually the first legitimate cool front of the season, to jam bait against structure hard enough to set off sustained surface feeding. We don't have that yet. What we do have is a building moon tightening the tides through the weekend, which should concentrate bait further in the harbor and along the North Shore ledges. If you're picking a window, the outgoing tide at Deer Island in the first two hours of light, or the last of the flood at Nahant's outer rocks in the evening, are the two best bets right now. Keep working live bait and bucktails rather than blind-casting for blitzes — this is a stage-and-wait week, not a run-and-gun one. If the first real cool night hits before next weekend, expect that to be the trigger that turns the staged bass into the fall pattern everyone's waiting for, and Plymouth and Duxbury Bay should start lighting up on the same schedule as the bait pushes south.
