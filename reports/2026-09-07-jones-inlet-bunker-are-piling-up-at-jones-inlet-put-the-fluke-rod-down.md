# Bunker Are Piling Up at Jones Inlet — Put the Fluke Rod Down
*While the fleet keeps drifting Sloop Channel for shorts, the real story this week is bunker stacking at the inlet mouth and the first real bass shadows showing up behind them.*

**FREEPORT, NY — September 7** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Half the boats out of Freeport and Point Lookout are still grinding Sloop Channel for fluke, and I get why — muscle memory, that's been the summer program for three months. But that bite's thinning out fast, and anybody still centered on the bay this week is missing what's happening thirty yards off the beach at the inlet. Bunker are stacking up thick right at the mouth of Jones Inlet, blues are already tearing into them on the surface, and stripers are starting to shadow those pods instead of just picking off stragglers. That's the tell. That's the fall pattern lighting the fuse.

We flagged this exact setup a few days back — said peanut bunker were showing in good numbers along the beachfront and that the building tide would squeeze those schools tighter as the week went on. That's exactly what's happened. The full moon on the 6th and 7th did its job pulling extra water and pushing bait hard against the beach and jetty rocks, and now with the new moon building toward September 10, the tidal flow is only going to get stronger. That's the trigger point. When that new moon tide comes in, the bunker schools get squeezed into tighter, more desperate balls right at the inlet mouth, and that's when this thing turns into a real blitz instead of scattered surface activity.

Right now the blues are doing most of the damage. Boats working the deeper water off the east jetty and the outer bar at Jones Inlet are into aggressive blues in the 3-to-6-pound class, chopping bunker on top most of the last two hours of the outgoing tide. Bunker spoons behind a wire leader, white bucktail teasers, and 2-ounce Ava jigs bounced through the froth are all getting bit. If you're working topwater, a big polystyrene popper worked erratic through a bait ball at first light has been drawing violent strikes — no finesse required right now, blues don't care.

The bass are the more interesting story because they're not fully committed yet. Guys working the rock pile at the east jetty and the deeper cut off Short Beach are picking up stripers in the 28-to-34-inch range mixed in under the bluefish activity, mostly at last light and into the first hour of dark when the blues thin out and the bass move up to work the wounded and dying bunker. A 5-inch white or bunker-colored swimbait on a 1-ounce jighead, or a bunker-imitation darter worked slow across the surface right at dusk, has been the play. Don't waste time trying to out-cast the blitz — work the edges where the bait is getting pushed against structure, because that's where the bigger fish are sitting, letting the blues do the work of breaking up the school.

Inside the bay, fluke are winding down the way they always do this time of year. Sloop Channel is still giving up fish, but it's mostly shorts now with the occasional keeper mixed in on white Gulp paired with a 3/4-ounce bucktail, drifted slow over 18 to 24 feet on the last of the outgoing. If fluke is all you've got time for, get there early — that bite dies fast once the sun climbs and the water starts pushing warm.

The better story in the back bay right now is weakfish. They've been showing up again in decent numbers around the Wantagh Bridge pilings and the deeper holes in South Oyster Bay, which is a nice change from a few years where you barely heard the word mentioned. Bucktails tipped with a strip of squid or a curly-tail grub, fished slow along the bottom on the last two hours of the ebb near the bridge structure, have been picking up weakies in the 16-to-20-inch class. Nothing to build a whole trip around yet, but worth a few casts if you're already in the area working the bridge for bait.

Porgies are still biting steady off the jetty rocks and around the channel edges on sandworms and clam, nothing exciting but reliable if you want dinner. Blackfish are still a few weeks out — the water hasn't cooled enough yet to pull them tight to structure, and most of the tautog specialists are holding off until closer to the season opener.

Looking ahead, the next few days are about watching that tide build toward the new moon on the 10th. As the tidal push strengthens, expect those bunker schools at the inlet to get squeezed tighter and the blitz activity to intensify — that's the widely expected turn where this stops being scattered surface chop and turns into the real thing, birds working, bass mixed in heavy with the blues. Best windows continue to be the last two hours of the ebb and the hour around dusk, when the bait gets funneled out through the inlet mouth past the jetty. If you can only get out one evening this week, make it Thursday or Friday as the tide builds — and don't be afraid to leave the fluke rod in the truck.
