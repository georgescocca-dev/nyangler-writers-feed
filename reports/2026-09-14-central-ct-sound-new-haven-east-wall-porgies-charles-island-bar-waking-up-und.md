# New Haven East Wall Porgies, Charles Island Bar Waking Up Underneath
*Bottom bite stayed steady on the rockpiles this week while bunker pods pulled the first real striper interest into the harbor mouths.*

**NEW HAVEN, CT — September 14** — _by Lena Marchetti, Zone Writer_

Porgies to 14 inches came over the rail off the New Haven Harbor east breakwall most mornings this week, stacked up tight to the rock on the last two hours of the incoming, mixed in with keeper sea bass running 13 to 15 inches. That's the read from the regulars working the wall with high-low rigs and squid strips — small hooks, size 4 to 6, because the porgies have been nipping short on anything bigger. Nothing spectacular, nothing to write home about size-wise, but it's been a dependable bite, and dependable is worth something in September.

The bigger story is what's stacking up underneath and around that structure bite. Kings Point is reading 70.9 and New London's at 69.4, which tells you the western half of the Sound is still holding a touch more warmth than the east end — that gradient matters right now because it's exactly the kind of setup that keeps bait tight to structure instead of scattering. Peanut bunker and silversides have been thickening up around the river mouths from the Housatonic down through the Hammonasset flats, and where bait concentrates, stripers start paying attention. We flagged this last week — said the fall pattern would build through the Sound as bunker schools tightened up — and that's exactly what's showing now. The bass aren't blitzing yet, but there's real interest building at Charles Island, not just stragglers picking at the edges.

The Charles Island bar has been the one to watch. On the last hour of the flood into the top of the tide, working the sandbar with 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, or swimming a 5-inch white paddletail through the deeper edge where the bar drops into the channel, has been picking up schoolie bass in the 22 to 26-inch range with the occasional better fish mixed in. It's not consistent enough yet to call it a program — more like two good windows out of five trips — but the trend line is pointing the right way as bunker keep funneling past the island on their way out of the harbor systems.

Falkner Island continues to be the more honest blackfish spot right now. The tautog bite there has been slow to build compared to a normal September, but the rockpiles on the north and east sides are starting to give up fish in the 16 to 18-inch class on green crab, fished tight to the structure on the bottom of the tide when current slacks enough to hold bottom without a ton of lead. Patience is the name of the game out there — two or three solid bites in a morning, not a parade, but the fish are legitimate and the pattern should tighten as water eases toward the 60s over the next few weeks.

Stratford Shoal has been more of a mixed bag. Sea bass are there in decent numbers on the humps in 25 to 40 feet, taking Gulp swimming mullets and clam strips on bucktail jigs worked slow off the bottom, but the porgy action out there has been spottier than the harbor walls — likely because that structure is more exposed and gets pushed around more by wind and current than the protected breakwall stretches. If you're making the run out to the Shoal, plan around slack tide or the first hour of either direction; anything with real current running has been tough to hold bottom on out there this week.

Milford Gulf has quietly been one of the better porgy producers, with fish running a little bigger than the harbor average — several in the 13 to 15-inch range coming off small pieces of clam on the bottom during the last two hours of the ebb, when the current funnels bait through that channel structure. Worth a look if the harbor gets crowded on a nice weekend morning, which it has been.

Bluefish have shown up as a wildcard rather than a headline. Small to mid-size blues, mostly 2 to 4 pounds, have been slashing through peanut bunker pods near the mouths of the Housatonic and the harbor entrance at dawn, and a metal lip swimmer or a simple diamond jig worked fast through the surface commotion has been good for a few fish before they move off. It's not the kind of sustained blitz you're seeing further west and out toward Montauk, but it's a sign the same bait movement pulling bass interest is also waking up the bluefish.

Looking at the next several days, the setup favors continued incremental improvement rather than any sudden switch flipping. Tidal range is building toward the upcoming moon, which should tighten up current on the bars and rockpiles and make both the porgy/sea bass bite and the emerging striper pattern at Charles Island more consistent. If the wind stays out of the north or northwest and holds off from stirring things up, the better window over the next few days looks like the last two hours of the flood at Charles Island for bass, and the top of the incoming on the New Haven breakwalls for porgies and sea bass. Blackfish guys should keep an eye on Falkner Island as water continues easing — that bite typically strengthens through late September into October, and there's no reason to think this year breaks that pattern.
