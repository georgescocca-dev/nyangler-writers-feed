# Niantic Fleet Loading Porgy Boxes at Black Point While the Albie Scouts Work Fishers Island Sound
*Bottom bite is the bread and butter right now, but the party boat radio chatter is all about what might show up on the troll by the weekend.*

**NIANTIC, CT — September 14** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Talk to any mate coming off the Niantic docks this week and you get the same rundown before you even ask: porgies are stacked, blackfish are cooperating, and everybody's got one eye on the horizon waiting for the albies to show up in numbers. That's the state of the eastern Sound heading into the back half of September — a solid, honest bottom bite carrying the fleet while the fall run builds underneath it.

Water's cooled off enough to matter. New London is reading 69.4 degrees, and that's a real number when you stack it against what's happening west of us — Kings Point up around 70.9. That gradient matters because it tells you the eastern Sound is a few days ahead on the seasonal clock. Bait's already thinking about leaving, and the fish that eat bait are starting to think the same thing. We flagged this exact pattern last week — said the fluke bite would keep tapering as the water eased and fish started sliding toward deeper water, and that's exactly what the drift boats are seeing out of Niantic Bay right now. Fluke are still there, but the counts are down from August, and the fish that are biting are running smaller, holding tighter to the deeper edges of the bay and out toward the mouth rather than spread across the flats. If you want one more good fluke trip before they thin out, work 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, slow-dragged in 20 to 28 feet off the Niantic Bay channel edges on the last two hours of the incoming. That window's closing, though — give it two more weeks, tops.

Where the energy really is right now is on structure. Black Point has been the porgy factory all week — 25 to 35 feet of water, clam and squid strips on standard porgy rigs, and the boats are limiting out on scup running a pound to a pound and a half with some bigger ones mixed in. The bite's best on the top of the tide through the first couple hours of the ebb, when the current's still got some push to it but hasn't gone slack. Blackfish are riding shotgun on the same trips — green crab and Asian crab worked tight to the rock piles at Twotree Island Channel and around Hole-in-the-Wall are producing keeper tautog in the 3-to-6-pound range, with the occasional 8-pounder showing up to remind everybody why this fishery is worth the crab money. That structure bite should only get better as the water keeps easing into fall — blackfish love this transition, and the eastern Sound's rock piles are about to become the most reliable thing on the board.

Striped bass are doing exactly what we said they'd start doing. Bunker schools are tightening up along the shoreline from McCook Point through Niantic Bay, and the bass are showing real interest now instead of just picking at stragglers on the edges. It's not a blitz yet — nobody's calling it that — but liveline a bunker or work a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig near the river mouth and structure around McCook Point on the last of the outgoing, and there's enough bass around to make it worth the rods. Bluefish are mixed into that same bait, and they've been the more aggressive player so far — schools of peanut bunker pushing through the approaches to Fishers Island Sound have blues slashing on top in the early morning, easy pickings on top-water plugs or a simple diamond jig if you can find the surface activity before the sun gets high.

The albie question is the one everybody at the dock is asking. The push has been working west out of Rhode Island water, and the eastern approaches — Fishers Island Sound, the deeper rips off Twotree — are the logical next stop as that bait continues funneling this direction. Nothing concentrated has shown up inside our zone yet, but the ingredients are lining up: cooling water, bait getting squeezed into the corridors, and a fishery that's been building steadily to our east. If you're rigged for it, running an epoxy jig or small Deadly Dick off the back of a porgy trip near the Fishers Island Sound approaches isn't a bad way to spend twenty minutes between drifts.

Looking ahead, the next few days should hold mostly stable — no major blow in the forecast to stir things up, which is good news for the bottom bite staying consistent at Black Point and the tog spots. Tidal range starts building again as we push toward the next moon, and that usually means more current push through Twotree and Hole-in-the-Wall, which tends to switch blackfish and porgies on harder during the tide changes. If there's only one day to fish this week, make it a morning tide change at Black Point for porgies and tog, then keep a rod rigged with a bunker chunk near McCook Point on the way back in — that's where the fall bass pattern is starting to show its teeth.
