# Monhegan to Matinicus: pollock thick on the ledges, bass tucked into cool coves
*Forty years of late August on Penobscot Bay says the pattern holds until the nights turn cold — this week did nothing to argue otherwise.*

**STONINGTON, ME — August 27** — _by Rusty Pelletier, Zone Writer_

Ask any old-timer around Stonington what August looks like on Penobscot Bay and he'll tell you the same thing every year without missing a beat: the mackerel show first, the bass tuck into the coves and river mouths, and the groundfish sit tight on the ledges until the water finally cools enough to move them. Forty years of hauling traps and running charter trips out of these harbors hasn't changed that rhythm one bit, and this week proved it again. While the coast south of Cape Cod is dealing with warm-water stress pushing bass into structure and night programs, Midcoast Maine is sitting comfortable on the northern edge of all that — we flagged this last week, said conditions here would hold steady while the south cooked in it, and that's exactly what happened. Water temps in the bay and around the islands stayed in the range that keeps fish honest and predictable rather than lockjawed.

Striped bass have been working the classic late-summer program — river mouths, cove edges, and the mud flats where warm outflow meets cooler bay water. The Damariscotta and Bagaduce river mouths have both been giving up fish on the last two hours of the outgoing tide, with bass in the 24 to 32 inch range holding in 8 to 15 feet of water where the current pinches down. Live mackerel on a simple three-way rig has been the most consistent producer, but a white or bone RonZ worked slow along bottom on the drop has taken its share too, especially in the hour around dead low when the fish are less committed to chasing and more willing to eat something presented right in their face. Boothbay Harbor's inner coves have been holding smaller schoolies on the incoming, mostly a light-tackle affair with soft plastics and topwater walking baits at first light before the sun gets up and pushes them down.

Mackerel have been thick around Eastern Egg Rock, same as they've been all summer, and that's been doing double duty — feeding the puffins the birders come out to see and feeding the bass and bluefish that shadow the schools. Sabiki rigs and small Diamond jigs worked in 20 to 40 feet on the tide change have been filling coolers fast, and that fresh mackerel is exactly what's been going down on those bass rigs upriver. Bluefish have been mixed in with the mackerel schools around Egg Rock and off the Cuckolds, nothing huge, mostly 2 to 4 pound choppers, but enough of them to keep rods bent for anyone jigging bucktails through the bait.

The groundfish story is where this zone earns its keep this time of year, and Monhegan's ledges have been holding up their end. Pollock have been thick on top, busting bait right on the surface around the eastern side of the island on the last of the ebb, and a diamond jig or a big soft plastic swimbait worked through the boils has been good for fish in the 8 to 15 pound class, occasionally bigger. Drop below that, and cod have been coming off the deeper structure in 100 to 150 feet, mostly on cut mackerel or clams fished tight to bottom on a slow drift. Haddock have been mixed in on some of the deeper humps out toward Matinicus, though that fishery stays spottier than the pollock and cod — some days the fleet does well, other days it's a lot of jigging for not much to show. Isle au Haut's channels and the deeper water off Vinalhaven have both been producing pollock and the occasional cod for boats willing to work the tide change carefully, since the current runs hard enough through those cuts that timing matters more than location.

Wind's been the cooperative kind this week, mostly out of the southwest in the 8 to 12 knot range, which keeps the bay workable without kicking up the kind of chop that shuts down jigging over the ledges. That pattern looks to hold through the next couple of days, which means the pollock bite off Monhegan should stay dependable on the ebb, and the bass should keep working the same river-mouth program on the outgoing tide morning and evening. The moon's building toward full over the next week, and when that happens the tidal swings sharpen up — worth watching the river mouths a little closer as the current gets more current, because bass tend to stack tighter against structure when the water's really moving rather than spreading out over the flats.

Looking a few days out, there's no sign yet of the fall push starting up here — that's a mid-September story once the nights get properly cold and the bay starts shedding heat in earnest. For now this is a summer holding pattern, stable and readable, which after forty years on this water is about the best thing anyone can ask for in late August. If there's one day to plan around this week, make it the morning after the wind lays down following any afternoon southwesterly — clean water and a settling tide off Monhegan will put the pollock back on top where the jigs can reach them, and the bass will still be waiting in the coves when the boat comes back in.
