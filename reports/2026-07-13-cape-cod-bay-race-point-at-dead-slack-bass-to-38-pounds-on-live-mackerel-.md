# Race Point at dead slack: bass to 38 pounds on live mackerel, cod stubborn on Billingsgate
*The rip gave up a good hour of big fish this week, but you had to be sitting on it when the tide stopped moving.*

**PROVINCETOWN, MA — July 13** — _by Donny Costa, Zone Writer_

Tuesday morning, six of us on the boat, and I had my hand on the wheel watching the current line off Race Point go from a hard push to nothing in about fifteen minutes. That's the window. I've fished that rip long enough to know the bass don't care about the tide running — they care about the moment it quits. We had three rods out, live mackerel on 5/0 circle hooks, no weight, just letting them swim naturally off the stern as we drifted the seam where the green water meets the darker water pushing off the Peaked Hill Bars. Right at slack, rod number two just about got yanked out of the holder. Forty-five minutes later we had a 38-pounder in the box and two more over thirty that we let swim. That's the kind of morning that keeps you coming back to that piece of water for three decades.

The mackerel showed up thick again this week, which tells you a lot about why the bass are stacking where they are. I've seen bait balls off Race Point holding tight to the rip since late June, and the big fish know exactly where that structure funnels the bait when the tide turns. If you're jigging mackerel for yourself — small diamond jigs, no bait needed, they'll hit bare metal — do it early, before the boats pile up on the spot. Once you've got a well full of mackerel, that's your ticket for the afternoon slack.

Peaked Hill Bars has been more of a bluefish show the last ten days. Choppers in the six to nine pound range, blitzing on the surface some mornings when the light's still low and the water's calm enough to see them working. Poppers and metal — Hammered Kastmasters, or a bucktail with a swimming plastic if the blues are being finicky — worked fast across the top gets bit. It's not subtle fishing. When they're up, you can see the whole thing happen, gulls diving, water exploding, and it doesn't last more than twenty minutes before they push off to deeper water. I wouldn't plan a whole trip around it, but if you're out there anyway working the bass rip, keep a popper rigged and ready.

Billingsgate has been tougher than I'd like to admit. Cod fishing in July on that structure is never a sure thing — the water up top warms and the fish that are left slide down into the deeper holes off the shoal, 60 to 80 feet, where it stays cooler. We've been jigging diamond jigs and skirted bucktails tipped with clam, working them slow off the bottom on the drift, and getting maybe two or three keeper cod a trip where in the spring we'd have had a dozen. That's just where we are in the season. If cod is your main target right now, I'd rather send you to the deeper structure north of Billingsgate toward the edge of the Bay than the shoal itself — the fish that are still around this time of year want the depth and the cooler water, not the shallower rubble.

Fluke fishing off Sandy Neck and out toward the flats by Barnstable and Brewster has actually been the steadiest thing going. Sandy bottom, six to fourteen feet on the drift, working white Gulp swimming mullets on three-quarter ounce bucktails, and we've had a good run of keepers in the eighteen to twenty-two inch range, with a few pushing four pounds mixed in. That bite likes the last two hours of the outgoing, when the water's pulling bait off the flats and funneling it into the deeper guts. Slow drag, don't work it too fast — fluke on that bottom want it crawling, not swimming.

Overall I'd call it a mixed week. The bass bite at Race Point and Peaked Hill was legitimately good when you hit the slack right, but it's a tight window, maybe forty minutes on either side of the tide change, and if you show up mid-tide with the current still ripping, you're better off jigging mackerel for bait than expecting a bass to eat. The cod fishing has thinned out the way it always does by mid-July, and anybody telling you Billingsgate is stacked right now isn't being straight with you. The fluke bite has been the most dependable thing on the calendar, day in and day out, which is usually how it goes once the water settles into summer pattern.

Looking ahead, the moon's working toward the next quarter, which should tighten up the tide swings a little and might actually help the Race Point slack bite — shorter, sharper tide changes sometimes concentrate the bait better than the big moon tides do. If the wind stays out of the southwest like it has been, the water off Provincetown should stay clean enough to sight the mackerel schools from the surface, which makes locating the bass a lot easier than blind-drifting. I'd plan around the morning and evening slacks at Race Point this coming week, keep a jig rod rigged for mackerel to restock the well, and treat the cod fishing as a bonus rather than the plan. If you've only got one trip in you, that's where I'd put it.
