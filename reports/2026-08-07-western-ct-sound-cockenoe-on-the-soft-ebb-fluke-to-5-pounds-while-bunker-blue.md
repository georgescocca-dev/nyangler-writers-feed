# Cockenoe on the Soft Ebb: Fluke to 5 Pounds While Bunker Blues Bust Off Greenwich Point
*With the current gone slack under this neap moon, I pulled off Penfield's rockpile and let Cockenoe's deeper edges do the work — and the fluke followed.*

**NORWALK, CT — August 7** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Every week this time of year I have to make the same call — Penfield or Cockenoe — and this week Cockenoe won, easy. Here's the thinking. We're sitting in a neap tide right now, the current soft and lazy on both ends of the swing, and Penfield Reef needs some push to work right. That reef fires when the ebb is ripping hard enough to pin bait against the rockpile and give blackfish and porgies something to fight against. Soft water off Penfield just means fish sitting tight to structure, not feeding aggressively, and boat traffic drifting through with no drift to speak of. Cockenoe, on the other hand, has enough depth variation — that 20 to 35 foot break running off the northeast corner — that fluke will still slide and feed even when the current's barely moving. So that's where I put my time, and it paid.

Fished the last two hours of the ebb Tuesday and again Thursday morning, working 4-inch chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along that break where the reef drops into the channel. Boated four keeper fluke both trips, biggest going just over 5 pounds Thursday, plus a pile of shorts that needed measuring twice. The bite wasn't a blitz — you had to cover water and hit the drop repeatedly — but it was steady enough that I never doubted the spot. With the current this soft, downsizing the jig head mattered. A full ounce just sat there; the 3/4 let the plastic breathe on bottom without plowing.

The real story this week, though, is the bunker. We flagged this coming — said the peanut pods would spread and the cocktail blues would follow — and that's exactly what's happening off Greenwich Point and around the Cows. Kings Point is reading 74.8 degrees, a good three degrees cooler than what's showing up toward New London, and that west-to-east gradient is keeping the bunker schools stacked tight on our end of the Sound instead of scattering east. I watched adult bunker balled up off the Point Wednesday evening with blues underneath punishing them — surface explosions, birds diving, the whole show. If you can get out there in that last hour of light, a topwater popper or a bunker spoon thrown right into the boil will get bit almost every cast. Just don't idle through the school on the way in — that's the fastest way to shut it down for the next boat.

Sea bass and porgies are holding true to form on the Cows and around Sheffield Island structure, 25 to 40 feet, high-low rigs with squid strips and clam. Nothing spectacular, but it's dependable — the kind of bite you build a half-day trip around when the fluke aren't cooperating or the wind's wrong for the reef. Sea bass are running mixed sizes, plenty of shorts mixed with keepers, so bring the ruler and don't assume every bend of the rod is a fish worth the cooler.

Striper fishing has gone fully nocturnal, which is normal for early August with surface temps sitting in the mid-70s across most of the western Sound. Daytime, they're down deep on structure — the drop-off edges around Sheffield Island and the rock piles off Greenwich Point — and not real interested in much. After dark is a different animal. Anglers working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs off the Norwalk Islands are picking up stripers in the 28 to 34 inch range on the last two hours of the incoming, right as bait gets pushed up against the island edges. If you're going to make the trip, that tide window is worth building the whole night around — showing up two hours too early just means sitting on dead water.

Blackfish is technically fair game but I'm not putting real effort in yet — that bite doesn't turn on with any consistency until the water starts cooling in September and the current builds back up. A few guys are picking at them opportunistically on the reef structure, but I wouldn't plan a trip around it this week.

Boat traffic on the weekends has been what you'd expect for early August — thick around the Norwalk Islands mooring fields and stacked up on Cockenoe by midday Saturday. My move has been getting out before 7 AM to fish the tail of the overnight tide before the parade starts, then switching to the Cows or deeper Sheffield Island water in the afternoon where the pleasure boats don't bother anchoring.

Looking ahead, this neap phase should start building back toward the new moon around the 12th, and once that current firms up, I expect Penfield to come back into play — that reef wants push, and it'll get it. If the bunker stay thick off the Point through the weekend and the weather holds calm, I'd keep chasing those blues in low light. The fluke bite on Cockenoe should hold steady regardless of tide strength given the depth variety out there, but I'd watch for the bite to sharpen noticeably once real current returns midweek.
