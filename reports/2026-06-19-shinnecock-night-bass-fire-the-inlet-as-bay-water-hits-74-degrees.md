# Night bass fire the inlet as bay water hits 74 degrees
*Outgoing tides after midnight producing keeper stripers on live peanuts and plugs.*

**SHINNECOCK INLET, NY — June 19** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The night bite is absolutely on fire right now. Water temps in the bay hit 74 degrees this week — a solid 6-degree premium over the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is stacking bait on every flood tide and holding bass on the ebb. I've been working the inlet from 11 PM to 3 AM, and the action starts the moment that tide turns.

Best action is coming on the outgoing, especially that first hour when the current really gets moving. Bass are staged right at the inlet mouth in 15 to 25 feet, ambushing peanut bunker and sand eels getting swept out of the bay. Live peanuts on a fishfinder rig are absolutely deadly — 3/0 circle hook, 18 inches of 30-pound fluoro, fished tight to the jetty rocks on the south side. I'm getting hits within minutes of the bait hitting bottom.

For artificials, the Daiwa SP Minnow in bunker pattern has been my go-to plug. Cast it up-current and let it swing through the rip on a slow retrieve. The bass are hitting it hard right in that transition zone where the bay water meets the ocean. Had three keepers Tuesday night between 1 AM and 2:30 AM, all on that plug.

The weakfish are starting to show at Ponquogue Bridge too. Nothing huge yet — mostly 14 to 16 inchers — but they're there. Pink Deadly Dick with a teaser rig is producing on the incoming tide around the bridge pilings. Fish it slow, almost dead-sticking it in the current.

Fluke action in the bay has been solid but not spectacular. The better fish are coming from the deeper holes near the canal entrance in 25 to 30 feet. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are the ticket, dragged slow on the drift. Had a nice 22-incher yesterday morning on the incoming tide, plus several shorts. The key is staying mobile — these fish are scattered, not schooled up tight.

What's really driving this bite is the water temperature spread. That 74-degree bay water versus 67-degree ocean water is creating a feeding highway right through the inlet. Every tide change concentrates baitfish in that mixing zone, and the bass know it. The southwest winds we've had this week are also helping, pushing surface water and bait toward the inlet on the flood.

Bait situation is excellent. Peanut bunker are thick in the bay, especially around the marinas in Hampton Bays. Sand eels are showing in good numbers too, particularly on the outgoing tide. I'm seeing birds working consistently from Tiana Beach west to the inlet — always a good sign.

One thing to watch: the new moon is Friday, which means we're building toward spring tides. That extra current flow should really fire up the inlet bite over the weekend. Plan to be there for the first two hours of the outgoing, especially the late-night tides. The bass seem most active between midnight and 3 AM when boat traffic dies down.

Looking ahead, water temps should hold steady or climb slightly as we move deeper into summer. The thermal gradient between bay and ocean is the key — as long as that 5-degree spread holds, the inlet will keep producing. Weekend warriors should focus on the night tides, but early morning can produce too if you're there right at first light when the tide starts to ebb.
