# Cape May Rips Grade: C-Minus — Fluke and Triggers Save the Week
*Summer water pushed the rip bass deep and cranky, but the inlet fluke and the first triggerfish of the season kept rods bent.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — July 13** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

If I'm handing out grades this week, the Rips get a C-minus. That's not me being cranky for the sake of it — that's just where they're at in mid-July. The bass are there, but they're not stupid, and they're not shallow. Water off Cape May Point has settled into that heavy, warm summer stack, and every year around this time the stripers that were slashing bait on top in May and June slide down and get lockjawed and lazy. You can still find them, but you're working for every fish, not picking them off a boil.

Best window I had all week was the top of the outgoing at the Point, first light, before the boat traffic stirred everything up. Ran bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs down in 35 to 45 feet where the rip line sets up hard against the shoal, and picked at three keepers over two mornings — one a solid 28-incher that ate on the second drop. That's the pattern right now: get there before the sun's fully up, fish the strongest push of the tide, and don't expect a repeat performance an hour later. By 8 a.m. the bite shuts like someone flipped a switch. If you're trolling bunker spoons or umbrella rigs through there midday, you're burning gas for the memory of it.

The good news — and the reason this week wasn't a total wipeout — is Hereford Inlet. Fluke fishing has actually been solid, not spectacular, but honest. Drifting the channel edges on the outgoing, 25 to 35 feet, with 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullet in chartreuse, and we've been putting keepers in the box most trips. Nothing over 4 pounds, but steady action, the kind where you're not sitting on your hands between fish. Same pattern working off Wildwood and down toward the Cape May Canal mouth — anywhere there's current pulling bait off the flats into deeper water, the fluke are stacked up waiting for it.

Bluefish have shown up in patches along the beachfront at Cape May Point, mostly at dawn, chopper-sized, blitzing peanut bunker pushed tight to the wash. If you catch that window — and it's a short one, twenty, thirty minutes some mornings — poppers and 1-ounce metal will get bit on nearly every cast. Miss it and you're just watching gulls work water that's already gone quiet.

Now, the triggerfish. First real reports of the season came off the reef sites — Cape May Reef and the Wildwood Reef — guys bottom fishing clam and squid strips over structure in 60 to 80 feet finding a few keeper triggers mixed in with the sea bass. It's early yet, this fishery really turns on in August, but the first ones showing up now tells me the water's holding steady and warm enough offshore to bring them up onto the wrecks. Worth a look if you've got a boat that can reach the reef sites and don't mind soaking bait patiently — triggers aren't a numbers game, they're a persistence game.

On weakfish — I'll be straight with you, that fishery isn't what it used to be around here. We're not seeing the tiderunners my father used to talk about working the Bay in the old days. What we are getting is the occasional legal weakfish mixed into the fluke drift in the back bay channels at last light, usually on the same bucktail-and-Gulp combo. If you're targeting them specifically, you're going to be disappointed. If you're fishing fluke and one shows up in the mix, consider it a bonus.

And yes, before anyone asks — the Delaware Bay black drum run is done. That's a May fishery, and it wrapped up weeks ago. Fortescue and the Brandywine Shoal area are quiet on that front now; what's left up the Bay this time of year is a scattering of resident stripers around structure and current breaks, nothing worth a dedicated trip. If you're still hearing chatter about drum from somebody at the dock, they're talking about last season or they're confused.

Looking ahead, I don't expect the Rips to open up much until we get a stretch of cooler nights or a wind shift that mixes the water column — that's usually late July into August around here. Until then, my play is early mornings on the strongest tide push, fluke in the inlet on the outgoing, and keeping an eye on the reef sites as the triggerfish bite builds through the month. If you've only got one morning to fish, I'd put it on the Hereford Inlet fluke drift over the Rips right now — it's the more honest bet. But if you want a shot at a bass, first light at the Point on the top of the outgoing is still your best window, even if it's a short one.
