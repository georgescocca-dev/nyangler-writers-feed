# Bay water pushing 79° sends the fluke running for the inlet
*Great South Bay's gone soup-warm, so the doormats and the stripers both slid toward moving water — here's where to find them on the new moon spring tides.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 10** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay basically turned into bathwater. We've been sitting up around 78-79 degrees in the shallower stretches off Sayville and Patchogue, and that's too warm for a fluke to sit comfortable on a mud flat all day. What happened is exactly what happens every summer once the bay crosses that threshold — the doormats packed up and moved to where the water moves. Robert Moses bridges, the Fire Island Inlet channel, Democrat Point rip — anywhere current keeps things stirred and a few degrees cooler. I had a morning last Thursday on the outgoing where the bay-side flats gave me nothing for two hours, ran down to the inlet, and boated four keepers in forty minutes. That's not luck, that's fish behavior. Water finds its comfort zone and takes the bait with it.

This week the moon's swinging new on the 13th, which means we're building into spring tides right now — bigger swings, more water moving in and out of that inlet on every cycle. That's good news and bad news. Good because it flushes more bait through the cuts and sharpens the current lines fluke and bass like to sit on. Bad because if you're not paying attention to the stage of tide, you'll either be fighting too much push to hold bottom or sitting there at slack wondering where everybody went. Winds look manageable through the weekend, nothing that should tear up the surf too bad, and the upwelled ocean water sitting around 71 degrees off the beach is a real gift right now — noticeably cooler and cleaner than what's cooking in the back bay. That temperature break right at the inlet mouth is exactly why the action's concentrated there instead of spread out across the whole system.

Fluke fishing has been the story of the week, and it's decent, not lights-out. Best drifts are still the classic ones — the Robert Moses bridge pilings out to about 28-32 feet, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, and don't be shy about tipping with a strip of squid when the current's ripping on a big outgoing, it holds up better than bait alone in that flow. Democrat Point drop-off has been giving up better average size — guys are picking through shorts but the keepers are running 4 to 6 pounds, mostly on chartreuse bucktail-and-Gulp combos bounced slow along the sand-to-rock transition. Timing matters here: the two hours around the top of the outgoing has been the sweet spot, when the bait gets funneled out of the bay and everything with fins lines up to eat.

Stripers have been a night bite mostly, which tracks with how warm everything's gotten. We're getting some daytime action right at first light at the inlet on the last of the outgoing — bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs fished tight to the rocks at Democrat Point picking up fish in the 20s to high-20s, occasional better fish mixed in. But the real window's after dark. Guys working topwater plugs and darters along the surf at Cedar Beach and the Fire Island beaches east of the lighthouse are connecting after sundown into the new moon build. With spring tides stacking up through the 11th to 13th, that late incoming right at dusk pushes clean water and bait right up against the beach — that's your window if you're willing to walk out there with a headlamp and patience.

Bluefish have shown up in decent numbers chopping bait on top around the inlet on the morning outgoing — nothing giant, mostly 2 to 4 pounds, but they'll save a slow morning. Metal — Deadly Dicks or diamond jigs — worked fast through the surface commotion gets bit fast. Weakfish, I'll be honest, remain a non-event. Water's been plenty warm enough for them for over two weeks now and I haven't heard a single solid report out of the bay or the inlet. That old rule of thumb about weakfish showing once water crosses 70 just hasn't held up the last couple seasons, and this year's no different. I wouldn't plan a trip around them right now.

Worth keeping an eye on — there's early bonito and Spanish mackerel activity showing on the ocean side of inlets to our east, and with clean incoming water pushing through Fire Island Inlet on the right stage, it wouldn't shock me if we start seeing some show up right off the jetty at Democrat Point in the next week or two. If you're out there working bucktails for fluke and something small and fast rips line off a light spinning rod, don't assume it's a snapper blue.

Looking ahead — I'd fish the building spring tide through the weekend. Outgoing water at the inlet in the early morning for fluke and blues, then swing back after dark for the striper window along the beaches as that new moon tide pushes bait tight to the sand at dusk. If the bay-side bite stays dead from the heat, which I expect it will, don't fight it — follow the moving water. That's the whole story of this stretch: comfortable fish go where the current keeps things livable, and right now that's the inlet, full stop.
