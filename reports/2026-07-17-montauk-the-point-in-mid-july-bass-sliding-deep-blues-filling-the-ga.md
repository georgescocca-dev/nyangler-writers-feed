# The Point in Mid-July: Bass Sliding Deep, Blues Filling the Gap, Bluefin Knocking at the Door
*The Lighthouse rip has gone quiet on top but the boulder field is still holding fish for those willing to work the bottom third of the water column.*

**MONTAUK, NY — July 17** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

Mid-July at the Point is its own animal. This isn't the frenzy of October, and it isn't the first push of resident bass off the beaches in May. This is the lull that separates the two — the stretch where the Point tests whether you actually know how to read it or whether you were just along for the ride during the easy months. I've fished this window my whole life and it never gets easier. It just gets more honest.

The bass are here. They just aren't where the tourists expect them. With Montauk water sitting around 70 degrees, the big resident fish have slid off the visible boils on North Bar and dropped into the deeper cuts of the boulder field, thirty to forty-five feet down, tight to structure on the last two hours of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing at the Lighthouse. I've been dragging a 1.5-ounce bucktail — white with a chunk of pork rind or a 5-inch white Gulp trailer — across the current edge where the rip breaks off the rocks, letting it sink clean past the seam before working it slow along bottom. That's where the take comes. Long-bombing into the open water where the surface looks angry is a young man's game right now; the fish have moved down and in, and the presentation has to follow them.

Shagwong Reef has been more honest than the Point itself the last two weeks. Slot fish in the 28-to-34-inch range, with the occasional overslot mixed in, holding on the drop-off in eighteen to twenty-five feet. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig, weight just heavy enough to hold bottom on the outgoing, have been the most consistent producer — not flashy, but it's meat and potatoes fishing that's put fish in the boat for guys willing to sit on the anchor through a slow tide change. Eels after dark off the same structure have accounted for the better fish, including a 41-pounder a friend of mine stuck two nights ago working the rip on the last of the ebb.

Bluefish have filled the void where the bass thinned out on top, and I mean real bluefish — six, seven, eight pounds, tackle-busting fish that will shred a bucktail trailer in one pass. They're running the surface at Turtle Cove and off the North Bar in the early morning, and if you want to have some fun with light tackle, poppers and pencils worked fast through the froth will get hit within a few casts. Just bring wire or heavy fluoro leader, because these blues aren't shy about cutting you off.

Fluke fishing has been the honest workhorse of the week. Drifting the flats off Frisbees Dock and out toward the Elbow in twenty-eight to thirty-five feet, white bucktails tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullets have taken fish to five and six pounds on the moving water, both stages of the tide but noticeably better on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current sweeps bait off the sandy patches near the mussel beds. It's not a doormat parade — you're culling through a lot of shorts to find keepers — but there are fish there if you keep the boat moving and stay disciplined about drifting structure edges instead of soaking in one spot.

Porgies at Shagwong and inside Fort Pond Bay have been steady on the bottom, small hooks and clam or squid strips getting bit within minutes of anchoring in fifteen to twenty feet. Nothing glamorous, but it's a good afternoon for kids and it fills a cooler when the bigger stuff is being stubborn.

The story that's got my attention for the coming weeks is offshore of all this — bluefin, schoolie class, pushing south of the Point closer than they've been in years at this point in the season. Small boats have been running out and connecting on their first tuna of the year, working bird activity and slicks a few miles off the Light. That's not something I'd have written about in a Montauk column a decade ago, but the bait's been thick enough and the water clean enough that these fish are showing earlier and closer than the old patterns suggest. Worth keeping an eye on if you've got a center console and a window of calm water — but I wouldn't build a whole trip around it yet. It's a bonus fish right now, not a program.

What's really going to change the complexion of the Point in the next couple weeks is what's building to our east. Squid are stacking up hard around Block Island and into Nantucket Sound as that water climbs into the high 50s and 60s in the protected coves, and that's the trigger that historically supercharges the rip bite here. When those squid schools push west and start showing in the Montauk rips — usually late July into early August — the bass that have been sulking deep in the boulder field come back up and get aggressive again, especially on the building tide at North Bar and off the Light itself.

If I had one day this week, I'd fish the Lighthouse rip on the last hour of the incoming into the first of the outgoing, working a bucktail deep and slow, and I'd have a bunker chunk rig ready as backup for Shagwong if the top-water bite doesn't materialize. This is a patient man's week at the Point. The fish are here. You just have to go down and get them.
