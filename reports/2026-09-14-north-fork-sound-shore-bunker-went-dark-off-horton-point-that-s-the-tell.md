# Bunker Went Dark Off Horton Point — That's the Tell
*The bass are stacking on bait, not stragglers, and the blues right behind them look like the real fall push.*

**MATTITUCK, NY — September 14** — _by Pat "North Fork" Reilly, Zone Writer_

Old Ted Kowalski used to stand on the bulkhead at Mattituck Inlet with his coffee and tell you the whole season by the color of the bunker's back. Silver and skittish, he'd say, means the blues haven't found them yet. Dark and oily, rolling instead of running — that means something's pushing them from behind. Ted's been gone eleven years, but his rule held up again this week. The bunker coming past Horton Point Tuesday and Wednesday were rolling, not running, packed tight and low in the water, and by Thursday the gulls were working them hard enough that you could hear it from the bluff parking lot.

That's the tell we've been waiting on. We flagged it last week — said the new-moon tide was going to tighten the bait through the Sound and the bass would start showing real interest instead of just picking at stragglers — and that's exactly what's setting up now. The water's got a real gradient to it this week, warmer down at the west end near Kings Point pushing 71, cooling off as you come east toward New London in the high 60s. That kind of stair-step keeps bait funneling east along the North Fork shoreline instead of scattering, and it's why the bunker schools have been holding tight to structure at Horton Point and Rocky Point rather than spreading thin across open water.

Striped bass are on those schools now, and the pattern is textbook early fall — fish shadowing the pods, not chasing them, sitting just off the edges waiting for something to get separated. The better boats have been drifting live bunker on a fish-finder rig off Horton Point on the last two hours of the ebb, letting the bait swim naturally down-current into the deeper water off the point. Snag-and-drop is working too when the schools bunch up tight enough to hook one clean. Sizes have been mixed — plenty of schoolies in the 20s mixed with slot fish in the low 30s, and a few boats reporting fish pushing 38, 40 inches worked out of the deeper cuts off Rocky Point on bucktails cast into the edges of the bait. It's not a blown-open bite yet. It's a building one. Worth noting the difference.

Bluefish are the sharper story right now. Montauk weighed in an 18-plus pound blue this week, and that kind of size class doesn't show up alone — it usually means a serious wave of big fall choppers working bunker from the ocean side in through the Race. We're starting to see the leading edge of that at Plum Gut approach and off Orient Point, blues in the 8 to 12 pound range mixed into the bass schools, hitting bunker chunks and hammering topwater poppers thrown into the froth when the bait gets pinned against structure. If that Montauk-sized class pushes west along the Sound side the way it usually does this time of year, expect the blues at Rocky Point and Horton Point to get bigger and meaner over the next couple weeks, not smaller.

Porgy fishing hasn't needed any of this drama to stay good — it's been the one bite you can set a clock by all summer, and it's holding through September the way it always does. The reef structure off Horton Point and the rockpiles at Rocky Point are still stacked with scup in the 10 to 12 inch range, better fish mixed in for anyone soaking bloodworms or clam on a high-low rig tight to the bottom. This bite doesn't care much what the bass are doing and it'll stay dependable well into October before the fish start sliding off to deeper water.

Blackfish are just starting to show, which is right on schedule. The first tautog of the season have been coming off the rockier stretches around Rocky Point and the Orient Point structure as the water eases off its summer high, taken on green crab and Asian crab fished tight to the rocks. Numbers are still thin — this is the front edge of it, not the bite itself — but that'll build steadily through the rest of September and into October as more fish move onto the structure.

Fluke are the fading half of this story. The bite's still there in the deeper channels approaching Plum Gut and off Orient Point, bucktail-and-Gulp combinations still picking up keepers on the drift, but the fish are thinning out as the seasonal exodus toward deeper water gets underway. If fluke is the priority, the next week or two is the window before they're gone for the year. Weakfish have been scattered and honestly not much to report — a few picked up incidentally around the inlet structure on bucktails meant for something else, nothing worth planning a trip around.

Out at the Gut itself, there's been early albie interest building as fish push west along the Sound from the Rhode Island side. Nothing consistent enough yet to call a pattern, but small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dicks worked fast through bait-rich rips near Plum Gut approach have connected on a few. Worth a look if the tide and wind cooperate, but treat it as a bonus, not the plan.

Looking ahead, the building tide range into the next few days should keep tightening bait through the Sound, which is good news for both the bass and the blues chasing them. If the wind stays out of the north or northwest and lets the water clean up along the bluffs, the outgoing tide at Horton Point in the first two hours of daylight is the window worth building a morning around. Southwest wind and dirty water would push that timeline back — keep an eye on it before committing a whole tide to one spot.
