# Steady Week, No Drama: Warm Water Holds Spencer to Norfolk, Yellowfin and Mahi Keep Filling Coolers
*The canyon complex is riding the same warm-water regime it's had for weeks, and the fleet from Cape May and Ocean City is still finding fish — just working harder for the marlin.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 27** — _by Capt. Ray Lugo, Zone Writer_

Call it a B-plus week. Nothing spectacular, nothing that had the radio with hero numbers, but four canyons in a row that put fish in the box for boats willing to run the extra miles and read the water instead of just running to the same number as last trip. Spencer, Lindenkohl, Poor Man's, and Norfolk are all sitting under the same stubborn warm-water regime that's held the whole Mid-Atlantic complex together through most of August, and that consistency is exactly why the bite has stayed workable even as the calendar turns toward fall.

Here's the setup: stable high pressure has parked over the region, southwest winds running 8 to 12 knots most days, seas laying down enough to make the canyon runs comfortable out of both Cape May and Ocean City. That kind of weather window doesn't force anybody to change plans mid-trip, which matters this time of year because the fish are spread across four different pieces of structure and picking the wrong one costs you a whole day's fuel. The warm water sitting on the edges hasn't broken down or retreated, and until it does, the multi-species mix — yellowfin, some bigeye, mahi, wahoo, and a thinning but still-present white marlin bite — should keep showing up the way it has for weeks.

Spencer remains the go-to when boats want the shortest run with the most reliable yellowfin. The temperature break along the northeast edge has held tight, and the chunk bite there has been steady on butterfish and bunker chunks fished on circle hooks, slow-trolled or drifted right on the color change. Boats working sunrise and the last two hours of daylight have done best, with fish running 30 to 55 pounds mixed in with the occasional bigger class fish for those willing to soak a bait deeper on a fluorocarbon leader. Trolling spreads pulling naked and skirted ballyhoo have also connected, especially green-and-yellow and pink-and-white combinations worked just outside the temp break rather than dead center on it — the fish have been sitting on the edges of the warm water, not stacked in the middle of it.

Lindenkohl has fished almost as a mirror of Spencer this week, which makes sense given how the same warm tongue runs across both pieces. The mahi bite has been the story here — smaller fish, two to six pounds mostly, but plenty of them holding under any floating grass or debris line boats can find. Small spreader bars and cedar plugs pulled at moderate speed have worked to locate them, then a pitch bait or a bucktail tossed right into the pod once they show up under the boat has kept rods bent. Anybody running Lindenkohl this week without a pair of long-handled dip nets ready has been leaving fish in the water.

Poor Man's has been the tougher read of the four. The white marlin bite is still alive there, but it's thinned out compared to earlier in the month, and the boats connecting have needed to cover more water to find them — trolling ballyhoo dink baits and small Islanders along temperature seams rather than committing to one spot and waiting. Two or three releases on a good day is realistic right now; boats expecting double-digit marlin days like July are going home disappointed. That's the honest read — it's a fishery in transition, not a dead one, but it demands more running and more patience than it did a month ago.

Norfolk continues to be the play for boats willing to make the longer run and stay overnight. The bigeye bite on the deep drop and slow-troll has been the most consistent producer down there, particularly in the pre-dawn hours when boats have worked butterfish chunks and glow squid baits over the deeper structure. Wahoo have also turned up on the high-speed troll for boats running wire line and deep-diving lures like Ilanders rigged behind cedar plugs, typically picked off on the run out or run back rather than on a dedicated wahoo drop.

The moon is building toward full over the next several days, and that's going to tighten up the bite windows across all four canyons rather than spread them out. Expect the best action to concentrate around the tide changes and current edges as the moon strengthens, with slower stretches in between. If the southwest wind pattern holds through the next 48 hours as expected, boats have a genuine window to work any of the four canyons without weather forcing a bailout — that's the time to commit to the longer run to Norfolk if bigeye or wahoo are the target, or stick closer with Spencer and Lindenkohl if the goal is filling a fish box with yellowfin and mahi on a shorter day.

Looking three days out, the warm-water regime that's been carrying this whole complex should still be in place, keeping the tuna and mahi bite solid even as the white marlin fishery continues its slow seasonal fade. The bigger question for early September is how long that warm pool holds before the first real cool-down starts working its way south. Until it breaks, the smart play is the same one that's worked all week — run to whichever canyon matches the target species and be ready to move if the first stop doesn't produce inside an hour.
