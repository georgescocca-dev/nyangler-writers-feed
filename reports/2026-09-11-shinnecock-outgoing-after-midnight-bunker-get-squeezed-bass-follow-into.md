# Outgoing After Midnight: Bunker Get Squeezed, Bass Follow Into the Inlet
*The new moon tide is doing exactly what it's supposed to do — pinning bait against the jetties after dark, and the bass know it.*

**HAMPTON BAYS, NY — September 11** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

If you're only fishing one tide this week, make it the back half of the outgoing, after dark, at Shinnecock Inlet. That's the call, and it's not a hard one to make right now. The new moon on the 10th pulled the strongest tidal swing of the young fall run, and every bit of current that comes with it is squeezing bunker schools tight against the beach, the jetties, and the inlet mouth from here down through Jersey and up into the Sound. That's not scattered bait activity — that's bass committing, and it's the first real sign the fall run is turning from a rumor into a pattern.

We flagged this exact setup a few days back — said the bunker schools were tightening under the building moon and stripers were starting to show real interest instead of just picking at stragglers. That's precisely what's happening at the inlet right now. The bunker are getting corralled against the rock piles on the ebb, and the bass are working them in the dark, when the bait has nowhere left to run but through the current seam at the inlet mouth.

Water's still holding summer-warm through the bay proper, but it's easing off its peak, which matters more than people think. Fluke fishing has been telling that story all week — the keeper-grade fish that were working the flats off Tiana Beach and Cold Spring Pond earlier in the season have largely cleared out for deeper, cooler structure outside, leaving mostly short fish behind in the bay. We called that fade coming too. If you're still working bucktails and Gulp on the bay drifts, expect a lot of measuring and not much filleting — the better grade of fluke has already made its move offshore ahead of the real cool-down.

The inlet itself is where the action is concentrating, and it's a night game right now. The deep hole off the south jetty, down near the Coast Guard station, is holding stripers up to the mid-20s with the occasional overslot fish mixed in, working bunker pods that get pushed through on the last three hours of the outgoing. Start around an hour after the tide turns and fish through until it goes dead slack — that window from roughly 11 p.m. to 2 a.m. has been the most consistent stretch for boats and surf guys working the rocks. Bucktails in the 1 1/2 to 2-ounce range, white or chartreuse with a white curly-tail trailer, worked slow along bottom on the current seam, have been doing the damage. If the bass are up higher chasing bunker on top, a black or bone Danny-style swimmer worked with a slow wobble across the surface will draw strikes that you feel more than see in the dark.

Ponquogue Bridge is holding its usual dusk-into-dark weakfish pattern around the pilings. These aren't big numbers of fish, but they're real — legal weakfish coming on bucktails tipped with squid strips, or a soft plastic curly-tail grub in white or pink, bounced slow along the current break where the light from the bridge meets the shadow line. That shadow edge is everything with weakfish — they sit right on it and let the tide bring the bait to them. Snapper blues are thick in the back bay creeks off Tiana and around the canal mouth right now, which is exactly the kind of precursor activity that usually means bigger bluefish aren't far behind as the water keeps cooling. Worth keeping an eye on if you're fishing the bridge after dark — adult blues have already been showing up mixed in with the weakfish on some nights, snapping at anything that swims near the light line.

Porgies and blackfish are both improving on the reef structure and inlet rocks as the bay eases off its summer heat. Green crab and clam on the bottom around the jetty boulders is producing a mixed bag of both species, nothing spectacular yet but a bite that should keep building through the month as the water keeps sliding down.

The albie and bonito push that's been working its way along the south shore from the east should keep extending in this direction over the next several days — that's a call we made last week, and the early signs support it. Nothing confirmed inside Shinnecock yet, but the geography and the warm-water edge line up for it to show at the inlet mouth or along the ocean side of the jetties before the week is out. Worth having a light spinning rod rigged with a small metal or soft plastic on standby if you're already out there working bass after dark.

Looking ahead, this tidal push should keep the bass committed hard through the next few tide cycles as the moon works its way off new phase — expect the bite to hold strong for another few nights before the current eases and the fish spread out a little. If the wind stays manageable, the next two nights on the outgoing at the inlet are the best bet this week. Keep a spare rod rigged light for weakfish or an albie surprise, because right now the bay's not giving you just one story — it's giving you three or four, and they're all worth chasing after dark.
