# Peanut Bunker Piling Into the Narrows — Bass and Blues Underneath, Bonito Watch Is On
*Bait is stacking hard between Stratford Shoal and the Norwalk Islands on the Harvest Moon tides, and the bass and blues know it — keep one eye on the horizon for the first false albacore.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — September 21** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

The bait told the story before any boat did this week. Peanut bunker started balling up thick off Middle Ground Light early Monday, dense enough that the sounder painted solid red from 15 feet to bottom, and by Wednesday those same pods had pushed west into the mouth of the Norwalk Islands with gulls working nervous circles overhead — not diving yet, just hovering, which usually means something bigger is pushing the bait from underneath rather than crashing through the top. That's exactly what showed up by Thursday: stripers stacked under the bait balls, holding 10 to 20 feet down, using the current lines off the Norwalk Islands' outer humps to ambush bunker getting pushed sideways by the tide.

The Harvest Moon is doing exactly what it should this time of year — bigger swings, harder current, and bait getting shoved around with less room to hide. Kings Point is still reading warm at 70.9 degrees while New London has cooled off to 69.4, and that west-to-east gradient matters — it's kept the bunker schools dense and slow-moving through the Central Sound corridor instead of blowing straight through. Stratford Shoal, sitting right in that thermal seam, has been holding both bait and blues in the same 20 to 35 foot zone where the rip kicks up on the tail end of the ebb.

Bluefish have been the more reliable player this week, and we flagged this pattern building last week — bait getting flushed south and east was going to intensify the blitz activity, and that's precisely what's happening. Blues in the 3 to 6 pound class are ripping through bunker schools off Charles Island and along the CT beaches from Stamford to New Haven, especially on the last two hours of the outgoing when bait gets funneled out of the harbors and rivers. Diamond jigs and bunker spoons trolled at 4 to 6 knots have been the go-to, but if you can get on top of a visible surface blitz, a topwater popper or a wooden Danny-style swimmer thrown right into the froth is drawing violent strikes.

Striped bass have been more selective, which tracks with what tends to happen once the bait gets this concentrated — fish get choosy about presentation instead of just crushing anything that moves. The better producers have been drifting bunker chunks or whole peanut bunker on a fish-finder rig in 25 to 40 feet along the western edge of Stratford Shoal on the last hour of the flood into the first hour of the ebb, when the current compresses the bait against the shoal itself. Live-lining snagged peanuts off the back of the boat near Middle Ground Light has also produced better-grade fish, several pushing into the mid-20s, though most of the bass mixed into these bunker schools are still in the schoolie-to-low-20s range. If you want a shot at something bigger, working bucktails — 1 to 1.5 ounce white or chartreuse — along the deeper current seams off the Norwalk Islands at first light has been the more consistent play for slot-class fish.

Away from the bunker circus, the bottom bite has quietly turned into the better bet for anyone who wants a full box rather than a highlight reel. Porgies and sea bass are thick on the reef structure off Port Jefferson and around the Norwalk Islands rock piles, holding in 20 to 45 feet, and we called this improving trend last week as water temps eased back — it's held up. Green crab and squid strips on high-low rigs are producing steady counts of porgies to 14 inches, with sea bass mixed in on the deeper structure, especially any hard bottom holding mussel beds. Blackfish are just beginning to show on that same structure, which again lines up with what we expected as fall settles in — a few tautog in the 3 to 5 pound range have come off green crab worked tight to the rocks around Charles Island, and that bite should only build as water continues cooling into October.

Fluke have hung on better than expected for late September, another byproduct of that warmer western Sound water. Drifting Gulp Swimming Mullets and bucktails tipped with squid strips along the sand-to-structure transitions off Oyster Bay and Port Jefferson Harbor's outer mouth has still been producing keeper-class fish to 5 pounds, though the numbers are thinning as the days shorten.

The real story to watch is what's pushing west from Fishers Island Sound. The bonito and false albacore push has been building steadily along the eastern approaches, and if the current thermal pattern holds through the weekend, there's a real chance the first albies show up working bait schools around Stratford Shoal or the outer Norwalk Islands within the next several days. Keep watching the bird activity and any nervous, unexplained bait movement on top — tight, fast-breaking rips of small fish scattering with no visible predator underneath is usually the first tell before anyone sees a fish actually jump. Small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dick-style metals in the 1/2 to 1 ounce range should be in the rocket launcher just in case.

Looking into the weekend, the bigger Harvest Moon tides should keep concentrating bait hard at the structure points — Stratford Shoal, Middle Ground Light, and the Norwalk Islands humps are the three spots worth prioritizing on the tide changes. If a cold front moves through as expected late in the week, look for the fluke bite to tighten up first while the bass and blues push harder into the bunker for one more good run before the water drops another notch.
