# One Eel, One Take — A Gut Bass Over 35 at 2 A.M.
*The current's bleeding toward neap, and the eastern Sound's bass are compressing into the last hard water hours of the tide.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 3** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Two-fifteen Monday morning, chest-deep on the rocks below Orient Point, I put a 12-inch snake eel on the swing into Plum Gut current that was already softer than it should've been for this stage of the tide. One cast. One thump on the drop. Thirty seconds later I've got a bass pushing 36 inches thrashing in the wash, gill plates flaring in the headlamp. That fish told me everything I needed to know about this week.

We flagged this exact move last week — said as the tides softened toward the coming neap, bass would pull off the scattered pattern and compress into the strongest current hours, with night eels at the Gut and the bridges becoming the play. That's exactly what's happening. The Race and Plum Gut are both easing off as we drift toward the neap around August 5-6, and instead of five-knot water blasting through on every stage of the tide, you're getting maybe two solid hours of hard current bracketing the peak of the ebb or flood. Outside those windows, it's soft, it's slow, and the fish know it.

During the day, forget the top of the water column. Bass are holding deep on structure — Valiant Rock, the reef off Fishers Island, the drop-offs along the Gut's western edge — sitting in 30 to 50 feet and refusing to commit until the light drops. I've had one solid afternoon this week jigging a 3-ounce diamond jig tipped with a strip of squid down into 40 feet off Race Rock, and it took patience — long enough that I second-guessed the spot twice before a 28-incher finally kissed the bottom bounce and stayed hooked. That's the story right now: the bite is real, but it's not falling in your lap during banker's hours. Dawn, dusk, and full dark are where the current concentration and low light line up, and that's where the size shows.

Bluefish are riding shotgun with the bass wherever bunker pods are stacking, and there are real pods moving through the Gut and off Orient Point right now. When they collide with current edges, it gets violent fast — I watched a blitz break loose off the Orient Point rip Tuesday evening, blues in the 8-to-12-pound class shredding bunker on the surface for maybe twenty minutes before it shut off as quick as it started. Poppers and metal-lip swimmers thrown into the froth will draw strikes, but you need to be there when it happens. These aren't blitzes that hold — they're moving targets tied to bait, not schedule.

Fluke fishing has been the most dependable thing going. Drifting the edges of Plum Gut and the Orient Point rip in 20 to 35 feet on the last two hours of the outgoing has produced consistent action — 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with white Gulp Swimming Mullets, dragged slow along bottom, working the current seams where sand meets rock. Nothing giant this week, mostly 16-to-19-inch fish with the occasional 4-pounder mixed in off the deeper stretch near Fishers Island Sound, but the ratio of keepers to shorts has been solid enough that it's worth a full tide if you've got the boat for it.

Porgies and blackfish are exactly where you'd expect them — stacked on structure. Bartlett Reef and the rockpiles around Fishers Island are loaded with porgies right now, and sandworms or pieces of clam on a simple high-low rig will keep a rod bent all afternoon if you don't mind the smaller fish mixed in. Blackfish season's open through the end of August, and green crab worked slow on the same structure has been picking off tautog in the 3-to-6-pound range for guys willing to anchor up and be patient instead of drifting through.

Weakfish have shown up sparingly — a few scattered fish taken on bucktails near the mouths of the tidal creeks off Orient, nothing you'd plan a trip around, but worth a cast or two if you're already working that water for bass.

Water's still holding a real gradient across the zone — New London reading in the high 60s, cooler than the western Sound, which keeps this end of the beat fishing more like early summer than the bathwater conditions further west. That cooler water is exactly why the bass here are still aggressive and why the structure bite has stayed strong instead of collapsing into a dawn-only pattern like you're seeing closer to the city.

Looking ahead: as the tide range keeps narrowing into the neap, expect the pattern to sharpen rather than fade — shorter windows of hard current, but the fish jammed into them tighter. If I've got one night this weekend, I'm back on the rocks at the Gut fishing the last two hours of the ebb into slack, eel on a fish-finder rig, no split shot, letting it swing naturally into the seam. Daytime, I'd rather jig structure at Race Rock or Valiant Rock than blind-cast open water — the fish are down there, they're just not coming up until the light and the current both cooperate. Keep an eye on the temperature breaks off Fishers Island and Orient over the next couple weeks too; that's typically when this zone gets its first look at fall speedsters, though nothing's shown yet and I'm not chasing ghosts until I see actual sign.
