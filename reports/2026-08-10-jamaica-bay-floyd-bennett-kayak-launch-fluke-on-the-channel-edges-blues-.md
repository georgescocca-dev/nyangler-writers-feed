# Floyd Bennett kayak launch: fluke on the channel edges, blues thick off Canarsie Pier
*The bay's sitting near its ceiling and the ocean's a washing machine, so this week the smart money stayed inside — and it paid off on fluke, snappers, and one good bass at the bridge.*

**FLOYD BENNETT FIELD, NY — August 10** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

I launched the kayak out of Aviation Road at Floyd Bennett Field before sunrise Tuesday, and I picked that ramp on purpose. The ocean's been ugly all week — buoy data off the Bight had it running better than two and a half feet of chop with a short six-second period, the kind of slop that turns a nice ocean beach session into a workout for nothing. Inside the bay, though, it's flat and it's warm, and warm water with bait in it is exactly where I want to be paddling.

We flagged this last week — said the post-frontal stir off that Sunday blow would cool and re-oxygenate the bay right as the neap softened the current, and that's more or less what happened. Tuesday and Wednesday both had a cleaner, more comfortable feel to the water than the flat-out soup we had the week before. It didn't last long — by Thursday afternoon the bay had gone quiet again on that slack neap current, which tracks with what we expected. But that two-day window was worth being out there for.

Fluke were the play, and they've been the most honest fishery in the bay all summer. I worked the channel edge off Grass Hassock, 16 to 20 feet, dragging a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail nice and slow along bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing. Picked up four keepers in that stretch, biggest going about 22 inches, plus a handful of shorts that came off the hook or measured just under. The bite wasn't nonstop — I marked bait on the fish finder in three, maybe four tight pockets and had to hunt between them. If you're not marking bait, you're not marking fluke right now. Same story working the edge along the Cross Bay Bridge channel — a few boats anchored up there doing well on strip bait and squid combos fished right on bottom as the tide turned.

Canarsie Pier has been a snapper blue factory, and if you've got kids or you just want a bend in the rod every other cast, that's your spot. Small blues, 8 to 12 inches, are stacked in the shallows around the pier lights and along the bulkhead, eating tiny silver Kastmasters and 1/8-ounce Deadly Dick spoons fished fast on light spinning gear. It's not glamorous fishing but it's steady, and it's about the only daytime bite that doesn't require you to think too hard about tide stage — those blues are on bait and they don't seem to care much about the clock.

Stripers are a different animal right now. Peak summer heat has them locked into a strict low-light pattern — this isn't the May run where you could find fish cruising the flats at noon. The daytime bite has gone quiet almost everywhere except the current-swept structure, and the Marine Parkway Bridge is exactly that kind of structure. I fished the pilings from the kayak on the last of Wednesday's outgoing, right at last light, and worked a bucktail with a white Fin-S trailer tight to the current seam on the down-current side. One fish, but it was a good one — mid-20s, thick, clearly staged up waiting on the tide to bring bait through. That's the pattern right now: bass holding at the bridge, at Breezy Point tip, wherever there's hard current and shade, and they're feeding hard for a short window around dusk and through the night rather than all day long.

If you want to fish that pattern from shore, Fort Tilden and Breezy Point are still your best bets, and both still require the NPS Fishing Access Pass at all times — the 193rd Street lot and the 222nd Street lot both take it, and yes, the annual passes are sold out for the year, so if you don't already have one, you're looking at day passes if they're available or fishing the free access at Canarsie Pier or Marine Park instead. Breezy Point's tip is the classic night bass spot for a reason — the rip where the bay meets the ocean concentrates bait hard on the outgoing, and that funnel effect only gets better as the moon builds.

And that's really the story for the coming week. We're heading into the new moon springs on the 11th through the 13th, and that's going to re-arm the current at the bridge and at the inlet in a way the neap just couldn't. Bigger tidal swings mean harder-running ebbs, which means more bait getting pushed through the pinch points, which means the bass that have been holding tight to structure should get a real reason to feed. If I had one night to fish this week, I'd want it under that new moon, working the outgoing at Marine Parkway Bridge or the Breezy Point tip with a bucktail or a swimming plug worked slow through the seam. Daytime, I'd stick with the fluke drift off Grass Hassock and the Cross Bay channel edges — that bite doesn't need a big tide to work, just bait, and there's been bait.

Worth noting the porgies have been mixing in around the bridge structure on bottom rigs with clam and squid, nothing to build a trip around but a nice bonus while you're anchored up waiting on the tide to turn. Blackfish season's still closed here, so that's one for the fall notebook. For now, work the moving water, fish the low light for bass, and don't expect the ocean side to fish clean until that swell backs off.
