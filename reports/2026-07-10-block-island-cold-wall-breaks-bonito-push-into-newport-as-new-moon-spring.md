# Cold Wall Breaks, Bonito Push Into Newport as New-Moon Springs Juice the Rips
*The Block Island–Rhode Island Sound temperature line collapsed this week, spreading bonito west while big bass stack the Southwest Ledge on the building tide.*

**BLOCK ISLAND, RI — July 10** — _by Jack Sullivan, Zone Writer_

Last week the Sound did something I don't see every summer — that cold wall that usually pins bonito and false albacore tight to the island's south side just fell apart. We're talking a ten-degree-plus jump in Rhode Island Sound water in a matter of days. That line between Block Island Sound and the colder RIS water has been the fence keeping the pelagics penned up around the Southeast Light and the Ledge. With it gone, there's nothing stopping bonito from working west into Newport and up to Point Judith. I had a buddy running out of Third Beach see fish crashing bait off the Fort Adams side Tuesday morning — that's not a normal July report for that stretch of water, and it tells you how far this warm push has moved.

This week the moon is the story for the bass crowd. We're building toward new moon, and that means spring tides stacking bigger and bigger through the weekend, peaking right around the 11th through 13th. Bigger tides mean more water moving through the rips — the North Rip and the Southwest Ledge both come alive with current on the new-moon push, and that's exactly the kind of water big migratory bass want to sit in. The first evening flood-into-sunset window we've had since the June full moon lines up perfectly with this build. If you've got one evening to fish this week, make it Saturday or Sunday, with the tide flooding hard into last light.

On the bass — and this has been a good stretch for them, not a great one, but good — we're seeing giant-class fish holding on the Southwest Ledge in that 25 to 40-foot band where the rip stands up on the last two hours of the ebb. Eels fished on a fish-finder rig, or bucktails dressed with a sassy shad trailer bounced right along bottom where the ledge drops off, have been the ticket. Bunker chunks fished on wire down in the rip current have also produced — a couple of fish over 40 pounds came off the North Rip on chunk baits over the past ten days, which tells me the resident bunker schools pushing through are keeping the big cows in the neighborhood. Don't overlook the last hour of daylight through the change of tide — that's when the biggest fish seem to commit.

Fluke have been shifting out of Narragansett Bay and into the passages and the open water off the island, and the why is simple: the upper bay water got too warm, pushing toward 78 degrees in the shallows, and fluke don't want that. They're stacking instead where the upwelled ocean water — running closer to 71 — meets structure. The mouth of the East Passage below the Newport Bridge, the drop-off off Sandy Point, and the sandy flats south of the Ledge have all been holding keeper fish. White and chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter-ounce bucktails, drifted slow with the tide, have out-produced bare bucktails two to one this week. Fish are running 3 to 6 pounds with the occasional 7 mixed in — nothing to write home about size-wise, but a lot more consistent than the bay bite has been.

Bluefish are doing what bluefish do — showing up in short, violent windows and then vanishing. Harbor entrances and the rips off Watch Hill have had blitzes on bay anchovies in the early morning, chopper-class fish mixed with smaller choppers. Bring a wire leader and don't waste time on finesse — a simple diamond jig or a Hopkins worked fast through the melee will get bit as long as the bait's balled up.

Bonito, as I said, are the wildcard this week. The traditional spots — the rip off the Southeast Light, the drop just outside the breachway at Charlestown — are still holding fish, but with the cold wall gone, I'd be checking the water off Point Judith Light and even up toward Narragansett Town Beach on calm mornings. Small epoxy jigs and Deadly Dicks worked fast on top, right at first light before the wind builds, have been the play. This bite is thin and technical — you need to see them showing before you commit — but the window is real and it's early enough in the season that it should only build from here if this warm water holds.

Looking ahead: if the new-moon spring tides do what I expect, the Southwest Ledge and North Rip should fish well right through the weekend on that PM flood-into-sunset window, especially Saturday evening with clean water and moderate wind. The fluke bite should stay steady in the passages as long as that 71-degree ocean water keeps pushing bay fish out. The bonito situation is the one to watch closest — if that warm plume holds through the week, we could see a real corridor open up from Block Island clear to Point Judith, which would be a first for a lot of guys who've only ever chased them off the island itself. I'd have my light spinning gear rigged and ready either way, because between the bass on the ledge and bonito potentially showing anywhere from Newport to the breachway, this is shaping up to be a week where you want options.
