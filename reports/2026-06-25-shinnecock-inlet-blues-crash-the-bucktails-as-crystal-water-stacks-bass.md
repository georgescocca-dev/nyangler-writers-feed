# Inlet blues crash the bucktails as crystal water stacks bass on the rip
*Shinnecock's gin-clear current is firing on all cylinders — if you can handle the boat traffic.*

**SHINNECOCK INLET, NY — June 25** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The inlet's running crystal clear this week, that tropical blue-green that makes every fish look like it's swimming in an aquarium. And they're taking advantage of it — big bluefish are crushing everything from Mag Darters to bucktails in the rip, while stripers are holding tight to structure on the incoming water.

I've been working the inlet hard after dark, and the action's been consistent. Three-quarter-ounce bucktails with white Zoom fork tails are the ticket for bass up to 17 pounds. The key is positioning — you need to be on one side or the other of the jetty structure to get your bait down in this current. Cast into the rip and work a pop-pop-pause retrieve. The fish are hitting far out in the moving water, not tight to the rocks like you'd expect.

The bluefish bite has been insane during daylight hours. These aren't cocktail blues — we're talking 30-plus-inch choppers that'll straighten hooks if you're not running at least 50-pound leader. When they're blitzing on top, the 5-inch Yozuri Mag Darter is old faithful. But here's the move: when everyone's throwing big plugs and the blues are going nuts, swing a bucktail underneath the school. That's where the stripers are sitting, and they'll eat that jig on the drop.

The current's been ripping with these spring tides, and that flat-head design on the Mag Darter gives you subtle action even on the pause. The water pushes against that face and keeps the bait working — it's not just dead-drifting like a round-head plug would. That D-wood drag action is deadly in moving water.

Only problem is the boat traffic. Every time you get a school fired up, some 40-footer comes blasting through the inlet and scatters everything. It's gotten worse as word spreads about the bite. I'm sticking to early morning and late evening when the weekend warriors are tied to the dock.

The bay's been producing too, especially around the Ponquogue Bridge on the incoming tide. Weakfish are starting to show in decent numbers — nothing huge yet, but fish to 18 inches on small bucktails and Gulp. The water's warming nicely in the back bay, running a solid 4 degrees warmer than the ocean side of the inlet. That temperature gradient is what's stacking bait on the flood and holding predators on the ebb.

Fluke fishing's been steady but not spectacular. The better fish are coming from the deeper holes in 30 feet, dragging white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails. Slow and steady wins this game — let the current do the work and keep that bait on bottom.

Looking ahead, we've got a new moon Friday, which means big tides and serious current. I'll be watching the inlet drain at sunset for the first real push of weakfish. The bay water's hitting that magic 66-degree mark, and when you combine that with the moon pull, things should get interesting. Just hope the boat parade takes a night off.
