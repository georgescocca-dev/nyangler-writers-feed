# SW Ledge Slack at 6:40: Bass Holding Deep, Sea Bass Filling the Cooler, Bonito Still a Rumor
*The rip's producing on the bottom half of the tide while everyone waits for squid to push the albies and bonito east.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 13** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack at SW Ledge ran 6:40 this morning, and I had the boat sitting over 42 feet of green water with the fishfinder with bait from the bottom third of the column right up to about 25 feet. That's the tell this time of year — when the marks are stacked mid-water instead of hugging bottom, the bass are up feeding on something other than crabs, and this week that something has been sand eels pushed tight against the ledge by the current.

I'm not going to tell you it's been easy. It hasn't. The bite at SW Ledge has been a two-hour window on either side of the tide change, and if you're not anchored up or drifting the edge precisely when that current starts to build, you're fishing empty water. Best fish have come on the last two hours of the ebb, working parachute jigs — the 4-ounce white ones — bounced off bottom and swum up through that 25 to 35 foot band where the marks show thickest. We've had stripers to 33 pounds doing that, mixed with a good number of schoolies in the mid-20s that make you think there's a bigger class of fish still staged somewhere east of us, maybe holding off Block until the water settles.

Speaking of Block — I had word of a genuinely giant bass coming off the island earlier this week, the kind of fish that reminds you why guys make the run out there in July even when the numbers aren't stacked. That's not an everyday occurrence, but it tells you the bigger migratory fish haven't abandoned the area, they're just scattered and picky. Fluke fishing off Sandy Point has actually been the more consistent producer — drifting the sand in 25 to 40 feet on the last of the incoming, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid. We've boated fluke to 6 pounds doing that drift, with decent numbers of keepers mixed with a lot of shorts that keep you busy but don't fill the cooler fast. Patience pays there — cover ground slow, feel for the bottom change where the sand gives way to broken shell, and that's usually where the bigger flatties sit.

Black sea bass have been the most dependable meat in the boat. The humps south of the Ledge, anywhere from 60 to 80 feet, are holding fish that don't care what the tide's doing — you can catch them on the slower parts of the current too, which makes them the fallback plan when the bass window closes. Green crab and squid strips on a double-hook bottom rig, dropped right to the structure, and you're not waiting long between bites. Sizes have been solid, plenty in the 16 to 18 inch range with a few pushing 20.

Now, the question everyone's been asking me at the dock: where are the bonito? Not here yet, not in numbers worth talking about. I know some of you saw a stray fish flash on the surface off North Light last week, and that's a real signal, but it's early. What's got me paying attention is the squid pushing into the Sound and up toward Nantucket water — that's the bait that usually tightens up the whole rip pattern out here. When squid concentrations build off the island, the bonito and false albacore aren't far behind, because that's the forage that draws them off the open water and into casting range at Clay Head and off the North Light rip. Right now the water temp profile still favors that happening in the next two to three weeks rather than this week — Montauk's sitting around 70, and our water off the island has been tracking close to that. Once it firms up in the high 60s to low 70s and stays there through a tide cycle, that's when I start looking for breaking fish off the rocks at first light.

Bluefish have filled in the gaps nicely, honestly better than I expected for mid-July. Working the rip on the outgoing near SW Ledge, we've had jumbo blues in the 8 to 12 pound class hitting everything from bucktails to metal — Deadly Dicks and Ava jigs both getting bit when you work them fast through the boils. If you're getting frustrated with finicky bass, blues will save your trip and they're not shy about it.

Looking at the week ahead, I'd stick with the same pattern until something changes it — fish the SW Ledge on the last two hours of ebb for bass, work Sandy Point on the incoming for fluke, and use the humps for sea bass when the tide's dead or the wind's up. If the squid keep building around the island like they have been, I expect the albie and bonito conversation to get real fast, probably closer to the end of the month. I'll be watching the bait piles off North Light closely, and the first morning I see them nervous on the surface, that's the trip I'm calling. Until then, it's bottom bites and patience, and that giant bass off Block is proof there's still a reason to keep a rod rigged heavy just in case.
