# Peaked Hill Bars on the Full Moon: Bass Sliding onto Sand Eels
*Big tides pushed bait tight against the bars this week, and the first real striper interest of the fall showed on the rips from Race Point to Sandy Neck.*

**PROVINCETOWN, MA — September 7** — _by Donny Costa, Zone Writer_

The full moon tide this week did what it's supposed to do out here — it squeezed the bait. Sand eels that had been scattered thin across the flats and rip lines all August balled up tight against Peaked Hill Bars and the deeper edge of Race Point, and the striper interest that's been lukewarm most of the summer finally sharpened into something worth planning a morning around. We flagged this building pattern a few days back — said the bite would keep coming as long as temps held — and that's exactly the shape it's taken. Nothing wild yet, no blitz you'd call somebody home from work for, but the fish are feeding with purpose instead of picking.

The rundown for the week: big spring tides off the full moon on the 6th and 7th moved a lot of water fast through the rips, which is exactly when Race Point and Peaked Hill Bars fish best — current stacks bait against structure and bass sit downcurrent waiting for it to spill over. Boats working the last two hours of the ebb off Race Point have been marking good sand eel balls in 25 to 40 feet, with bass mixed in tight to bottom and occasionally pushing up when the current eases toward the turn. Live mackerel, when the fleet can net or jig them off the rip itself, has been the difference-maker on the bigger fish — a school of tinker mackerel showing up on Peaked Hill has kept livewells full and put some 30-to-38-inch bass on the deck on slow-trolled or drifted macks fished just off bottom. For the artificial crowd, sand eel imitations — 6-inch epoxy jigs, RonZ paddletails in white/silver, and diamond jigs tipped with a sliver of belly — have been the call, worked on the drift with the boat set up to swing bait naturally through the seam.

Bluefish have stayed part of the mix, mostly smaller choppers in the 2-to-5-pound range working the surface pushes off Sandy Neck and along the beach toward Barnstable. Poppers and 1-ounce metal fished fast through the froth have been getting hit, though the blues have been more sporadic than the bass — some mornings the surface goes off for twenty minutes and then goes quiet, which tracks with bait moving through rather than holding. Fluke action on Billingsgate Shoal and the flats off Wellfleet is winding down from where it was in July, but there's still a keeper mixed in for boats working 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with white Gulp in 15 to 22 feet on the last of the outgoing — worth a stop on the way out or in, not a whole trip by itself anymore. Cod haven't shown in any real numbers yet on the deeper structure — that's still a few weeks off as the Bay cools further — but there's chatter starting about marks holding bait on the deeper humps north of Billingsgate, which is usually the first sign before the cod slide in for the fall.

What's setting up next is the new moon on the 10th, and if the pattern from the last several years holds, that's the tide that tightens everything up further and starts the first real blitz behavior — bunker and sand eel schools getting squeezed hard enough that bass stop picking and start slashing on top. The building moon has already done half the work; the water's cooling a degree or two each night now, which pushes bass out of their summer holding pattern and onto the move, following bait instead of waiting on it. If that new moon push follows the coastwide pattern that's been setting up from Raritan Bay on up through Narragansett, the Bay should see its version of it within the week — most likely showing first at Race Point and Peaked Hill Bars where the current does the concentrating, then spreading down toward Sandy Neck and Barnstable Harbor as bait gets pushed along the beach.

For anyone with limited time this weekend, the smart play is still the last two hours of the ebb at Race Point or Peaked Hill Bars, working live mackerel on a fish-finder rig just off bottom in 25 to 40 feet, or covering water with sand eel jigs on the drift if the bait's not cooperating for netting. Early morning has been outproducing afternoon — the wind's been building a light southwest breeze most days by midday, enough to put a chop on the rip and make the drift sloppier than it needs to be. If the forecast holds calm through the weekend, that's the window — get out early, let the tide do the work, and don't be surprised if the first real sign of blitz behavior shows up sooner than the calendar suggests. Sandy Neck surfcasters should keep sand eel patterns and small metal in the bag for the bluefish pushes, and Billingsgate is still worth a fluke stop, but the story this week and into next is the sand eels tightening on the bars and the bass they know it.
