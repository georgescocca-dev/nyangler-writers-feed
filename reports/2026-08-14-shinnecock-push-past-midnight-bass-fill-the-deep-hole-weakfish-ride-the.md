# Push Past Midnight: Bass Fill the Deep Hole, Weakfish Ride the Bridge Lights
*Two days off the new moon, the outgoing tide is doing the heavy lifting at Shinnecock — if you're willing to fish it after dark.*

**HAMPTON BAYS, NY — August 14** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday night to skip the evening incoming and wait for the midnight ebb, and it was the right one. Two days past the new moon, the tidal push through Shinnecock Inlet is stacking up exactly the way I figured it would — current running harder, longer, and pulling bait through the cut in a way that daytime anglers just don't get to see. If you're only fishing the inlet on weekend mornings, you're missing the best window of the week.

Here's the thing about this inlet: the bass don't leave when the sun goes down, they show up. The deep hole on the inside of the east jetty, the one that drops off fast just past the rocks where the current from the bay meets the ocean draw, has been holding fish stacked from twenty to thirty feet down on the back half of the outgoing. I've been running a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig down into that hole starting about two hours before dead low, and it's produced slot bass steady, with a couple pushing past 34 inches mixed in. If you'd rather work artificials, a 2-ounce bucktail with a white pork rind trailer, bounced slow along the bottom on the drift through the same stretch, has been just as effective and lets you cover more water.

The bunker are the whole story right now. Peanut schools have been getting pushed through the inlet on the ebb, and everything with fins is on them — bass, and jumbo bluefish that have shown up in real numbers over the past week, chopping bait right at the surface some mornings at first light near the jetty rocks. If you see the birds working tight over the water on the ocean side around sunrise, that's your signal — get a bucktail or a topwater plug in there fast, because those blitzes don't last.

Ponquogue Bridge has been quietly doing what it always does this time of year — holding weakfish tight to the pilings, but you have to fish it right. I've had my best luck starting about an hour into the outgoing, after dark, working the shadow lines where the bridge lights meet open water. Small white swim shads, three inches, on a light jighead, worked slow with just enough action to keep them ticking near bottom — that's been the ticket. Nothing huge, mostly 16 to 20 inches, but steady enough for a few keepers most nights, and it's a different kind of fishing than the bass game — quieter, more patient, more about reading the current against the pilings than covering ground.

Fluke have been a mixed bag, and I'll be honest about that. The structure right at the inlet mouth — the rock piles and the drop-off where the channel meets the ocean — has been giving up better numbers than the open bay lately, which lines up with what I said last week about the new moon tide sharpening things up at current-driven spots. We called that one, and it's holding. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on quarter-ounce bucktails, drifted slow along that structure on the outgoing, have put a few keepers in the cooler each trip, but you're working for them — I covered a lot of drift lanes off Tiana Beach and the bay side flats near Hampton Bays for a handful of fish each outing. It's not a bay full of doormats. It's a spot-specific bite, and you need to know where the bottom changes.

Porgies are still there for the taking on the inlet rocks, especially right on the tail end of the outgoing when the current slacks just enough to hold bottom with sandworms or clam on a simple two-hook rig. Not glamorous, but it's reliable, and it's a good way to fill an hour while you wait for the tide to turn back for the bass.

If you're thinking about running the canal through to the Peconic side, this is a reasonable week for it — just time it around the lock schedule and don't fight the current unnecessarily. I haven't heard much off the Peconic side worth chasing right now, so I'd stay put unless you've got a specific reason to make the run.

Looking ahead, I don't see any reason for this pattern to break. The tide's still building strength coming off the new moon, the bunker schools show no sign of clearing out of the inlet, and the bass and blues are going to keep riding that bait as long as it's there. Water's still plenty warm for weakfish to hold tight to the bridge structure at night, and I'd keep working that program until it tells me otherwise. One thing worth watching — with warm water and bait this thick working up and down the south shore, there's a real chance something unexpected shows up around the inlet mouth before the month's out. Nothing confirmed yet, but I'll be out there after dark either way, so I'll let you know if the cast of characters changes.
