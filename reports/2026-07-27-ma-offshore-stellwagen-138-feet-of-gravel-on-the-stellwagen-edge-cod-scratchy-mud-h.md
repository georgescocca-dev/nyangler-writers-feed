# 138 feet of gravel on the Stellwagen edge: cod scratchy, mud haddock steady, bluefin on the meatballs
*Whales and mackerel are stacked on the same water off the northwest corner, and the tuna are starting to notice.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — July 27** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

138 feet, gravel bottom, northwest corner of the Bank — that's where I had the anchor down Tuesday morning with two charters aboard and a sounder full of marks that turned out to be mostly dogfish. That's the honest start to this report. Cod have been a grind out there this week, not a bust, but you're earning every fish.

We worked that gravel patch off the northwest corner for two hours on the last of the ebb, jigging diamond jigs and teasers tipped with clam, and came away with six keeper cod in the 6 to 12 pound range, mixed in with a pile of shorts we had to shake off quick. The water on the surface up there was pushing into the mid-60s, which is about right for late July, and the fish were sitting tight to the hard bottom where the current breaks over the ledge. If you're chasing cod on the Bank right now, don't waste time on the flats — go find the gravel edges and fish them slow and vertical. The bite windows are short, usually tied to the tide change, and once the current slacks the fish seem to shut down for an hour before picking back up.

Haddock have been the more dependable play, and that's typical for this time of year. We ran out to the Stellwagen Basin, 190 to 210 feet on the mud, and found solid marks holding just off bottom. Standard haddock rigs — high-low with a pink or chartreuse tube fly and squid strips — have been putting fish in the box steady, nothing giant but a good grade of 2 to 4 pound haddock with enough 5-pounders mixed in to keep it interesting. That mud bottom out there stays productive right through the summer because the water column holds cooler and more stable than up on the crown of the Bank, and the haddock like it that way.



Now for the fun part. The bluefin have shown up chasing mackerel, and it's been enough to get boats excited without being a sure thing yet. We've had mackerel schools breezing the surface off the eastern edge of Stellwagen most mornings this week, right around the same water where the whales have been working — humpbacks and finbacks plowing through bait balls, which is about the best sign you can ask for that there's forage stacked up. Where you find the whales tearing into the mackerel, the bluefin aren't far behind, usually sitting a little deeper and picking off stragglers.

We flagged this last week — said the bluefin interest would keep building around the bait concentrations on the Bank, and that's exactly what's playing out. Since the July 3rd trophy fish closure, the recreational slot fishery has stayed a steady, workable pace rather than anything explosive, and that's held true here too. We've seen fish in the 50 to 70 inch class come tight to a green machine trolled behind a spreader bar, and a couple boats working chunk lines off drifting mackerel schools have connected on live bait rigs — a single mackerel on a 6/0 circle hook, no weight, just fed back into the chum slick. It's not a bite where you're going to load the boat, but if you put in the time around active bait and working whales, you've got a real shot.

Tillies Bank has been quieter for us this week — less bait sign, less whale activity, and the couple times we ran out there the water felt slack, no real current push. I'd stick closer to home on Stellwagen proper until that changes.

Moon's waxing toward full in the first week of August, and that should start sharpening the tide swings on the Bank, which is good news for both the groundfish and the tuna. Stronger current means better current breaks on the gravel for cod, more defined edges on the mud for haddock to stage against, and tighter bait concentrations for the bluefin and whales to work. If that current builds the way it usually does heading into a full moon, I'd expect the haddock bite on the mud to stay the most reliable meat-in-the-box option, with cod picking up on the gravel as the tide starts moving harder again.

For the tuna, the pattern to watch is simple — go where the mackerel and the whales are, not where they were yesterday. That bait has been moving around the eastern and northeastern edges of the Bank day to day, and the bluefin are following it, not holding a fixed piece of structure. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd run out early, find the whale activity, and work the edges of that bait with a mackerel rig before committing to a groundfish plan. Worth the run if the weather holds — and right now it looks like it will.
