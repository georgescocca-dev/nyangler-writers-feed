# Bunker Pinned to the Ledges from Sakonnet to Napatree, Albies Filling Every Breachway
*The new moon tide squeezed bait against the rocks all week, and stripers, blues, and false albacore all showed up to cash in.*

**NEWPORT, RI — September 11** — _by Billy Tanaka, Zone Writer_

Peanut bunker have been stacking against the rockweed on the low tide all week, thick enough that you can see the water nervous from thirty yards out. That's the tell right now — not birds, not bait balls on the graph, just that flat calm patch of water suddenly rippling against the base of a ledge like someone's shaking a rug. From Sakonnet Point down through Second Beach, along the Jamestown shoreline, and out to Napatree and Watch Hill, the bunker have been getting pinned tight to structure by the tide, and the predators followed right behind them.

The September 10 new moon gave us the hardest tidal push we've seen since spring, and it did exactly what we said it would last week — bass moved from picking at stragglers to committing hard to the bait. We flagged that the blitz activity would build as bunker tightened along the beachfront under the strengthening tide, and that's precisely the pattern the fleet has been working this week. Kings Point is reading 70.9 and New London 69.4, which tells you the whole stretch from Newport down through Watch Hill is still holding water warm enough to keep bunker comfortable and shallow, but cool enough that stripers are feeding with real purpose instead of just cruising through.

The better bass action has been on the last two hours of the ebb and into the first hour of the new flood, when current rips off the points at Sakonnet and Napatree and bait gets funneled through the skinny water. Guys working the rocks at Sakonnet Point have done well on white and bone SP Minnows and 2-ounce Savage Gear sand eels, worked just off the wash where the bunker are getting nervous. Second Beach has been more of a bait-ball game — cast a bunker-colored popper or a Doc's Goofy Jig past the school and rip it back through, and the fish sitting underneath will find it. Napatree's outer rocks are holding stripers up to the low 30-inch range on bigger paddletails, fished on the last of the ebb when the current pulls hard around the point and stacks bait against the boulders.

Bluefish have been the more consistent player of the week, and the snapper blues thick in the back bays and salt ponds behind Charlestown and Matunuck are a good sign that the adult run is close behind. Anyone fishing bunker chunks off the jetties at Watch Hill or drifting cut bait through the Weekapaug breachway has been getting into cocktail blues in the 3-to-6-pound range steadily, with the occasional bigger fish mixed in on the tide change.

The albie bite is the story of the month, and it's holding up. False albacore have gone from a tentative push a couple weeks back to a stabilized run across the whole south shore — Block Island, the breachways at Weekapaug and Quonochontaug, and pushing into Sakonnet and toward Newport. They're pinned to the warm edge that's still sitting on the surface, and that edge has been holding bait tight against the rock piles and current seams at the mouths of the breachways. Small epoxy jigs — half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce, silver or pink — and 3-inch Albie Snax on light spinning gear have been the ticket. The pattern's been the same every morning: watch for nervous water or a slashing surface bust just outside the breachway mouth on the early outgoing, get a cast in front of the school rather than into the middle of it, and strip it back fast. It's not a guaranteed hookup every time you see them — albies will follow a lure right to the rod tip and turn off — but the numbers of fish showing has been strong enough that persistence pays.

Fluke have been tapering the way we expected. The keeper-grade fish that were around Jamestown and the East Passage earlier in the season have mostly slid off into deeper water, and what's left inshore skews small. If you're set on fluke, the better bet right now is working deeper drops off Newport in 40-plus feet of water with a heavier bucktail and Gulp combo, rather than beating the shallow flats where the shorts are thick.

Tog are still a few weeks out from being the headline, but there's chatter starting about the cracks and ledges off Beavertail and around Napatree holding fish as the water eases down through the 60s. Worth scouting now with a handful of green crabs if you're planning ahead for the fall opener, but the real blackfish push is still a few weeks off.

Looking at the next several days, the tide is still carrying enough push from the new moon to keep bait tight against structure, so the window for blitz-style action along the beaches and breachway mouths should hold through the weekend before it eases as we move away from new-moon strength. If you've only got one morning, fish the last two hours of the ebb at a breachway mouth — Weekapaug or Quonochontaug — with albie gear rigged and a bucktail rod ready for whatever's sitting underneath the surface activity. That combination has been the most consistent play of the week, and there's no reason to expect it changes much before the tides start slacking off toward the next quarter moon.
