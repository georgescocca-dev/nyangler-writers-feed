# East end pre-dawn ebb, 4-knot push: slot bass on top, squid stacking the lights
*New-moon current has the Canal doing what it does best — concentrating bait against structure and turning slot stripers into surface predators before the sun's even up.*

**BOURNE, MA — August 17** — _by Mike Devlin, Zone Writer_

0517, east end, ebb running somewhere around four knots off the Bourne side rocks — and the water in front of me exploded like someone dropped a manhole cover in it. That's the number I've been chasing all week. When that current hits its stride on the front half of the ebb, right around first light, the bass stop thinking and start eating.

Monday through Wednesday this week gave us the best window of the summer so far. New-moon minus tides pushed extra water through the ditch, and that extra flow does exactly what thirty years of watching this place has taught me it does — it pins bait against the rip line and structure until the bass can't help themselves. Slot fish and a fair number of over-slot stripers were blowing up on top within casting range of the rocks, mid-canal out to the east end, mostly in that last hour of darkness into the first hour of real light. Nobody had to work hard for it. A white or bone Super Strike Zig-Zag walked along the seam, or a 2-ounce Hab's Lure bucktail bounced just under the surface wash, both got hit before the retrieve even settled in.

We flagged this exact setup a week ago — said tidal flow was building toward its strongest point of the month and that it'd sharpen the bite on both bass and mixed bluefish inside 72 hours. That's precisely what happened. The bluefish have been in the mix too, mostly 3 to 5 pounds, slashing through the same bait pushes as the stripers, and they'll tear up a soft plastic fast so I've been running wire leader on the topwater rods just for the blitz windows.

The why here isn't complicated. Peanut bunker and pogies have been thick in the current seams, and when the ebb starts really moving — that four-, five-knot stretch through the mid-canal narrows — those baitfish get trapped against the rock walls with nowhere to go. Bass and blues don't need low light to feed when the buffet's pinned in place like that. That's the same pattern that's had fish blowing up in daylight from Narragansett clear up through Plymouth this month — bait density beating out the normal dusk-and-dawn caution. The Canal's just got the added kick of current doing the concentrating for free.

West end has been a different animal. Water temps in Buzzards Bay and around Wings Neck and Phinneys Harbor have been sitting warm enough that I'd bet on false albacore and maybe an early bonito showing before Labor Day — there's already word of bonito working Nantucket Sound and warmer water pushing exotics further up the Cape than usual this season. Nothing confirmed inside the Canal itself yet, but if that warm pulse holds, don't be shocked to see fin flashes off Mashnee Island or around Hog Island on a calm evening tide change. I'd keep a small epoxy jig or Deadly Dick rigged on a spare rod through Labor Day just in case.

Fluke have been a grind. West end drifts off Scusted Beach and down toward the Mashnee flats on the last of the ebb into dead slack have produced a few keepers, nothing to write home about — mostly shorts mixed with the occasional 18- to 19-incher on a Gulp Swimming Mullet in white, 3/4-ounce bucktail, dragged slow along the bottom where the current eases off. If fluke are your target, fish the slack itself, not the meat of the tide — too much current in the ditch and they just hunker down.

Night bite worth mentioning: squid have been stacking up thick around the light structure at both bridges, especially the Sagamore and Bourne pilings after dark. That's an extra forage layer sitting right under the bass, and it's probably part of why the topwater bite has stayed so consistent even outside the prime tide windows — there's always something for a hungry striper to eat down there right now.

Here's where I'd be careful. The moon's coming off its new-moon peak, so tidal push has already started easing slightly heading into the weekend. It's still strong enough to keep this topwater pattern alive through the next few days, but as we slide toward the weaker neap tides ahead of the August 28 full moon, expect the blitz windows to shorten and get pickier about timing. Instead of a 45-minute window of chaos, you might only get 15 good minutes right at the tide change. I'd fish tighter to the exact turn of the ebb — east end for stripers, west end structure for the outside chance at albies — rather than camping out for a long soak.

Looking ahead, tidal strength is going to build right back up as we approach that full moon at the end of the month, and that's when I'd expect this current-driven bite to sharpen again, maybe even outdo this week. If the warm water pattern holds in Buzzards Bay, the west end could turn into the more interesting story by early September, with albies and maybe a bonito mixing into the bass and blue game. For now, if you've only got one shot this week, take the pre-dawn ebb at the east end rocks. That's where the numbers — and the fish — have been lining up.
