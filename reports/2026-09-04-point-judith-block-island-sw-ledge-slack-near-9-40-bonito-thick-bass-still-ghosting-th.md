# SW Ledge Slack Near 9:40: Bonito Thick, Bass Still Ghosting the Bunker
*The Block's bonito run is peaking on schedule while striped bass sit on bait and wait for the moon and cooler nights to push them.*

**GALILEE, RI — September 4** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

SW Ledge has been fishing its tide the way it should this time of year — the last hour of the ebb into that 9:30 to 9:50 slack window has been the money stretch, with bonito stacking on top of the structure in 15 to 25 feet as the current eases off and bait gets pinned against the rip line. That window has been consistent enough over the last five days that the charter fleet is setting watches by it instead of guessing. The bonito arrived off the Block right on the calendar this year, and now that westward push everyone expected has them showing at Sandy Point, North Light, and pushing bait around Clay Head on the making tide too — not just the Ledge. We flagged this exact move last week, said the leading edge would build as more fish stacked in behind it, and that's exactly what's happened. The run has thickened, not thinned.

Water's still holding warm out here — low-to-mid 70s inshore, which is part of why the bonito bite has stayed hot while the striped bass side of the ledger has been slower to develop. Bass are around. They're just not committing the way they will in three weeks. Bunker schools are thickening up in Narragansett Bay right now, same story playing out on the LI south shore and Raritan Bay, but the water's still too warm for those schools to tighten into the kind of dense, panicked balls that trigger real blitz behavior. Fish are staged on bait, not attacking it. That changes as the moon builds toward full on the 6th and 7th — stronger tidal push should start squeezing those bunker schools, and the first real fall blitz activity in this region is likely within the next week to ten days, not this week.

On the bonito, the fish have been finicky in the way late-summer bonito always get once they've been picked at for a few weeks — they're clearly on micro-bait, probably bay anchovies or tiny silversides, and that's made small-profile presentations outfish the standard metal. Half-ounce and 3/8-ounce Deadly Dick and Hogy epoxy jigs in bone or olive have been the better call over the bigger Point Jude wobblers, worked with a fast, erratic retrieve right through the surface wash where the bait's getting pushed. Fly guys working the North Light rip on the making tide have done well with small Clouser-style patterns in olive-over-white, cast into the boils and stripped fast. The key at SW Ledge specifically is fishing the transition — the last twenty minutes of ebb through slack — because that's when the current stops ripping the bait out of the zone and the bonito can actually pin it against structure instead of chasing it through open water.

Striped bass fishing around the Block has been a mixed bag, which is the honest read. There are fish scattered along the rips off Southwest Ledge and out toward the deeper structure past Block Island, holding on bunker and peanut bunker pushing through, but they're not schooled tight enough yet for a reliable pattern. Jigging bucktails — 2 to 3 ounce, white or chartreuse — dropped to the bottom on the deeper edges of the Ledge on the last of the flood has produced some quality fish in the 20 to 30 pound class, but it's been a "put your time in" bite, not a sure thing. Live-lining bunker off Sandy Point on the outgoing has also worked for the boats willing to soak baits and wait rather than run-and-gun.

Bluefish have been the most dependable thing going in these waters right now, and that's tracking with what's happening basin-wide — strong reports out of Montauk, the Canal, and Nantucket Sound all point to the same thing, which is bluefish riding the bunker and peanut density hard through Labor Day week. Off Point Judith and around the Block, blues in the 4 to 8 pound class have been mixing right in with the bonito on the same small metal, and when a school of peanuts gets pushed up shallow near Clay Head, both species show up in the same five minutes of surface chaos.

Black sea bass are still holding true to form on the deeper structure around the Block — 60 to 90 feet on the rock piles and the drop-offs off the southwest side — and that bite has stayed steady all summer while fluke has done its predictable early-September fade. Fluke numbers are thinning fast as the doormats abandon the shallower structure for deeper ocean-side water ahead of their fall push, and while there are still a few keeper-class fish coming off the deeper drops, it's not the kind of bite worth planning a whole day around anymore.

Looking at the next few days, the bonito window at SW Ledge should hold through the building moon, with the slack tide timing sliding earlier each day — worth checking a current tide table before running out, since that window moves fast this time of year. The bigger story to watch is the bass. Once the bunker schools in Narragansett Bay tighten up behind this full moon push and the first real cool nights hit, that's when the switch flips from scattered bait-stagers to an honest blitz. Right now, if there's only one trip in the schedule this week, fish the SW Ledge slack for bonito and keep a bucktail rigged for any bass showing along the edges — the blitz isn't here yet, but it's close enough to start paying attention every morning.
