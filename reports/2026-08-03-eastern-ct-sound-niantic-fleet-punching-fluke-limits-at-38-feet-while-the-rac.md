# Niantic Fleet Punching Fluke Limits at 38 Feet While the Race Goes Quiet on the Neap
*Captain Terry's boat put six keeper doormats in the box Tuesday and the mate told me the sea bass bite off Black Point hasn't missed a day in two weeks.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 3** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Ran into one of the mates off the Niantic fleet at the tackle shop Wednesday morning buying squid strips, and he had that look guys get when the boat's been putting fish in the box. Told me Tuesday's half-day trip limited out on fluke in under four hours, mostly in that 36- to 40-foot stretch between Twotree Island Channel and the mouth of Niantic Bay. Biggest fish went six and a half pounds on a white Gulp Swimming Mullet teased behind a bucktail, drifted slow on the last two hours of the incoming. That's been the program all week — nothing fancy, just gulp and squid strips on a spreader rig, working the sand-to-mud transitions where the current pushes bait up against the channel edge.

The water's cooler out here than it is west of the river — New London's reading around 69.6, which is a good ten degrees behind what Kings Point's showing down toward the western Sound. That gap matters. It's kept our fluke and sea bass biting steady instead of shutting down the way warmer water can do to bottom fish in August. Guys grumble about it being "only" high 60s, but that's exactly why the doormats are still cooperating out here while other zones start slowing down on the fluke.

Black sea bass have been the more dependable of the two, honestly. Hole-in-the-Wall and the rock piles off Black Point are holding fish stacked tight to structure — 20 to 35 feet, and you want to be right on top of the rocks, not drifting past them. Green crab and squid combo on a high-low rig, bounced bottom on the anchor, has been the ticket. Party boat scuttlebutt says limits of keeper sea bass most trips this week, with a few 3-pound-plus fish mixed in when they hit the deeper edges of the reef structure. If you're going after them yourself, fish the slower part of the tide — dead low or right around the turn — because when that current really rips through there it's near impossible to hold bottom without snapping off in the rocks.

We flagged this last week — said the coming neap would soften current speed through here — and that's exactly what's happening now heading into this weekend's first-quarter neap. Twotree Channel and the Race have both gone noticeably slower on the last couple drifts I've heard about, which is actually good news for fluke guys because it lets you fish lighter bucktails, down to 3/4-ounce, and keep better bottom contact without getting dragged off the structure. If you're used to fighting 2-ounce jigs through there on a big tide, back off — you'll actually present better with less lead right now.

Striper action has settled into the pattern you'd expect for early August — fish are down deep on structure during the day and coming up to feed at the edges of light. Guys working the Niantic River mouth and around the bridges after dark with eels have been picking off decent fish, mostly mid-20s to low-30s inches, nothing spectacular but steady enough to be worth the late nights. Daytime, if you want a shot at a bigger fish, it's live-lining or jigging deep over structure — McCook Point and the rock piles off Black Point in 25 to 40 feet have given up a few better bass to guys working bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, worked slow along bottom. Nothing blitzing, nothing you'd call easy, but consistent if you put the time in during that dawn and dusk window.

Bluefish have been scattered rather than schooled up out here — nothing like the bunker-driven chaos happening to our west. When you do find them, it's usually because a pod of peanut bunker got pushed into the bay, and the blues show up within a tide or two of that bait arriving. Keep an eye on the surface around Niantic Bay and the outer edge of the channel — diving terns are still your best early warning system. Topwater poppers or a simple diamond jig will get bit fast once you find the pod, but it's not the kind of thing you can plan a trip around right now; it's opportunistic.

Porgies have been a nice bonus fish mixed in with the sea bass around Black Point and the reef structure off Hole-in-the-Wall — bloodworms or squid on a simple two-hook rig will keep the rod bending between bigger bites.

Looking ahead, this softer neap tide should hold through the weekend into early next week, which means lighter presentations and more patience on the drift, but also more comfortable fishing for anyone bringing kids or newer anglers out. I'd focus effort on the fluke grounds in that 35- to 40-foot band off Twotree and Niantic Bay while the current's manageable, and keep working sea bass around Black Point on the slower tide stages. Water temps out east aren't likely to jump much given how the Sound's laid out right now, so I don't expect a dramatic shift in what's biting — just a continuation of what's already working. If a squid push does show up along the eastern edges the way it's been building toward Rhode Island, that could sweeten things further for the reef fishing, so I'll be watching that closely and reporting back.
