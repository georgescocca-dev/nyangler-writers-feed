# Execution Rocks bunker balls tighten up — blues crash first, bass creeping in behind
*The bait's finally stacking from Throgs Neck to Matinecock, and the full moon just turned the lights on for the first real fall pattern.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — September 7** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Stood at the counter Thursday and a regular who runs out of City Island asked me point blank: "Frank, are the stripers behind those bunker yet or is it just blues?" Good question, and one worth answering properly, because the whole zone hinges on it right now. Short answer — it's mostly blues still, but the signs are there that bass are starting to lean into the party.

The bunker have been massing hard off Execution Rocks and up into the Throgs Neck corridor for about ten days now, and this week they finally tightened into the kind of dense, nervous balls that get bluefish stupid and bass interested. Water's still warm for early September — Kings Point is reading 70.9 degrees, which tells you the western end of the Sound hasn't turned the corner yet. Compare that to New London out east sitting at 69.4, and you've got a real west-to-east gradient building. That gradient matters because it means the warmest water and thickest bait are both sitting right in our backyard — Western Sound is holding more mature bio-mass right now than the water east of us, and that's exactly the kind of setup that historically primes the pump for a proper fall run.

We flagged this pattern back in late August — said elevated bluefish activity through the tournament window could pull stripers in behind the blues if the bunker held past Labor Day. That's precisely what's happening. The bunker never left. They just kept building, and now with the full moon that hit September 6-7 tightening those schools further, the stage is set. I'm not saying it's blitzing yet — it isn't — but the ingredients are on the table.

Bluefish have been the reliable show through the week. Boats working the pods off Matinecock Point and drifting the edge of Hart Island are finding cocktail blues in the 3 to 6 pound class hammering peanut bunker imitations — small Deadly Dicks, half-ounce Gotcha plugs, and diamond jigs worked fast through the bait balls. When the blues get on top and start busting bunker on the surface, poppers thrown right into the wash produce instant, violent strikes. That bite has been most consistent on the last two hours of the incoming, when the tide pushes bait against the rocks and pins it up.

Striped bass have been more of a mixed bag — present, but not committed the way they will be once that new moon tide arrives September 10. The better bass action this week came off Stepping Stones on the outgoing, working live bunker on a fish-finder rig with just enough weight to keep it near bottom in 20 to 30 feet. A few slot fish and a couple pushing into the high 20s came off that drift in the gray light of the morning tide change. Umbrella rigs trolled slow along the Stepping Stones light have also picked at fish, though the bite's been more pick than pile — two or three keepers on a good drift rather than a stacked box.

Fluke are winding down as expected this time of year but haven't shut off completely. The channel edges off Sands Point and the deeper water outside Manhasset Bay are still giving up fish on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and bucktail combos, drifted slow on the last of the ebb. Sizes have been modest — most fish just over the line — but there's enough interest that it's worth a drift if fluke's the priority before they slide out for the season.

Porgies remain the honest fallback everywhere from Execution Rocks to the mouth of Hempstead Harbor. Bloodworms and sandworms on high-low rigs in 20 to 35 feet are producing steady action, nothing glamorous but reliable enough to fill a cooler while you wait for the bigger fish to show. Blackfish season isn't quite in full swing yet but the rockpiles around Execution Rocks and the reefs off Hart Island are starting to hold fish as water temps ease, worth scouting now for when the October opener really turns them on.

Looking at the next few days, the wind and tide line up in our favor. If the bunker schools stay concentrated the way they have been — and there's no sign yet they're breaking up — the approach to that September 10 new moon should be the trigger point. New moon tides push harder water through the pinch points at Throgs Neck and around Execution Rocks, and that's when bunker get corralled tight against structure and bass finally commit rather than just shadowing the edges. If you can only fish one window this week, make it the last two hours of the ebb into the start of the new flood on September 9 or 10, working live bunker or large bunker-imitation swimbaits tight to the rocks at Execution Rocks or along the Stepping Stones reef. That's where thirty years of watching this water tells me the first real blitz of the fall is going to break — assuming the bait holds and the moon does what it usually does out here. Keep the blues as your consolation prize if the bass stay coy a little longer; they're not going anywhere.
