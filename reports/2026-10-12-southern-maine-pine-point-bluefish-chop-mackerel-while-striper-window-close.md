# Pine Point Bluefish Chop Mackerel While Striper Window Closes Fast
*Bass are still around on the last of the incoming, but the real story this week is teeth — bluefish and a mackerel push that's buying the dying striper season some time.*

Word out of the Pine Point jetty this week was bluefish chopping mackerel into chum right at the surface, close enough to the rocks that a long-rod guy could reach it with a Hopkins without wading past his knees. Here's the shape of the week: water's dropping fast through the 50s, and that's pulling the plug on the striper bite we've been riding since August. We said last week that striper activity would keep tapering over 72 hours and that groundfish and mackerel would become the more dependable play heading into mid-October — that's exactly what's playing out. Bass haven't vanished, but they're thinning, pushing south on schedule with the broader coastal migration that's got the Race and Block Island with blitzes we're not seeing up here anymore. What Southern Maine's got instead is bluefish riding the back of dense mackerel schools, which is a fine consolation prize if you like bent rods and don't mind retying leaders.

Biddeford Pool on the incoming is still the best hour of the day for anyone chasing the last bass of the season. The current funnels bait hard through the gut there, and stripers that are left in the system are stacking on that moving water rather than roaming the flats. Reports have fish to 28 pounds coming on the second half of the incoming, mostly on black Danny plugs and a 2-ounce Atom swimmer worked slow across the current seam where the deep water meets the sandbar. If you're fishing it on foot, give the plug time to sink and swing before you start the retrieve — the fish holding there are eating things that look like they got caught in the current, not things racing past.

Pine Point and the Scarborough River mouth are the bluefish story right now. Mackerel are thick enough that you can jig them on small diamond jigs or tiny Swedish Pimples in 15 to 20 feet just off the breakwater, and the bluefish are working those pods hard on the outgoing, especially in the last two hours before low. Bucktails and poppers both work when blues are actively surface-feeding, but when they're down on the bait, a silver Kastmaster worked with a steady, fast retrieve gets more takes than anything topwater. Bluefish have been tracking the striper pattern closely all week, same as we expected — once the bass thin out further, expect the blues to thin right alongside them, since neither species loves water much colder than what we've got now.

Prouts Neck and Cape Elizabeth are holding a mixed bag — some smaller schoolie bass still working the pocket water off the rocks, occasional bluefish passing through, and flounder reports starting to pick up in the softer-bottom spots tucked behind the points. If you're after flounder, bloodworms or a strip of clam on a simple hi-lo rig fished slow on the bottom in 10 to 15 feet is the move, and the better bite's been on the last of the dropping tide when the current slows enough for fish to actually find the bait.

Wells Harbor and Ogunquit have been quieter on the striper front but worth mentioning for cod and groundfish starting to show interest as the water cools. Nothing dramatic yet, but jigging over structure outside the harbor mouth with a diamond jig tipped with a strip of squid has produced a few keeper-class cod for boats willing to work the deeper water. That's the trade Southern Maine makes every October — surf bass fade, bottom fish wake up.

Looking ahead, expect the next 48 to 72 hours to keep squeezing the striper window tighter as water temps keep sliding and the migration south continues on schedule. The better bet through the weekend is treating bass as a bonus and planning around bluefish and mackerel at Pine Point, plus groundfish starting to firm up off Wells and Ogunquit. If you've only got one evening left in you before the surf season really shuts down, Biddeford Pool on the incoming is still the smartest few hours on this stretch of coast — just don't expect it to last much longer.
