# Bay water hits 74 degrees as fluke bite fires on the ebb at Marine Parkway
*Pre-full moon tides flush bait through the inlet while northwest winds stack surf bass on Rockaway Beach.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — June 19** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The bay is cooking at 74 degrees this week, and that temperature spike is lighting up the fluke bite like I haven't seen since early May. I've been watching the thermometer climb steadily — we're running a solid 7 degrees warmer than the ocean side of Rockaway Inlet, and that gradient is doing exactly what it should: stacking bait on the flood and holding fish on the ebb.

The Marine Parkway Bridge has been the money spot all week. I'm talking keeper fluke to 22 inches coming over the rail on white Gulp Swimming Mullets rigged on 3/4-ounce chartreuse bucktails. The key is working that 20 to 30-foot zone on the outgoing tide, especially the last two hours of the ebb when the current really starts ripping. I've been dragging the bucktail slow — just enough to keep it off bottom — and letting the Gulp do the talking.

What's making this bite special is the bait concentration. The northwest winds we've had the past few days have pushed massive schools of spearing and peanut bunker tight to the bridge pilings. You can see them dimpling the surface on the slack, and when the tide starts moving, the fluke are right there waiting. I watched one angler boat six keepers in two hours yesterday morning, all on the same white Gulp and chartreuse combo.

The Cross Bay Bridge has been producing too, but you need to adjust your approach. The water's a bit shallower there — 15 to 25 feet — so I've been dropping down to 1/2-ounce bucktails to slow the fall. Pink Gulp has been outfishing white at Cross Bay, probably because there's more sand eel activity in that stretch.

Weakfish are mixed in with the fluke, which tells me the water temperature is dialed in perfectly. I picked up three weakies to 18 inches on Tuesday, all on the same bucktail rig but worked a bit more aggressively. These fish want movement — sharp jigs up off bottom, not the slow drag that's working for fluke.

Canarsie Pier has been solid for porgies and the occasional short bass. The porgie bite is textbook summer fishing — drop a high-low rig with sandworms or clams, and you'll have dinner in an hour. But the real story at Canarsie is the bait. I'm seeing massive schools of bunker staging in the deeper water off the pier, and where there's bunker, bass aren't far behind.

Speaking of bass, the surf at Breezy Point and Rockaway Beach is starting to show signs of life. The northwest winds have been perfect for stacking bait against the beach, and I'm hearing reports of schoolies in the 20 to 26-inch range hitting bucktails and soft plastics on the incoming tide. Nothing huge yet, but the setup is there. The key is fishing the last two hours of the incoming, right around sunset when the bait gets pushed tight to the beach.

The water clarity has been excellent throughout the bay — that northwest wind pattern has kept the stirred-up water from the ocean side from pushing into our back-bay spots. Visibility is running 4 to 6 feet in most areas, which is perfect for sight-fishing and lets the fish really key in on your presentation.

Looking ahead, we've got a full moon this weekend, which means spring tides and serious current movement. That's going to flush even more bait through the system and should fire up both the fluke bite and potentially bring some bigger bass into the mix. I'm planning to be at the Marine Parkway Bridge at first light Sunday morning — that flood-to-ebb transition on a spring tide is when the magic happens.

The only caution I'll throw out is for kayak anglers. These northwest winds, combined with the outgoing tide, can push you offshore faster than you think. I saw a rescue situation near Canarsie earlier this week — nothing serious, but a good reminder to check your drift and stay aware of the wind direction.

Bottom line: if you've been waiting for the bay to turn on, this is your week. The water temperature is perfect, the bait is thick, and the fish are feeding. Get out there and get bent.
