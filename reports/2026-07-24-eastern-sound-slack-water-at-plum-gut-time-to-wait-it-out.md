# Slack Water at Plum Gut — Time to Wait It Out
*The neap has gutted the current everywhere I fish, and the bass know it — here's where I'm putting my time until the tide rebuilds.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 24** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Two a.m. Wednesday, chest-deep on the Fishers Island side of the Gut, and I'm throwing a 2.5-ounce black Darter into what should be five knots of ripping current. Instead I get a lazy swirl, barely enough push to load the plug. That's the neap talking. We flagged this last week — said the first-quarter neap on the 21st would soften the Gut's current and change the presentation window, and that's exactly what's happened. The problem is it's softened it more than I wanted. This isn't the controlled, easier-to-work current we like — it's borderline dead water during the mid-stages of the tide, and the bass have noticed.

Here's the read: with Kings Point pushing 70.9 and even New London sitting at 69.4, the whole Sound's warm enough that stripers have gone full nocturnal, and they want current to ambush from. Take the current away and they scatter off the structure instead of holding tight to it. I worked The Race Tuesday night on the last of the max ebb — still got a push there, it holds current longer than the Gut on a neap — and connected on two bass in the mid-20s on a 3-ounce bucktail with a white pork rind trailer, bounced right along the eastern edge where the rocks drop into 40 feet. But the bite window was tight. Fifteen, twenty minutes around the peak, then it went dead calm and the fish just quit.

Plum Gut itself has been a grind. I fished it four nights this week and only two produced anything worth keeping — a 28-incher and a fish just under 30 that came off a swimming plug worked slow along the north side rip line, right where the sandy bottom meets the rock pile off Orient Point. Both fish came in that narrow window right before max current when the flow was building but not yet howling. That's the pattern right now — you're not fishing the peak, you're fishing the approach to the peak. Show up too early, nothing's moving. Show up right at max on a neap tide, there's not enough push to matter anyway. The sweet spot has shrunk to almost nothing, and if you're not standing in the right spot at the right ten minutes, you're just casting into flat water.

Fluke have actually been the more dependable program this week, which tells you something about how tough the bass bite's been. I've been picking up keeper-class fish to 21 inches on the drift off Plum Island in 35 to 45 feet, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, working the deeper humps where the current still moves enough to keep bait stacked. Mixed in with a lot of shorts, but the quality fish are there if you're willing to grind through them. Same story as everywhere else in the Sound right now — warm shallow water has pushed the good ones deeper, and you've got to fish the humps and channel edges instead of the flats.

Blues have been sporadic out here compared to what's happening to the west. I haven't seen the kind of bunker-driven blitz that's tearing up the South Shore and pushing into western Long Island Sound, but I've had pockets of jumbo blues — eight, nine pounds — show up unannounced off Orient Point breaking on peanut bunker schools mid-morning. When that happens, it's fifteen minutes of chaos on top water poppers, then they're gone. Worth having a rod rigged and ready if you're out there, but I wouldn't plan a trip around it yet. If that bunker migration keeps pushing east like it has been west of here, that changes fast — and given how aggressively it's been spreading, I wouldn't be shocked to see a real blitz develop off Orient or Fishers Island within the week.

Porgies and blackfish have been the honest, blue-collar program right now — steady, not exciting, but they're feeding. I've been anchoring up on structure off Fishers Island in 25 to 30 feet, clam and green crab on standard porgy rigs, and putting decent numbers in the cooler even on the slower tide stages. That's your fallback when the current-dependent spots go quiet, and this week it's been a legitimate fallback, not a last resort.

Looking ahead: the moon's working toward full in early August, which means the current in the Race and the Gut should start rebuilding fast over the next several days as we move off this neap cycle. That's when I expect the bass bite to snap back into shape — the current edges will sharpen, the ambush points will reload, and the nocturnal feeding windows should get a lot more productive because the fish will actually have moving water to work with. If I had one night to fish this weekend, I'd wait for the current to build back toward the third or fourth day past the neap and hit the Race on the last two hours of the ebb, eel or a big swimming plug, right along that eastern rock edge in 35 to 45 feet. Until then, I'm splitting time between fluke on the humps and blackfish on structure, and I'm not wasting energy fighting dead water in the Gut waiting for a current that isn't there yet.
