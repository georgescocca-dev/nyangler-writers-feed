# Blues Crash Higgins Beach, Mackerel Flood the York River Before the New Moon Push
*Full moon bait schools tightened up this week and the first real blitzes are showing — here's where the plugs are connecting from Kittery to Cape Elizabeth.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — September 7** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

Guys working the beach at Higgins this week watched bluefish push a mackerel school right up onto the sandbar in broad daylight, water churning white for ten minutes before it just shut off. That's the tell. The full moon that peaked last night pulled the bait schools tight the way it always does this time of year, and the predators are starting to lean into it instead of just picking at the edges. We flagged this — said the full moon on the 6th and 7th would tighten those bait schools up and the first legitimate fall blitz activity was coming within the week. This is the start of it.

Mackerel are running thick in the tidal stretches around Kittery and York, and that's the engine driving everything right now. Where the mackerel go, the bass and blues follow, and they're following them right into the river mouths on the push. The pattern is textbook early fall: cooler nights, tightening bait, stripers shaking off the summer sulk and starting to hunt with purpose. It's not a full-blown blitz everywhere yet — plenty of stretches were quiet this week, especially midday with high sun and slack water — but the bookend tides, first light and the last hour of dusk, are producing.

Biddeford Pool remains the best hour in Southern Maine fishing, and this week proved it again. The incoming tide pushing water into the Pool concentrates bait against the sandbar and the rocks off the eastern shore, and bass have been stacking on that current seam waiting for it to get pushed to them. Guys throwing Danny plugs and Gibbs pencil poppers on the last two hours of the incoming have been connecting with bass in the high 20s to low 30s, with a few better fish mixed in on the darker, more turbulent stretches of tide near the mouth. If you fish it, get there before the tide turns — once it starts spilling out the other way, the bite dies fast.

Higgins Beach is where the bluefish have been putting on the show. Mackerel schools getting run up onto the bar have blues crashing through them in the mid-morning and again toward evening, and anyone with a metal — a Kastmaster or a Hopkins in the 1- to 2-ounce range — has been able to get in on it when the birds show. Bluefish have run from 3 to 6 pounds, nothing giant, but plenty of them, and they don't require finesse. Cast into the working birds, retrieve fast, hang on.

Pine Point has been more subdued but not dead — a mix of school bass and bluefish working the channel edges on the outgoing, best worked with bucktails tipped with a strip of mackerel belly for scent, bounced along the bottom in 8 to 12 feet as the tide empties out past the jetty. Prouts Neck has needed the right wind to fish well this week, and when it swung southwest for a couple of days midweek, it turned on briefly — bass working the rocks on the north side, eating swimming plugs worked slow through the wash. When the wind goes onshore out of the east, that spot turns into whitewater soup and isn't worth the walk out.

Cape Elizabeth's ledges are still holding a few cod for guys willing to drop bait — clam or mackerel chunks on a high-low rig, fished tight to structure in 30 to 50 feet off the rockier points. It's not a numbers game this time of year, more a bonus fish while you're working the area for stripers, but a few keepers have come up. Flounder have been scattered but present in Wells Harbor and up in the Ogunquit River on the calmer tides, best on sandworms or clam strips fished on a simple two-hook flounder rig in the softer mud bottom near the marina, mostly a morning bite before boat traffic picks up.

Water along the beaches has been reasonably clean, no major runoff issues, and the moon's already done its work tightening the bait. The next real trigger is the new moon arriving September 10, and that's the tide to watch. New moon tides run harder and push more water, which typically means more bait getting shoved into the river mouths and estuaries — exactly the kind of setup that turns scattered activity into the real blitzes. Expect bass numbers in the rivers around Kittery and York to build through the weekend as nights keep cooling, with Biddeford Pool likely to be the most consistent single spot through the coming week regardless of what the open beaches are doing.

If you've only got one tide to fish in the next few days, make it the incoming at Biddeford Pool in the two hours before high, working a Danny plug or a bone-colored pencil popper along the current seam off the eastern rocks. Backup plan is Higgins at first light — watch for the birds working the mackerel schools, and if they're up, get metal in the water fast because those windows have been closing quick. Prouts Neck is worth checking only if the wind holds out of the southwest; otherwise save the gas.
