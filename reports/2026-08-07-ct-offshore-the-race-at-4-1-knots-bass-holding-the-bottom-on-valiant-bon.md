# The Race at 4.1 Knots: Bass Holding the Bottom on Valiant, Bonito Still a Rumor at Plum Gut
*Building current into the new moon has stripers locked to structure on wire line while the Gut waits on its first albie of the season.*

**FISHERS ISLAND SOUND, CT — August 8** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

4.1 knots. That's what The Race was running on the back half of Thursday's ebb, and that number tells you almost everything you need to know about this week. When the current's cranking that hard through Race Rock, stripers don't chase — they park. They tuck behind the boulders and let the tide do the work, and that means wire line and bucktails bounced tight to structure, not swimming plugs worked through open water.

We flagged this last week — said building tidal flow into the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure — and that's exactly what's happening at Valiant Rock right now. The rock itself sits in 35 to 50 feet depending on where you set up on it, and the fish have been holding on the down-current side, right where the rip folds over the structure and drops into the eddy. I've been running wire trolled bunker spoons through there on the last hour of ebb, then switching to 3- and 4-ounce bucktails — white with a chartreuse tube, or plain bone-white when the water's clean — for the slack itself. This week's slack window at Valiant has been running late morning, roughly ten to twelve workable minutes before the rip fires back up going the other way. Miss that window and you're fighting the current instead of fishing it.

The fish themselves are a mixed bag, and I'll be straight about that. Some drifts through Race Rock we're picking at schoolies in the low twenties, other passes we're connecting on fish pushing into the upper thirties, occasionally better. It's not a slam-dunk trip — I had one charter last Tuesday where we worked the rock for ninety minutes and only had two fish to show for it, then Wednesday morning the same spot gave up six keepers in forty minutes on the identical tide stage. That's the nature of fishing a rip this strong — the bite window is narrow and you need to be sitting on the spot when the current speed and the bait line up, not just anywhere near it.

Water's reading 69.4°F down at the east end of the Sound, and that's clean, cool water compared to what's sitting up in the Western Sound near 71. That gradient matters — the bass holding on Valiant and Race Rock are sitting in water that hasn't been beat up by the same heat stress you're seeing further west, and it's part of why this stretch keeps producing quality fish through the dog days while other parts of the Sound go quiet on the bass.

Plum Gut and the Sluiceway have been a different story this week — and a slower one. The current through the Gut has been ripping just as hard as The Race, 3 to 4 knots on the bigger tides, but I haven't had a single confirmed albie or bonito report out of there yet. The push everyone's watching is still working its way out of the Rhode Island corridor and into Buzzards Bay — it hasn't crossed the Sound to us. Given how fast that building tidal range through the August 12 new moon usually pulls bait and predators west, I'd bet on the first bonito or albie showing in the Gut or off Montauk within the next week, but it's not here yet, and I'm not going to tell you to run out and burn gas on epoxy jigs before the fish arrive. Have your Deadly Dick and half-ounce epoxy jigs rigged and ready, because when that push does show, it shows fast and it's usually gone just as quick if you're not on it.

Sea bass have been the reliable play while we wait on the bonito. Little Gull Island and Great Gull Island both have humps and rockpiles holding fish in 40 to 60 feet, and jigging bucktails tipped with squid strips has been steady — not a blowup bite, but a consistent one, fish in the 14 to 17 inch range with the occasional bigger knothead mixed in. If you want a sure thing this week, that's closer to it than the bass rips.

Bluefish have been building back in on the western reefs of the Sound, and if that pattern keeps sliding east the way it usually does in mid-August, I expect we'll start seeing gator blues showing up on the same structure we're fishing for bass — Race Rock, Valiant, the edges of the Gut. Worth having a wire leader rigged behind the bucktail just in case, because a bluefish will cut you off on a plain mono leader before you even feel the strike.

One more thing worth passing along if you're running east toward Block Island — there's a real shark predation problem on released stripers over there right now, mortality on released fish running uncomfortably high. It hasn't been an issue on our side of the Sound, but if you're making that run, keep your fight times short and get fish back in the water fast.

Looking ahead: the tidal range is only going to keep building into the August 12 new moon, which should sharpen the bass bite further at Race Rock and Valiant — bigger swings mean harder current, and harder current on this structure has been good to us all week. My money's on the bonito push finally crossing into Plum Gut and the eastern Sound sometime in the next seven to ten days behind that stronger tide. Until then, I'd spend my time on wire and bucktails over the rocks and keep the epoxy jigs in the bag, rigged and waiting.
