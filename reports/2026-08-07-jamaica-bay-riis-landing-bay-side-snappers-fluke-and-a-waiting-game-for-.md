# Riis Landing Bay Side: Snappers, Fluke, and a Waiting Game for the Bridge
*With the ocean still churned up from last week's blow, I launched off the bay side and let the tide do the sorting — here's what showed up.*

**FORT TILDEN, NY — August 7** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

I made the call Tuesday morning to skip the ocean beach entirely and put the kayak in at Riis Landing, on the bay side of Fort Tilden. That's the move when the surf's still got a leftover shoulder on it and the wind hasn't fully settled — you get protected water, you can actually see what you're doing, and honestly this time of year the bay side wakes up earlier in the day anyway before the sun cooks the flats. I've fished that stretch enough summers to know it rewards patience more than horsepower, and this week that patience paid off in bits and pieces rather than one big score.

We flagged this last week — said the post-frontal stir after that SSW blow would cool the water and clean it up right as the neap softened the current, and that's more or less what happened. Tuesday through Thursday gave me a noticeably better window than the days right after the front came through. The water off Riis Landing had that clean, slightly green color instead of the churned-up brown it had over the weekend, and the fish responded to it — not a blitz, but a real bite window in the two hours around the last of the outgoing.

Snapper blues are thick in the channels right now, and I mean thick — anywhere the current runs past a break in the marsh grass or funnels past a culvert, you'll find pods of them chasing whatever's small and silver. If you've got a kid or a buddy who just wants bent rods, small silver spoons or a sliver of spearing on a snap swivel under a bobber is all you need, fished right off Canarsie Pier or the Ave U bridge up in Gerritsen Creek. No permit hassle at either spot, which matters this year with the Gateway passes sold out — Canarsie Pier and the creek mouth at Marine Park are your no-fuss options if you didn't get in on that March sale.

Fluke are still around but they've pushed off the shallow flats and settled onto the deeper channel edges and drop-offs where the bottom stays a touch cooler under all this August sun. I picked up two keepers and a handful of shorts drifting the edge of the channel off Riis Landing in 12 to 15 feet, white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a half-ounce bucktail, working it slow along bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing. That's been the pattern all week — the fluke bite dies fast once the tide goes dead, so you've got a window and you need to be in position for it, not driving around looking for it.

Stripers are the harder story right now. Daytime bay fishing for bass in August is mostly a bust — they know better than to sit in shallow warm water with the sun beating down, so if you want them you're fishing the dark hours, and the spot everybody with a brain points to is the Marine Parkway Bridge on the outgoing. The current sluicing through those pilings at night pulls bait past the structure and stages fish right in the seam. I had one short bass and lost what felt like a better one to a piling on 20-pound braid fishing a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig off the rocks there Wednesday night, incoming tide bleeding into the first hour of the outgoing. It's not a numbers game right now — one or two fish a tide if you put your time in, which is honestly about right for early August in the bay.

Porgies have been a reliable backup when the rest of it's slow. Working bloodworm or clam on a hi-lo rig around the Cross Bay Bridge structure and the deeper holes off Floyd Bennett Field will put fish in the cooler even on the days everything else goes quiet. Weakfish reports have been thin and scattered — a couple of guys mentioned marking them on the flats near Motts Basin but I haven't put one in the boat myself this week, so take that as a maybe, not a target.

Here's the thing about this stretch — we're sitting in that in-between spot on the moon. The neap that softened everything out on the 5th made for slack, lazy tides, which is exactly why the daytime bite got a little better even as the nighttime bass bite stayed sluggish; less push means less urgency for a fish that wants current to work. That changes fast. The new moon springs building for August 11-13 are going to re-arm those bridge ebbs with real current, and that's when I expect the Marine Parkway pattern to sharpen back up — bigger tide swings mean more bait getting pushed and pulled past that structure, and bass respond to that the way they always have. If I've got one night to fish next week, that's the window I'm circling — first big push of moving water on the outgoing, right at the bridge, bunker chunks or a swimming plug worked along the current seam.

For access, if you're bay-side and don't want to fight for a Gateway permit spot, Riis Landing and Canarsie Pier are your friends this month. If you want the ocean structure and don't mind the walk, Fort Tilden's 193rd Street lot still has the Fishing Access Pass system running and the groins there hold bass on the outgoing once the surf settles down from this latest blow. I'd give the ocean side another few days to lay down before I commit a whole trip to it — the bay's been the more honest bet this week, even if it's not blowing anybody's doors off.
