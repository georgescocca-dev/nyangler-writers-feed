# Trophy bass crash Reynolds Channel as bunker schools pack the inlet
*42-pound striper highlights explosive action from the jetties to the back bay bridges.*

**JONES INLET, NY — June 25** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

The inlet's been on fire this week, and I'm talking about the kind of fishing that makes you forget about everything else. A 42-pound bass came off the back jetty at Reynolds Channel on fresh bunker chunks — that's the kind of fish that reminds you why we do this. But that monster wasn't alone.

The bunker schools are thick from the inlet mouth all the way back to the Wantagh Bridge, and the bass are following them like a dinner bell. I've been watching this setup build for two weeks now, and it finally exploded. The key has been the outgoing tide — start of the drop is when these fish move. The inlet's been weedy, sure, but that's not stopping anybody who knows how to fish it.

Fluke action's been solid but not spectacular. The Meadowbrook Bridge area is holding fish, with boats stacking up on the southeast side during the incoming. Most of the action's been shorts in the 15-17 inch range, but there's enough keeper potential to make it worthwhile. White bucktails with smelt have been the ticket — hook the smelt behind the head and let it work in the current. The fish want that natural presentation.

What's really got my attention is the weakfish showing up. That 26-inch, 7-pound fish that came out of Fire Island Inlet tells me the comeback is real. I've been hearing whispers about weakies in the back bays, and with the bait concentrations we're seeing, it makes sense. These fish follow the same patterns as the bass — look for them on the moving water.

The sunset bite has been phenomenal at Jones Beach. Diamond jigs with green tails are working the sandbars just before the trough. The fish are sitting shallow at low light, and if you can get your jig past the break into that wash, you're in business. SP Minnows after dark are cleaning up for the guys who stay late.

Bait situation is excellent. Peanut bunker are everywhere, and the sand eels are thick in the surf zone. If you're fishing the inlet, fresh bunker chunks on fishfinder rigs are producing the biggest fish. For the fluke guys, squid and smelt combo is still the standard, but don't overlook sea robin strips — they're working better than they should.

Water's been clean despite all the boat traffic, and the temperature gradient between the bay and ocean is holding fish on both sides of the inlet. The birds have been working overtime on the small bait, which tells me the food chain is stacked up right.

Looking ahead, we've got a new moon coming Friday, which means spring tides and serious current. That's when the inlet really comes alive — the big tides flush everything out and concentrate the bait. I'm expecting the bass bite to get even better, especially on the evening outgoing. The fluke should spread out more as the stronger current gives them better feeding opportunities.

If you're planning to fish this weekend, get to the inlet early for the bass bite, then work the bridges and channels for fluke as the tide builds. The action's there — you just need to be in the right place when the water moves.
