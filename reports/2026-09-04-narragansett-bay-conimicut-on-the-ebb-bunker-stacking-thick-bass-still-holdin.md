# Conimicut on the ebb: bunker stacking thick, bass still holding their cards
*The bait's arrived in force from Conimicut to Rocky Point, but the stripers are staged, not blitzing — yet.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — September 4** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Walk the piers off the Providence River this week and you can't miss it — bunker balled up thick enough to shadow the bottom, from the flats off Conimicut Point down through Greenwich Bay and around Nyatt Point. Guys working the bridges are seeing peanut bunker pushed up against the pilings on the last of the ebb, and there's talk up and down the docks that this is the heaviest bait has looked in the upper bay since the herring run cleared out of Buckeye Brook back in the spring. That's a different bite trigger than herring — this is bunker season, and it's the one that sets up the fall run every year.

The water hasn't cooled off enough to flip the switch yet. Numbers coming out of the Sound side — Kings Point running near 71, New London closer to 69 — line up with what the bay's holding right now, and that's still warm enough to keep stripers content to sit on the bait instead of running it down on top. We flagged this pattern building last week, said the bay's bunker schools were thickening up the same way they were in Raritan Bay and along the LI south shore, and here we are — the bait's arrived, but the blitz hasn't tripped off yet. That's the honest read. Anybody telling you the bay's exploding right now is getting ahead of the calendar.

What's going to change that is the moon. It's building toward full over the next couple days, and that means more water moving through the bay on every tide — Conimicut, the East Passage, the flats off Rocky Point are all going to see stronger current than they have in weeks. Stronger current concentrates bait. Concentrated bait is what turns staged bass into committed bass. If there's a cool night or two mixed in behind that moon, even better — that's usually the spark. Right now it's a matter of days, not weeks, before this tightens up into real surface activity somewhere in the bay. Conimicut Point and the mouth of Greenwich Bay are the first places worth checking when it does.

In the meantime, the fishing that's actually happening is solid if you work it right. Striped bass are there, they're just not stupid about it. The better bite has been on the last two hours of the ebb at Conimicut, working bucktails — half-ounce to an ounce, white or chartreuse — bounced along the bottom where the current pulls off the point into deeper water. Guys live-lining bunker off Nyatt Point on wire or a simple fish-finder rig have been picking off keeper-class bass in the 28 to 34-inch range on the front of the incoming, right where the current stacks against the rip. Snag-and-drop with a bunker on a treble is still the most reliable way to connect when the schools are balled tight and not chasing anything — snag one, let it flutter down, and hang on.

Fluke are on their way out of the upper bay, and that's right on schedule for early September. The back-bay numbers around Greenwich Bay and the Providence River flats have thinned out fast over the past two weeks as the bigger fish slide toward deeper structure down toward Prudence Island and out past the East Passage channel edges. There are still keeper fish being picked off drifting Gulp on bucktails in 20 to 30 feet along the channel drop-offs, but it's not the numbers game it was in July. If fluke's the target, the next couple weeks are the window before they clear out of the bay entirely for ocean-side structure.

Bluefish have been the most dependable thing going in the bay right now, and that tracks with what's happening basin-wide — blues are chewing hard wherever bunker sits, and the bay's no exception. Rocky Point and the rips off Nyatt Point have had steady action on bunker spoons and topwater poppers worked through the peanut bunker schools, especially on the tide change when the bait gets pinned against structure. These aren't giants, mostly 3 to 6 pounds, but they're aggressive and they're consistent, which is more than you can say for the stripers right now.

Scup are stacked on the rocky ledges the way they always are this time of year — around the Nyatt Point rocks and the drop-offs near Prudence Island, bloodworms or squid strips on a simple high-low rig near bottom on the last of the flood has been steady. Black sea bass are holding on the deeper structure around Prudence, still worth a stop if you're passing through with a few clam-baited jigs, though that bite's better suited to a mixed bag trip than a target run at this point in the season.

Looking ahead, the next several days are about watching the moon build and keeping an eye on the overnight air. If a real cool front slides through behind this full moon, the upper bay could tip into its first legitimate blitz window within the week to ten days — that's the pattern this setup usually follows. Until then, work the tide changes at Conimicut and Nyatt Point, stick with bunker imitations and live bait over anything flashy, and don't be surprised if the bass stay quiet on top even while they're feeding hard underneath. That's exactly what staged fish on heavy bait look like right before they commit.
