# Bunker Density Builds on the Flats — Bluefish Cash In, Bass Still Waiting on the Moon
*Flynn's Knoll and the Verrazzano approaches are stacked with peanut bunker and blitzing blues, but the real striper trigger is still a week or two out.*

**KEYPORT, NJ — August 31** — _by Marisol Duarte, Zone Writer_

If there's only one call to make on the bay this week, it's this: fish the bunker, not the moon. The peanut bunker schools that have been building in Raritan Bay all month have tightened up into real density now, especially off the Verrazzano approaches, on the flats south of the shipping channel, and around Flynn's Knoll. That's where the boat traffic is, that's where the birds are working, and that's where bluefish are cashing in daily. We flagged this last week — said the bunker schools were building fast and the blitzes on peanuts would become a daily occurrence off the Knoll and near the Verrazzano side — and that's exactly what the fleet is finding right now.

The decision for this week is simple: split your time between the bunker blitzes for blues and the channel edges for fluke, and treat the striper bite as a bonus, not the primary target. Water in the bay is still carrying summer heat, and stripers haven't committed to the bait the way they will once real tidal flow kicks in. The moon builds toward full on September 6, and that's the trigger to watch — more push on the tide means more bait getting corralled against structure, and that's when Raritan Bay stripers stop picking at the edges and start showing up in numbers on top.

Right now, Flynn's Knoll on the incoming tide is holding bluefish in the 3-to-6-pound class, working peanut bunker balled up in 8 to 15 feet of water. Metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Kastmasters and Deadly Dicks — worked through the surface commotion is getting bit as fast as you can put them back in the water. If the blitz shows itself, don't overthink it: match the bunker size, keep the retrieve erratic, and expect follow-up strikes right at the boat. Topwater walkers like a Zara Spook or a Yo-Zuri Mag Popper will also draw blues up when the peanuts are pinned tight against the surface, which has been happening most mornings on the flats south of the channel near the Verrazzano side.

Stripers are mixed into those same blitzes but in smaller numbers and mostly on the shorter end of the slot. The better bass have been coming from the edges of the action rather than the middle of the chaos — working bunker spoons or white bucktails with a curly-tail trailer along the drop where the flat falls into the channel, particularly on the last two hours of the incoming into Flynn's Knoll and the flats off Keyport. If you want a shot at a better fish, work that transition zone slow, right where the bunker school thins out and the bigger predators sit and pick off stragglers.

Fluke fishing has settled into its typical late-August pattern — fair, not fast, and increasingly concentrated on structure and channel edges rather than open flats. The warm bay water has pushed fluke toward the deeper, better-oxygenated cuts: the edges of the Chapel Hill and Ambrose channels, the deeper water off Romer Shoal, and the drop-offs along Keyport Flats where the bottom falls from 15 feet down into the 25-to-35-foot range. Gulp Swimming Mullets in white or pink shad, paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along bottom on the outgoing tide, have been the most consistent producer. This is a normal seasonal transition, not a collapse — fluke should stay catchable on those channel edges through September before the offshore migration really takes hold, so there's still time to put a few keepers in the cooler if you're willing to work the deeper structure instead of the shallow flats.

The Navesink and Shrewsbury rivers are quieter right now but worth a look on evening tides, especially for anyone chasing weakfish around the deeper holes and bridge structure — that bite has been sporadic, best described as a fish here and there rather than a pattern, and it's not worth building a whole trip around yet. Porgies and blackfish remain a backup play around Romer Shoal and the rockier stretches off the Hook, best fished on the bottom of the tide with clam or green crab when the wind allows for a clean drift.

Looking at the next few days, expect the bluefish blitzes to keep firing as long as the bunker stay concentrated, and that's likely through the holiday weekend given how tight those schools have packed in. The bigger story is what happens as the moon fills. If the tidal flow builds the way it should heading into September 6, look for the first real signs of the fall striper push — bigger fish showing up tight to the bunker schools instead of picking at the margins. That's the shift to watch for at Flynn's Knoll, the flats off Keyport, and the deeper edges around Romer Shoal. Until then, work the blitzes for blues, work the channel edges for fluke, and keep an eye on the tide charts leading into Labor Day weekend — that's when this bay usually tips from summer pattern into fall pattern, and this year looks right on schedule.
