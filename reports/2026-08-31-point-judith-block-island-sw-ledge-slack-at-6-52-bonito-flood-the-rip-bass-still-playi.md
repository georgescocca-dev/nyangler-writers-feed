# SW Ledge Slack at 6:52: Bonito Flood the Rip, Bass Still Playing It Coy
*The bonito finally showed in numbers off Block Island this week, right on schedule — the bass push is close but not here yet.*

**GALILEE, RI — August 31** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack water hit SW Ledge at 6:52 this morning, and for about twenty minutes on either side of it the surface out there looked like it was boiling — not blitzing bass, bonito. That's the story of the week down here. We flagged it last week, said the bonito numbers would build as the days went on, and that's exactly what happened. The push that was flashing off the south side of Block Island a few days ago has thickened into a real bite, with fish showing consistently on the rips off SW Ledge, out past North Light, and along the tide lines running off Sandy Point on the last two hours of the ebb.

Water's still carrying summer heat — Montauk read 69.1 this week and New London 69.4, and the water around the Block has been tracking in that same range, which is exactly the kind of thermal window bonito like before they push further west. The bait's tiny — silversides and rain bait getting pushed around by the current — and that's why the standard albie game is working: half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce epoxy jigs in pink/white and bone, small metal like Deadly Dicks and Hogy epoxies, and soft plastics like Albie Snax on light leader, 20-pound fluoro max, because these fish get leader shy fast when the bait's this small. Cast into the boils, let it sink for a two-count, then work it back with a fast, erratic retrieve — anything too smooth gets ignored. The bite windows have been tight, mostly tied to the tide changes at the Ledge rather than time of day, which tracks — moving water concentrates the bait, and moving water is what's turning these fish on.

False albacore are starting to show the same pattern up in Nantucket Sound and off the Cape, and if the last few Augusts are any guide, that push works its way toward us before it works toward Long Island. Worth watching the water off Clay Head and the east side of the island over the next week — albies tend to show there first locally once they arrive, working the same small-bait pattern as the bonito but usually a touch fussier about presentation.

Striped bass are the one piece of this puzzle that hasn't fallen into place yet. There's fish around — some being picked off structure off SW Ledge on bucktails during the tide change, and reports of stray bass mixed in with the bluefish working bunker pods closer to Point Judith — but this isn't the fall push yet. The trigger for that is bait density, and right now the bunker schools building down through Narragansett Bay and along the south shore aren't concentrated enough to pull the bigger class of fish into a real pattern. The moon's building toward full over the next week, which means more tidal push, which is usually what tightens up those bunker schools and gets the bass committing in numbers. If you want a bass this week, the better bet is working bucktails — white or chartreuse, 1 to 1.5 ounces — slow along the bottom on the SW Ledge tide change, rather than expecting a blitz on top.

Fluke fishing has done what it typically does this time of year — it's softened. Boats working the deeper edges off Block Island and the channels near Galilee are still picking fish, but it's a fair bite, not a red-hot one. Warm bay water has pushed fluke toward the inlets and ocean-front structure where there's better oxygen and cooler bottom temps. Gulp Swimming Mullets in white or pink shad, paired with a half-ounce to one-ounce bucktail, dragged slow along channel edges and drop-offs, is still the most consistent combination. This window's closing as we move into September, so if fluke's the target, the next week or two is the time to fit it in before attention shifts fully to the fall run.

Bluefish have been the most dependable fish in the water all week, and that's not surprising with bunker schools thick from Narragansett Bay out past the Ledge. Warm water's kept them aggressive, and they've been busting up on top most mornings, especially where bait's getting pinned against structure on the last of the ebb. Poppers and metal lip swimmers work when they're up top; when they're not, diamond jigs bounced through the school get eaten fast. Black sea bass remain a solid, if unglamorous, bycatch on the deeper structure around the Ledge and out toward the Block — steady fishing on squid and clam baits fished tight to bottom, and that should hold into the early fall transition without much drama.

Looking at the next few days: the bonito bite should hold, and likely improve, as more fish filter down from the Cape and Nantucket Sound behind the leading edge already here — that's the pattern this species has followed for years, and there's no reason to expect different this time. The bass situation is the one to watch closely. If the moon-driven tidal push over the coming week concentrates bait the way it usually does heading into Labor Day, expect the first real signs of the fall bass bite to show up in the bunker-heavy stretches before it shows on the open rips. Until then, SW Ledge on the tide change is still the smartest use of a morning — bonito on top, bass and bluefish underneath, and enough happening to make the trip worth the fuel.
