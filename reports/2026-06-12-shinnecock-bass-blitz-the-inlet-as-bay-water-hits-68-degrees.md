# Bass blitz the inlet as bay water hits 68 degrees
*Outgoing tides after dark are producing keeper stripers on live bunker and plugs.*

**SHINNECOCK INLET, NY — June 12** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The night bite is absolutely on fire right now. Bay water finally cracked 68 degrees this week, and that thermal gradient between the cooler ocean and warmer bay is stacking bait on every flood tide and holding bass on the ebb. I've been working the inlet from sunset to midnight, and the action has been consistent enough to set your watch by.

Best fishing starts about an hour into the outgoing tide when the bay starts draining hard. Bass are staging just outside the inlet mouth in 25 to 30 feet, ambushing peanut bunker and spearing getting swept out on the current. Live-lined peanuts on a simple fishfinder rig are absolutely deadly — 3/0 circle hook, 30-pound fluorocarbon leader, just enough weight to get down in the current. Let the bait swim naturally in the flow, and when a bass picks it up, count to three before setting the hook.

Plugs are working just as well for guys who prefer artificials. Swimming plugs like the Bomber Long A in bunker patterns, worked slow and steady through the current breaks, are getting crushed. The key is staying tight to the structure — those bass are hugging the jetty rocks and the channel edges where the current creates eddies and confusion.

Sizes have been impressive. Most fish are running 24 to 32 inches, with a few pushing the 36-inch mark. Had one angler show me photos of a 38-incher he released Tuesday night on a live bunker. The fish are fat and healthy, clearly feeding well on the abundant bait.

Fluke fishing in the bay has been solid during daylight hours. The Ponquogue Bridge area is producing steady action on the drift with bucktails and Gulp. White and chartreuse 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with 4-inch Swimming Mullets are the go-to rig. Fish the deeper holes on either side of the bridge in 20 to 25 feet. The current flow under the bridge creates a natural feeding lane, and fluke are positioned along the edges waiting for bait to get swept through.

Weakfish are starting to show up in their traditional spots. The canal area has been producing a few nice ones on the evening tides. Small bucktails with pink or white teasers, bounced along the bottom in 12 to 15 feet, are getting picked up. These fish are spooky, so light tackle and quiet presentations are essential.

Bait situation is excellent. Peanut bunker are thick throughout the bay, especially around the deeper channels near Hampton Bays. Spearing are schooled up tight to the docks and bulkheads. For those throwing nets, early morning and late evening are prime times when the bait moves shallow.

Water clarity has been good despite some afternoon thunderstorms. The bay is running clean with good visibility, which is helping the fluke bite during daylight hours. Ocean side is slightly off-color from recent weather, but not enough to shut down the inlet fishing.

Looking ahead, we've got a new moon this weekend, which means spring tides and stronger current flow. That should really fire up the inlet fishing as more bait gets pushed around. The forecast shows lighter winds through the weekend, so conditions should be prime for both day and night fishing.

The weakfish bite should continue to improve as we move deeper into June. These fish typically show up in better numbers as water temperatures stabilize in the upper 60s. Keep an eye on the Tiana Beach area as well — that's been a sleeper spot for weakfish in years past.

For night fishing the inlet, remember to bring a good spotlight and know your way around in the dark. The jetties can be treacherous, and boat traffic picks up on weekends even after sunset. Fish smart, fish safe, and you'll be rewarded with some of the best striper fishing Shinnecock has to offer.
