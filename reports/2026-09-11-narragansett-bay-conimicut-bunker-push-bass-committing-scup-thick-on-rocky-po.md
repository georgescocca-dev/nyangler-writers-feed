# Conimicut bunker push: bass committing, scup thick on Rocky Point
*The new moon squeezed bunker into the corners of the bay and stripers finally started eating them instead of just following them around.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — September 11, 2026** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

Down at the piers on the Providence River, the guys chunking bunker off the old commercial docks went through three flats of bait by Wednesday afternoon, which tells you everything about how this week turned. That never happens in late August when the bass are just shadowing the schools and picking at the edges. Something flipped after the new moon on the 10th — the tide swung harder, bunker got squeezed tight against the beaches and points from Conimicut down through Nyatt and into the mouth of Greenwich Bay, and the stripers stopped following and started eating. We flagged this last week — said the bunker schools tightening under the building moon would set up the season's first real fall blitz window, and that's exactly what's playing out on the upper bay right now.

The pattern is textbook new-moon squeeze. Extra tidal push means more current running through the Providence River channel and around Conimicut Point, and that current is pinning bunker against structure instead of letting them spread out in open water. Bass don't have to work for it. Rocky Point's outer rip, the flat off Nyatt Point, and the bar running off Conimicut have all had bait balls getting worked over on the last two hours of the ebb, right when the water funnels off the flats and concentrates everything into the channel edges. Morning light has been the best window — before boat traffic scatters the bait and before the sun gets high enough to push fish down.

Striper sizes have been a mixed bag but a good one. Most of what's coming over the rail on bunker chunks and snag-and-drop rigs off Conimicut is schoolie to mid-20-inch class fish, but there have been enough 30-to-34-inch stripers mixed in around Rocky Point and off the east side of Prudence Island to keep people out there. If you're bunker-snagging, a treble on 20-pound mono ahead of a three-way rig with an 8-ounce sinker will get you into the school; once you're hooked up, drop the weight and free-line the live bunker back into the current seam. Trollers working bunker spoons behind planers along the Prudence Island shoreline have done well on the last of the ebb into the start of the flood, working the drop where 12 feet of flat falls into 25 near the north end of the island.

Fluke are a different story, and it's worth being straight about it. The keeper-grade fish that were around Greenwich Bay and the mouth of the Providence River in July have largely cleared out — the better fish are staging toward deeper, cooler water ahead of their fall exit, same pattern showing up everywhere from the mid-Atlantic north. What's left in the shallow bay is mostly short fish, one after another, on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and half-ounce bucktails worked off Nyatt Point in 15 to 20 feet. There are still a few keepers mixed in on the last two hours of the ebb when the water's moving enough to make them commit, but don't plan a trip around box-filling fluke right now. That bite belongs to the Sound this month.

Bluefish are picking up steam from the bottom up. Snapper blues are thick in Apponaug Cove and the shallows of Greenwich Bay, which is the reliable early sign that bigger blues are close behind. That's already showing — chopper blues in the 4-to-6-pound class have been busting bunker schools right alongside the stripers off Rocky Point, and anyone throwing a Deadly Dick or a bunker spoon into that surface commotion is getting bit fast. Wire leader isn't optional out there right now; the blues will cut you off on straight mono in about four casts.

Scup are stacking up the way they always do this time of year, piling onto the rock piles and ledges before they push out toward deeper water for the winter. Rocky Point's rocky drop and the ledges off Nyatt Point have both been holding good numbers in 15 to 25 feet, and squid strips or sandworms on a high-low rig with just enough weight to hold bottom on the last of the ebb will load a five-gallon bucket fast. It's some of the most dependable action in the bay right now, and it's a good option on days when the wind kicks up and makes the open bunker schools tough to fish.

Black sea bass are improving on the structure around the east side of Prudence Island as the water eases out of summer highs — nothing to build a whole trip around yet, but worth a few drops on the way past if you're anchored up on structure anyway. Weakfish have been scattered and light — a few showing on soft plastics worked slow through the Providence River channel, but not enough of a pattern yet to call it a bite.

Looking ahead, the tidal energy from the new moon should keep working through the system for a few more days before it eases off, so the window for tight, aggressive bunker-driven blitzes should hold through the weekend. If the wind stays manageable in the mornings, Conimicut and Rocky Point are the first stops — get there on the last two hours of the ebb and look for diving birds over nervous water. If it blows hard out of the south, shift into Greenwich Bay where the fetch is shorter and the snapper blues and scup will keep rods bent while the bigger stuff sorts itself out on the outside.
