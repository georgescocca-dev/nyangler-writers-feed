# New moon springs load up The Race as first bonito show at Fishers Island
*Bigger tides and ripping current are stacking bass in the eddies while bluefish and porgies keep the rail bent from Plum Gut to Orient.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — July 8** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

Last week the Sound was still shaking off the June full moon hangover — current running hard but not violent, water in the low 70s and holding steady, and the bass scattered between the classic structure instead of stacking anywhere reliable. Wind sat out of the northeast for a few days, which is usually a gift out here because it sharpens the thermal breaks along the edges of The Race and Plum Gut, but it also kept the surface a little lumpy for the guys running small boats. I fished four tides, connected on two, and both times it was about finding the exact seam where the moving water met the slower stuff — not the middle of the rip, the edge of it.

This week changes the math. We're building into new moon springs, peaking around July 11-13, and that means bigger tidal exchange than we've seen since before the last full moon. More water moving through Plum Gut and The Race in less time — current that'll run harder and longer on both the ebb and flood. For a guy who fishes current for a living, that's not a inconvenience, that's the whole game. Big springs push bait out of the back bays and eelgrass flats and funnel it straight through the pinch points. Stripers know this. They don't fight 4-5 knots of water head-on — they sit in the eddies behind Race Rock, behind the humps off Orient Point, behind the down-current side of the reef at Fishers Island, and let the current deliver dinner. The harder the water runs, the tighter those fish hold to structure, and the better the eddy bite gets right at the tide change when the current starts to slacken just enough for them to leave the shadow and feed.

Fluke have already started making the move we expect this time of year — out of the warm, stagnant bay water and into the inlets and channels where there's exchange and oxygen. Gardiners Bay and Orient Harbor water has been sitting warm and a little soft, and the fluke that were spread through there are pushing toward Plum Gut and the deeper cuts off Orient Point where the tide keeps things clean. Best fluke action this week has been on the outgoing at Plum Gut, working 4-ounce bucktails tipped with a strip of squid or a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet, bounced along bottom in 25 to 40 feet. You want to be right on the edge of the main channel where the current slows just enough to hold bottom without your bucktail skipping off into the rocks. Keeper-size fish, some pushing 5 pounds, mixed with a lot of short fish you're throwing back — a decent bite but not a slam-dunk, so cover water and don't marry one drift.

Striped bass have been the story at first light and last light, which tracks with where we are on the moon. Working the rocks at Orient Point in the dark hours, big soft-plastic swimbaits and Danny-style surface plugs have drawn strikes from fish in the 20 to 30 pound class, mostly on the last two hours of the incoming when the current softens enough to let a plug swim instead of getting swept sideways. The Race has fished better by boat — drifting bunker chunks and live eels through the rip on the last hour of the ebb, right where the current dumps into the deeper basin south of Race Rock. That's classic big-bass water, and with springs building, that current lane is going to get more violent before it gets easier, which means the eddy game becomes even more important. If you're throwing plugs from the rocks, fish the slack pockets tight to structure — don't waste casts into the screaming middle of the rip.

Bluefish have been the reliable bite when the bass go quiet, and they're not shy about it. Chopper blues in the 8 to 12 pound range have been busting bait on the surface off Fishers Island and out toward the Race on calm mornings, and if you see the birds working, a simple Hopkins or Kastmaster thrown into the melee will get bit on nearly every cast. Porgies remain a dependable bottom bite around Plum Gut and the rockpiles off Fishers — small hooks, sandworms or clam, fished on a hi-lo rig in 20 to 35 feet on the slower stages of the tide when they're not getting pushed off structure by current.

The most interesting development is what's showing up at the edges of the zone. The cold wall that's normally kept warm-water pelagics penned up around Block Island broke down hard, and that's opened a corridor for bonito and Spanish mackerel to spread west toward Newport and Point Judith. That warm water is close enough to Fishers Island that it's worth watching — I haven't seen confirmed bonito inside Sound proper yet, but the signs are lining up, and the first fish showing anywhere near Fishers wouldn't shock me before the pelagic window closes out this weekend. Worth running light spinning gear rigged with small metals if you're already out there chasing blues.

Looking ahead: if the new moon springs build the way they should through the weekend, I'm fishing the eddies at Race Rock and the down-current side of the Orient reef on the last two hours of the incoming — that's where the current math favors big bass without punishing the angler. If the bass don't cooperate, the fluke bite at Plum Gut on the outgoing is the fallback, and it's been solid enough to fill a cooler even on a slow bass day. Either way, respect the current this week — springs like these can turn a comfortable drift into a genuine hazard fast, especially through the Gut.
