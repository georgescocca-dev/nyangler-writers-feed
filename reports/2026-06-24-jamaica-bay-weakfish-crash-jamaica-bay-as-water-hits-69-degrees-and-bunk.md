# Weakfish crash Jamaica Bay as water hits 69 degrees and bunker schools thin
*Keeper weakies mixing with slot fluke and jumbo sea robins on the incoming tide.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — June 24** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The bay is alive right now, and I mean really alive. Water temps hit 69 degrees this week, and that magic number has flipped a switch. The weakfish have moved in hard — I'm talking keeper-sized fish up to 19 inches, with some pushing over two pounds. This is the Jamaica Bay special we've been waiting for: weakies, keeper fluke, and sea robins the size of small stripers all hitting on the same drift.

I've been working the incoming tide from the Marine Parkway Bridge wall back toward the deeper channels, and the action has been consistent. The weakfish are taking fresh clams and lugworms on simple fishfinder rigs, nothing fancy. Drop it down, let it sit, and when that rod tip starts dancing, set the hook. These fish are feeding aggressively in 12 to 18 feet of water, especially where the current creates those little eddies along the bridge pilings.

The fluke bite has stepped up too. I pulled a solid 25-inch doormat last week on a white bucktail tipped with Gulp, working it slow along the bottom on the drift. The key is staying patient with the retrieve — these bay fluke want that bait crawling, not racing. I'm seeing fish from 16 inches up to that keeper size, with most of the action happening on the first three hours of the incoming.

What's really interesting is how the bait situation has changed. The massive bunker schools that usually pack the bay this time of year are noticeably thinner. I'm still seeing pockets of them, especially near the Cross Bay Bridge on the outgoing, but nothing like the acres of bait we typically have by late June. That might actually be working in our favor — less bait means the fish are more willing to hit our offerings.

The sea robins deserve a mention because they're absolutely massive right now. I'm talking fish that would make you think you hooked a keeper fluke until you see those wing-like fins. They're hitting everything — clams, worms, even bucktails. While they're not target species, they're great indicators that the bottom is active and feeding.

Striped bass are still around but transitioning. The bigger fish are starting to move out as the water warms, but I'm still picking up slot fish in the 28 to 32-inch range, especially on live eels fished tight to structure on the outgoing tide. The Marine Parkway Bridge remains the go-to spot — fish the shadow line on the outgoing, work the pilings on the flood.

Weather has been cooperating with light northwest winds most mornings, keeping the bay relatively calm. That 69-degree water temp is right in the sweet spot for multiple species, and with the new moon approaching this weekend, I expect the tides to really start moving bait around.

Looking ahead, I'm watching for that water to push toward 70 degrees, which should bring even more weakfish into the bay. The full moon tides next week will flush a lot of water through the inlet, and that's when I expect to see the fluke fishing really turn on. If you're planning to hit the bay, focus on the incoming tide, bring plenty of clams and lugworms, and don't be surprised if you end up with the trifecta — weakies, fluke, and maybe a slot bass to round out the day.
