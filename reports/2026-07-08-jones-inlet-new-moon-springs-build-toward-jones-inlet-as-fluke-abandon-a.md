# New moon springs build toward Jones Inlet as fluke abandon a bathwater bay
*Great South Bay hit the high 70s and pushed the fluke out — the inlet and the outgoing tide are carrying the load right now.*

**JONES BEACH, NY — July 8** — _by Sal Moretti, Zone Writer_

Last week the bay did what it does every July — it got too warm for its own good. Great South Bay pushed close to 79 degrees in the shallows, and that's bathwater for a fluke. When that happens I don't fight it, I follow the fish, and the fish went where they always go when the flats turn into soup: the channels, the humps, and the inlet itself. We had a decent stretch of southwest wind through the middle of the week that kept things stirred but not blown out, and the moon's been building since the last new moon back in mid-June — we're past full, waning toward the next new moon, and the tides have been climbing every day toward what's going to be a real push this weekend.

That's the story for the week ahead too. New moon lines up around July 13, and that means spring tides stacking in hard from Friday through Monday — bigger exchange, more water moving through the inlet on both ends of the tide, stronger current at the jetties and in Sloop Channel. When the moon does that, I stop thinking about the bay as one big flat piece of water and start thinking about it as a plumbing system — the inlet's the drain, and everything the bay doesn't want gets flushed through it twice a day. Water's still trending warm, but the ocean side is getting a boost from upwelling — we're seeing cleaner, cooler water pushing in on the incoming, something in the low 70s, and that's exactly what a fluke wants after baking in 79-degree bay water for two weeks.

So here's where I've been putting my time, and where I'd send you. The fluke bite has shifted hard into Jones Inlet proper and down into Sloop Channel — the drop-offs along the edges, 15 to 25 feet, where the channel bends off Short Beach and where the current rips past the old workboat cuts. Outgoing tide is the play, no question. That's when the warmer bay water gets pushed out and the fluke stack up right in the seam where it meets the incoming ocean water — think of it as a dinner bell. Three-quarter-ounce white bucktails tipped with a five-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet have been the go-to, dragged slow along bottom on the drift, and don't be afraid to bounce it — these fish have been a little lazy in the heat and they want it right in their face. A few boats working the deeper hole off the inlet mouth, close to 30 feet, have had better luck with a heavier jig, an ounce or more, just to hold bottom against that new-moon current once it really starts running this weekend. Size has been mixed — plenty of 16 to 18-inch fish that go back, but there've been keepers in the 20 to 22-inch range mixed in, and I heard of one better fish pushing 5 pounds taken on a chartreuse bucktail-and-Gulp combo right at the turn of the tide.

Striped bass are still around but this isn't a blitz — don't let anybody tell you it is. What we've got is resident fish working the inlet mouth and the edges of Jones Beach on bunker that's scattered through the surf and the back bay. Best window has been dawn, right at first light, working topwater plugs and darters along the Short Beach jetty rocks and the outside bar at Jones Beach. If you can find a pod of bunker getting nervous, a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig fished tight to the bottom in that same 15 to 20-foot zone off the inlet has been picking off a few better fish in the 28 to 34-inch class, mostly on the last two hours of the outgoing. It's not consistent — I've had trips where I worked three different stretches of beach for one bass, and trips where I got two keepers in twenty minutes. That's where we are right now: fish are there, but you have to find the bait first.

Weakfish — I want to be straight with you here because there's a lot of hope floating around about a comeback, and hope isn't the same as fish in the boat. The water's been warm enough for weeks now, plenty above 70, and that's necessary but it clearly isn't enough on its own. I've heard of exactly one solid weakfish taken in the bay recently, a nice one over 7 pounds, but that's one fish, not a pattern. If you want to chase them, work the deeper holes in South Oyster Bay on the last hour of the outgoing with a bucktail and squid strip, but go in with realistic expectations.

Snapper blues have shown up in the back creeks around Freeport and Wantagh, which is a good sign bigger blues aren't far behind — small metal lures and Kastmasters will keep the kids busy while you work bigger stuff.

Looking ahead: that new moon spring tide building through the weekend is the thing to watch. Bigger tides mean a harder flush through the inlet, which should concentrate bait and predators right in that mouth on the outgoing. If I had one shot this week, I'd fish the Friday or Saturday evening outgoing at the inlet, right into dusk — that's when the current's strongest and the bass have shown the most willingness to commit. Plan B is the Sloop Channel fluke drift if the bass don't cooperate, because that bite's been the more dependable of the two.
