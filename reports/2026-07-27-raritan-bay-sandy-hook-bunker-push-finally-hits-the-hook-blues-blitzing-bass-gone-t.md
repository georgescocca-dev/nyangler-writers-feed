# Bunker Push Finally Hits the Hook — Blues Blitzing, Bass Gone to Nights
*The bait we've been tracking up the Jersey beach all month is stacking on Flynn's Knoll and Romer Shoal, and the bluefish found it first.*

**PERTH AMBOY, NJ — July 27** — _by Marisol Duarte, Zone Writer_

Here's the call I made this week: mornings on the Flats and the Reach for fluke, evenings at the Rip, and I'm keeping one eye on Flynn's Knoll and Romer Shoal all day because that's where the bunker are finally piling up. We flagged this a few days back — said the blitz pushing north out of the central Jersey shore was going to land here by the weekend, and it did. Wednesday morning off Flynn's Knoll I watched three separate bluefish blow-ups inside twenty minutes, bunker getting pushed into balls so tight the surface looked like it was boiling from underneath. That's not a metaphor, that's what six-pound blues do to peanut and adult bunker schools when they corner them against the Knoll on a dropping tide.

If you want blues right now, don't overthink it. Run out to Flynn's Knoll or Romer Shoal on the last two hours of the ebb, find the diving gulls and the nervous water, and throw poppers or big Ava jigs with a topwater teaser trailing behind. You don't need finesse — these fish are eating anything that moves fast and looks panicked. I've had guys tell me they got bit on bare bunker spoons just skipped across the top. Slot-sized blues, four to seven pounds, some bigger mixed in, and they're not shy about it. The bite windows are short — twenty, thirty minutes of pure chaos then it shuts off as the school moves — so stay mobile. I've been running from the Knoll to Romer Shoal to the mouth of the Raritan chasing the same bait ball basically all week.

Stripers are a different story right now, and if you're expecting daytime bass the way we had them in April and May, that's not what late July gives you. The bay's sitting warm — shallow water on the Keyport Flats and up in the Navesink has been running into the mid-70s by afternoon, and that pushes bass off the flats and down into deeper holds where they go nocturnal. I'm not chasing bass in daylight right now. I'm fishing the Rip at the Hook after dark, working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs down near bottom in twenty-five to thirty-five feet, right where the current runs hard against the tip. Two nights out of three I've put keeper bass in the box this week — nothing huge, mostly 26 to 32 inches, but steady enough that it's worth losing the sleep. The other trick that's working: live eels drifted through the same stretch on the last of the incoming, right as the tide starts to slacken around the Hook structure. That's when the bigger fish seem to show.

The fluke program has softened up some, which I want to be straight about. The open Flats bite that was so reliable in June has gone quiet in the shallow stuff — too warm, too much bait pressure from the blitz probably scattering things. What's still working is the channel edges out toward the Reach and along the drop-off on the Keyport side, twenty-five to thirty feet of water where there's still some cooler water moving. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter-ounce bucktails, dragged slow right along the lip where the sand drops into the channel — that's where I've been finding keeper fluke, four fish for me on Tuesday, two of them over 20 inches. It's not the wide-open drift bite from a month ago. You've got to work the structure now, not just cover water.

Porgies remain the honest bonus fish in this bay and they haven't slowed a bit. Anywhere there's a wreck or rockpile near Romer Shoal or off the Highlands, bloodworms or clam on a hi-lo rig will keep you busy between bluefish runs. It's not glamorous but it's a guaranteed bend in the rod while you're waiting on the next blitz to show up on the surface.

Weakfish have been a whisper more than a pattern this month — a few fish reported out of the Navesink on soft plastics fished slow along the deeper bends, nothing I'd call a fishery yet. Worth a cast or two if you're already in the river working the last of the ebb, but I wouldn't build a trip around it.

Tide-wise, we're coming off a stretch of building current as the moon works toward full in early August, and that's going to matter. Stronger current means the bait gets pinned harder against structure — good news for the bluefish blitz continuing to intensify around the Knoll and Shoal, and good news for the Rip at night because more current means more bass willing to feed against it. If the wind stays out of the south and light like it has been, I expect the blitz to hold here through the weekend before it either burns out or pushes further north toward the Sound. My money's on it lingering — the bunker schools I've seen the last two days are thick, not thinning out.

What I'd do this week: mornings and evenings at Flynn's Knoll and Romer Shoal chasing surface blitzes on poppers, fluke midday on the channel edges off the Reach with bucktail-Gulp combos, and bass after dark at the Rip on chunks or eels. It's a three-shift day right now if you want to hit everything, but that's late July on this bay — the fish are around, you just have to fish the right water at the right hour.
