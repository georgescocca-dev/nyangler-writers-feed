# Cape May Rips Picky, Bay Weakfish Waking Up — Drum Run Long Buttoned Up
*Fluke are sliding off the beach toward structure, sea bass are holding on the wrecks, and the first stripers are nosing into the bay mouth as the nights cool off.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 7** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Report card for the week: Cape May Rips, picky. Hereford Inlet, mixed but trending up. Delaware Bay, waking up on weakfish with croaker and kingfish riding shotgun. Drum run — closed for the year, and if you're still asking about black drum in Fortescue, save the question for May.

Here's the why. That full moon on the 6th and 7th put some real muscle into the tide, and anybody who's run the rips knows what a stronger moon tide does out there — it stacks bait harder against the structure and it either turns the fluke on for twenty minutes or it blows them right off the sandbars into deeper water. This week it did the latter. The fluke that were holding tight to the inshore edges of the rips all August have started sliding toward hard bottom and the reef sites, which is exactly the pattern we called last week — said the fluke would keep sliding toward structure while sea bass held steady on the wrecks, and that's precisely what the fleet found. Sea bass are the more reliable box-filler right now, coming up steady in 60 to 90 feet on Gulp Grub/squid combos and simple double-hook bottom rigs, while the fluke bite up top has gone from a numbers game to a patience game.

On the ocean side, the Rips are fishing the way they always do this time of year when the current is strong and the bait hasn't fully committed — good for an hour, dead for two. The best window has been the last two hours of the ebb running into the turn, when the outflow off Cape May Point collides with the incoming ocean water and stacks bunker and mullet right at the color change. Bucktails in the 3/4 to 1-ounce range, white or chartreuse, tipped with a strip of squid or a white Gulp Swimming Mullet, have been the ticket for keeper fluke in that 25 to 40 foot depth range off the Point. Bluefish are still mixed in on the drop, snapping up bucktails meant for something else, which isn't a complaint — a few 3 to 5 pound blues in the mix keep the rods bent between fluke bites.

Hereford Inlet has been the more honest bite of the two ocean-side spots. The jetty rocks and the deeper holes on the inside edge of the inlet have been holding fluke on the incoming, working live killies or peanut bunker on a simple fishfinder rig, with a scattering of shorts mixed with the occasional keeper up to 20 inches. Bluefish are running the inlet on the last of the outgoing, chopping up spearing schools right along the surface — small stuff mostly, cocktail-size, but they'll eat a diamond jig fast and hard if you want a change of pace from bottom fishing.

Delaware Bay is where the real story is developing. Water's still sitting in the mid-70s, and the weakfish bite that's been quiet most of the summer is coming back to life around the reef structure and Brandywine Shoal. High-low rigs with bloodworm or squid strips, fished right on bottom in 15 to 25 feet as the tide turns, have been picking up weakfish in the 16 to 20 inch class, with croaker and kingfish showing up on the same rigs — a legitimate mixed bag for anybody willing to sit on the bottom and wait out a slow pick rather than run and gun. Crabbing remains strong all up and down the bay side, and that's not incidental — the same warming shallow water and bait density that's holding crabs is what's tightening up the weakfish pattern.

As for stripers, the early staging pattern everybody's been waiting for is starting to show at the mouth of the bay. Peanut bunker and spot are beginning to filter out of the back creeks as the nights cool, and that's the trigger — bass start showing interest in structure right at the bay mouth before they commit to a real blitz pattern up the coast. It's not fireworks yet down here, more a handful of fish showing on marks and a couple of incidental catches on bucktails meant for fluke, but the ingredients are there. If the pattern holds the way it usually does this time of year, expect that staging to tighten up through the next week to ten days as the moon builds back toward the next phase and the bait gets pushed harder out of the marsh.

Looking ahead, the next few days should keep the same shape — fluke bite thinning out on the beach structure but holding on deeper hard bottom, sea bass steady on the wrecks for anybody who wants a limit over finesse, and the weakfish window in the bay worth the trip if you can fish the turn of the tide around Brandywine Shoal. If there's only one day to spend this week, put it on the bay side chasing weakfish and croaker on the last of the incoming — that bite has been the most consistent thing going, and it's trending the right direction while the ocean side sorts itself out.
