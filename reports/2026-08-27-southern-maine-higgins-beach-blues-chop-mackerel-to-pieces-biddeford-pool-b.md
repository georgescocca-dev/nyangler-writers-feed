# Higgins Beach blues chop mackerel to pieces, Biddeford Pool bass hold to the incoming
*Cooler Gulf of Maine water is keeping Southern Maine honest while the rest of the coast cooks — the pattern we called last week is holding.*

**BIDDEFORD POOL, ME — August 27** — _by Dutch Osterberg, Zone Writer_

There was a solid hour off Higgins Beach midweek when the water actually turned white — bluefish, three to five pounds, working a pod of mackerel right up against the sandbar on the last of the incoming. Guys in the parking lot said the birds gave it away twenty minutes before the blues did, gulls dropping straight down onto bait that had nowhere left to run. That's the story of the week up and down this stretch: mackerel schools pushing tight to the beaches, and everything with teeth taking advantage of it before the tide turns them loose again.

We flagged this last week — said the cooler, more stable Gulf of Maine water would keep this fishery clean while the water south of us cooked into a stress pattern, and that's exactly what's playing out. Southern Maine is sitting in the low-to-mid 60s, comfortable water, and it's kept both the bass and the bluefish feeding on a normal schedule instead of shutting down at midday like the fish are doing from the Sound on south. This isn't the fall run yet — nowhere close. It's the late-August holding pattern: fish still up here, still feeding hard, still using the same rocks and river mouths they've used since July, and they'll keep doing it until the nights start cooling off for real, which is usually a mid-September story around here, not a Labor Day one.

Biddeford Pool remains the best hour in the state when it's working right, and this week it worked more often than not. The bite builds on the first two hours of the incoming, when water starts stacking through the mouth and pushing bait — mackerel, pogies, the occasional school of peanut bunker — up against the sandbar and into the channel by the Pool itself. Guides working eels have done well in the deeper cuts on the drop, but the plug bite has been the more consistent story: black Slug-Go on a weighted swimbait hook worked slow along the current seams, and a Gibbs Danny or a Doc's swimming when there's enough push to make it walk right. Fish have been mostly schoolies and mid-twenties, but there've been enough 30-inch-plus bass mixed in on the last hour of the flood to keep rods bent. Once the tide tops out and goes slack, shut it down and wait — the bite dies hard at the top and doesn't turn back on until the ebb gets some current moving again.

Pine Point has been a flounder program more than anything else this week — sandworms and clam bellies fished on a simple two-hook flounder rig in the mud channels behind the point, best on the last two hours of the ebb when the water's draining out of the marsh and pulling bait with it. Nothing giant, mostly 12 to 15 inch fish, but steady enough to fill a cooler if you're patient and willing to move around to find where the bottom's holding the most bait.

Prouts Neck has been the better bass bet on any day with a southwest wind, which has been most of this week. That wind pushes bait tight against the rocks on the south side and gives the stripers a reason to sit right in the wash instead of roaming. A 2-ounce Rebel Jumpin Minnow or a bone-colored pencil popper worked through the white water at first light has been drawing strikes from fish in the 20-to-28-inch range, with the occasional bigger fish following a popper right to the rod tip before committing. Wear a belt and watch your footing — that ledge gets slick and the swell's been running enough this week to make it interesting.

Wells Harbor and Ogunquit have been mackerel country, which isn't glamorous but it's fun and it's filling bait buckets for everyone targeting bass with live bait elsewhere. Small Deceivers or a three-hook mackerel tree worked off the docks and jetties has been producing steady action, with snapper blues mixed in tight to the pilings on the last of the incoming. Cape Elizabeth has had a few cod reports from boats working the deeper rockpiles off the point, but that's a small-boat, know-your-numbers game this time of year — nothing a shore angler's going to stumble into.

Looking ahead, the pattern holds. Southwest wind in the 8-to-12 range is expected to stick around, which keeps Prouts Neck and the ocean-side beaches working and keeps that mackerel bait pinned tight to shore. The moon's building toward full over the next several days, which should sharpen the tide swings and tighten up the bite windows at the Pool and at the river mouths — meaning shorter, more concentrated pushes rather than an all-day grind. If there's only one tide to fish this weekend, make it the incoming at Biddeford Pool in the two hours before the moon's peak influence kicks in — that's where the numbers and the size have both been best. Keep the Prouts Neck rocks as the backup plan if the wind holds southwest, and don't be surprised if the mackerel show up somewhere new by midweek — they've been moving fast, and everything that eats them has been following close behind.
