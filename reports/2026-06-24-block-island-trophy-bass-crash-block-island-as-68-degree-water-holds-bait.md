# Trophy bass crash Block Island as 68-degree water holds bait tight to the ledges
*Big stripers stacked on bucktails and flutter spoons as perfect water temps create feeding frenzy.*

**BLOCK ISLAND, RI — June 24** — _by Jack Sullivan, Zone Writer_

The water around Block Island hit that magic 68-degree mark this week, and the striped bass responded like someone rang the dinner bell. I've been fishing these waters for two decades, and when the temperature sits right in that sweet spot — not too warm, not too cool — the big girls move in and feed hard.

The Southwest Ledge has been absolutely loaded with quality fish. I'm talking 30 to 40-pound class stripers that are hitting bucktails like they haven't eaten in weeks. White and chartreuse 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with pork rind or 5-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets are producing the best action. The key is working them just off the bottom in 35 to 50 feet of water along the drop-off. Don't just cast and retrieve — let that bucktail flutter down, then work it back with sharp lifts and drops. These fish want action, not a steady swim.

Flutter spoons are absolutely deadly right now, especially when you mark fish suspended in the water column. I've been dropping 3-ounce chrome spoons right on top of marked schools and getting crushed on the fall. The fish are holding about 15 feet down over 40-foot bottom, probably following the bait that's getting pushed around by the current.

The North Rip has been producing some serious fish on the outgoing tide. The current has been moderate — not the screaming rips we sometimes get with the new moon — which makes presentation much more manageable. I've been anchoring uptide and letting live peanut bunker drift back on fishfinder rigs. The hits have been violent, and several fish have gone over the 40-inch mark.

What's really exciting is seeing the first bonito of the season showing up. They're small schools right now, but they're here, which means the water quality is prime. When bonito move into Block Island waters, it's always a good sign that the ecosystem is firing on all cylinders. The cool, oxygenated water that draws these pelagics is the same water that holds our trophy stripers.

The wind farm area has been surprisingly productive for sea bass, with some quality fish in the 3 to 5-pound range coming over the rails. High-low rigs with clam or squid strips are working well in 60 to 80 feet of water around the turbine bases. The structure is definitely concentrating baitfish, and the bass are taking advantage.

Porgies have been thick around the traditional spots — the rocky areas south of the island in 30 to 40 feet. Small hooks, light sinkers, and pieces of clam or sea worm are all you need. It's been a great way to fill the cooler when the stripers aren't cooperating.

The water clarity has been exceptional — that gin-clear blue that tells you everything is right with the world. Visibility is easily 20 feet, and you can see the bottom in 30 feet of water. This clarity, combined with the perfect temperature, is creating ideal hunting conditions for predators.

Tide timing has been crucial. The best action has been on the last two hours of the outgoing and the first hour of the incoming. The moderate current is moving enough bait to get the fish active without making it impossible to fish effectively. Spring tides from the recent new moon have been flushing baitfish out of the protected areas and concentrating them along the ledges where the big bass are waiting.

Looking ahead, we're approaching the full moon on Friday, which should bring some serious tidal movement. I expect the fishing to get even better as those big tides push more bait around and get the fish really aggressive. The water temperature should hold steady in that perfect 68-degree range, and if the weather cooperates, this could be the week that produces some real monsters. Get out there and get tight to those ledges — the fish are here and they're hungry.
