# North Shore Rocks Beat the Harbor This Week: Blues on the Rip, Bass After Dark
*With the moon building to full Thursday, the current's tightening up bait from Nahant to Cape Ann while the harbor's bass slide into a night-bite pattern.*

**BOSTON HARBOR, MA — August 27** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

If the fleet's asking where to point the bow this week, the call is North Shore rocks over the harbor islands — at least during daylight. The moon builds to full tomorrow, and that's already tightening the tidal swings from Nahant down through Gloucester and Cape Ann. Stronger current means bait gets pinned against ledges and rock piles instead of spreading out over open water, and that's exactly the kind of squeeze that turns a mediocre bluefish bite into a good one. We flagged this pattern last week — said the moon building toward full would strengthen the current that concentrates bait — and that's precisely what's setting up right now.

Inside the harbor, the bass have settled into their late-August holding pattern. This isn't the fall push south, not yet — that starts once nighttime temps really start dropping, likely mid-September at the earliest. Right now the surface water's warm enough that stripers around Deer Island and Spectacle Island are doing most of their feeding low-light and after dark, sliding up onto the flats and channel edges once the sun's off the water. Dawn and dusk remain the most reliable windows, same as we called it — bait balls get pushed toward the surface in that first and last hour of light, and the bass follow them up. If you're working the Deer Island flats on the drop, live mackerel or a rigged eel drifted along the edge where the flat spills into deeper water has been the most consistent producer, especially the last two hours of the outgoing. Daytime bass are there but sulking tighter to structure — bucktails in the half-ounce to one-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked slow along the drop-offs, will still pick up fish for anglers willing to grind it out in the heat.

Flounder are steady but unspectacular on the harbor flats around Deer Island and the flats south toward Squantum — sandworms and squid strips on a standard spreader rig, fished on the bottom during the slower stages of the tide, are filling coolers with keeper-sized fish, nothing huge but a dependable box if bass aren't cooperating.

The better energy this week is outside the harbor. Nahant's ledges are holding cocktail bluefish in good numbers, and they're not shy about it — tins like Deadly Dicks and Swedish pimples worked through the rips at first light are getting hit hard, with some fish pushing into the 3- to 5-pound class mixed in with plenty of smaller choppers. Mackerel schools are stacked around the same structure, and where you find the mackerel, the bigger blues and the occasional bonus striper aren't far behind. A mackerel jig dropped through a school, then swapped for a bucktail once you mark bigger arcs underneath, has been the move for boats working Nahant and pushing up toward Gloucester.

Cape Ann and the deeper ledges off Gloucester are worth the run for anyone chasing bottom fish. Cod and haddock have been showing on the deeper structure for boats willing to work jigs and diamond jigs tight to bottom — it's not the kind of numbers game you get in the spring, but there are fish there for patient anglers marking the right bumps on the bottom machine and holding position through the drift. Bring extra weight; the current out there this week is running harder than it has in a month, and that's a direct result of the moon building toward full.

Down south, Plymouth, Duxbury Bay, and Scituate are fishing about how you'd expect for late August — a mixed bag, honest work required. Bass are scattered across the flats in Duxbury Bay, most active on the last two hours of the incoming when warmer water floods back over the sand and pushes bait tight to the edges. Live mackerel or a topwater plug worked at first light along the Scituate rocks has drawn some blitz-style feeding, but it's short-lived — fifteen, twenty minutes of surface chaos and then it shuts down until the next tide stage. Flounder fishing in Duxbury and around Plymouth Harbor remains steady on the same sandworm-and-squid combo that's working up in Boston Harbor.

Looking at the next few days, expect the pattern to hold and likely sharpen. With the full moon Thursday, tidal current will keep building through the weekend, which should keep bait pinned to structure and the bluefish bite consistent at Nahant, Gloucester, and Cape Ann. Winds look manageable out of the southwest in the 8 to 12 knot range, which keeps the North Shore ledges fishable without the swell that can shut down a rock-fishing program. If there's only one window this weekend, make it the dawn bite on the North Shore rips — that's where the moon, the bait, and the blues are lining up. For bass, stick with the after-dark program in the harbor until nights start cooling — that shift, when it comes, will be the real signal the fall run is starting to stir.
