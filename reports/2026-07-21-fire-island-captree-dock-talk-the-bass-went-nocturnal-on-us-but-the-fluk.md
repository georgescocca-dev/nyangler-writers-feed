# Captree dock talk: the bass went nocturnal on us, but the fluke never got the memo
*A regular asked why his daytime stripers dried up — the answer's in the warm water and the after-dark bunker bite building at the inlet.*

**CAPTREE, NY — July 21** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

Ronnie caught me gassing up at the dock Tuesday morning, already annoyed before I even had my coffee. "Mari, I fished the inlet three mornings this week, live-lined bunker off Democrat Point like always, and got nothing but a couple bluefish for my trouble. What gives?" I told him the same thing I've been telling everybody who'll listen — the bass didn't leave, they just clocked out for the day shift. Water's warm enough now, low 70s out in the ocean and not much cooler inside the bay, that the bigger fish have gone strictly low-light and after-dark. If you're fishing Democrat Point or the rips off Captree Island at nine in the morning with the sun already up, you're fishing for bluefish, not bass. That's just where we're at in July.

So here's what I told him to do instead: fish the last hour of light into full dark, live bunker or fresh chunks fished tight to structure — the rock piles off Democrat Point, the deeper cuts along the Robert Moses bridges, the seam where the inlet channel spills into the bay on the outgoing. That's where the bass are holding and feeding once the light drops off. It's not the flashiest fishing, standing there in the dark with a rod in a holder, but it's producing. A buddy of mine anchored up on the incoming tide right at dusk this week off the West End and put three fish in the mid-30s in the boat before ten o'clock, all on bunker chunks soaked on the bottom near the rocks. That's the play right now — not bucktailing the flats at noon.

The good news, and it's real good news, is the fluke haven't gotten the message that they're supposed to slow down in this heat. That bite has stayed steady all through the mid-70s water, and it's still the most dependable thing going in this bay. The Robert Moses bridges are still the classic spot — anchor or drift the pilings on the outgoing tide in 25 to 35 feet, white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid if you want extra scent in that current. We've also been picking at fish along the channel edges off Kismet and out toward the Fire Island Lighthouse flats, working the drift slow, bouncing bottom, letting the bait do the work rather than ripping it. Sizes have been mixed — plenty of shorts to sort through, but keepers in the 3 to 5 pound range aren't hard to find if you keep moving and stay on structure. Nothing to write home about size-wise this week, but it's a bite you can count on, which counts for something in late July.

Bluefish have been the wildcard, and honestly the most fun. Bunker schools are working their way along the Fire Island beach the same way they've been pushing up the Jersey coast, and when those pods get inshore of the bar you'll see the whole surface erupt — gulls diving, blues slashing through, the water actually boiling. I had a morning last week off Cedar Beach where the blues were so thick on a bunker school you could practically walk on them. Bucktails, poppers, anything with flash thrown right into the melee will get bit instantly. The catch is timing — those schools move, and what's happening off Kismet at 7am might be off Point O'Woods by 9. Keep an eye out for diving birds and slicks on the surface; that's your tell.

Weakfish have been quietly building too, and we flagged this pattern last week — said the bite would rebuild on calm, warm evenings, and that's exactly what's shown up. Guys working the deeper holes off Patchogue and Sayville on calm nights with soft plastics or bucktails tipped with squid strips have been picking at weakfish in the evening slack, nothing epic, but real fish where a couple weeks ago there was nothing. If we get another stretch of settled weather, I'd expect that to keep building.

Out front on the ocean side, I'd tell you to be patient. That 44025 buoy reading is showing almost 4-foot swell at a short 5-second period, which is a sloppy, disorganized sea — not the kind of surf you want to be wading into for stripers off the beach right now. It's kicking up enough whitewater that the surfcasting has been rough and inconsistent, and honestly most of the good fish are inside the inlet and bay anyway with the thermal setup we've got. Save the beach trips for a calmer window.

We're sliding into a neap tide off today's first-quarter moon, which means softer current for the next several days. That's actually good news for the night bass bite — a slower moving tide widens out the feeding window instead of compressing it into one hard hour of moving water. If I had one evening to fish this week, I'd be anchored on the inlet seam at Democrat Point right at sunset with bunker on the bottom, riding that softer tide well past dark. And I'd keep the fluke rods rigged for the next morning, because that bite isn't going anywhere yet.
