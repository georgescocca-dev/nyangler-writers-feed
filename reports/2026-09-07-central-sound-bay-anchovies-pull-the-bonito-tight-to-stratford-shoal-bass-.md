# Bay Anchovies Pull the Bonito Tight to Stratford Shoal — Bass Watching the Bunker Behind Them
*Micro bait is running the show in the Central Sound this week, and the fleet that noticed early is cashing in on albies while the stripers stack for what's coming.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — September 7** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

Before anything else this week, look at what's actually in the water. Bay anchovies — tiny, glassy, an inch and a half of nothing — have been pouring through the rip off Stratford Shoal and pushing into the lee of the Norwalk Islands for the better part of ten days. That's the tell. When the bait shrinks down like that, the predator behavior changes with it, and that's exactly what's been happening on the bonito and albie front. Fish are around Stratford Shoal, Middle Ground Light, and the drop-off east of Charles Island, but they've been sticky and selective — showing on top, sipping at anchovies, and ignoring anything that doesn't match the hatch. Small profile is the word. Half-ounce epoxy jigs, size 2 Deceivers, and anything in the anchovy-green/silver combo have been outfishing the bigger Deadly Dicks and Hogy jigs that worked a month ago when peanut bunker were the primary forage.

Underneath and behind that anchovy show, there's a second story building — bunker. Pods of peanut and adult bunker have been tightening up from the western Sound out past Eatons Neck and into the Norwalk Islands corridor, squeezed by the building moon. We flagged this exact pattern last week — said the moon swinging toward full on September 6-7 would draw those bait schools tighter and that the first real fall blitz activity was likely within the coming week — and that's precisely the shape things are taking now. Stripers have gone from picking at stragglers to actually working bunker edges with intent. It's not blitz behavior yet, not the kind where the birds are screaming and the water's exploding, but the interest is real and building, and that's the difference between late-summer bass and fall bass.

Water temps explain some of the east-west split anglers are seeing. Kings Point is sitting around 70.9 degrees, while New London out east reads closer to 69.4 — a real gradient across the Central Sound that's been funneling bait and fish toward the warmer western half. That's part of why the Norwalk Islands and the water off Eatons Neck have felt more alive than the stretch out toward the Race this week. As nights cool and that gradient narrows, expect the whole middle Sound to fish more evenly, but for now, western structure has the edge.

On the bass, the pattern to fish is bunker schools with current under them. Drift live bunker or snag-and-drop rigs along the edges of the pods working the flats off Eatons Neck and around the western end of the Norwalk Islands on the last two hours of the incoming tide — that's when the bait tightens against the current and the stripers show up underneath rather than just shadowing from a distance. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5-ounce range, white or bunker-colored, worked slow along bottom near Middle Ground Light have been picking at slot fish in 20 to 35 feet, best on the top of the tide swings rather than dead slack. Nothing is stacking yet the way it will once that new moon arrives on the 10th, but the fish are getting more serious about eating.

Bluefish have stayed the most reliable player in the Central Sound all week — no subtlety required there. Anywhere the bunker schools show, blues are underneath tearing at them, and they'll hit almost anything with flash: diamond jigs, Hopkins, poppers worked fast on the surface. If a session feels slow on bass, the blues are the backup that almost never fails right now, and they're running good-sized fish, not just snappers.

Porgies remain the steady, boring-in-a-good-way bite around the rock piles — Stratford Shoal itself, the reef structure off Charles Island, and the rubble edges around Middle Ground. Sandworms and clam on standard hi-lo rigs in 15 to 30 feet continue to produce solid catches, and that fishery hasn't shown any signs of slowing as the calendar turns toward mid-September.

Fluke are winding down, as expected this time of year, but there's still a window. Drifts along the Middle Ground and the deeper edges around Stratford Shoal are still giving up keeper-class fish on bucktail-and-Gulp combos, white or chartreuse, especially on the last of the outgoing when bait gets pulled off the flats. That bite will keep thinning through the rest of September as fish start their move offshore, so this is closer to the tail than the body of the season.

Looking ahead, the new moon arriving September 10 is the number to circle. If the pattern holds the way it has the last two fall seasons, that tide should be the pivot — bait gets squeezed harder, bass commit fully instead of just showing interest, and the Central Sound sees its first legitimate blitz-style action of the fall around bunker schools from the Norwalk Islands down through the western reaches. The anchovy-driven albie and bonito bite should also firm up as water temps ease slightly and those fish get less picky about bait size — worth watching Stratford Shoal and the Charles Island rip closely over the next several days. If there's only one window to fish this week, target the two days bracketing the new moon on the incoming tide near bunker concentrations, and keep a small epoxy jig rigged for whatever's chasing anchovies on the side.
