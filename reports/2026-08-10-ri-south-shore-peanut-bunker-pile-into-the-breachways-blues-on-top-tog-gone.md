# Peanut bunker pile into the breachways — blues on top, tog gone quiet on the ledges
*Full moon current is stacking bait at Charlestown and Weekapaug while the tog slide deep and the albies stay offshore for now.*

**JAMESTOWN, RI — August 10** — _by Billy Tanaka, Zone Writer_

Walked out on the rocks at Beavertail Tuesday morning and the bait told me everything I needed to know before I made a cast. Peanut bunker, thick as smoke, pushed up against the ledge on the last of the incoming, with silversides working the edges where the current split around the boulders. That's the tell this time of year — when the peanuts show that heavy along the rock, the blues aren't far behind, and sure enough I didn't have to wait long.

We flagged this a week back — said the peanut bunker spread would pull cocktail blues into the back bays and breachways inside two weeks, and here it is showing up right on schedule. Charlestown Breachway and the mouth at Weekapaug both had that nervous water Wednesday evening, bunker skipping on top, blues underneath cutting through them. Small Hopkins and half-ounce Deadly Dicks worked fine once you found the school, but honestly a bare bucktail with a strip of squid outfished the metal on the slower tide stages — these aren't the giant choppers yet, mostly 2 to 4 pound cocktails, but they hit like they mean it and they're consistent, which counts for something in August.

The full moon has the current running hard through all the breachways right now, and that's been the story for bass too, just not in daylight. Striped bass have gone strict low-light on me the last two weeks — nothing surprising given how warm it's sitting, but the pattern's been reliable if you're willing to fish the dark. Sakonnet River mouth on the last two hours of the outgoing has produced the better fish, stripers sitting on the current seam waiting on bunker and eels getting pushed out of the river. I've had my best luck there with a swimming plug worked slow just off the seam, right at the color change where the river water meets the Sound. Jamestown's breakwaters near the bridge have held fish too, but it's a short window — an hour either side of the tide change before the current gets unmanageable for anything but bait fishermen.

Tog have been tough, and I'll say that plain instead of dressing it up. This heat pushes them tight into the cracks and they don't want to move much off the bottom. I worked the ledges off Beavertail and out toward Brenton Reef on green crabs, fishing the slack right at the top of the tide when the current eases enough to hold bottom without dragging a ton of lead. Got a couple keeper-class fish out of maybe fifteen bites, most of which were just pickers stripping the crab clean off the hook. If you want tog this week, fish smaller pieces, half a crab instead of a whole one, and be patient — the good bite's still six weeks out when the water starts to slide the other direction.

Fluke have been the most dependable thing going, which tracks with how it's gone everywhere south of the Cape this summer. The channel edge at Charlestown Breachway on the dropping tide has been steady — 4 to 5 pound fish mixed with a lot of shorts, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow right where the sandy bottom drops into the deeper cut. Watch Hill Ledges and the water off Napatree Point have fished similarly on the outgoing, a little deeper, 25 to 30 feet, and worth the extra run if the breachway's crowded on a weekend tide.

Scup are about the one thing you can set a watch by right now. Newport Bridge pilings and the rocks off Jamestown have been thick with them all week, small hooks, squid strips or sandworms, any depth near structure. It's not glamorous fishing but it's the kind of steady action that keeps a rod bent while you're waiting on the tide to turn for something bigger.

No albies yet, and I wouldn't tell you otherwise. The bait's building the way it needs to — peanut bunker, bay anchovies starting to show along Second Beach and out toward Sakonnet Point — but the fish themselves haven't pushed into the breachways. Historically that first wave doesn't show at Napatree or Watch Hill until late in the month, sometimes not until the first week of September, and this year doesn't feel early. Worth watching the bait daily from here, because when the albies do show, it's usually fast — one morning nothing, the next morning boils all through the mouth of the breachway.

Looking ahead, the moon starts easing off full by the weekend, which should settle these breachway currents back into something a little more fishable for the tog crowd and take some of the sting out of the bass bite timing. If the wind stays light out of the south, I'd put my time into the Sakonnet mouth at first light and the Charlestown edge on the evening outgoing — that's where the bait's been heaviest and the fish have been the most honest about showing up.
