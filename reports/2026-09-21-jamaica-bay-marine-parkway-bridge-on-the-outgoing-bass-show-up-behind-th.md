# Marine Parkway Bridge on the Outgoing: Bass Show Up Behind the Bunker
*Bunker schools stacking in the inlet corridor are pulling the first real striper push of the fall, while snapper blues keep the bay lit up for anyone with a light rod and twenty minutes.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — September 21** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one access point to burn this week, make it the rocks under Marine Parkway Bridge on the back half of the outgoing. That's where the bunker schools funneling through the Rockaway Inlet corridor are getting squeezed against the current, and where the first real striper commitment of the fall has been showing up behind them. Boat guys working the pilings and the channel edge on the drop are seeing the same thing kayak anglers are seeing from the rock pile on the Rockaway side — bait balling up tight, then getting worked from underneath. That's the tell. When the bunker start getting nervous and pinning up against structure instead of swimming through clean, the bass are usually not far behind, and this week they finally showed.

The Harvest Moon is doing exactly what it's supposed to do — bigger swings, harder current, more water moving in and out of the bay on every tide. That's pushing peanut and adult menhaden schools through the inlet and back into the bay itself, and it's concentrating everything at the pinch points: the bridge, the Cross Bay span, and the deeper holes off Canarsie Pier. We flagged this pattern last week, that bluefish activity inside the bay would build as more bait got pushed in on the tide cycle, and that's exactly what's happened — snapper blues are thick from the pier pilings out to the flats, and there are bigger cocktail blues mixed into the bunker schools now too.

Water's been clean, green, and holding late-summer warmth for this point in the calendar, which is part of why the fluke bite hasn't completely shut down yet. It's fading, though, and everyone on the docks knows it. The channel edges by Cross Bay Bridge and the deeper drop-offs toward the East Pond flats are still giving up a fluke or two on the last of the incoming, mostly on white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, but the window's shrinking fast. Any real cold snap over the next week and that fishery's done for the season — enjoy it now if you're going to chase it at all.

The striper story is the one to watch. Right now the bass showing at Marine Parkway are mostly schoolies and low-slot fish, 22 to 28 inches, holding on the current seam where the bridge shadow meets open water on the outgoing tide. Bunker chunks fished on a fishfinder rig, 3-ounce egg sinker, 20-pound fluorocarbon leader, are getting bit, and so are bunker-imitation swimmers — Savage Gear Sandeels and 5-inch SP Minnows in bunker or white — worked with a slow, erratic retrieve right through the seam. If you're on the rocks and see bait getting nervous on the surface, a bunker-spoon or an Ava 27 jig cast past the melee and swum back through it will draw strikes fast, before the fish push off.

Canarsie Pier is the play if you want steady action without needing a boat or a kayak. Snapper blues are stacked around the pilings and along the drop-off just off the pier, especially on the last two hours of the incoming when bait gets pinned against the structure. Small gold Kastmasters, quarter-ounce, or spearing fished under a bobber are all you need — kids and first-timers are filling coolers with cocktail-size blues there right now, and it's some of the most reliable action in the whole bay this week regardless of skill level.

Cross Bay Bridge remains the best call for anyone working from a kayak who wants a shot at both bass and blues without dealing with boat traffic. The channel on either side of the bridge holds current all through the tide, and working soft plastics or small bucktails along the pilings on the last of the ebb has been producing mixed bags — snapper blues on top, occasional keeper-class bass working the bottom third of the water column. Breezy Point surf is still mostly a bluefish and occasional bass proposition after dark, and with the moon this big, the night tides there are worth a look if you don't mind the walk out to the point.

Blackfish season isn't open yet, but the rock piles at the inlet mouth near Breezy Point are already holding fish, and porgies are still around the deeper holes inside the bay on bloodworms and clam for anyone bottom fishing off a boat or kayak in 15 to 20 feet.

Looking ahead, the next several days should keep building on what's already started. The Harvest Moon tides don't fully ease off until early next week, and that means more bait getting pushed and pinned, which means more consistent feeding windows at the structure spots — the bridge, the pier, Cross Bay. If the bunker schools keep holding tight to the inlet corridor instead of scattering, expect the bass numbers at Marine Parkway to keep climbing through the weekend, with bigger fish mixing in as more forage stacks up. The wildcard is weather — any real cold front pushing through would accelerate the fluke fade and could push the bigger migratory bass south past the bay faster than the bunker can hold them here. If you've got a weekday morning to spare before that happens, the outgoing at Marine Parkway is where the time belongs right now.
