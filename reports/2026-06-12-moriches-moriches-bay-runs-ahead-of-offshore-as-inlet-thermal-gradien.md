# Moriches Bay runs ahead of offshore as inlet thermal gradient sharpens
*With 44025 offline and 44097 intermittent, the bay-to-ocean temperature break at Moriches Inlet becomes the controlling variable for this week's bite.*

**EAST MORICHES, NY — June 12** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

The primary offshore reference is dark this morning. NDBC 44025 — the Long Island buoy that normally anchors any honest read on South Shore ocean state — is offline, and 44097 off Block Island has been feeding intermittent water-temperature data through the last package cycle. The most recent SST composite in the catalog is dated June 10, two days stale, and the overnight fishing-report intake produced nothing fresh from the zone. That leaves us reasoning from the June 10 thermal field, the seasonal trajectory, and what the bay itself is doing under our feet. Mid-June on Moriches means the back bay is climbing fast — historically through the high 60s and brushing 70°F on a sunny afternoon outgoing — while the nearshore Atlantic outside Cupsogue typically lags by six to ten degrees this time of year. With no live buoy to pin a number to, treat the inside-outside delta as the dominant feature and fish it.

That gradient is the engine. Every ebb out of Moriches Inlet right now is dumping warmer, bait-rich bay water into a cooler ocean, and the seam sets up just outside the jetty tips and along the east rip toward Cupsogue. Bass stage on the ocean side of that seam and feed up-current into it. The slot-class fish — 28 to 31 inches — are the dominant class through the inlet on the dropping tide, with the occasional overslot working the deeper scour hole on the west side where the channel pinches to roughly 18 to 22 feet. Fluke are the other half of the program: the bay is in its skinny-water phase, three to ten feet over the sand edges from Tuthill Point east toward the inlet flats, and the drift discipline question is the same one it always is in June — are you slow enough to keep a jig on the bottom in clean water without dragging through the warm pockets the keepers won't sit in.

Inlet structure is doing what it does post-spring: the storm cuts from the winter have re-sorted the sand on the ocean side, and the bar configuration off Cupsogue is holding a sluice that fishes best on the last two hours of the drop and the first hour of the flood. Bucktails in the half-to-three-quarter-ounce range tipped with Gulp are the honest answer when the current is ripping; soft plastics on a quarter-ounce jighead take over on the slack and the early flood when bass slide up onto the flats to hunt spearing and peanut bunker. The Westhampton side of the bay, west of the inlet up toward the East Moriches channels, has been the more reliable fluke grid — edges of three to seven feet, sand-to-grass transitions — while the bigger ocean-grade fish are pulling through the inlet itself on tide changes.

Weakfish are the wildcard worth mentioning. With bay temperatures climbing into the high 60s, the deeper holes off Tuthill and the channel bends behind Smith Point are the textbook setup for a late-incoming-into-slack bite on small plastics fished slow. Porgies have settled onto the harder bottom inside the bay and along the inlet rockpiles; blackfish remain a structure-and-crab game on the jetty rocks but are secondary to the fluke and bass program right now.

Look-ahead through the weekend and into early next week hinges on two things. First, whether 44025 comes back online — without it, we are flying on bay thermometers and satellite composites, and any sudden southerly blow will rearrange the inlet bait picture faster than we can confirm it. Second, the moon is building toward the full on the 29th, but the current set of tides is moderate, which favors the technical drift fisherman over the brute-force bait soaker. If a south-southwest wind sets up for two consecutive days, expect the ocean side to warm a degree or two and the bass bite at the inlet mouth to pull a half-tide earlier as the thermal seam slides inshore.

I'd be at the inlet on the last of the drop with a bucktail and a backup spinning rod rigged light for plastics, and I'd be willing to run west into the skinny water the moment the current goes slack. The data picture is thinner than I'd like this week, but the mechanism is not in doubt: fish the temperature break, fish the tide that moves it, and let the bay's heat do the recruiting for you.
