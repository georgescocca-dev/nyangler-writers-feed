# Deer Island Rip on the Outgoing: Live Mackerel Bass, Peanut Bunker Blues Inshore
*With the ocean running warm and slick, I stayed inside this week — and the harbor paid the bills better than the open rocks did.*

**BOSTON HARBOR, MA — August 10** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

I had three options Monday morning standing on the dock with coffee going cold: run north to the Cape Ann ledges, push south to Duxbury and the Plymouth flats, or stay put and work the harbor structure I know better than my own driveway. With the full moon pumping extra current through President Roads this week, I made the call to stay inside. That decision paid off.

Deer Island Light on the back half of the outgoing has been the most consistent thing I've fished all August. The rip that forms off the light as the tide pulls out of the inner harbor is stacking mackerel against the ledge, and the bass know it. I've been anchoring up-current of the seam and drifting live mackerel on a fish-finder rig with 3 feet of 40-pound fluoro leader, letting the bait swim naturally into the boil. Fish have been running 28 to 34 inches, with a few close to overslot mixed in on the last two hours of the drop. If you don't want to fuss with livies, a white 2-ounce bucktail with a chunk of pork rind worked slow along the bottom edge of the rip has produced too — just not with the same consistency.

Nahant's backside rocks are still holding fish, but that program has flipped hard to a low-light game. Midday out there right now is mostly dead water — the open ocean side has warmed enough that the bass have pulled off the exposed granite and tucked into the shade and current breaks until the sun drops. Dusk into the first hour of dark has been the window. Guys working topwater plugs off the rocks at Bailey's Hill and the Nahant causeway have had a few good bass, but it's a short window and you need to be there before the light actually fades, not after.

Spectacle Island and the flats off Long Island have turned into a bluefish program, and this is exactly what I flagged last week — the peanut bunker pushing into the harbor was going to pull cocktail blues right in behind it, and that's precisely what's happening now. Winthrop's shoreline and the mouth of the Chelsea Creek have snapper blues thick enough that a kid with a bobber and a piece of mackerel can load the cooler in an hour. The adult cocktail-sized blues, 2 to 4 pounds, are mixing into that bunker pod on the drop tide off Spectacle Island, and small metal — half-ounce Deadly Dicks or Kastmasters — thrown right into the nervous water has been money. No need to overthink it; match the peanut size and stay in the melee.

Gloucester and Cape Ann have been a mixed bag. The backside ledges off Eastern Point and Bass Rocks are still giving up bass on the change of tide, mostly at night now with live mackerel or eels drifted along the structure in 20 to 40 feet. Cod and haddock inshore are basically a non-event this time of year — that fishery belongs to spring and fall around here, and anyone telling you different this week is stretching it. If you want bottom fish right now you're better off running out to the deeper Gulf of Maine structure, and even then it's a grind, not a program.

South of the city, Plymouth and Duxbury Bay have been the flounder show. The flats inside Duxbury on the last of the incoming and the first hour of the ebb have been holding decent flounder in 6 to 10 feet — squid strips and sandworms on a simple spreader rig, fished slow right on bottom over the mud-sand transitions. It's not a fast bite, but it's a steady one, and it's a nice change of pace if the bass game has you frustrated. Bass in Duxbury Bay itself have been mostly a dawn deal, working the channel edges near the Powder Point Bridge on the first push of the incoming.

The pattern right now, north of the Cape, is that the Gulf of Maine's cooler water is keeping our estuaries and rivermouths more alive through the middle of the day than what's happening south of us — the harbor mouths and the Deer Island rip are fishing on a schedule closer to normal, while the open, sun-baked rock structure at Nahant and Cape Ann has shoved everything into the shoulders of the day. If the current stays strong behind this moon through the weekend, I'd keep working the Deer Island outgoing and the Winthrop bunker pods during the day, and save the exposed rock game for last light. If the wind stays light and the harbor keeps its color, I don't see a reason to change the program — but I'll be watching for that peanut bunker pod to push further up into the Mystic and Weymouth Back River, because when it does, the blues will follow it right up the ladder.
