# Jeffrey's in 190 feet: haddock thick on the mud, cod holding tight to the gravel break
*Groundfish are settling into their fall pattern on the Ledge while a handful of bluefin still work the bait balls topside.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — September 11** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Run the numbers on Jeffrey's Ledge right now and they tell the story better than any forecast could. West side gravel, 175 to 195 feet, is holding cod that stack tight to the hard bottom where the current breaks over the rise. Slide east into the mud basin toward Scantum, 210 to 240 feet, and that's where the haddock have been sitting — not scattered, but bunched in pockets you have to hunt for with the sounder before you commit a drift. That split, gravel for cod and mud for haddock, is about as textbook as it gets for early September, and it's held steady for going on two weeks now.

The water's doing what it does this time of year — cooling from the top down, which pushes the thermocline deeper and pulls groundfish up out of the real depths where they've been sulking all summer. That's the mechanism behind the improved bite. Boats working the west side gravel edge on Jeffrey's have been finding cod in the 8 to 15 pound class, with the occasional better fish mixed in, on diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid or fresh clam belly, worked slow off bottom on the up-current side of the drift. Haddock over the mud have been more consistent in numbers if not size — most running 2 to 4 pounds, taken on standard haddock rigs, twin hooks baited with clam, fished right on bottom with just enough weight to hold in the current. Nothing explosive, but a steady pick, which after a slow August is worth noting.

Platts Bank has been quieter for groundfish this week but still worth a look for pollock, which have been schooling up over the humps in 100 to 130 feet and hitting jigged diamonds or swimming plugs worked through the water column rather than bounced on bottom. Tillies Bank has produced similar pollock action along its edges, along with mackerel showing in tighter schools than they were mid-summer — a sign the bait is starting to consolidate the way it does heading into fall, which matters for everything else that eats.

On the tuna side, we flagged last week that the bite on the Ledge should hold steady as bait kept concentrating there, and that's exactly what's played out. Giant bluefin haven't shown in real numbers yet — that's an October story on Jeffrey's, and everyone running out of Rye Harbor knows it — but scattered fish have been working the bait balls on the surface, particularly early morning when the water lies down and you can spot them rolling on pogies and mackerel. A few boats have connected trolling small spreads through those areas, more by being in the right place at the right time than anything else. It's not a program yet. It's a preview.

Moon's past full and easing toward the next quarter, which has kept tides moderate — not the ripping current you get around new and full, which honestly has helped the groundfish bite stay consistent rather than getting blown out by hard drift days. Wind's been the bigger variable. A few days of building southwest breeze mid-week kept some of the smaller boats closer to Cape Ann and inside toward Thatcher Island rather than making the run all the way out to the Ledge, but nothing that shut the fishery down — more a matter of picking your day.

Water color out on Jeffrey's has stayed clean, which is typical for this stretch of September before the fall gales start stirring things up. That clean water and the cooling surface layer is exactly why the cod are tightening up on the gravel edges instead of scattered across open bottom — they're following the bait and the temperature break, and right now that break sits right where the gravel gives way to the drop-off on the west side.

Looking ahead, the next few days should hold this pattern rather than flip it. If the wind stays out of the north or northwest and lays under 15 knots, that's your window to make the run to Jeffrey's and work both bottoms — start on the gravel early for cod while the tide's still building, then slide over to the mud basin as the drift eases for haddock. If you've only got one day this weekend, I'd rather have you on the west gravel edge with clam-baited jigs than gambling on the tuna, which are still too spotty to plan a whole trip around. That said, keep a rod rigged and an eye on the surface on the run out — those bait balls aren't going anywhere yet, and as the water keeps cooling, the numbers on the bigger bluefin should start climbing through the back half of the month. Platts and Tillies remain good secondary stops if the Ledge gets crowded or the drift isn't setting up right, especially for pollock and mackerel to fill out a mixed-bag day.
