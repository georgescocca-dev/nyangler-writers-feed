# Niantic Fleet Finds Fluke Stacked at 35 Feet — Sea Bass Steady Off Twotree
*Neap tide easing the current just enough to put keeper fluke and quality sea bass in the box from Niantic Bay to Hole-in-the-Wall.*

**NIANTIC, CT — July 20** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Ran into one of the Sunbeam mates at the bait shop Thursday morning loading up on squid strips, and he didn't waste time telling me the fluke bite off Twotree Island Channel has gone from decent to genuinely good over the last ten days. That tracks with something I said last week — that back bay fluke would push out toward the inlet mouths and ocean structure as the water kept warming. Well, here we are. Niantic Bay proper has gone quiet on the flatties, but the channel edges and the rock piles off Hole-in-the-Wall are holding fish that mean business.

Water at New London is sitting around 70 degrees, which is right in that sweet spot where fluke stop sulking on soft bottom and start setting up on structure where the bait gets pinned by current. The drift boats working Twotree are marking fish in 35 to 40 feet, and the program that's putting them in the cooler is simple — 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, dragged slow on the down-current drift. Guys jigging bare bucktails without the Gulp trailer are getting outfished two-to-one right now. When the drift speeds up on the bigger tides, bump up to a full ounce so you're still ticking bottom instead of sailing over their heads.

Here's the timing piece that matters this week: we're sliding into the first-quarter neap on the 21st, and that's going to soften the current through the Race, the Twotree rip, and around Black Point. I've seen this pattern plenty of times — when the tide range narrows like this, the fluke bite doesn't die, it actually gets more comfortable to fish because you're not fighting a screaming drift all day. Slower current means you can slow your presentation down too, which is exactly what these fish want in July heat. If you've been getting your bucktail ripped out of the strike zone on the bigger tides, this is your window.

Sea bass have been the more consistent story all month, and Black Point continues to be the go-to. The rock piles off the point in 25 to 45 feet are stacked with keepers, and I heard secondhand about a fat one pulled off structure near Plum Gut pushing 28 inches and better than 5 pounds — that's the kind of quality that's out there right now if you find the right piece of bottom. Standard sea bass rigs, double hook, fresh clam or squid, fished tight to structure on the drift or at anchor on the up-current side of the rocks. Don't be afraid to move around McCook Point too if Black Point gets crowded on a weekend — the bottom there holds fish nobody's leaning on as hard.

Porgies are filling out the box nicely for anyone bottom fishing bloodworms or clam around the same structure — Bartlett Reef and the rockier stretches off Black Point have been thick with them, and they're a good consolation prize on days the sea bass get finicky.

Striped bass is where I have to be honest with you — the daytime bite in this stretch of Sound is tough right now. Warm surface water has these fish keyed into low-light and after-dark feeding, same as everywhere south of the Cape. The guys doing well are fishing dusk into full dark around the current breaks near the Race and off Black Point, working live eels or big swimbaits on the drop as the tide starts moving. If you're a daylight-only angler, I'd shift my striper expectations down and lean harder into the fluke and sea bass, which don't care about the sun the way stripers do this time of year.

Bluefish have started showing more consistently inside Niantic Bay and around the river mouth, chopping through peanut bunker on the surface in the early morning. Nothing blitz-scale like what's happening in the western Sound and down the Jersey beaches, but enough that a few guys working topwater poppers or bunker chunks off the jetties have had good mornings. Worth having a rod rigged for it if you're already out there for fluke.

Looking ahead, I'd expect the fluke bite to keep building through this neap and into next week as the current window widens for slow presentations — that's usually when Twotree and Hole-in-the-Wall really turn on. Sea bass should stay steady at Black Point as long as the structure keeps holding bait, though I wouldn't be shocked if the bigger fish thin out a little as more boats find that Plum Gut-area piece. Striper fishing is going to stay a dawn-and-dark game until we get a real cool-down, so plan your trip around the tide change at first light or after sunset if bass are the target. No sign of albies or bonito in this stretch yet — that push is still working its way east, and eastern Connecticut is typically last on that list, so don't burn gas looking for them just yet.
