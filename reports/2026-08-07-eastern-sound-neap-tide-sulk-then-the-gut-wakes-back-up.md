# Neap tide sulk, then the Gut wakes back up
*The Race ran soft and quiet for three days straight — then last night's current found its legs, and the stripers found the wire.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 7** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

4:12 a.m., Plum Gut, standing on the rocks at the Orient side with a needlefish in the current seam. Three casts, nothing. Then the fourth swings through the eddy behind the boulder and a striper stops it cold — mid-30s, not a giant, but it came off the bottom hard on a current that finally had some shoulder to it. That fish told me everything I needed to know about this week: the rips are waking back up.

We flagged this last week — said The Race would go soft and the bass would spread out and sulk through the neap, and that's exactly what happened. For three, four days the current at The Race and Plum Gut was running weak, maybe 1.5 to 2 knots on the max instead of the 4-plus we like, and the bite went from violent to lazy. Bass scattered off the hard structure, blues stopped showing on top, and anybody fishing those rips like it was July got humbled. But tides are rebuilding fast toward the coming full moon, and last night at The Race I watched the surface texture change in real time on the last two hours of the ebb — current stacking up again off Valiant Rock, birds working the disturbed water, and bass showing under them for the first time in days.

Water's warm right now — New London reading up around 77, Kings Point closer to 75 — which pushes this fishery into that classic late-summer pattern south of the Cape: bass go deep and nocturnal, feeding hardest on the dark side of the tide. That's exactly why the 3 a.m. program at Plum Gut and the rocks off Orient Point is producing better than anything you'll find at noon right now. If you're fishing days, fish deep structure — the drop-offs on the west side of The Race in 40 to 60 feet, working bucktails and diamond jigs tight to bottom on the strongest stage of the tide you can find. If you're fishing nights, work the current seams and eddies with big swimming plugs and needlefish, cast across the seam and let it swing dead through the slack pocket behind the rock — that's where that 30-plus fish ate.

Bluefish are riding the bunker pattern hard right now, same as everywhere else in the Sound. Adult blues are chopping bunker schools in open water off Fishers Island and out toward the Race, and when you find working birds over a bunker pod, that's your green light — snap jigs or bunker spoons trolled fast through the melee will connect. Snapper blues are thick in the back creeks around Orient and up into the Mystic and Niantic river systems, giving light-tackle guys easy, steady action on small Kastmasters and Gotcha plugs. We called the peanut bunker push two weeks out for cocktail blues in the back bays, and with bait already showing thick in those same creek mouths, that timeline's tracking right on schedule.

Fluke is the fishery carrying this zone through the current lull, and it hasn't missed a beat. Niantic's fluke boats have been punching limits consistently in 38 feet, and that pattern's held exactly like I expected — the bottom fishery doesn't care whether the rip is running hard or soft, it just wants clean structure and moving bait. Same story working the edges of The Race itself and through Fishers Island Sound — drift bucktail/Gulp combinations, white or chartreuse, tipped with a strip of squid, along the drop-offs where the sandy bottom meets rock and gravel. Depths from 25 to 45 feet have been the sweet spot, best drift speed around a knot to a knot and a half. Slow that drift down too much in the soft current and you lose bottom contact; too fast and you skip right over the fish. When the tide was weak this week, guys who adjusted lead weight down and slowed their retrieve kept catching. That's the fluke game in a nutshell — it's neap-proof if you're willing to adapt.

Porgies are stacked on the reef structure around Fishers Island, especially the rock piles off the north side — small hooks, sandworms or clam, right on bottom, and you can fill a bucket in an hour if you find the school. Good option for a slower tide day when the rips aren't worth the trip.

Looking ahead: as the moon builds toward full, expect The Race and Plum Gut current to keep sharpening through the weekend, and that should tighten up both the bass and bluefish bite considerably. If the current comes back the way it should, I'd fish the last two hours of the ebb at Plum Gut before first light — that's historically where the bigger fish show. Keep an eye on bait movement in the back creeks too; if peanut bunker keep pushing the way they have, the cocktail blue bite in the estuaries should turn from steady to genuinely good within the next week or two. Bring a rain jacket regardless — this is still hurricane season, and any tropical system spinning up out there could scramble this whole pattern fast.
