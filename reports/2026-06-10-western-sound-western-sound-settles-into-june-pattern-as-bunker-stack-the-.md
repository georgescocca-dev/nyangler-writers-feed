# Western Sound settles into June pattern as bunker stack the middle grounds
*With the analyst feed thin on live buoy numbers, structure and bait position — not water temperature — are dictating the bite from Throgs Neck to Matinecock.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — June 10** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The oceanographic desk is running lean this morning. The Dr. Fish analyst package shows no live buoy readings for the western basin, and the offshore stations that normally frame the regional picture — 44017 off Montauk, 44039 in central Long Island Sound, 44040 west — are flagged offline. The most recent SST composite in the catalog is dated June 8, two days stale, and there is no fresh wind data from the inner Sound mooring network. That means today's report leans harder than usual on structure, tide, and the bait signature anglers have been putting on the water the last ten days. What we can say with confidence: the Sound is firmly into its June thermal regime, the spring push of slot-class bass that filled the western basin in May has not left, and adult bunker are now the dominant forage from the Throgs east to Matinecock Point. The water has that early-summer green that comes when the spring phytoplankton bloom is winding down and the bait is settling into predictable holding patterns along the deeper edges.

What that means for this beat is straightforward. With bunker schools tight to structure and the thermocline still shallow or non-existent in 30 to 60 feet of water, the bass are not yet pushed deep — they are pinned to the same rockpiles and current breaks they worked through May. Execution Rocks, the Stepping Stones light, Hart Island's western edge, and the rubble west of Matinecock Point are all in play on moving water. The outgoing has been the more productive stage out of Execution and Hart, while the last two hours of incoming into the slack is firing better around Stepping Stones and the inside of Matinecock. Slot fish dominate the body of the class, with a sprinkling of overs mixed in when you find the bigger bunker pods.

The fleet picture has gotten crowded fast. The middle Sound between Hempstead Harbor and the Connecticut side has been carrying boats stacked three-deep over the bait — trollers running mojos and bunker spoons working the same water as livelining crews and jig-and-pop guys. That kind of pressure is why the chew has been turning off mid-tide even when the marks stay thick on the sounder; fish get sounded and stop committing once they have been worked over for two hours. The honest read is that the best bites in the last week have come either at first light before the fleet shows up, or on the tide change after most of the boats have moved off. Live bunker drifted on a snag-and-drop rig over a clean rockpile is still the highest-percentage presentation when bait is in the area; when it isn't, a slow-rolled soft plastic on a half-ounce head along the same structure has been pulling fish that won't commit to wire and spoons.

Fluke remain a secondary story in this zone and the reports reflect it. The Oyster Bay to Lloyd's Neck drift has been heavy on shorts with the occasional keeper, and the bottom east of Matinecock is fishing better than anywhere inside Hempstead Harbor. Bucktail-and-Gulp on a slow drift, weighted to hold the deeper edges in 35 to 50 feet, is the working approach. Porgies are starting to show on the harder bottom around Hart Island and the deeper edges of Execution, but the run hasn't fully turned on yet — expect that to firm up over the next ten days as bottom temperatures climb through the mid-60s.

Looking ahead three to five days: the controlling variable is whether the adult bunker hold in the middle Sound or push east toward the Norwalk Islands. They will not leave on their own — they leave when something pushes them, either a big body of bass herding from below or a sustained wind event stirring the water column. Without offshore buoys, I cannot give you a clean SST trajectory, but the seasonal arc says the western basin should pick up another two to three degrees over the next week, which will start to spread the bass off the tightest structure and onto the broader flats between Execution and Matinecock. Watch the moon — we're working through the lunar cycle into stronger tides, and the bigger water will favor the deeper edges over the shallow rockpiles.

I'd be on the water before five a.m. this week, running directly to whichever piece of structure is closest to the freshest bunker mark, and I'd have both a livewell and a bag of six-inch soft plastics rigged before leaving the dock. The fish are here, the bait is here, and the only thing standing between you and a clean morning is the fleet that shows up at seven.
