# SW Ledge Slack Runs 6:40 AM — Bass Are Deep, Bonito Are Still a Rumor
*The rip is stacked with bait and bottom-hugging stripers, but the albie/bonito window everyone's watching hasn't cracked open yet.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 31** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Slack at SW Ledge hit 6:40 this morning, and for about twenty minutes the whole rip went glassy before the current turned and started stacking again. That's the window I want — the last of the ebb pulling bait off the ledge, bass sitting in 55 to 65 feet waiting for it to spill over. We marked fish stacked tight to bottom on the west edge, right where the ledge drops off into the channel, and it took a 2-ounce bucktail dressed with a chunk of fresh bunker to get down to them fast enough before the current picked back up. That's the story of this whole week out here — you've got a short window to work with, and if you're not precise about the tide stage you're fishing empty water.

Water's warm, and that's pushing the bass into a pattern that's more nocturnal and more structure-locked than I'd like for the end of July. Surface temps out toward the Ledge and down around North Light have been sitting in the mid-to-high 70s, well above that 68-to-72 window stripers actually want to live in. So the good fish — and we're still finding fish in the 28 to 35 pound class — are holding deep during the day and only really committing to feed on the tide change, at first light, or after dark. Eel-and-bunker chunk combos fished on the bottom during the last two hours of the flood at SW Ledge have been the most consistent producer for us this week, but consistent doesn't mean easy. We're putting in the hours to get two or three quality fish a trip, not filling a cooler.

Fluke have been the more dependable program, and if you want a fish that'll actually cooperate right now, that's your play. Drifting the drop-off between Sandy Point and the rip line in 25 to 35 feet, we're picking up keepers mixed with a lot of shorts on white Gulp Swimming Mullets teamed with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow right along the sand-to-mud transition. The better fish — a few pushing 6 pounds — are coming off the harder structure closer to Clay Head, where the bottom breaks up and current sweeps bait past the rock piles. Black sea bass and tautog are backing up that pattern nicely on the same structure; if you're marking bait and current is moving, drop a green crab or a squid strip on a jig head and you'll usually connect within a few drifts.

Now, the question everyone at the dock keeps asking: where are the bonito? Honest answer — not here yet, not in numbers anyone should build a trip around. We flagged this a few days back, said the water temperature at Block Island and Montauk had crossed the threshold where bonito typically show, and that building tide into the next new moon on August 12th would be the trigger. That call still holds, but I'm not going to tell you they're stacked off North Light when they're not. What I will say is the bait is right — we're seeing good pods of sand eels and small bunker working the surface off Sandy Point and around the Ledge itself, and that's exactly the kind of forage that pulls albies and bonito in fast once the temperature break lines up. If you're itching to scout, I'd be doing exactly that — running light spinning gear with small Deadly Dicks or epoxy jigs, working the surface around North Light and Cormorant Rock on the calmer mornings, just in case you stumble into the first wave. But I wouldn't plan a whole charter around it yet.

Bluefish, on the other hand, are exactly where you'd expect them with water this warm — they don't mind the heat the way stripers do, and they're the ones actually blitzing right now. We've had good runs of choppers in the 4 to 8 pound class working peanut bunker schools along the beach south of the breachway and out toward the Ledge on the tide change, and they'll hit just about anything with flash — Kastmasters, poppers, doesn't matter much when they're fired up like that.

For boats able to make the run, the offshore edges are still holding tuna on the warm-water lines, and that piece of the puzzle hasn't changed much — worth the trip if you've got the range and the weather window, but that's a long day from Galilee and not something I'd send a half-day charter after.

Looking at the week ahead: the tide's building back toward that August 12th new moon, which should start reconcentrating bait around the rip lines and structure instead of letting it spread out in this slack summer current. If a temperature break sets up on that warm-water edge in the next several days — and the signs are pointing that direction — this is the window where I'd expect the first credible bonito or albie report to break loose off the Block. Until then, I'm fishing SW Ledge on the tide changes for bass, working the fluke drift off Sandy Point during the day, and keeping a rod rigged light just in case something fast shows up on the surface when I'm not looking for it.
