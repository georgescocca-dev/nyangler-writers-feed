# SW Ledge Slack at 6:52: Bass on Eels, Bonito Closing the Gap
*A softening neap tide is stretching the bite window at the Ledge, and the water off Block Island is finally warm enough to start watching for the first bonito.*

**GALILEE, RI — July 21** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

6:52 AM. That's when SW Ledge goes dead slack this Tuesday, and if you've fished this rip as long as I have you already know the trick — you don't fish the slack, you fish the twenty minutes on either side of it. That's the window this week, tighter than usual because we're sliding into a first-quarter neap, and the current off the Ledge just doesn't have the shoulders it had ten days ago. Less push means the bait sits lower in the water column longer, which is exactly why the bass have been hanging on the down-current edge in 40 to 55 feet instead of blasting through the top third of the rip like they do on a big moon tide.

We've been running eels on that edge — 12 to 14-inch live eels on a fish-finder rig, 3 ounces of egg weight, letting them swim naturally into the seam where the sandy bottom drops off toward the deeper water east of the Ledge. Fish to 35 pounds have come off that program in the last week, though I want to be honest — it's not every drift. We're averaging one to two quality fish per tide change, sometimes a double, sometimes nothing until the last five minutes before the current builds back. The softer neap current is a double-edged sword: it widens your workable window, but it also means the bait isn't getting pushed as hard into the ambush zone, so the bass have to be actively hunting rather than reacting. Patience matters more than usual right now.

Bunker chunks on the bottom in that same stretch have also produced, especially in the last hour of the outgoing when the tide is still moving enough to carry scent but not so hard you're constantly re-baiting. If you're marking bunker on the meter anywhere between the Ledge and Sandy Point, that's worth a slow pass with cut bait before you commit to the eel program — some days the bigger fish want the oil in the water more than a swimming profile.

South of the Judith, the reef grounds are still the most dependable thing going, and I said as much last week — fluke and sea bass down there have been the backbone of this port all summer, and nothing's changed. We're working 40 to 60 feet on the humps south of the breachway, dragging 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails on the drift, tipped with a squid strip for the sea bass that come up on the same drops. Fluke to 6 pounds have shown up on that combo this week, with a good number of shorts mixed in — keep moving until you find the size class you want, because it's patchy from hump to hump. Sea bass have been thick and cooperative, 14 to 17 inches on average, sometimes stacked so tight you're picking through doubles just to find the keepers. That bite doesn't care much about tide stage — it's more about getting your bait down clean, so fish it on the slower push if you can, less scope, better bottom contact.

Now here's the one I've been watching closely, because we flagged it last week — said bonito and albies should show within a week or two, and the water's finally getting there. Off the Southeast Light and out toward North Rip, the surface temps are edging into the same range that's had Montauk holding around 69 degrees and the open ocean off the Bight pushing past 71. That's the number that matters. Bonito don't show on a calendar, they show on a thermometer, and we're right at the doorstep. I haven't had a confirmed hookup yet — nobody I've talked to at the dock has either — but the bait's right, small silversides and rain bait are stacking up off Clay Head and around the North Light rip, and that's the setup. If you want to be the first guy with a bonito story this year, keep a light spinning rod rigged with a half-ounce Deadly Dick or a green machine epoxy jig ready on the rail when you're running out to the Ledge. You'll know it when you see it — the terns will tip you off before the fish do.

Bluefish have been mixed in with the bass on the Ledge drifts, mostly 3 to 5 pounds, happy to jump on the same eels and chunks, which is fine by most of my charters but can shred a bag of eels fast if they show up in numbers. No giant blitzes locally yet, though the bunker pods pushing along the mainland beaches to our west have kept that bite alive elsewhere on the coast, and if that bait keeps drifting our way, expect the blues to thicken up around Galilee and the breachway in the next week or two.

Looking ahead, the neap tide keeps softening through midweek, which should keep stretching that bass window at SW Ledge — more patience required, but a longer runway if you're willing to sit on the drift. The reef bite south of the Judith should stay steady regardless of tide phase; that's been the safe bet all summer and I don't see it slowing. And if this water keeps creeping warmer around North Light and Clay Head, I wouldn't be shocked to hear about the first bonito of the season inside the next seven to ten days. I'll be watching the rips closely — if they show, you'll hear about it here first.
