# Moriches Inlet's West Rip Deepened a Foot — Blues and Fluke Found the New Edge
*Storm-carved structure off Cupsogue is holding bait and predators while the bay waits on its first real bunker push.*

**CUPSOGUE BEACH, NY — August 31** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

Walk the west jetty at Moriches Inlet on a dropping tide right now and you can see it before you ever wet a line — the scour hole off the rocks has deepened noticeably since the last blow pushed through in mid-August, and the sandbar that usually pinches the channel down off Pattersquash has shifted maybe fifteen to twenty yards east. That's not a huge move by Moriches standards, this inlet has always been a living thing, cutting and filling depending on what storm energy hits it and from what angle. But it matters for where the fish are sitting right now. The new edge of that bar is holding a sharper drop-off than we've seen all summer, and current is ripping across it hard enough on the last two hours of the ebb to stack bait against the structure instead of just sweeping past it.

That's the mechanical reason the bite has concentrated so tightly around the inlet mouth and the Cupsogue side this week instead of spreading out into the open bay like it does in high summer. Water temps off the ocean beach are still running warm — the open Atlantic buoy off the Bight is reading in the low 70s with some wave energy mixed in, nothing dramatic, three to four foot at five seconds, enough to keep the surf a little sloppy but not enough to shut down the bar. Inside the bay, the shallow flats around Tuthill Point and up toward East Moriches are heating up hard on afternoon sun, which is exactly why the fish that can move are pulling toward the inlet where the water column stays mixed and cooler on the bottom third.

We flagged this pattern last week — said Moriches would keep fluke on structure near the inlet where the current does the mixing, snapper blues thick in the bay, and the first bonito or albie scouting reports within a week or two as the fall forage shift gets rolling. That's exactly how it's playing out. Nothing revolutionary, but it's tracking.

Fluke are the honest story right now — call it a fair bite, not a hot one. The keepers are coming off the channel edges on the west side of the inlet, in twelve to twenty feet where that new drop-off is cutting, and along the deeper stretch running past Tuthill Point toward the day markers. Three-quarter ounce white bucktails teamed with a five-inch Gulp Swimming Mullet in white or chartreuse, dragged slow along bottom on the last of the ebb into the start of the flood, has been the most consistent combination reported dockside. Shorts are still thick in the bay proper — anybody fishing the flats around East Moriches or up in the Forge River is going to sort through a lot of them before finding a keeper. The window for fluke here is closing the way it does every late August; expect the bite to hold fair through September on structure and in the deeper channels before the real push offshore starts, but don't expect the numbers game of July anymore.

Bluefish are the one thing nobody's complaining about. Snapper blues are thick in the bay — East Moriches docks, the flats off Smith Point, anywhere there's current and small bait, kids with a bobber and a piece of spearing are into them all afternoon. The bigger harbor blues, three to six pounds, are working the bunker pods moving through the inlet mouth itself and along the Cupsogue jetty. Poppers and metal — Deadly Dicks, half-ounce and one-ounce Ava jigs — worked on the surface at first light and again on the last hour of daylight have been drawing consistent strikes right off the rocks. That bunker movement through the inlet is modest right now, nothing like a full-blown push, but it's building, and bluefish activity is going to track directly with how dense those bunker schools get over the next couple weeks.

Weakfish are scattered and mostly a dusk-into-dark proposition — a few reports of fish coming off the deeper holes near the Moriches Inlet Coast Guard station on bucktails tipped with a strip of squid, worked slow right along bottom on the last of an outgoing tide. Nothing to build a trip around yet, but worth a rod in the spread if you're anchored up after dark working fluke or bass water. Porgies are steady and underrated right now — the jetty rocks at Cupsogue and the riprap on the inside of the inlet are holding good numbers on small hooks baited with clam, an easy bite for anyone who wants dinner without overthinking it.

Striped bass remain the wildcard everybody's watching. There's nothing happening inside Moriches yet that looks like the fall push — that trigger is bait density, and the bunker schools staging up and down the south shore need to concentrate a lot more before stripers commit here in blitz numbers. With the moon building toward full over the next several days, tidal flow is going to increase, which should start pulling bait tighter into the inlet and setting the stage. If there's a day to check the inlet mouth on the last two hours of the ebb into the turn, this week and next is when that first real striper sign is most likely to show. Keep an eye on the west rip — that new scour hole is exactly the kind of structure stripers use to ambush bait once the bunker finally stack up against it.
