# Night tide on the channel edge: bass go dark, bunker bring the blues topside
*With the bay pushing into the low 70s, I'm working two different clocks this week — stripers after sundown, fluke and blitzing blues in the heat of the day.*

**PERTH AMBOY, NJ — July 18** — _by Marisol Duarte, Zone Writer_

Here's the call I made this week, and it's the same one I'd make again: I'm splitting my trips by the clock instead of by the spot. Mornings and afternoons I'm running the channel edges off Keyport Flats and the drop into the Chapel Hill Channel for fluke. Nights, I'm parking the boat on the deep water off Flynn's Knoll and the edge of Romer Shoal and letting bunker chunks soak on the bottom. Two totally different programs, same week, same bay — and the reason is water temp.

Kings Point is reading 71.6 on the Sound side, and our water isn't far behind that — the bay's pushing solidly into the low-to-mid 70s now that we've had a stretch of calm, sticky weather. That's warm enough that the striped bass have made their move — not out of the system, just out of the daylight. They're sliding into deeper structure and current breaks and feeding almost entirely after dark now. This isn't a disappearing act, it's a thermal retreat, and if you're still fishing the Knoll at noon wondering where the bass went, that's your answer. Shift your trip to the last two hours of light through the middle of the night and fish the deeper cuts — 18 to 30 feet off Flynn's Knoll, the edge of the Chapel Hill Channel, and the deep bend by Romer Shoal. Bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig, 3-ounce sinker to hold bottom against the current, fished on the front half of the incoming when that cooler water starts pushing back into the bay off Sandy Hook. That's when I've been marking the better fish on the graph — not big numbers, but real 28-to-34 inch bass willing to eat once the sun's off the water.

During the day, forget the bass and go to work on fluke, because that bite hasn't blinked with the heat. Channel edges are still the program — the drop-off along Keyport Flats where the flat spills into 12 to 18 feet, and the edges of the main channel running past the Highlands toward the Hook. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift, tipped with a strip of squid if the current's light. I'm also seeing decent counts on the deeper edge by the Rip at the Hook when the tide's really moving — that structure holds bait and the fluke sit right on the seam waiting for it to get swept past. Nothing giant this week, mostly 16 to 19 inches with a few keepers mixed in, but it's a dependable bite, and in July that counts for something. If you want a shot at a bigger one, work the deeper side of the channel on the last of the outgoing when the water's cleanest — that's usually when the better-grade fish show.

Now the wildcard: bunker. There are big schools stacking up around the Hook right now, the same pods that have had blues blitzing the surf from Belmar on north all month. That pattern is starting to push into the bay, and if it fully arrives — and I think it will, because the bunker aren't showing signs of scattering with this stable weather — we're going to have blitz-style bluefish activity right off the beach at Sandy Hook and up into the lower bay. Keep a topwater rod rigged and ready. If you see birds working or bunker getting nervous on the surface off the Hook or Romer Shoal, run to it and throw a pencil popper or a bunker spoon — blues under bait like that don't make you work for it.

Weakfish are the other piece worth watching. We flagged last week that the bite would rebuild on calm, warm evenings, and that's exactly the window opening up right now in the Navesink and Shrewsbury. Both rivers have been quiet through the heat of the day, but the last hour of light into dusk, on the tail of the outgoing, has been producing weakfish in the 16-to-20 inch range on light jigs — quarter-ounce bucktails tipped with a Gulp shrimp, worked slow along the deeper bends near the Rumson bridges. It's not a numbers game yet, one or two fish an evening if you put the time in, but it's building, not fading, and that's the direction you want to see it going in mid-July.

Sea state offshore has been a little rough — 44025 out in the Bight has been kicking up 2 to 3 foot chop on a short period, which is nothing to us inside the bay but it does mean bait and predators alike are staying tucked into the protected water rather than pushing out toward open ocean. That works in our favor. It keeps the bunker schools tight to the Hook and keeps the fluke locked onto the channel structure instead of scattering.

Looking ahead: we're running toward the first quarter moon on the 21st, current easing off a bit heading into that. I'd expect the night bass bite to stay steady through the week as long as the heat holds, the fluke to keep producing through the middle of the day on the channel edges, and if that bunker pod holds position off the Hook, I wouldn't be surprised to see the first real inside-the-bay blitz of the summer by the weekend. Have a topwater rod rigged just in case — you don't want to be digging through the rocket launcher when the blues show up under the birds.
