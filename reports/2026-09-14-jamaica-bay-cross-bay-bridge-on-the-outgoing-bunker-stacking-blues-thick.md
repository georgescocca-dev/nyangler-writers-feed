# Cross Bay Bridge on the Outgoing: Bunker Stacking, Blues Thick, Bass Starting to Show
*The inlet mouth is loading up with peanut bunker and the tide range is building toward the new moon — this is the access point to be working right now.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — September 14** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one shot at the bay this week, the play is the Cross Bay Bridge on the back half of the outgoing, working the pilings and the current seam where the channel narrows. That's where the bait is being squeezed right now, and squeezed bait is exactly what turns a quiet September evening into a real bite. Bunker pods have been building near the Rockaway Inlet mouth for two weeks, and this week they finally started pushing up into the bay proper on the tide, funneling past Cross Bay and Marine Parkway Bridge in tighter, nervous schools instead of the loose scattered pods we had in late August. We flagged this last week — said the bunker schools would tighten and the bass would start showing real interest as the tide strengthened toward the new moon — and that's exactly the shape of things now. It's not a blitz yet. But the ingredients are stacking the way they're supposed to this time of year.

Water's holding in the low 70s across the bay, which is still warm enough that this isn't urgent migration behavior from the stripers — more like early interest, fish shadowing bait without fully committing to it. That's an important distinction for anyone planning a trip. The bluefish, on the other hand, are not being subtle about anything. Snapper blues remain thick inside the bay — Bergen Basin, the flats off Canarsie Pier, the mouth of Hawtree Creek — and they'll hit anything small and flashy: 1-inch Kastmasters, tiny Deadly Dicks, bits of bunker on a snapper popper rig under a bobber for the kids. Where it gets more interesting is at the inlet itself, where adult bluefish in the 3-6 pound class have been mixing into the bunker schools on the last two hours of the ebb, working the rip line that forms off the Rockaway Inlet jetty as water pours out past Breezy Point. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig, 2-3 ounces of lead depending on how hard that current is running, have been the standby there, but wire leader isn't a bad idea right now — these blues aren't picky and they're not gentle with mono.

Stripers are still the wait-and-see story. The staging pattern that's been building from Raritan Bay up through Narragansett is showing early signs inside our zone too — bass have been spotted working the underside of bunker schools near the Marine Parkway Bridge on the outgoing, particularly around the second and third hour when current stacks bait against the bridge structure. Nobody's calling it consistent yet. The fish present are mostly in the 20-28 inch range, holding deep under the bait rather than crashing it on top, which tells you they're interested but not desperate. If you're targeting them specifically, a bunker chunk fished on bottom in 12-18 feet of water on the bay side of the bridge, right where the current breaks around the pilings, is the smart move over blind-casting swimmers. As the tide range keeps building toward the new moon over the next week, expect that interest to sharpen into something closer to commitment — that's the pattern every fall, and there's no reason to think this year breaks from it.

Fluke are winding down but haven't shut off completely. The channel edges off the JFK Airport marsh flats and the deeper water near Canarsie Pier have still been giving up keeper fish in the 18-20 inch range on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with a strip of squid, worked slow along bottom on the last of the incoming. It's a fading bite — bigger picture, the fluke are starting their seasonal push toward deeper water outside the bay, and every week from here forward that bite gets thinner. If fluke is the goal, this weekend and next are probably the last realistic window before the exodus accelerates.

For access, Canarsie Pier remains the easiest no-boat option if the wind is up and you don't want to fight a kayak launch — good for snapper blues and the occasional keeper fluke on bait, and it lets you fish comfortably through a full tide change. Kayakers looking for the bunker-and-bass interaction should be launching near Cross Bay and working the flats and channel edges rather than fighting current in open water — that inlet rip is not a place to be in a plastic boat when the ebb is running hard. Surf anglers at Breezy Point and along Rockaway Beach have had a quieter week overall, mostly snapper-sized blues and the occasional small bass at first light, but that's a spot worth watching closely — once the peanut bunker schools that are building inside the bay start getting pushed back out through the inlet on a strong outgoing, that beach can turn on fast, and it's historically one of the better fall blitz locations in the whole zone.

Looking ahead, the next few days should keep building rather than backing off. Tidal range is increasing toward the new moon, which means stronger current, more bait movement, and more reason for bass to commit rather than just shadow. If the wind stays cooperative, the smart bet is working the bridges — Cross Bay and Marine Parkway both — on the outgoing over the next several tide cycles, watching for that shift from bass sitting under bait to bass actually crashing it. That's usually the tell that the real fall run has arrived inside the bay, and by the read of things this week, it's close.
