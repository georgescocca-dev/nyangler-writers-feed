# The Cut Off Cupsogue Deepened Two Feet — Fluke Found the New Edge Fast
*A storm-carved trench on the west side of Moriches Inlet is holding fish while the ocean stays lumpy and the bass go nocturnal.*

**MORICHES INLET, NY — July 20** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

I ran the west bar at Cupsogue on the last of Thursday's ebb and the bottom read different than it did two weeks ago. There's a new scour hole cut into the inside edge of the bar, maybe 40 yards off the point, running eight to ten feet deeper than the flat around it — classic post-swell reshaping. That kind of groundswell we've been getting off the ocean, two-footers at 14-second intervals, doesn't look like much from the beach, but that long period pushes a lot of water and it's been quietly re-carving the inlet's throat all month. The inlet doesn't sit still. It never has. Every time we get a period like this, sand moves off the bar and drops into the channel, and the fish find the new structure before we do.

That's exactly where the fluke have been stacking. The edge of that new hole, 16 to 22 feet, has been holding fish on the last two hours of the ebb and the first hour of the flood — moving water pulling bait off the flat and dropping it right into that trench. Chartreuse and white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the drop, have been the ticket. A few guys working the same water with strip baits on standard fluke rigs are doing fine too, but the bucktail-Gulp combo is getting more bites because it stays down in that heavier current better than bait alone. Fish have run to five and six pounds off that edge, with a lot of short-to-borderline fish mixed in — so bring the ruler and don't assume. We flagged this corridor last week as the one to watch through July, and it's holding up: Moriches into Shinnecock has been the most consistent fluke water on the whole South Shore right now, better than what the ocean-side beaches are giving up with this swell running.

Striped bass are a different animal entirely this time of year, and anyone still fishing them at noon in this bay is fighting the water temperature, not the fish. Once that sun gets up and the shallows in the back bay push into the mid-70s, bass shut down and slide into whatever depth and shade they can find. The program right now is after dark, full stop. I've had better luck working bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs along the rocks at the west jetty from two hours before high through the top of the tide, and eel guys working the deeper cuts off Pattersquash Island after 9 PM have been doing quiet, steady work on schoolies with the occasional slot fish mixed in. Tuthill Point's flats hold bait after dark too — small bunker and spearing get trapped against the marsh edge on the dropping tide, and that's pulled some bass shallow right at last light. Nothing blitz-worthy, but real, catchable fish if you're willing to fish the clock instead of the calendar.

Bluefish have been the wildcard. The bunker pods pushing along the beach from the west are the same schools that blitzes further up the coast, and there's no reason that bait doesn't keep drifting east into our stretch. I haven't seen a full-on blitz off Cupsogue yet this week, but there's been bird activity — terns working nervous water just off the second bar around dawn a few mornings running, which usually means blues are underneath pushing bait to the surface. If you're walking the beach at first light with a metal — a Deadly Dick or a Hopkins — that's the window. It hasn't been consistent enough to plan a whole trip around, but it's worth having the rod rigged and ready if you're already out there for stripers.

Weakfish have been quiet, which tracks with where we are in the season — they like it calm and warm at last light, and we haven't had a full stretch of settled evenings to let that pattern build. When we do get a run of slick, glassy evenings on the incoming, that's when to work soft plastics — bone or pink shad-tail swimbaits — slow along the channel edges in the back bay behind Cupsogue and around the Moriches Bay side of the inlet. It's not there yet, but the conditions that build that bite are close.

Looking ahead: we're rolling toward the first-quarter moon on the 21st, which means neap tides through the back half of this week. Weaker current through the inlet sounds like bad news, but it's actually going to widen the fluke window — less brutal current speed means that scour hole off Cupsogue stays fishable longer instead of just the peak hour of the ebb. I'd fish it from mid-tide through the last two hours rather than trying to hit it exactly at max flow. For stripers, the softer current should also stretch the after-dark bite a little longer into the morning before the sun pushes them back down. Ocean's still carrying some leftover swell energy, so I'd keep surf efforts at Cupsogue focused on the calmer stretches between sets rather than fighting white water all morning. If that bunker push keeps sliding east, don't be shocked if the bluefish window in the early mornings turns into something worth planning a whole trip around by this weekend.
