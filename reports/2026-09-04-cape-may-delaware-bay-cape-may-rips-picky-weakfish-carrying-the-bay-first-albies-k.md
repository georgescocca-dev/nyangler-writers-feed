# Cape May Rips picky, weakfish carrying the bay, first albies knocking on the door
*Fluke are sliding off the flats toward structure, the drum run is long gone, and there's early word of bonito working the beach south of us.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — September 4** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Grade for the week: C-plus on the ocean side, B-minus in the bay. That's about where early September usually sits around here, and this year's tracking to form. The rips have been picky — not dead, but you've got to work the tide stage right or you're soaking bait for nothing. Delaware Bay's carrying the week on the back of a strong weakfish bite that nobody's talking about loud enough.

Here's the read. Water in Delaware Bay is sitting in the mid-70s, which is warm enough to keep weakfish and kingfish active in the back bay but not warm enough to trigger any kind of aggressive feed on the ocean side. That's the story of this whole stretch — everything's a half-step slow because the water hasn't turned over yet. The building moon toward full on the 6th and 7th is going to tighten up bait schools everywhere from here to Raritan Bay, and when that happens the striper bite that's been scattered and non-committal all week should start to organize. It's not blitzing yet. Bunker and peanut bunker are thick around the reef sites and holding bluefish hard, but the bass are still staged on bait rather than actively working it over. IF the moon does what moons do this time of year and the tide flow picks up behind it, look for the first real push of activity within the next week to ten days, not this weekend.

We flagged this back at the end of August — said to keep an eye on the rips for bonito and false albacore working down the beach from the central Shore, and that's exactly what's starting to show. Nothing stacked yet off Cape May Point, but there's enough warm water sitting in this zone that it's a logical next landing spot, and a few scattered sightings off the Wildwood side back that up. Worth having a small metal rigged and ready — half-ounce Deadly Dick or a Got-Cha plug — if you're out on the rips this week and see any nervous water or diving birds working bait on the surface. Don't go looking for a blitz. Go looking for a window.

On the fluke side, the story is exactly what it should be for this time of year: the doormats are done with the back bays. They're pulling off the flats and sliding toward structure — the Wildwood ridge and the deeper edges of the rips — ahead of their move offshore. If you want a shot at a legit fish over four pounds, that's where to put your bucktail, not in the skinny stuff. Six-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a three-quarter-ounce bucktail, dragged slow along bottom on the last two hours of the ebb, has been the combination producing quality fish rather than just numbers. Numbers are thinning out fast across the whole shore, so don't expect a fifteen-fish day. Expect a couple of good ones if you're patient and fish the structure right.

Hereford Inlet has been steady, not spectacular. The current there is doing what it always does this time of year — pushing bait through the cut and holding bluefish tight to the edges on both the incoming and the first two hours of the outgoing. Bunker chunks or peanut bunker on a fishfinder rig, fished tight to the jetty rocks, has been the play. Cobia have shown up occasionally around the reef sites where the anchovies are thick — not a fishery you plan a trip around, but if you're bottom fishing bunker chunks near structure and something bulldogs off with your rod tip buried, don't be shocked.

Down in Delaware Bay, weakfish are the real story right now. Fortescue and the mud flats up toward Brandywine Shoal are holding fish on the drop of the tide, and they're eating shedder crab and Fishbites in the mid-70s water without much hesitation. Kingfish are mixed in on the same bottom, which makes for a nice change of pace if the weakfish go quiet for a stretch. As for black drum — that run's done. Won't see it again until May, and anybody telling you different this time of year is selling something.

Triggerfish have been showing around the rocks and wreck structure off the Cape May side, typical late-summer pattern when the water's warm and clean. Small pieces of clam on a light jig head, dropped right to structure, is the way to pick them off if you've got the patience for it.

Looking ahead: expect the rips to stay picky through the weekend unless that moon-driven tide flow kicks the bait density up a notch. The better bet for the next few days is the bay side — weakfish on the drop tide out of Fortescue, fluke pushed to structure on the ocean side, and bluefish still reliable around the reef sites wherever the peanut bunker are stacked. If there's only one window to fish this week, make it the last two hours of the outgoing at the rips or the Wildwood ridge, and keep a rigged metal rod on deck just in case that albie push finally makes the turn into Cape May water. It's close. It's just not here yet.
