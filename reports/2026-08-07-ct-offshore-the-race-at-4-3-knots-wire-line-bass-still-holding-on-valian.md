# The Race at 4.3 Knots: Wire-Line Bass Still Holding on Valiant Rock
*Currents are rebuilding out of the neap, the bass are deep and current-locked, and the first bonito are still a county away.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — August 7** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

Four-point-three knots. That's what the max ebb was running through the Race on Wednesday afternoon, timed off Valiant Rock, and it's the first real number I've clocked all month that tells me the current's rebuilding out of that neap slump we limped through in late July. That extra push matters more than any water-temp reading I could give you, because in this rip, current speed is the tide. When it's running under three knots, the Race fishes soft and scattered. Above four, the boils stack up off Valiant and Race Rock proper, bait gets pinned against structure, and the bass show up to eat.

New London's reading 77.4 at the surface this week — warm, no question, and warmer than Kings Point at the west end of the Sound, which tells you the whole eastern basin has been soaking under this stretch of light air. But surface temp doesn't run the Race. What runs the Race is the cold water the current rips up off the bottom, and on that Wednesday max ebb I had 30 feet of wire out over 55 feet of water off the northeast corner of Valiant, and the transducer thermometer showed a good eight degrees colder than what New London was reporting at the surface. That's the whole game out here in August — find the cold seam the rip is pulling up, park a bunker spoon or a black bucktail with a pork rind trailer right in it, and let the wire do the work getting it down through the churn.

Slack came at 1:42 that afternoon, and I mean slack — not the loose, sloppy version you get on a weak tide, but a real stand-still where you could feel the boat go quiet under your feet. We worked five minutes either side of it with bucktails, straight vertical over the rockpile, and connected on two bass in the low 30s and lost a better fish that came unbuttoned right at the boat. That's honest work for this time of year — the Race in August isn't a numbers game, it's a timing game, and if you're not within that fifteen-minute window around the turn, you're fishing empty water no matter how good your gear is.

Plum Gut has been the more consistent producer the last several days, mostly because the bunker have pushed hard into the Gut's western edge, right off Orient Point, and the bluefish have followed them in thick. We flagged this coming two weeks back — said the peanut bunker push would spread cocktail blues into the back bays within the window, and that's exactly what's happening now along the inshore flats behind the Gut. Snapper-sized blues are stacked in the shallows, and the adult bunker schools pushing through the main current are getting hammered by bigger blues on top, blitzes lasting ten, fifteen minutes at a stretch on the last two hours of the ebb. Epoxy jigs in bone or chrome, cast right into the froth, get bit almost every drop. No albies or bonito in the mix yet, but that's the next shoe to drop.

On the bonito front — we called the sequence on this one too. Rhode Island breachways and the Vineyard rips first, Block Island and Montauk behind that within the week, east-end Long Island inlets coming in last. Montauk's sitting at 74.5 right now and climbing, which puts it right at the edge of that threshold. I haven't marked a single bonito or albie in the Gut yet, and I wouldn't send anyone out here specifically hunting them this week — but with water this warm pushing east and the bait already staged, I'd expect the first fish to show around the Gut, Little Gull, and Great Gull within seven to ten days, not weeks. When they do, it'll be epoxy jigs and small metal worked fast through the rip on the last of the ebb, same as every year.

Bottom fishing has quietly been the most dependable thing out here. Black sea bass are thick on the reef structure around the Sluiceway and Bartlett Reef, holding in 50 to 65 feet, and they're not picky — chartreuse bucktails tipped with squid strips are getting bit steadily on every drift, keeper fish mixed with a lot of shorts you'll have to sort through. Fluke have been spottier, mostly smaller fish off the edges of the reef where the current softens up, and I'd call that bite mixed at best right now — worth a stop on the way out or in, not worth building a whole trip around.

Offshore, the seas have had a little lump to them — 3.6 feet at a six-second period out past the open water, out of the south, which isn't dangerous but it's been enough to make the ride past Race Rock uncomfortable on an outgoing tide when wind and current oppose each other. Pick your window on the back half of the ebb when it flattens, or wait for the morning glass before the sea breeze fills in.

Look ahead: with the current rebuilding and no tropical trouble on the radar yet, I expect the Race and the Gut to keep sharpening through the week. The slack windows are going to keep shifting later each day as we move through the cycle, so check your tide chart before you leave the dock — five minutes late to the turn out here and you've missed the whole show. I'd bet on the bass holding to the same pattern, deep and current-locked, and I'd keep half an eye toward Little Gull for the first bonito showing. If the buildup keeps going the way it has been, I don't think we're far off.
