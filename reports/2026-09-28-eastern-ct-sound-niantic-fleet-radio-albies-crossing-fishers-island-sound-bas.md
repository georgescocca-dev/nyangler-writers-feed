We flagged this pattern last week — said the bonito and albie push out of Rhode Island would keep working west through Fishers Island Sound, and that's exactly what's showing up now. Captains working the grounds between Black Point and McCook Point are reporting surface activity that has nothing to do with bluefish — quick, slashing hits, fish that vanish as fast as they appear. That's albie behavior, and it's paired with a striped bass pattern that's arguably the bigger story for most readers on this beat. The Harvest Moon tide is pushing serious current through the Niantic River mouth and Hole-in-the-Wall, and that current is doing what strong tides do in late September: flushing bait hard and turning bass and blues into aggressive, visible feeders.

Water temps back that up. New London is sitting at 63.7°F, still warm enough to hold bait shallow but cool enough that fish are starting to feed with purpose rather than lounging. That's a comfortable number for this time of year — not a crash, not a heat wave, just steady seasonal cooling that's nudging everything toward the fall pattern regulars around here know well.

On the bottom, sea bass action out toward Twotree Island Channel and the deeper water off Black Point has stayed dependable. Boats drifting there are reporting decent numbers of keeper sea bass on standard bucktail-and-Gulp combos — a 4- or 5-inch white or pink Gulp swimming shad on a half-ounce jighead, bounced right off structure, is still getting bit. Porgies are mixed in on the same drifts, and anyone soaking clam on a high-low rig near rockpiles is filling a bucket without much drama. That's the honest read: not a blitz, just steady, reliable bottom fishing that's held up for weeks now.

Fluke are winding down, as expected this late in the season, but there are still holdover fish being picked off the deeper drifts in Niantic Bay on the last of the outgoing tide. Nothing to build a trip around specifically, but if you're already out there for sea bass, a bucktail tipped with a Gulp strip dragged slow along the bottom is worth a few extra drifts before you commit to the reef bite.

The striper story is where the fleet chatter gets louder. Bass are being worked at the Niantic River mouth and around structure near Hole-in-the-Wall on peanut bunker pushed by the moon tide — snap jigs, small metal, and swimming plugs worked through the current seams are drawing strikes from schoolies up through mid-20-inch fish. Bluefish are mixed into the same bait, which tracks with what's happening up and down the coast right now — wherever there's bunker getting pushed hard by current, blues show up uninvited. Nothing unusual there, just bait density doing what it does.

The albie question is the one worth watching over the next few days. If that warm-water signature keeps sliding west the way it has been, expect more sightings closer to McCook Point and into Niantic Bay itself, especially on calm, glassy mornings when sight-casting actually works. Light spinning gear, small epoxy jigs or Deadly Dick-style metals in the half-ounce to three-quarter-ounce range, cast well ahead of breaking fish rather than into the middle of them — that's the standard approach and it hasn't changed. These fish don't stick around a spot long, so the boats that are connecting are the ones running and gunning on visible signs rather than anchoring and waiting.

Looking at the next 72 hours, the setup favors the tide more than the weather. Keep an eye on the bigger flood and ebb swings around the new moon push — that's when bait gets flushed hardest through the river mouth and Hole-in-the-Wall, and that's when the bass and blues have shown up most reliably this month. If the albie push keeps building the way it has, this could turn into a genuine multi-species window before the water drops another few degrees and shuts the door on the light-tackle stuff. Worth getting out for if your schedule allows it — just don't expect the albies to hold still long enough to plan a whole trip around them just yet. The tautog opener is also creeping onto the calendar as water continues cooling, so keep that in the back pocket for early October.
