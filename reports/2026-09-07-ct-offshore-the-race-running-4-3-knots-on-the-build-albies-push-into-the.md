# The Race Running 4.3 Knots on the Build: Albies Push Into the Gut, Bass Staging Deep
*New moon current on the way in behind a full moon that already tightened the bunker — here's where the rips are setting up for the fall's first real push.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — September 7** — _by Capt. Sabrina Cole, Zone Writer_

Four-point-three knots. That's what The Race is throwing on a solid ebb right now, and it's a number worth paying attention to because it's about to get bigger. We're six days out from full and three days into the wind-down toward the new moon on the 10th, and the current at Valiant Rock and Race Rock is still ramping. Water temp at New London is sitting at 69.4, Montauk's holding at 69.1 — that's a tight, stable range for early September, and it means the thermal picture isn't pushing fish anywhere. The moon is. This is the week the rip country flips from summer pattern to fall staging, and the fleet out of Fishers Island and the Plum Gut side is starting to see it happen in real time.

We flagged this last week — said the full moon would tighten the bunker schools further and that legitimate blitz behavior was a matter of days, not weeks. That's exactly what's setting up now. Bunker are balling tighter through the Gut and along the Sluiceway edges, and the stripers sitting on them aren't just picking at stragglers anymore — mates working Plum Gut on the last of the ebb this week reported bass coming up hard on bait pushed against the rip line, the kind of surface commitment you don't see in August. It's not a blitz yet. It's the fish getting ready to throw one.

The bass bite itself has been mixed but honest. Wire line trolling bucktails through Valiant Rock on the top third of the ebb has been the most consistent producer — 6 to 9-ounce bucktails in white or chartreuse, worked deep and slow along the seam where the rip breaks over the rock pile in 40 to 60 feet. Fish are coming from the bottom third of the water column, not up high, which tells you they're still keyed on structure and current break rather than chasing bait on top. Slack water at Valiant — and this week that window is tightening to a scant 15-20 minutes around the turn — is when the bigger fish show. Miss that window and you're bouncing bucktails through 4-plus knots of moving water with no real shot at a clean presentation. Timing the turn is the whole game right now, more than usual, because the new moon build is going to compress those slack windows even further by the weekend.

Bluefish have been the story nobody's complaining about. They're aggressive, abundant, and chewing through bunker schools from The Race out to Little Gull and Great Gull Island edges. An 18.49-pound blue took a derby at Montauk this week, which gives you a sense of the size class moving through — these aren't rat blues, they're mature fish showing up ahead of the bass in numbers. Diamond jigs and bunker chunks fished on wire off the Gull Islands have been steady producers on the drop, particularly on the first two hours of a dropping tide when bait gets pushed off the flats into deeper water.

The albie and bonito situation is the one to watch hardest over the next several days. That push has been building hard through Rhode Island and Buzzards Bay, and it's actively moving west and north — which puts the eastern end of the Sound, the Race approaches, and the Fishers Island rips directly in its path. It hasn't fully arrived at Plum Gut yet, but the water temp and bait profile are right for it, and reports out of the Fishers Island side this week suggest scattered fish already showing on the surface early morning, keying on bay anchovies and peanut bunker. Small profile is the key detail — these fish have been selective on bigger offerings elsewhere in the region, so half-ounce epoxy jigs in bay anchovy patterns and small Deadly Dicks worked with a fast, erratic retrieve are the smart call over standard Deceivers or larger metal. If you're rigging for the Gut this weekend, carry both albie gear and bass gear, because you may see both inside an hour on the same rip.

Fluke are winding down but not done. There's still a window on the drift through the deeper cuts near the Sluiceway and along the edges of Plum Gut on bucktail and Gulp combos, particularly on the last two hours of the incoming when bait gets funneled through. Don't expect the numbers from July — this is a taper, and it'll keep tapering through the month as fish start their offshore drop. Sea bass remain the dependable mixed-bag option on structure around Race Rock and the reef pockets off Fishers Island — nothing spectacular, just steady fish in the 14 to 18-inch range for anyone jigging bucktails tipped with squid strips.

Looking ahead: the new moon on the 10th is the pivot everybody's watching. If the bunker stay concentrated through the Gut and the Sluiceway the way they have been, the stronger tidal flow that comes with the new moon should be the trigger for the season's first real blitz activity in this zone — surface-feeding bass on bait, not just deep-water pickers. That's the scenario worth planning around. If the bait scatters instead of tightening further, the pattern holds as-is: deep bucktails on the slack at Valiant Rock, wire and diamond jigs on the blues around the Gull Islands, and albie gear rigged and ready in case the Rhode Island push finally crosses the line into Plum Gut. Either way, the currents are only getting stronger through the weekend, so know your slack windows before you leave the dock — that's not a suggestion in this stretch of water, it's the difference between a hookup and a snapped leader.
