# 180 feet on the mud: haddock steady, cod scratchy on the gravel edge
*Jeffrey's is holding fish in two very different places this week — and the bluefin are starting to show sand eels on the surface.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — July 20** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

One-eighty on the sounder, soft mud, twelve degrees cooler than the surface — that's where the haddock have been sitting all week on the northeast corner of Jeffrey's, inside the Scantum Basin flats where the bottom flattens out and holds that cold layer. I've had charters limit on haddock there three days running, working diamond jigs tipped with a strip of squid, working the jig just off bottom in a slow lift-drop so it stays in that thin zone where the fish are stacked. That's the mud pattern, and right now it's the more reliable of the two.

The gravel bite for cod has been tougher. I ran the ledge proper — the hard bottom stretch running north-south in about 130 to 150 feet, where the gravel breaks up into little cobble humps — and found fish, but scattered. Not the kind of structure-hugging school you can anchor on and grind. We're picking up cod in the 6 to 12 pound range on cut bait, herring chunks fished on a high-low rig, but you're working for every one. My theory is the water's warmed enough on top that the cod have pushed down tighter to structure and aren't roaming the flat gravel like they do in cooler months. I'd rather find three good marks on the ledge and work them hard than blind-drift the whole stretch hoping to stumble into fish.

Platts Bank has been quieter than I'd like for both species this week, but Tillies Bank redeemed itself Thursday — a pollock bite that surprised me, fish in the 15 to 20 pound class hitting jigs worked fast through the water column, 80 to 100 feet down over structure that runs about 200 feet total depth. Pollock like that kind of aggressive vertical presentation, and when they're on, they'll hit a bucktail jig or a diamond jig just as readily as a haddock rig. If the cod stay stingy on the gravel, Tillies is worth the extra run for a mixed bag.

Now the tuna. We flagged the new moon pattern a week back — said it would push bait and concentrate fish on sand eels, mackerel as the backup forage — and that's exactly what's showing up on the ledge now. We're seeing scattered bluefin working the surface off the southern end of Jeffrey's, chopping through balls of sand eels in loose pods, not the tight, boiling schools you get come October, but definitely present and definitely feeding. A few boats have hooked up jigging vertically through the bait balls with metal jigs, and there's been some success trolling small spreader bars along the edges where the sand eel schools are getting pushed up against structure. It's early yet — this is the leading edge of the run, not the main event — but the pattern lines up with what I expected watching the moon phase build bait concentrations through the second week of July.

We're heading into a first-quarter neap around the 21st, which is going to soften the current on the ledge for a few days. That typically spreads groundfish out a little more and can make the haddock bite on the mud even more consistent since they're less pinned by strong tidal flow — but it can also slacken the current enough that the cod on the gravel get lazier about feeding, which might explain part of what we saw this week. I'd expect the haddock pattern to hold steady into early next week regardless, since that basin bite doesn't depend much on tide strength the way the ledge structure bite does.

Mackerel have been thin around the boat this trip, which is a little unusual for late July — usually by now we're marking bait schools thick enough to snag right off the transom for fresh cod bait. We found a few pockets working bait rigs off Thatcher Island on the way out, enough to fill a bait bucket, but nothing like the walls of mackerel you'd expect. That thinness might be tied into why the tuna are working sand eels instead — less mackerel around to hold them, so they're keying on the smaller forage.

For next week, my plan is straightforward. I'll keep working the Scantum mud for haddock since that's been the bankable bite, run a few passes on Tillies for pollock since that fishery showed real promise, and keep an eye on the sand eel schools on the south end of Jeffrey's for early tuna activity. If the neap tide holds the current down like I expect, I'd look for the cod bite on the gravel to stay slow through midweek and then pick back up once the tide builds toward the next moon phase. Anyone heading out should have a haddock plan and a backup — right now the mud is paying better than the gravel, and that's not the way it usually runs on this ledge in July.
