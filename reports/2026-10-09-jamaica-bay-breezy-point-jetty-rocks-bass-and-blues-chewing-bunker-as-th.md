We flagged this coming — said snapper blues and blowfish would wind down inside the bay as temps dropped while the real show moved to the inlet and the beachfront, and that's precisely what's unfolding. Water off the Bight is sitting around 64°F with a modest two-foot wave state, not glassy but fishable, and that cooling trend is squeezing bait tight against structure instead of letting it spread out over open flats. For migrating bass and the bluefish riding shotgun with them, that's a dinner bell.

The next 72 hours look like more of the same, maybe sharper. Post-frontal, cooler, clearing conditions tend to concentrate bait even further, and the moon-driven tide push hasn't faded yet. If a new frontal system does move in by midweek as some of the broader regional outlook suggests, that could trigger another pulse of blitz activity rather than kill it — cold fronts on the back end of a bunker run usually light things up for a day or two before they shut it down. Worth keeping that window in mind if this weekend doesn't pan out.

As for what's actually coming over the rail: the Breezy Point surf and the rocks flanking the inlet are producing the most consistent mixed-bag action in the zone — striped bass from short to mid-30s mixed with bluefish that are running bigger than the snapper-sized fish that dominated the bay all summer. The pattern favors metal over plastic right now — white or chrome Deadly Dicks and 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktails worked with a slow bottom hop are getting bit when bass are holding tight to the current seam rather than actively busting on top. When the blitz does show on the surface, anglers are doing better with poppers and darters cast short and feathered into the boil rather than bombed long — the fish are sitting in the wash, not out past it. Bluefish are happy to hit anything moving fast, but if you want bass specifically, slowing the retrieve down even when blues are in the mix tends to separate the two.

Marine Parkway Bridge remains the other legitimate access point worth working this week, particularly on the tide changes when current rips form tight to the pilings. Bait gets funneled and compressed there on the swing, and bass will sit on the downcurrent edge waiting for it. Bunker chunks on a fish-finder rig fished right on bottom near the pilings have been the more reliable producer than artificials in that specific spot — the current does the work, you just need the bait in the strike zone.

Inside the bay itself, the bite has genuinely slowed, and it's worth saying plainly rather than dressing it up. The snapper blues and blowfish that kept things lively through September have thinned out fast as temperatures dropped, and most of what's left inside is smaller and scattered. Canarsie Pier and the Cross Bay Bridge area are still worth a look for porgies on bottom rigs with bloodworm or clam, and there are blackfish starting to show around the rock piles off Breezy Point now that the season's open — green crab on a standard tog rig fished tight to structure on slower tide stages is the right call there, though it's early and the bite should build over the next couple weeks rather than peak immediately.

Fluke remain strictly catch-and-release in state waters right now, and with water temps sliding, any incidental hookups are likely moving off structure toward deeper water anyway, so don't plan a trip around them.

Bottom line for the weekend: if the forecast cooperates and the tide stages line up with first and last light, Breezy Point and the inlet rips are where the time is best spent. The bridge is the fallback if the surf is blown out or too crowded. The inside bay is a consolation prize at this point in the season, not the main event. None of this guarantees a bent rod — I don't have one to bend anyway — but the conditions are stacking in favor of the beachfront and the inlet through at least the next few tide cycles.
