# Jeffrey's in 180 feet: haddock holding the mud, cod thinning on the gravel edge
*Early September has the ledge in transition — mackerel thick on top, groundfish scattered below, and the first bluefin sign working the eastern flank ahead of the fall push.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — September 7** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

One hundred eighty feet of water on the north end of Jeffrey's Ledge tells you most of what you need to know this week. Drop a sounder over that mud basin and it's still holding haddock — not stacked the way they'll be in October, but enough scattered marks on the bottom third of the water column to make a drift worth setting up. Slide west onto the gravel and cobble in 120 to 150 feet, where the cod usually sit tight to structure, and the marks thin out fast. That split — decent mud, quiet gravel — has been the story on Jeffrey's for about two weeks now, and it's not a mystery why.

Water's still holding late-summer warmth through the upper column, and cod are cold-water fish first. They're pushed down and scattered along the deeper gravel edges rather than piled on the shallower humps where they'll show once the thermocline breaks down. Haddock tolerate the warmer water better and they're feeding on the mud where the current sweeps bait across flat bottom. That's basically been the pattern boats out of Rye have been working with all month — go to the mud for numbers, hit the gravel for quality but expect fewer bites per drift.

The moon is building toward new phase on the 10th, and that's worth planning around. Tide's going to strengthen through the weekend and into early next week, which usually tightens up the haddock bite on Platts Bank and the mud basin at Scantum. Stronger current means bait gets pushed and concentrated instead of drifting loose, and haddock respond to that better than cod do. If the last few new-moon cycles are any guide, look for the haddock bite on Platts and the deeper stretches of Jeffrey's to firm up over the next four or five days as that tide builds, then hold through the following week before it slacks off again.

On the water column right now, mackerel are thick — schools showing on the sounder from the surface down to 40 or 50 feet all across Jeffrey's and out toward Tillies Bank. That's a good sign for two reasons. First, it's bait, and where the mackerel sit the bigger predators aren't usually far behind. Second, it's an easy way to fill the bait tank before running out deep — a few Norwegian jigs or a small diamond jig worked fast through those schools will load you up in short order, and fresh mackerel on the drop is about as good as it gets for haddock and cod both.

For the groundfish itself, the standard high-low rig with clam or squid strips is still the most consistent producer on the mud in 180 to 220 feet. Fish it right on bottom, lift a foot or two on the drift, and let it settle back down — haddock will pick it up softly, so keep contact and don't set the hook too hard. On the gravel, a bucktail or diamond jig tipped with a strip of squid, worked with a slow lift-and-drop right in the rocks, is drawing the occasional quality cod, but it's a numbers game right now, not a bucket-filler. Anyone going out specifically for cod this week should expect a slower pace and plan to cover more bottom rather than anchoring up on one piece.

Pollock have been mixed in on both the mud and gravel, especially up higher in the water column on the drift — a natural byproduct of working jigs through 60 to 100 feet on the way down. They're a welcome bonus fish this time of year and worth keeping the rod in hand for on the initial drop rather than letting the jig free-fall straight to bottom.

On the bluefin side, the sign is starting to build but it's early. A few boats working the eastern flank of Jeffrey's and out toward Tillies have reported occasional singles and small pods showing on top, working through the mackerel schools in the early morning calm before the wind fills in. That's the classic September pattern here — scattered giants feeding on bait balls, not yet the sustained push that shows once the water starts cooling hard into October. Anyone chasing them right now should plan on first light, calm conditions, and patience — troll green machines or small spreader bars through visible bait and bird activity, or anchor up and chunk mackerel if fish are showing but not committing on the troll. It's a long way from the October giant bite that Jeffrey's is known for, but the pieces — bait, water temperature starting to slide, current building with the new moon — are starting to fall into place.

Looking at the next several days, the building tide into the new moon on the 10th should be the thing to watch. Expect the haddock bite on the mud at Jeffrey's, Platts, and Scantum Basin to strengthen as current picks up and concentrates bait. Cod will likely stay scattered on the gravel until the water column cools further, so don't expect a sudden turnaround there this week. And keep an eye on the mackerel schools — as long as they're holding thick over the ledge, there's a reasonable chance the early bluefin activity keeps building through the weekend, especially on calm mornings with good light for spotting fish on top.
