# Weakfish crash Jamaica Bay as 74-degree water stacks bait at the bridge
*Spring tides and warm bay temps trigger the best weakfish bite in years while fluke fishing heats up on the inlet.*

**JAMAICA BAY, NY — June 19** — _by Denise "Dee" Vasquez, Zone Writer_

The bay is absolutely on fire right now. Water temps hit 74 degrees this week — a full seven degrees warmer than the ocean side — and that thermal gradient is doing exactly what it's supposed to do: stacking bait and holding fish. I've been watching this setup build for days, and Wednesday morning it all came together.

The weakfish bite has been nothing short of spectacular. I'm talking limits and releases, fish from 16 inches up to 5-pounders, hitting everything from bucktails to soft plastics. The Marine Parkway Bridge has been the epicenter — fish are staging on the incoming tide, feeding hard as the warm bay water pulls bait through the channel. I've been working 3/4-ounce white bucktails with 4-inch chartreuse Gulp teasers 12 inches up the line, bouncing them along the bottom on the flood. The key is staying mobile — these fish are moving in pods, and when you find them, it's game on.

The Cross Bay Bridge has been producing too, especially on the back side where the current creates that perfect eddy. Early morning and late afternoon have been prime time, but honestly, with water this warm and bait this thick, they're feeding throughout the day. I watched one angler boat eight keepers in two hours Tuesday evening, all on the same white bucktail rig.

Fluke fishing is finally turning the corner. The inlet mouth has been consistent for keepers, especially on the start of the outgoing tide. That's classic Jamaica Bay — the fish stack up right at the choke point as the tide turns, waiting to ambush bait getting flushed out. I've been having success with the traditional fluke setup: 3/4-ounce bucktail on bottom, green teaser 12 inches up. The green has been key — something about this water clarity is making them favor that chartreuse-green combo over white.

Canarsie Pier has been producing mixed bags — short fluke, keeper porgies, and some surprise stripers in the 24-28 inch range. The bass are relating to the bait schools that have been thick in the back bay. Bunker and herring are everywhere right now, and that's what's driving this whole bite.

The weather pattern is setting up perfectly for the weekend. We've got southwest winds in the 10-15 knot range, which is ideal for the bay — it pushes bait toward the structure and keeps the water moving without making it unfishable. The pressure has been steady around 1003 millibars, and that stability is keeping fish in predictable patterns.

One thing to watch: the weed situation at the inlet has been challenging. The southwest winds are pushing grass into the channel, so if you're fishing the inlet mouth, be ready to clean your rigs frequently or move to cleaner water on the bay side.

Looking ahead, we've got a full moon Friday, which means spring tides and maximum current flow. That's going to flush even more bait out of the back bay and should trigger the best fishing of the month. I'm planning to be at the bridge for the evening outgoing — that's when the big weakfish typically show up. The thermal setup should hold through the weekend as long as these southwest winds keep the warm bay water from mixing too much with the cooler ocean.

This is exactly what June fishing in Jamaica Bay should look like. The water's warm, the bait's thick, and the fish are feeding. If you've been waiting for the right time to hit the bay, this is it.
