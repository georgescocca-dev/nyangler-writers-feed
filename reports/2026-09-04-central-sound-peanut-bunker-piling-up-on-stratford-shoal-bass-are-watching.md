# Peanut Bunker Piling Up on Stratford Shoal — Bass Are Watching, Not Yet Eating
*Bait is stacking hard through the Central Sound narrows this week, but the bass are still staged and picky while blues and building bonito rumors take center stage.*

**PORT JEFFERSON, NY — September 4** — _by David "The Harbor Scout" Han, Zone Writer_

Start with the bait, because that's where this week's story actually is. Peanut bunker and silversides have been pushing through the Stratford Shoal funnel in thick, nervous pods for the better part of ten days, getting squeezed by the same geography that's made this stretch productive for a century — the Sound narrows here, the current accelerates, and everything with fins gets pinched into a smaller lane. Terns and gulls have been working tight, jittery circles over the rip on the last two hours of the ebb, which is usually the tell that something underneath is pushing bait to the surface rather than just cruising through it. Off Charles Island and along the Norwalk Islands, the mullet run has thickened noticeably since Labor Day, and mixed in with it are early showings of small bay anchovies that are drawing porgies and the occasional weakfish up onto the flats at first light.

The thermal picture explains a lot of the behavior right now. Kings Point on the western end is reading close to 71 degrees, New London out east closer to 69 — a real gradient across the Central Sound stretch, and it's keeping bait comfortable enough to hold in loose, spread-out schools rather than getting hammered into tight bait balls. That matters because striped bass are clearly staged on this bunker and mullet right now, not committed. Boats working the Norwalk Islands and the flats off Oyster Bay have been marking good numbers of fish sitting under and around the bait on sonar, but the blitz behavior — the kind where the whole surface goes white — hasn't materialized yet. We flagged this pattern last week: the trigger is bait density, and until these bunker and mullet schools get squeezed tighter by stronger tidal flow, the bass are going to keep shadowing rather than committing. The moon is building toward full on the 6th and 7th, which should start doing exactly that job over the next several days.

In the meantime, the more reliable meat-and-potatoes bite has been bluefish, and it's been a genuinely good week for them. With the Surfside tournament crowd working the Sound hard, reports out of the Norwalk Islands and off Middle Ground Light have blues running 3 to 6 pounds, some bigger, chopping through peanut bunker on top during the last of the incoming and first hour of the outgoing. Poppers and metal — 1-ounce Deadly Dicks and white bucktails with a curly tail trailer — have been the move when they're up and feeding visibly; when they sink back down, a bunker chunk fished on a fish-finder rig in 15 to 25 feet off the Norwalk Islands or along the edge of the Stratford Shoal rip has kept rods bent. This bite should hold strong through the rest of Labor Day week — the warm water isn't going anywhere fast, and the bunker supply isn't either.

Fluke are doing what fluke do in early September, which is thinning out of the shallow structure and sliding toward deeper water ahead of the fall migration. The back bay and inlet numbers around Oyster Bay and Port Jefferson Harbor have dropped off noticeably compared to August, but there are still keeper-class fish coming off the deeper humps around Stratford Shoal and the drop-offs near Middle Ground Light, mostly on white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the last of the ebb in 30 to 40 feet. Don't expect the volume of a month ago, but the quality fish that are left are worth a drift or two if fluke is the target.

Blackfish season isn't quite in full swing yet for this stretch, but a few scouting reports off the rockier structure near Charles Island and the western Norwalk Islands have turned up decent-sized tog on green crab, a preview of what's coming once water temps drop another few degrees.

The wildcard worth watching closely is bonito. The push that's been building around Block Island and Point Judith has reportedly reached Fishers Island Sound and the Race, and if that westward creep continues at the pace it's been going, Central Sound waters off Port Jefferson and the eastern approaches to Stratford Shoal could see the first real bonito showings within the next week to ten days. Nothing confirmed in our zone yet, but the timing lines up with past Augusts and early Septembers when bonito have shown up on exactly this kind of bait and thermal setup.

Looking ahead, the next 72 hours should bring strengthening tidal flow as the moon builds toward full, and that's the mechanism that tightens scattered bunker and mullet into the kind of concentrated schools that actually trigger blitz behavior. If nighttime temps start trending down even modestly, expect the first real striper commitment to show up on that tightening bait sometime in the second half of next week — the Norwalk Islands and the Stratford Shoal rip are the two spots to watch first. Until then, the smart play is working the bluefish, which are dependable right now, keeping an eye on the fluke drops for a last quality fish, and paying attention to any bird activity east toward Port Jefferson that might be the first sign of bonito arriving early.
