# 75.7°F at the Buoy, a Harder Break Sits at the 100-Fathom Curve
*Yellowfin and bluefin are splitting a tightening thermal edge on the Hudson's northern wall, and a softening tide is starting to hand the edge back to the trollers.*

**MANASQUAN, NJ — August 3** — _by Capt. Tony Rosetti, Zone Writer_

Seventy-five point seven degrees at the surface off the Bight, three-foot swell running at seven seconds — that's a transit report, not a fishing report, and it told me what I'd find seventy-some miles out before I ever cleared the inlet. Surface water that warm sitting on top of a three-second gap between wave period readings means there's stratification happening below it, a layer of cooler water still sitting on the bottom of the mixed zone. That's exactly the setup you want going into the canyon in early August — warm cap, cooler subsurface, and a hard edge where the two meet along the shelf break.

I found that edge running along the 100-fathom curve near 39°48' N, 72°35' W, right off the northern wall where the canyon starts to open up before it drops toward the Dip. Color change was sharp — green, chlorophyll-heavy water pushing out from the coast on the inside, clean blue 74–76°F water sitting on the canyon side. That's the kind of break that holds bait, and it held bait again this week. Squid and small mahi-sized weed clumps were scattered right on the seam, with a few respectable windrows of sargassum drifting south of the Dip that had mahi stacked underneath — mostly schoolie-sized, eight to fifteen pounds, with a couple better fish pushing twenty coming off small yellow-and-green feather rigs pulled tight to the grass.

The yellowfin bite has been a two-part story this week, and I want to be straight about it because it wasn't a wire-to-wire green light. Trolling in the morning on the color change produced steady but not stacked action — green machines and small Ilanders in the 40–50 range, ballyhoo naked and skirted mixed through a nine-line spread, picking up yellowfin in the 25–45 pound class working the temp seam between 39°45' and 39°50' N. By midday, with the sun high and the surface glassy under that stable high pressure, the troll bite went quiet for stretches. The better numbers came after dark, chunking butterfish at anchor over 100 to 130 fathoms near the Dip and along the eastern edge of the Claw — that's where the bulk of the bigger yellowfin, some pushing 50-plus, came aboard on 30 to 50-pound fluoro leader, small circle hooks, and a slow, thinned-out chunk line to keep them working the boat instead of the current.

We flagged this a week ago — said the softening tide into the coming neap would start shifting the advantage back toward trolling, and that's exactly what's playing out. With the range narrowing into the August 5–6 neap, current on the canyon edge has eased noticeably compared to two weeks back, and the troll bite in the early morning window has picked up relative to the chunk bite, which had been carrying the week before. If that trend holds, I'd expect the balance to keep tipping toward trolling through the weekend, especially on that 100-fathom seam where the bait's been sitting tight.

Bluefin have been mixed into both patterns, mostly smaller class fish in the 40–70 pound range, taking the same trolled spread as the yellowfin rather than showing as a separate push. Nothing large-class showing consistently inside the canyon proper this week — the bigger bluefin action has stayed north, out toward the Gulf of Maine grounds, and what we're seeing here reads more like scouts working the same bait balls as the yellowfin rather than a dedicated bluefin push. Worth setting one flat line a little heavier — 50-pound mono instead of straight fluoro — if you want to hang onto one of the better ones without getting worked over on standard yellowfin gear.

White marlin reports have started to spread out from what was a tight cluster two weeks ago, and this week that pattern continued — scattered raises along the temp break south of the Dip, mostly on naked ballyhoo behind Sea Witches in the top slots of the spread, with a couple of confirmed releases in the 39°40' N corridor. It's not a stacked bite yet, more a scattered one, but the direction is right — more boats reporting fish each week rather than fewer, which is what you want to see heading into the heart of August.

Wahoo have been a bycatch story so far — a couple taken on high-speed gear run early, before the troll spread went out, working the drop-off edge in 90 to 110 fathoms where the bottom breaks hard. Nothing to build a dedicated trip around yet, but worth a couple of lines in the water if you're running out before first light.

Looking at the week ahead: the high-pressure pattern shows no sign of breaking, which keeps the surface calm and the canyon fishable, but it also means that afternoon glass-off that's been killing the midday troll bite is likely to repeat. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd run out early, work the 100-fathom seam near 39°48' N on the troll through mid-morning, then either push into the Dip to anchor and chunk after the bite slows, or stay mobile and hunt weed lines south for mahi if the troll goes cold. The neap tide should keep softening current through midweek, which favors the troll pattern holding its recent gains — but I'd still have the chunk gear rigged and ready once the sun gets high.
