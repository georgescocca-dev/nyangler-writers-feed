# Bunker Still Thick on the Western Sound, and the Blues Won't Leave Them Alone
*Execution Rocks to Matinecock Point, bluefish are working bait schools hard while stripers sit back and wait for the bunker to stack tighter.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — August 31** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Every third guy who came through the shop this week asked some version of the same question: where'd the bunker go, and why are the blues still all over them? Simple answer — the bunker never left. They're just moving around more, pushed by the tide and by every bluefish in the western Sound trying to eat one. That's been the story since the Surfside Hotel Greatest Bluefish Tournament kicked off, and 44 years running, that tournament tends to find where the bait is before anybody else does.

Water's still carrying summer heat. Kings Point was reading 70.9 the other day, and that number drops as you run east — New London down around 69.4 — which tells you the western end of the Sound is holding the warmth and holding the bait longer. That gradient matters. It's why Execution Rocks, Stepping Stones, and the water off Matinecock Point have stayed loaded with bunker pods all month while the eastern Sound starts to cool off and thin out. Wherever that bait sits, that's where the action is, and right now that's still squarely in our zone.

Stripers are a different conversation. They're around — nobody's saying they're not — but they're in what I'd call a holding pattern. A few fish come off the bunker schools early and late, usually chunk bait fished on the bottom in 20 to 35 feet off Execution Rocks on the last two hours of the outgoing, but it's not the kind of concentrated blitz activity you get once that bait truly balls up tight. We flagged this last week — said to watch the bunker school movement for the first read on where the fall striper bite would concentrate, and that's exactly the pattern setting up now. The bass are shadowing those bunker schools without committing hard yet. Give it another week or two of building moon and stronger tidal flow, and that changes.

Bluefish, meanwhile, are doing exactly what warm water and thick bait tell them to do — hammering bunker wherever the schools show on the surface. The tournament fleet has been finding fish blitzing from Throgs Neck Bridge out through Hart Island and up toward Matinecock, with the heaviest surface activity showing on calm mornings when the bunker pods get pushed shallow by tide and current. Bunker spoons trolled behind the boat, big poppers worked over visible slicks, and straight chunk bait fished on wire leader in 15 to 25 feet are all producing. These aren't dink blues either — plenty of 6 to 10 pound fish coming out of these schools, which is normal for late August when the bigger blues finally push through behind the smaller resident fish. If you're not fishing the tournament, the same spots are worth your time — Stepping Stones light on the last of the flood has been holding bait and blues both, and it's a short run from most of the marinas between City Island and Port Washington.

Fluke have softened into their typical late-August pattern, and there's no sugarcoating that. It's fair fishing, not great fishing. The bay water's warm enough that fluke are drifting toward the deeper channel edges and structure looking for cooler water and better oxygen — the channel off Hart Island and the deeper cuts inside Manhasset Bay have both given up a mix of keepers and shorts on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with squid strips, fished slow on 3/4-ounce bucktails right on bottom. Hempstead Harbor's deeper holes are worth a drift too if you're set up for it. This bite typically holds through September before the fish start their move offshore, so there's still time, but don't expect the fast counts you had in July.

Porgies and blackfish are quiet background noise right now — a few porgies coming off the rockpiles around Execution Rocks on bloodworms for guys soaking bait between bluefish drifts, nothing worth a special trip yet. Blackfish season's still a ways off in terms of real activity; that'll pick up once the water starts to turn in earnest.

Looking at the next few days, the moon's building toward full, and that means more tidal push through the rips at Stepping Stones and Execution Rocks — exactly the kind of current that starts concentrating bait instead of letting it spread thin. If the bunker schools that are scattered from Throgs Neck to Matinecock start balling up tighter under that increased flow, that's your signal the fall striper push is getting close. It hasn't happened yet, but the ingredients are lining up. For the next 48 to 72 hours, I'd keep working the bluefish pattern — it's the most reliable game in the western Sound right now, and it should stay that way through the holiday weekend as boat traffic and tournament pressure both pick up. If you've only got one trip in you before Labor Day, make it a bluefish trip on the bunker schools, and keep a bass rod rigged with a bunker chunk on bottom just in case one of those shadowing stripers decides to commit. That's usually how the first real fall bass shows up around here — not in a blitz, but as a bonus fish while you're doing something else.
