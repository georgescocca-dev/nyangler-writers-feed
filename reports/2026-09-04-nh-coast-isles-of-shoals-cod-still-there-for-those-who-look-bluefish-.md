# Isles of Shoals cod still there for those who look, bluefish own the coast
*Thirteen miles doesn't sound like much until you fish it like a hundred and thirty — groundfish on the ledges, blues everywhere else.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — September 4** — _by Cole Robinson, Zone Writer_

People hear thirteen miles of coastline and figure there's nothing to talk about. That's fine by me — means less pressure on water that fishes like a lot more than it looks on a map. Between the Piscataqua's current lines, the Isles of Shoals humps sitting eight miles offshore, and every rockpile from Odiorne to Great Boars Head, there's enough structure out here to keep a small boat busy through Columbus Day if you're willing to run early and read the tide right. This week proved that out again. Nothing spectacular, nothing to write home about on the striper side, but steady enough groundfish work on the Shoals and a bluefish bite that hasn't quit since August.

Water's still sitting warm for early September, upper 60s to low 70s depending where you check, and that's kept the striper schools loose rather than balled up and blitzing. They're around — bait's around too, pogies pushing into Hampton Harbor and mackerel schools working the mouth of the Piscataqua — but with the water this warm the bass aren't committing hard. We flagged this pattern last week and said not to expect a shift yet, and that's exactly what played out. The moon builds to full over the next couple days, which should tighten the tidal push, and the first real cool nights of the month are the piece still missing. Once nighttime temps start dropping into the 50s, that's when these scattered schools start acting like fall stripers instead of August stripers.
bluefish never got that memo. They've been the most dependable thing on this stretch of coast all week — steady action off Rye Harbor, around the Boars Head rip, and out past White Island at the Shoals, chopping through pogy schools with the same aggression they've shown since the water turned warm. That lines up with what we called last week too — warm water, wide open blues, and no sign of it backing off before Labor Day weekend wraps.

On the groundfish side, the Isles of Shoals are still doing what they do this time of year for the guys willing to make the run. Cod to eight pounds are coming off the ledges around Appledore and the backside of Star Island, mostly on the last two hours of the ebb when the current settles enough to hold bottom with a jig. Diamond jigs and bucktails tipped with squid strips are getting it done in 60 to 90 feet, right on the edges where the rock drops into mud. Haddock have been mixed in on the same drifts, nothing to fill a cooler fast but enough to make the trip worthwhile if you're patient and keep moving the boat rather than sitting on one mark. Mackerel are thick enough around the Shoals and off Odiorne Point that a rig with three or four small jigs can load up in minutes — good for the table, better for bait if you're planning to soak one for a bass or a bluefish afterward.

Stripers are still showing in the Piscataqua, particularly around the bridge pilings and the deeper bends where current stacks bait against structure. Fishing there has been a tide game — the last two hours of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing are producing the most consistent action, with swimming plugs and soft plastic paddletails worked slow along the seams. Nothing overslot to speak of this week, mostly schoolies and a few keepers mixed in, but enough activity to keep rods bent if you're working the right stage of the tide. Hampton Harbor has been a similar story — pogies pushing in on the flood are drawing bass up into the channel, especially around the inlet mouth and along the marsh edges where current cuts hard against the bank. Live-lined pogies or a big paddletail worked through that seam has been the better bet over blind-casting the flats.

Bluefish continue to be the fish you can count on right now, and that's not changing in the next few days. Look for them anywhere bait is stacking — off Rye Harbor, around the Boars Head rips, and scattered through the Shoals grounds — on cut mackerel, pogy chunks, or metal jigs worked fast through the top of the water column. They're not picky and they're not leaving until the water cools enough to push them south, which by the current trend probably means they stick around through most of September.

Looking at the next several days, the setup favors patience over hero trips. The moon pushing toward full will strengthen the tidal flow, which should help tighten up those loose bunker and pogy schools in the harbor and river systems — that's usually the trigger that gets bass to commit rather than just shadow the bait. If a cool front comes through behind that full moon and knocks nighttime temps down, that's the combination worth watching closely; that's when the first real blitz activity typically breaks open on this coast. Until then, the safer bet is running out to the Shoals early on a calm morning for cod and haddock while the tide's still working the ebb, then working back in toward Rye Harbor or the Piscataqua on the way home for an evening bluefish or striper pass. Keep an eye on wind — anything out of the southeast over 15 knots turns the water off the Shoals into a bad idea fast, and that stretch of open water doesn't forgive a small boat caught out there when it turns.
