# New Haven Breakwalls Stack Porgies, Sea Bass Sneaking In Behind Them
*The scup bite off the east wall is as thick as it's been all summer, and the first real bunker pushes are setting up the fall blitz everyone's been waiting on.*

**NEW HAVEN, CT — September 7** — _by Lena Marchetti, Zone Writer_

The east wall at New Haven Harbor gave up porgies by the dozen this week, rods bent two and three deep along the rocks as scup running eight to eleven inches piled into clam bellies and squid strips fished on high-low rigs in 12 to 18 feet. Mixed into those catches were a few keeper sea bass, 14 to 16 inches, sliding onto the same rockpile structure the porgies have claimed since June. That's the pattern right now up and down this stretch — Charles Island's boulder field, the rockpiles off Falkner Island, the riprap at Milford Gulf — anywhere there's structure and current, scup and sea bass are stacked and cooperative on the last two hours of the incoming tide into the top of the high.

Water temps are holding in that sweet 69 to 71 degree range across the western and central Sound, with Kings Point reading 70.9 and New London running a touch cooler at 69.4. That gradient matters — Central Connecticut is sitting right in the productive middle of it, warm enough to keep the bottom bite honest but cool enough that bunker schools are finally tightening instead of scattering. We flagged this coming — said the building moon toward full on September 6-7 would tighten those bait schools, and that's exactly what's showing up on the sonar screens this week. Peanut bunker are balling tighter off Charles Island and along the Hammonasset flats, and the bass and blues both know it.

Striped bass fishing has been a mixed bag but trending the right direction. The Charles Island sandbar remains the go-to on the dropping tide — bass are holding under the bait, not up top blitzing yet, so the better producers have been bouncing bucktails tipped with a strip of squid or dragging a 5-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet right on the sand in 8 to 15 feet as the tide pulls off the bar. A handful of fish in the 26 to 32 inch range have come from that stretch on the last two hours of ebb, with the occasional overslot mixed in for anglers working live eels along the drop-off on the north side. The bunker schools stacking up off the breakwalls and around Stratford Shoal are the tell — once the bass commit to busting on top instead of feeding underneath, that's when this turns into the kind of blitz fishing the fall run is known for. With the new moon tide arriving September 10, the setup is about as good as it gets — bigger tides, tighter bait, cooling nights. If there's one window worth circling this month, it's the few days on either side of that new moon.

Bluefish have been the more dependable player while the stripers sort themselves out. Blues are actively working the same bunker schools, and anyone throwing metal — Deadly Dicks, 2-ounce Kastmasters — into visible surface activity off Milford Gulf or along the Hammonasset shoreline has had steady action, including some chopper-class fish in the 5 to 8 pound range. The blues aren't picky right now, which is more than can be said for the false albacore and bonito showing intermittently around the New Haven breakwaters. Those fish are around — anglers have spotted them cutting through bait pods near the outer wall — but they've been keying on micro bait, tiny bay anchovies and silversides, and turning their nose up at anything bigger than a size 2 epoxy jig or a small Albie Snax. Downsizing leader to 20-pound fluoro and working small, erratic jigs has been the only consistent way to connect. It's frustrating fishing, but when it clicks it's worth the aggravation.

Fluke are winding down from the summer peak but Stratford Shoal is still giving up keepers for boats working bucktail and Gulp combinations along the edges where the current sweeps hardest, typically the last of the outgoing into dead low. Don't expect the numbers of July, but a few solid fish in the 18 to 22 inch class are still coming off that structure, along with the occasional short that needs to go back. This bite has maybe two or three more weeks in it before those fish start sliding out toward deeper water.

Blackfish are starting to show more interest as the water eases off its late-summer high, with a few anglers picking at smaller tog around the base of the Charles Island rockpile and the Falkner Island structure using green crab on conventional tackle. It's early yet and the real blackfish push is still weeks away, but worth keeping an eye on as October approaches.

Looking ahead, the next few days should hold the current pattern — porgies and sea bass steady on the breakwalls and rockpiles regardless of what the tide chart says, bass and blues improving as bunker schools keep tightening under the new moon build. If the wind stays out of the southwest and doesn't kick up too much chop, Charles Island on the dropping tide and the New Haven breakwalls on the flood are the two spots worth prioritizing through the weekend. Once that new moon tide hits September 10, expect the first real topwater blitz activity of the fall to show itself — that's the window to have bucktails and poppers rigged and ready.
