# Bass settle into the bay as Captree clammers and casters find keepers
*Striped bass bite has steadied through May out of Captree, with clam bellies and soft plastics both producing slots and overs after dark.*

**CAPTREE, NY — May 30** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The Great South Bay has finally found its rhythm. After a cold, wind-bitten April that had the Coast Guard plucking boaters out of the soup off Moriches, the bass have set up shop inside Fire Island Inlet and the night bite out of Captree is the most consistent story on the South Shore right now.

The template was set back on May 2, when **Snapprhead27** anchored up with clam bellies and put three overs and a slot in the boat — a welcome sign that the post-spawn fish were already staging in the bay. Two weeks later he was back at it, this time on a night trip that started slow on the clam.

> "Started clamming and nothing but doggies. We switched over to casting bass assassins and the bass started to show. Boat had 3 or 4 keepers and a bunch of over/under."

That 33-incher he landed on the 16th is exactly the class of fish working the inlet edges right now, and it's a useful lesson: when the dogfish take over the bait, pick up a rod and throw plastic. The fish are there — you just have to show them something that moves.

On the bottom-fishing side, the open-boat fleet has been grinding out a respectable start. **Bachala** fished the opening-day full-day trip on the King Cod back on May 5 and reported "fish were caught on every drift with a good bunch being keepers," with his own pool-winning fluke making the day. That was the South Shore's first real signal that the fluke had pushed in around the Robert Moses bridges and the inlet. With water now climbing — the May 27 SST package shows the nearshore bay warming nicely into the low 60s — those fluke should be stacking deeper on the bridge edges and along the inlet channel.

What we're **not** seeing yet in the source chatter: bluefish blitzes, weakfish reports, or any porgy intel from the Captree fleet. That's normal for the calendar. The bunker push will dictate when the choppers show, and I'd be watching the back side of Democrat Point and the West Channel as the water touches 64–65°F.

**Bottom line:** The Fire Island Inlet bass bite is on, with clam bellies on the anchor and soft plastics like Bass Assassins both working — best after dark on the moving tide out of Captree. Fluke are honest at the Moses bridges for the party-boat crowd, though it's still early-season grind, not a slam.

Watch the next three to five days for the first real bunker push into the bay; once that bait stages off Democrat Point, the daytime bass bite will catch up to the night bite and we'll start hearing about topwater eats on the Fire Island side at first light.

---

**Bottom line.** The night bite out of Captree is the most reliable game on the South Shore, with clam and plastics both producing keeper bass. Fluke are showing on the open boats around the Moses bridges, but it's still a grind-it-out pick rather than a runaway bite.

**Forward look.** Watch for the first big bunker push into Fire Island Inlet over the next 3–5 days as water climbs into the mid-60s. That should turn on a daytime topwater bite at Democrat Point and bring the inlet bass shallow on the outgoing.
