# Point bite holds on diamond jigs as offshore buoy network goes dark
*With 44025 offline and 44097 thermal data intermittent, the read on Block Island Sound rests on structure, tide, and bait behavior rather than fresh SST.*

**MONTAUK, NY — June 12, 2026** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The primary offshore reference for this stretch of coast is dark this morning. NDBC 44025 — the Long Island buoy that anchors any honest read on what's pushing into the Sound — has its sensor array offline, and 44097 down toward Block Island is feeding intermittent water temperature data. The most recent SST package in the regional catalog is dated June 10, so the satellite read on the rip is roughly 48 hours stale. That's the honest disclosure. What we do have is the seasonal frame: mid-June at the Point typically puts surface water in the upper 50s to very low 60s, with the rip line acting as a convergence between cooler Block Island Sound water sliding south on the ebb and warmer south-side water riding the flood up around the Lighthouse. Without a fresh buoy number I'm not going to pin a decimal on it, but the bait and bass behavior described over the past week is consistent with that classic early-summer thermal seam still being intact rather than blown out.

What that means for the beat is straightforward. The rip is fishing the way the rip is supposed to fish in the second week of June — bass stacked on the structure, willing to eat metal in the column, and sized up rather than schoolie-grade. When the thermal break is set up properly along the Lighthouse rip and out across North Bar, the larger class of fish holds on the down-current side of the boulders, using the structure to ambush sand eels and small herring getting tumbled through. That's a diamond jig bite, not a topwater bite, and the recent intelligence coming off the charter fleet confirms exactly that — multiple overslot fish to the mid-20-pound class, with the bulk of the catch sitting clearly above the slot rather than the 28-to-31 window we saw dominating in May.

North Bar on the ebb has been the higher-percentage piece for the bigger fish. The current ripping across the bar against a southerly breeze stands the water up and concentrates bait against the structure, and the fish are sitting in the 35 to 55 foot range depending on tide stage. Run the jig down, crank it ten turns, drop it back — the hits have been coming on the fall as often as on the lift, which tells you the fish are looking up and the bait is suspended rather than pinned to the bottom. Shagwong on the flood has been the second piece, particularly for boats wanting a softer ride, and Turtle Cove has held fish on the change for the surf and light-tackle crowd working bucktails along the boulder field.

Fluke on the south-side drops are doing what they should be doing for the calendar — a high ratio of shorts with a workable keeper bite on the better drifts, and the bigger fish coming on bucktail-and-Gulp combinations rather than dead-sticked bait. Sea bass are mixed in heavily on the same structure, which is the tell that the bottom temperature is right in the pocket where both species overlap before the fluke push further inshore into the skinny water as June rolls on. Porgies have set up on the harbor structure and the rockier pieces in Fort Pond Bay. Blackfish season is closed and weakfish remain a ghost on this end of the Island, as they have been for years.

Looking ahead three to five days, the controlling variable is wind. A sustained southwest flow will keep the south-side drift comfortable and push warmer surface water against the Point, which tightens the thermal seam and concentrates bass on the rip. A backing northeast — even moderate — will dirty up North Bar, push the rip south, and force the better bite to Shagwong and inside. The moon is working away from full toward last quarter late in the week, which softens the tide ranges and generally favors the dawn and dusk windows over the midday slack. I'd be watching for the first real sand eel push to firm up; once that bait locks in along the rip, the jig bite goes from good to the kind of week people drive five hours for.

The Point is fishing like the Point in June. Big fish are on structure, the metal is working, and the rip is doing its job. Until 44025 comes back online we're reading the water by what it's producing rather than by what the sensors say, which is how this place was fished for a hundred years before any of us had a phone.
