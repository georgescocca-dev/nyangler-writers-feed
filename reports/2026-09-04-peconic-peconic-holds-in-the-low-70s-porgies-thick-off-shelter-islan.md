# Peconic holds in the low 70s — porgies thick off Shelter Island, bass staging for the Gut
*Bunker pods are building through the bay and the bass are riding along, not blitzing yet, while porgies and fluke keep the rods bent through the work week.*

**GREENPORT, NY — September 4** — _by Bill Hubbard, Zone Writer_

The Peconic runs on its own clock this time of year, and right now that clock says late summer, not fall. Surface temps are still sitting in the low 70s across Gardiners Bay and up into Noyack, warmer than the open Sound thanks to the shallow, protected geography that's made this water a nursery and a feeding ground for two centuries. That warmth is good news for porgies and fluke, which are still locked into their summer patterns, and it's the reason the striped bass moving through right now are riding on bait rather than blowing it up on the surface. Water this mild keeps predator and prey in a loose, unhurried relationship. That changes fast once the nights start cooling off, and with the moon building toward full on the 6th and 7th, the tidal flow is about to get stronger through every cut and gut in the system. That's usually the first ingredient in tightening up scattered bunker schools into something a striper can commit to.

We flagged this a week ago — said porgy and fluke action would hold steady through the stretch leading into scallop season, with bonito potential building at the inlets — and that's exactly the shape of things on the water now. Porgy fishing off Shelter Island remains the most dependable bite in the zone. Boats working Cornelius Point and the drop just outside Coecles Harbor are anchoring up in 20 to 30 feet on the last two hours of the flood, dropping high-low rigs baited with sandworm or clam. Fish are running a mix of scup-sized keepers with enough jumbos mixed in to make it worth soaking a second rod with bigger bait. This bite doesn't need much finesse — bring enough bloodworms or clam and let the incoming tide do the work.

Fluke are still stacked in the channels, though the pattern is starting to show the first cracks of the seasonal shift. Robins Island's east and west channels and the deeper cuts off Jessup's Neck are holding fish in 15 to 25 feet, with the better drifts coming on the back half of the outgoing tide when the current pulls bait off the flats and funnels it through the deeper water. White and chartreuse five-inch Gulp Swimming Mullets on half-ounce bucktails are outproducing bare bait rigs right now, especially worked slow with a hop-and-drag retrieve rather than a straight drag. Sizes are respectable — plenty of 17 to 20-inch fish, with a scattered doormat in the 24 to 26-inch class turning up on the deeper edges near the mouth of Noyack Bay. The bite isn't as thick as it was a month ago, and that's expected; the same warm water holding fluke in the back bays elsewhere on Long Island is starting to nudge Peconic fish toward the deeper structure out near Orient and the Gut ahead of their fall exit.

The striped bass story right now is about positioning, not aggression. Bunker pods are showing along the north side of Shelter Island, off Cedar Point, and staging in the deeper water toward Orient Point, and the bass are shadowing them without committing to the kind of surface blitz you'll see once the water cools another few degrees. The better approach right now is a bucktail worked deep along the edge of a bait school rather than a topwater plug thrown into the middle of it — a 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktail dropped to the bottom edge of a bunker pod and swum back with a slow, rhythmic retrieve has been drawing strikes from fish in the mid-20 to low-30-inch range, with the occasional overslot mixed in for boats willing to work the deeper edges near Plum Gut on the last of the ebb. Live-lining a bunker off a fish-finder rig in that same water is worth the patience for anyone hoping for something bigger.

Bluefish have been the most reliable action for anyone who just wants bent rods without overthinking it. Peanut bunker pushing into Three Mile Harbor and Sag Harbor Cove have brought choppers up top on calm mornings, and metal — Kastmasters, Hopkins, anything that throws a long cast and flutters — is getting hit as soon as it lands. This bite should hold strong right through Labor Day week as long as the bait stays put, and there's no sign yet that it's thinning.

Weakfish are still showing as a bonus on the evening fluke drifts in Noyack and Three Mile Harbor, mostly smaller fish taken on sandworm rigs fished slow along channel edges after sundown, not a dedicated target yet but worth a look if the tide and light line up.

Looking ahead, the building moon into the weekend should start compacting those bunker schools tighter, which is the trigger everyone's watching for. If the pattern holds the way it usually does here, expect the first real surface activity from stripers working those tighter bait balls near Orient Point and through Plum Gut within the next week to ten days, especially if a real cool front finally pushes through and knocks a few degrees off the surface temps. Until then, porgies at Shelter Island and fluke in the channels remain the two bites you can plan a trip around with confidence, and the scallop season opener sitting on the horizon is starting to shift some boat traffic and attention toward the flats — worth keeping an ear open at the docks in Greenport and Sag Harbor for word on timing.
