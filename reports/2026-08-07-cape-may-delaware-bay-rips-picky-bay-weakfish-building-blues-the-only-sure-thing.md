# Rips Picky, Bay Weakfish Building, Blues the Only Sure Thing
*The Cape May Rips are grinding out short windows while Delaware Bay's weakfish pattern keeps filling in on schedule.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 7** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Rips are a C-plus this week. Not a disaster, not a banner week either — just honest, tide-dependent fishing that rewards guys who show up on the right stage of the water and go home skunked if they don't. My father used to say the Rips don't owe you anything, and August is when that lesson gets taught the hardest. Ocean side surface temps are pushing into the mid-70s, the current lines off Cape May Point are still stacking bait, but the window where fish actually commit to feeding has gotten short and specific — first two hours of the ebb, full stop. Miss that and you're just trolling pretty water.

Here's the breakdown. Bluefish are the one thing I can put in the log with confidence. Bunker schools are thick from the Point out toward the Bunker Grounds, and the blues are riding them hard — 3 to 6 pounds, some better, busting bait on top in that classic August pattern where you see the slick before you see the fish. Bucktails in white or chartreuse, ounce to ounce-and-a-half, worked through the boils, or if you want to keep it simple, a bunker chunk on a fish-finder rig soaking on the bottom in 25 to 40 feet right off the rip edge. That's producing all week, tide stages aside — blues don't care much about the clock like stripers do.

Stripers, though, have thinned out of the immediate rip structure the way they always do once the water gets into the mid-70s. What's left is holding deep on the reef structure and rubble near the mouth of the bay, and you need to fish it like a bottom fish now, not a run-and-gun surface bite. Jigging bucktails tipped with a strip of squid down in 35 to 50 feet on the last of the incoming has been the play for the boats still finding them, but I'll be straight — it's a grind. One or two fish a trip if you're patient, mostly schoolies with the occasional keeper mixed in. Not the kind of numbers that make for a good sea story yet.

Delaware Bay is actually the better story right now, and it's the one I flagged coming. We said the weakfish would track the peanut bunker into the bays through August and build into something real rather than a flash — and that's exactly what's showing up on the channel edges from Fortescue down toward Brandywine Shoal. Bay water is running warm, upper 70s in the shallows, which has pushed the weakfish and a good push of croaker onto structure where there's current relief — the edges of the shipping channel, the drop-offs near the Brandywine light, and the deeper holes off Fortescue. Evening tide is your window here, when the bay cools a couple degrees off its afternoon peak and the weakfish come up off bottom to feed. Bloodworms and Fishbites on a simple two-hook bottom rig, or a 3-inch chartreuse Gulp Shrimp on a light jighead if you want to work it more actively — both are taking fish in the 16 to 20 inch range, with croaker mixed in steady enough that nobody's complaining about the bycatch. This isn't the May drum run — that's long finished, and if you're still asking about black drum this time of year, you're a couple months late — but the weakfish pattern filling in behind it is legitimate and it's holding, which is more than I can say for some Augusts.

Fluke are still the most honest bite going if you want dinner. The party boats out of Cape May have shifted effort toward the Wilmington-adjacent grounds and the deeper reef structure offshore, mixing sea bass with a solid fluke count on white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with a light bucktail, drifted along the bottom in 60 to 90 feet. That's a fish-a-drift kind of trip most days right now, better than what's happening close to the beach where the shallow flats have gotten too warm to hold quality fluke consistently.

Wind's been cooperative — light out of the southwest most days, seas manageable inside and out — which is the only reason the rip bite has stayed fishable at all through these short windows. If that holds into next week I'd keep working the bluefish on the bunker schools as your bread and butter, work the bay weakfish on the evening tide since that pattern shows no sign of backing off, and treat the rip stripers as a bonus rather than a target — deep structure, patient jigging, low expectations, occasional reward. If we get any wind shift out of the east that stacks up a groundswell, I'd pull off the ocean side entirely and just fish the bay — Fortescue will still be fishing regardless of what the Atlantic's doing. That's the two-bay game this time of year: one side grinds, the other side pays off, and knowing which is which on a given morning is half the job.
