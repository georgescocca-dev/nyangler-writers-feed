# Cape May Rips: C-minus by day, B-plus at first light — bay weakfish picking up the slack
*Stripers are ghosting the rips in August heat, but bluefish, fluke, and a real weakfish bite on the bay side are keeping rods bent.*

**CAPE MAY, NJ — August 10** — _by Capt. Kate Brennan, Zone Writer_

Rips get a C this week. Not a failing grade, but nothing to brag about at the dock either. Daytime striper action out at the Rips has been thin to nonexistent — water's warm, sun's high, and those fish know better than to sit in fast current in eighty-degree light. If you want a bass out there right now you're fishing the first hour of gray light or the last hour before dark, working the edge of the Rips where the outgoing current dumps off Cape May Point into deeper water. Bucktails in the 1 to 1.5-ounce range, white or chartreuse, dropped down and bounced slow on the bottom through the seam — that's still putting fish in the box, just not many, and not big. Call it two or three keepers a trip if you're grinding it out, more shorts than you'd like.

What's actually working the Rips right now is bluefish and fluke, and that's where I've been putting my charters. Fluke on the drift over the humps and the deeper stretch between the Rips and the Sub Base grounds, 30 to 45 feet, has been steady — not a doormat bite, but a keeper-a-drift kind of morning on 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow with the tide. Best window is the last two hours of the outgoing into the start of the flood, when the current's still got some push but isn't ripping your line sideways. Bluefish have been the more consistent player — cocktail blues in the 2 to 4-pound class chopping bait right on the surface at the top of the tide near Hereford Inlet and off the Wildwood jetties. We flagged this a week back, said the peanut bunker moving through the back bays would pull the cocktail blues in tight, and that's exactly what's happening — anglers working the inlet mouth on incoming tide with small metal, half-ounce Kastmasters and Hopkins, are getting steady action, sometimes fish on every other cast when the peanuts get pushed up against the jetty rocks.

Bay side, the drum run is long done — that's a May story, and if you're still calling around Fortescue asking about black drum in August you're a couple months late. What's replaced it is a weakfish bite that's actually worth the drive. We also called this one — said the weakfish would track the peanut bunker into the bay through August, and that's showing up now on the deeper flats and channel edges from Fortescue down toward the mouth of the bay. Squid strips and bloodworms on a simple two-hook bottom rig, fished in 15 to 25 feet on the last of the falling tide and the first hour of the incoming, are producing weakfish in the 16 to 20-inch range, with a few better fish mixed in for guys anchoring tight to the channel drop near Brandywine Shoal. Croaker are riding along in the same spots, which tells you the water's holding that mid-70s warmth the bay likes for this pattern. It's not a fast bite — you're putting in bait time and waiting for the tide to turn things on — but it's real, and it's the most dependable thing going in the bay right now.

Triggerfish have shown up in ones and twos around the nearshore reef structure and wreck sites off the Cape, which happens most Augusts once the water settles into full summer mode. Small hooks, squid or clam, fished right on the structure — not a fishery to plan a whole trip around, but a nice bonus if you're anchored up there for sea bass or tog scraps.

Conditions-wise, we're riding a full moon this week, which means bigger tidal swings than normal — good news and bad news. Good news: it sharpens the bite windows at the Rips and at Hereford Inlet, concentrating bait and fish into tighter time frames right at the tide change. Bad news: if you're not on the water in that window, you're watching flat current and quiet rod tips. The ocean's been carrying a bit of a lump — nothing dangerous, two-to-three foot swell out of the south — but it's enough that I'd rather work the inlet and inside structure than grind it out on an exposed ocean drift this week.

Looking ahead, I expect the pattern to hold through the week — stripers staying nocturnal and picky at the Rips, fluke and bluefish carrying the daytime charters, and the bay weakfish bite building as more peanut bunker push down toward Fortescue and Egg Island. If the moon eases back off full by the weekend, tidal current should settle down a notch, which usually stretches the bite windows out a little longer instead of compressing them into that hard 45-minute slam around the turn. If I had one trip to plan this week, I'd fish the bay on the last two hours of the outgoing for weakfish, then swing over to Hereford Inlet on the incoming to chase the blues while the peanuts are still stacked against the rocks. Two different bites, two different bays — that's the game down here in August, and right now the bay's carrying more weight than the ocean side.
