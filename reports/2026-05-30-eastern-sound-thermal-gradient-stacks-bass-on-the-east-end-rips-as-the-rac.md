# Thermal gradient stacks bass on the East End rips as the Race hits striking distance
*Montauk at 59.4°F and a steep west-to-east Sound gradient are concentrating slot and overslot bass on hard current edges from Plum Gut to Fishers.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — May 30** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

The eastern Sound is sitting on a sharp thermal staircase right now. New London is reading 51.8°F at the NOAA tide station, Montauk is up at 59.4°F, and Kings Point on the western end of the system is 58.6°F. That is a near 8-degree spread across the basin and the steepest part of the gradient runs right through my zone — Plum Gut, the Race, and the Fishers Island drops sit on the seam where cold deep Sound water meets the warmer, bait-loaded Peconic and ocean push. Block Island buoy 44097 shows 56.1°F with 2.6 ft waves on a short 3-second period out of 304°, and NY Bight buoy 44025 is 57.0°F with 2.3 ft at 9 seconds. The open ocean is still cool, mildly unsettled, and the Montauk buoy (44017) is offline so we are reasoning around a data gap on the south side. Wind at the harbor entrance buoy is WNW around 12 knots, pressure 1016 hPa and steady.

What that thermal structure means for this beat: the rips are firing because the warm-water envelope coming out of the Peconics on the ebb is dumping bait directly into colder, deeper Sound water at the Gut and the Race. That temperature break is the feeding shelf. Fish are staged on the cold side of the seam, ambushing what gets flushed off the warm shoulder. The class of fish moving through is heavily slot and overslot — almost no shorts in the mix — which is consistent with a peak-migration body of bass that wants current, structure, and big bait, not back-bay schoolies. Bluefish are in the same water column and they are not small.

Plum Gut on the ebb is the highest-percentage striped bass play in the zone right now. The standard three-way with a white bucktail in the 1.5- to 3-oz range, fished hard on the bottom in 60 to 100 feet on the down-current side of the rip line, is putting fish in the boat from the first pass. When birds work the top end of the tide, a 1.5- to 2-oz pencil popper or small metal cast into the seam will pull fish off the bottom — that surface window has been opening up in the back third of the ebb as the rip stands up. The Race is fishable but it is a different animal: heavier lead, longer drops, and you are sharing it with a fleet. Best drift is the south-side ebb edge over the 120-foot ledges, and the bluefish problem there is real — expect to lose bucktails to gorillas once they move in.

Along the Orient surf and the rocks at the north side of Plum, the overnight and pre-dawn plug bite on the outgoing has the right water for big fish. Bigger profile darters and bottle plugs into the rip current off the points, working the back side of the wash where the warm Peconic water drains. Fishers Island's west side and the Race drops are the cleanest blackfish water you will find this week as well, with the spring tog bite still legal in New York through May 31 — short window, but fish are on the structure in 30 to 50 feet. Fluke remains a Peconic and inside-Gardiners game; the deeper Sound bottom is still too cold at sub-55°F to expect a consistent doormat bite. Porgies are stacking on the shallow Peconic humps as that water punches past 58°F.

Look-ahead through the weekend: the moon is building toward full on June 1, which means the strongest tides of the cycle land Saturday and Sunday — peak current at the Gut and the Race, peak bait flush, peak ambush window. Wind looks manageable WNW to W, which keeps the Sound side fishable and the south-side ocean still bumpy and short-period. Watch for Montauk SST to push past 60°F in the next 72 hours; once it crosses that threshold the squid push that is already building east of Block will arrive on the rips and the entire Montauk-to-Race corridor lights up on squid-pattern bucktails and big soft plastics.

Fish the ebb, fish the seam, and bring leader heavy enough to handle a 40-pound bass or a ten-pound blue because both are in the same water. I'd be at the Gut for the bottom of the ebb on the full-moon tide and at the Race the morning after — that is where this week's biggest fish gets weighed.
