# Stripers Gone to Ground — Execution Rocks Now a Night Shift
*Bluefish and fluke are carrying the daylight hours while the bass wait for dark and deep water on the Western Sound.*

**CITY ISLAND, NY — August 3** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

Guy at the bait shop on City Island asked me straight out Thursday: "Frank, where'd all the bass go? I fished Stepping Stones three mornings this week and got skunked." I told him the same thing I've been telling everybody who'll listen since the temps climbed into the low 70s — the bass didn't go anywhere. They just stopped punching the clock during business hours.

Kings Point is reading 70.9 this week, and that's warm enough to shut down a lot of the daytime feeding you'd expect around Execution Rocks and the Stepping Stones light. I've fished this stretch since before anybody had a chartplotter, and I've seen this pattern plenty of Augusts — when the surface layer gets soft and stable like this, the stripers slide down into the deeper basin water off Hart Island and along the Execution Rocks channel edge, and they wait for dark. New London's still sitting a touch cooler at 69.4, which tells you the whole Sound is holding this same soft, settled character right now, not just our end.

So that's where I've been putting my time — last two hours of light into full dark, and again right before first light, working live bunker and fresh mackerel chunks on fish-finder rigs in 25 to 40 feet off the northeast face of Execution Rocks on the outgoing tide. Had a solid mixed bag of slot fish Tuesday night, one pushing 34 inches that ate a whole bunker practically on the drop. The current there on the ebb pulls bait right off the rockpile and funnels it along that edge — it's been a producer for three decades and it isn't quitting on me now, it's just moved its hours. Stepping Stones is doing the same thing after dark — eels fished on a slow drift over the rubble bottom near the light have been worth the trip for anybody willing to stay out past ten.

Daytime, if you want steady action instead of a long wait for dark, the bluefish are doing the heavy lifting. Fresh fish have been pushing down into the Sound and they're stacked up around the Throgs Neck Bridge pilings and out toward Hart Island on the last two hours of the incoming. Diamond jigs and topwater poppers worked over visible surface activity — you'll see the birds working and the blues busting bunker on top — and it's about as close to a sure thing as this Sound offers right now. Bigger fish are mixing in with the smaller choppers, which tells me there's a fresh wave still filtering down from the east.

Fluke have been the most dependable read of the whole week, and honestly it's playing out about the way I figured it would. We said last week the keeper bite would stay mixed in with shorts rather than turn into anything explosive, and that's exactly what's happening on the drifts off Matinecock Point and through the Manhasset Bay channel. I've been dragging 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid, along the sandy edges in 20 to 30 feet on the tail end of the outgoing. You'll pick through a lot of 15-inch fish, but there's a keeper mixed in most drifts — nothing to write home about, but steady enough to fill a cooler if you put in the time and don't get frustrated culling shorts.

Weakfish have shown up in small numbers in the deeper holes off Hempstead Harbor, mostly at dusk on bucktails tipped with squid strips fished slow along bottom. Nothing like the old days when you could count on them stacking up there in August, but a few nice ones in the 4-to-5-pound class have come over the rail for guys who know to fish that last hour of light. Porgies remain about the most reliable thing going for anybody who just wants bent rods without overthinking it — sandworms on the bottom around Execution Rocks and the rockpiles off Matinecock will keep a rod busy all afternoon.

Looking ahead, we're building toward the new moon, and that stronger tidal push over the next few days should stir the water column enough to get a few more stripers moving on the edges during daylight, especially on the front half of the outgoing when there's real current running. I wouldn't bank on a full return to the old daytime pattern just yet — it'll take either a real push of current from the bigger tides or a cold front to break up this warm, settled layer and reset things. If I had one trip to make this week, I'd fish the outgoing at Execution Rocks starting an hour before sunset and stay through the first hour of dark, bunker chunks soaking on the bottom, bucktail rod rigged and ready in case the blues show up first. That's the way to cover both bases without wasting the tide.
