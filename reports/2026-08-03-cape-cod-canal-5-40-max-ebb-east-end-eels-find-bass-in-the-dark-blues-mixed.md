# 5:40 Max Ebb, East End: Eels Find Bass in the Dark, Blues Mixed Through
*The current's still building toward the August 12 new moon, and every strong stage is turning fish on somewhere between Scusset and Mashnee.*

**SANDWICH, MA — August 3** — _by Mike Devlin, Zone Writer_

5:40 this morning, max ebb, east end — 4.1 knots ripping past the Herring Run and I had two guys on eels before the sky even started to gray up. That's the number I care about right now. Not the air temp, not what the charter boats are posting on social media. The current speed on that ebb tide, because that's what's turning these fish on and off like a switch, and right now the switch is flipping harder every day as we build toward the new moon on the 12th.

Here's where we are. Surface water in the Canal is sitting warm — same story as the rest of the coast, low-to-mid 70s on top — and that's pushed the striper bite into a deep, nocturnal pattern almost everywhere except right here. The Canal stays cooler down in that channel, current keeps it turning over and mixing, so we're still getting some daytime activity, but the best fish are absolutely coming after dark on the stronger stages. I had a client Thursday night on the west end doing a slow swim with a 9-inch Gibbs pencil through the seam off Mashnee, right where that current pinches down along Hog Island, and he connected on a 34-incher on the second cast of the max flood. That's the pattern — you want to be fishing the two hours around max current, doesn't matter which end, and you want to be doing it in the dark or in that gray light right before sun-up or right after sundown.

East end has been the more consistent water this week. The rip off the herring run and down toward the Sandwich side keeps stacking bait against that current seam on the ebb, and the eel guys are cleaning up — I'm talking 10- to 12-inch live eels, slow-drifted right along the seam where the moving water meets the slower water, letting it sink and swing naturally instead of ripping it through. Fish from 28 inches up to a 38 I heard about Tuesday night, though that one came off boatside before the net found it. Bluefish are mixed in on both ends of the Canal right now, choppers up to 8 pounds hammering topwater and swimming plugs during daylight max current, which is honestly some of the more fun action going because you don't need darkness for them like you do the bass. We flagged this exact pattern last week — said the building full-moon push would sharpen the current and improve the feeding windows, and that's exactly what's played out. The blues never left, they just got more aggressive as that tide built.

West end, Mashnee and Wings Neck side, the current seams have been holding fish consistently on the last two hours of the flood specifically — that's when the water really starts ripping past the rocks and bass tuck into the eddies waiting for bait to get swept through. Swimming plugs — Sebile Magic Swimmers, black-back/silver-belly pattern — have been the ticket there after dark, worked with a slow, steady retrieve right along the current break. Don't rip it. Let the current do the work and just keep tension on the line.

Fluke have been a modest but honest bite for anyone working the sandy stretches down in Phinneys Harbor and along the flats off Mashnee on the slower stages of tide, when the current backs off enough to fish bucktails and Gulp without getting swept sideways. Nothing blitz-level — this is a steady pick, keepers mixed with shorts, three or four fish an outing if you're covering ground, mostly on white Gulp Swimming Mullets paired with 3/4-ounce bucktails dragged slow along bottom. That's held stable and I don't expect it to change much this week — no reason for it to, the water's calm and stable through that stretch.

Now the one everyone's asking about — albies and bonito. Nothing showing inside the Canal itself yet, but the word from the Buzzards Bay mouth and around the Elizabeth Islands is scout fish starting to show, and that lines up with what I'd expect this time of year as the tide builds toward the new moon. If that pattern holds the way it usually does, we should start seeing the first real push into the west end current around the 8th to 10th, right as those tides really start ripping. I wouldn't plan a whole trip around it yet, but if you're already out there working the west end for bass on the evening flood, keep a rod rigged with a small metal or an epoxy jig within reach. You don't want to be scrambling when they show.

Look-ahead: expect this same pattern to hold and intensify through the week. Current speeds are only going to build as we approach the 12th, which should sharpen every bite window on both ends — bass and blues on the strong stages, day or night, with the night bite staying the more reliable bet for the bigger bass. If a front comes through and knocks the surface temp down even a couple degrees, don't be surprised if the bass shift back toward more daytime activity across the whole system. Until then, watch the clock, fish the current, not the calendar.
