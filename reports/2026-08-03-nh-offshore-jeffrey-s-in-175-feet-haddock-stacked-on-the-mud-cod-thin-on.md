# Jeffrey's in 175 feet: haddock stacked on the mud, cod thin on the gravel edge
*A steady groundfish week on the Ledge, with the first real bluefin sign showing on Tillies as the water starts to soften south side.*

**RYE HARBOR, NH — August 3** — _by Capt. Sonya Nguyen, Zone Writer_

Ran the numbers on Jeffrey's three times this week and they told the same story each trip: 175 to 190 feet on the west side of the Ledge, hard mud bottom, marks stacked three and four feet off bottom on the sounder — haddock, and plenty of them. That's the pattern for early August out here. The mud holds the worms and the small crab, the haddock sit right on it, and once you find the depth that's holding fish, it doesn't move much day to day. We've been anchoring up in that 180-foot band north of the Bank, running clam bellies and herring chunks on standard cod rigs with 6-ounce bank sinkers, and it's been about as reliable as this fishery gets — limits of haddock, 20 to 24 inches average, a few pushing 28, in three to four hour drops.

The gravel edge is a different animal right now, and I want to be straight about it: cod have been thin. We're marking them on the transitions where the mud breaks into cobble and gravel, usually in that 140 to 160-foot range on the eastern flank of the Ledge, but the numbers are down from where I'd want them this time of year. A boat working that edge might put three or four keeper cod in the box over a full day of jigging diamond jigs and 6-inch Gulp Grubs on bucktails. That's not a skunk, but it's not the gravel bite we had in June either. My read is the cod have slid deeper and scattered with the water holding steady and warm on top — they're there, but you're earning every one right now, not filling a box on autopilot.

Platts Bank has been worth the extra run for guys willing to make it. Similar story — mud holds haddock in the 170 to 200-foot range, and we've had a few pollock mixed in on the drift, chunky fish in the 8 to 12 pound class that'll hit a diamond jig worked with a hard sweep off bottom. Tillies Bank has been quieter for groundfish this week, but that's where the interesting sign showed up — more on that below. Scantum Basin I'd skip for now; deeper, colder water down there and the marks just haven't been worth the run given what's producing closer to home.

Hake have been a solid consolation prize on the slow tides. We've picked up whiting-sized hake and a few nicer ones over 5 pounds on the mud in that same 175 to 190-foot zone as the haddock, usually right at the tide change when everything else goes quiet. If you're fishing bait anyway, leave a rod down — they'll show.

Now the part I've been watching closest. We flagged last week that this would be a stable, typical late-summer Gulf of Maine pattern, and that's exactly what's played out — steady groundfish, no big disruption. But stable doesn't mean static, and the first real bluefin sign of the season showed up on the south end of Tillies this week as warmer water has started working up against the Ledge's southern edge. Nothing giant yet — a few boats marking fish and getting shots on the troll with spreader bars and green machines in that softer, bluer water — but it's early. Historically the real giant bluefin push on Jeffrey's doesn't load up until late September into October, when the mackerel and herring get pushed up tight to the structure and the big fish come up onto the Ledge itself. What we're seeing now is scout fish, the first wave riding that warm edge north. Worth having a rod rigged if you're already out there for groundfish, but I wouldn't plan a dedicated tuna trip around it yet.

Mackerel have been spotty but present around Cape Ann and off Thatcher Island — enough to jig up bait for the trip out, not enough to call it a mackerel bite in its own right. If you need fresh bait for haddock or want livies for a tuna shot, work the top 30 feet with a string of mackerel jigs near the Island on the way out; it's hit or miss but costs you fifteen minutes.

Looking ahead: tides are building toward the new moon, and on this Ledge that usually means better current on the mud and a haddock bite that tightens up even more predictably around the slack windows. I'd expect the mud pattern to hold steady through the week — 175 to 190 feet, clam and herring, haddock and hake. The gravel cod bite needs a cooling trend or a bait push to really turn back on; until then I'm treating it as a bonus, not the plan. And I'll be watching that south Tillies water closely — if the warm push holds and strengthens instead of fading, we could see bluefin numbers build faster than the calendar usually allows. That's the trip I'd want to be ready to pivot into on short notice.
