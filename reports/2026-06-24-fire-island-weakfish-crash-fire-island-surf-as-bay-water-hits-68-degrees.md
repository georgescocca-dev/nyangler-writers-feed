# Weakfish crash Fire Island surf as bay water hits 68 degrees
*Trophy tide-runners up to 7 pounds mixing with keeper fluke on the Captree drift.*

**CAPTREE, NY — June 24** — _by Mariana "Mari" Reyes, Zone Writer_

The water's telling a story this week, and it's one I've been waiting all season to hear. Bay temperatures spiked to 68 degrees — a solid four-degree jump that's got bait stacked and predators feeding like it's September. When that thermal gradient sets up between the bay and ocean, magic happens at Fire Island Inlet, and we're seeing it now.

The headline catch came from the surf near the inlet — a 26-inch, 7.3-pound weakfish that fell for a 9-inch Gravity Tackle Sand Eel. That's not just a nice fish, that's a legitimate tide-runner, the kind of weakie that makes you remember why we chase these ghosts in the first place. The sand eel pattern is key here. With bay water warming faster than the ocean side, bait's getting flushed on every tide change, and the weakfish are staging right in the wash to ambush them.

Out of Captree, the Laura Lee fleet's been putting keeper fluke on the deck consistently. The 30-foot zone off the Robert Moses bridges is producing doormat potential on the outgoing water. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails are the ticket — drag them slow on the drift, let the current do the work. The fish are holding tight to structure as that warmer bay water creates feeding lanes.

What's driving this bite is textbook Fire Island physics. We've got 68-degree bay water meeting 64-degree ocean water at the inlet mouth. That temperature break is a bait highway, and everything with fins knows it. The new moon we just passed means spring tides are flushing bunker and spearing out of the back bay like a conveyor belt. Bass are following, stacking on the ebb tide from the inlet jetties all the way to the Robert Moses span.

The Captree party fleet's been crushing mixed bags in the ocean — sea bass limits coming easy on diamond jigs and sabiki rigs, with ling cod and porgies filling out the coolers. But it's the inlet and bay mouth where the real action's happening. Clam bellies fished on fishfinder rigs are producing slot bass on the anchor, while casting Bass Assassins and soft plastics is connecting with the more aggressive fish.

Conditions are lining up perfectly. Light northwest winds at 5 knots, calm seas, and that high pressure system sitting over us means stable weather through the weekend. Water clarity's been excellent — you can see bottom in 15 feet, which tells you the spring flush is working.

The key zones right now are the inlet mouth on the outgoing tide, the Robert Moses bridges during the flood, and the surf from Democrat Point east to the lighthouse. For fluke, work the 25 to 35-foot contour with bucktails and Gulp. For bass and weakfish, focus on the inlet jetties and the channel edges where that temperature gradient is sharpest.

Looking ahead, we're building toward the full moon Friday, which means big water movement and serious feeding windows. That lunar pull is going to amplify everything we're seeing now — more bait movement, more aggressive fish, and better opportunities for trophy weakfish in the surf. The bay's warming trend should continue, potentially hitting 70 degrees by week's end, which will only intensify that thermal break at the inlet.

This is Fire Island fishing at its finest — when the bay becomes a bait factory and the inlet turns into a feeding frenzy. Get out there while the getting's good.
