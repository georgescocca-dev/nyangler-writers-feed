# Penfield's Ebb Window Pays Off in Blackfish While Bunker Keep Blues Stacked on the Cows
*The reef clock is running on schedule right now — fish the last two hours of ebb and you're in business; miss it and you're just burning gas.*

**NORWALK, CT — July 31** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Tuesday I had a decision to make at 5 a.m. sitting in the driveway with the truck running: Penfield Reef on the tail of the ebb, or run out to the Cows and chase the bunker schools that had been getting worked by blues all weekend. I picked Penfield. Here's why — that reef doesn't fish on just any tide. You want the last ninety minutes to two hours of the ebb, right as the rockpile starts to show its teeth and the current is still moving hard enough to sweep crabs and worms off the structure into the current break on the down-tide side. Too early and the water's still too deep over the reef to concentrate anything. Too late and it's dead slack with no current to push bait. That window is narrow, and this week it lined up with first light, which meant I had the reef mostly to myself before the weekend warriors even had coffee going.

It paid off. Blackfish to five pounds on green crab, fished on a knocker rig right up against the rock face in 12 to 18 feet, with a couple of porgies mixed in on the same drift using clam on a simple high-low. That's the read right now on Penfield — the porgies are thick enough that if you're not getting bit within a few minutes of anchoring up, you're in the wrong spot, not the wrong day. Same story out at the Cows off Stamford, where the porgy and black sea bass bite has been steady on the moving tide, either side of the change, working bloodworm or squid strips over the rockpile in 20 to 25 feet. Sea bass have been running a little better than average size for late July — 14 to 16 inches isn't rare out there right now, which tells me the warmer water sitting around 71 degrees on the western end of the Sound is keeping them comfortable and feeding instead of shutting down like they sometimes do this deep into summer.

The bluefish, though — that's been the story of the week, and it's not really a surprise. We flagged this pattern last week, that the blitz conditions would hold as long as the warm water stayed put, and that's exactly what's happened. Peanut bunker schools have been getting run over by blues from the Norwalk Islands down through Sheffield Island and out toward the Cows, mostly a mid-morning to early afternoon program once the sun's up and the bait pushes to the surface. Poppers and metal — Deadly Dicks, half-ounce Kastmasters — worked right through the surface commotion has been about as close to a sure thing as this Sound offers right now. If you see the water nervous and gulls working low, don't overthink it, just get a lure in there before the boat traffic scatters the school, because on a Saturday that window can close in twenty minutes once six center consoles converge on the same slick.

Striped bass have been the tougher assignment, and I won't dress it up. With Kings Point reading 70.9 and the whole western basin running warm, the bass are doing what they do this time of year — sliding off the shallow structure during daylight and setting up on deeper current edges and holes, feeding mainly at first light, last light, and through the night on bunker and eels. I've had better luck working eels along the channel edge off Greenwich Point on the last of the incoming, right at dusk, than I have anywhere on the open reef structure during the day. If you want a daytime shot at a slot fish, the drop-off on the northeast side of Cockenoe Reef during the first hour of the ebb has produced a few, but it's a needle-in-a-haystack proposition right now compared to the reliability of the bottom bite.

Fluke are the honest middle-of-the-road fish this week — not a blitz, not a bust. Channel edges around the Norwalk Islands and the drop-off on the west side of Sheffield Island have been giving up keepers mixed with plenty of shorts on white Gulp swimming mullets teamed with 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow on the drift during the outgoing. Nothing that'll fill a cooler fast, but steady enough that if you're patient and willing to cull through shorts, dinner's not a problem.

Tides are easing down off the full moon push toward neap over the next several days, which usually means current speeds soften a touch on these reefs — worth noting because some of these structure bites, especially Penfield and the Cows, actually get a little more forgiving on timing when the tide isn't screaming. I'd expect the porgy and blackfish program to hold steady through the week, the bluefish blitz to keep showing up wherever the bunker schools settle, and the stripers to stay locked into that dawn-dusk-dark pattern until we get a real cool-down. If I had one morning to burn this week, I'd still take Penfield on the ebb — but I'd have the popper rod rigged and ready in case the gulls tip me off to something better on the way out.
