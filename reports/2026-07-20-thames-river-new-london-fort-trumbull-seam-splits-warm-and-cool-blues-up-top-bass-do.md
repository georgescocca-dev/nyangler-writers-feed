# Fort Trumbull Seam Splits Warm and Cool: Blues Up Top, Bass Down Deep
*Bunker pushed hard into the harbor this week, and the river's temperature break is sorting bluefish from holdover stripers by the yard.*

**NEW LONDON, CT — July 20** — _by Marcus Webb, Zone Writer_

Stood on the seawall at Fort Trumbull Tuesday evening watching the ebb line set up, and you could see it before you could feel it — that ripple crease where the outgoing river water, cooler and darker, slides out past the fort and meets the harbor proper. Twenty years of watching that seam and it still tells you everything. This week it split the bite clean in half: bluefish working the warmer surface lens, stripers holding low in the cooler push underneath.

New London's reading around 70 degrees right now, a good two degrees cooler than what they're seeing over in the western Sound off Kings Point. That gap matters more than people think. The Thames stays a notch cooler than the open beaches because of the river flow dumping out of the dam upstream, and in the middle of July that's not a bad thing — it means the bass in this system haven't gone full nocturnal the way fish south of the Cape have. We're still getting some daytime interest here, but the real window is still that hour around dusk into full dark, and the hour either side of first light.

Bunker showed up thick in the harbor over the last ten days, working up through the mouth and pushing into the flats off Pleasure Beach and up toward the Gold Star Bridge. We flagged this pattern last week — said the bluefish would keep following bunker schools up the coast as long as the weather held calm and didn't scatter the bait — and that's exactly what's playing out here. Blues have been blowing up on top off Ocean Beach in the last two hours of daylight, and inside the harbor itself, schoolie-to-mid-size blues are chopping through peanut bunker right off the fort's rock pile. Small metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Deadly Dicks and Kastmasters — worked fast just under the surface has been the ticket, along with white or bone Rebel Jumpin' Minnows if the blues are up and splashing.

Striped bass are a different conversation, and a more honest one. The keeper-class fish haven't been stacking up in the numbers you'd want in July — this is typically the slower stretch of the season in the river, and this year's tracking that. What's producing are the holdover-type fish and a scattering of migratory stripers holding deep along the pilings at Gold Star Bridge, 20 to 28 feet on the down-current side depending on which span you're fishing, and along the deeper edge of the Fort Trumbull rip where the channel drops off toward 30 feet. Eels fished on the bottom, slow-lifted and dropped back on the ebb, have out-produced everything else for me this week. If you don't want to mess with eels, a 6-inch white or bunker-colored swimbait on a 3/4-ounce jig head, bounced along bottom on the last two hours of the outgoing, has been getting bit around the bridge fenders. Best window has consistently been that last hour of light sliding into dark — the fish are there earlier, but they don't commit until the light drops off and that current seam goes quiet.

We're heading into the first-quarter neap tomorrow, and that's actually going to help the rip fishing more than hurt it. Weaker current means the Fort Trumbull seam and the water off Eastern Point won't rip as hard, which widens the productive window on either side of the tide change instead of it being a tight 40-minute sprint. If you've struggled to get a swimbait down through that current in the past, the next few days are the easier version of that spot.

Hickory shad are still around the bridge pilings, showing on the surface in the early morning making those little dimpling rings when they're picking off bugs and small bait. Light spin gear, 1/8-ounce shad darts or small Kastmasters, ultralight leader — good time to bring a kid down and let them catch fish that actually fight above their size class. They'll thin out as we get into August but right now there's still a solid morning window most days.

Porgy fishing remains the steady, unglamorous option that never lets you down this time of year. The reef structure off Pleasure Beach and around the Beacon are holding good numbers in 20 to 35 feet — squid strips or sandworms on a high-low rig, small hooks, light enough sinker to feel the bottom on the drift. If the striper bite's slow, this is where I send people who just want bent rods and a fish dinner. Blackfish are still closed for the summer stretch here in Connecticut waters, so that's a fall conversation — I'll pick that back up when the season reopens.

Looking ahead, I'd keep working that Fort Trumbull to Gold Star Bridge stretch through the neap, fishing the widened tide windows in low light for stripers and hitting the harbor mouth in daylight for blues while the bunker hold. If the bunker push further up-river the way they have the last few days, I wouldn't be surprised to see that bluefish action creep closer to the bridge itself by the weekend. Watch the surface — where the bunker go, the blues follow, and that part of this pattern hasn't missed yet this year.
