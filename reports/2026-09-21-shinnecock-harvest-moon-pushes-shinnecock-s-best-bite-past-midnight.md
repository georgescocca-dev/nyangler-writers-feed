# Harvest Moon Pushes Shinnecock's Best Bite Past Midnight
*Bigger tides are stacking bunker at the inlet, and the bass and weakfish are both feeding hardest when nobody else is on the water.*

**HAMPTON BAYS, NY — September 21** — _by Charlie "Night Owl" Ortiz, Zone Writer_

The call this week is simple: fish the back half of the outgoing, and fish it late. With the Harvest Moon pumping up the tide swings, the current at Shinnecock Inlet is running harder and longer than it has all summer, and that means the bunker getting flushed out of the bay on the ebb are moving in bigger, tighter packs — which means the bass and blues chasing them are showing up more predictably, but mostly after the boat traffic clears out. Daytime out there this week has been a mixed bag of small blues and short bass picking at the edges. After 10 p.m., with the moon up and the tide dropping hard through the pilings on the ocean side, that's when the bigger fish commit.

The why is straightforward. Bigger tides move more water, and more water moving out of Shinnecock Bay on the ebb drags more peanut bunker and adult menhaden through the inlet's throat. That funnel effect concentrates bait right where the current necks down along the east and west jetties, and predators know it. It's the same bunker-driven pattern setting up right now from the Jersey beaches through Montauk — big Harvest Moon water pulling everything into a tighter window at structure. We flagged this building last week, the inlet blitz pattern stacking up alongside an early push of bonito and albies working the deeper rips to the east, and that's exactly the shape it's taking now. The bunker showing at Shinnecock's mouth are the leading edge of that same warm-water push.

The striped bass bite has been the most consistent story. The deep hole on the ocean side of the inlet, inside the rocks where the bottom drops to 15 to 20 feet, is holding fish through the second half of the outgoing tide, roughly two hours before low through the last of the ebb. Bucktails in the 1.5 to 2-ounce range, white or chartreuse, worked with a slow lift-and-drop right off bottom, have been drawing strikes from bass in the mid-20s to low-30-inch range, with a scattering of overslot fish mixed in on the darkest nights around new and full moon phases. Live bunker on a fish-finder rig, drifted right along the current seam where the jetty rocks meet sand, has also been producing — that's the setup for anyone who wants a bigger bite over quantity. Don't sleep on the incoming either; the first two hours of the flood have been quietly productive right at the mouth for anglers who don't want to fight the ebb's current.

Weakfish are still showing at Ponquogue Bridge, and that bite has actually firmed up this week as water temperatures have held steady in the bay despite the cooler nights. Working the pilings on the last two hours of the outgoing, with the current sliding bait past the structure, has been the ticket — bucktail and Gulp combos in bay anchovy colors, bounced slow along the pilings in 10 to 15 feet, or bait fished on a high-low rig with squid strips. These aren't the giant tiderunners of years past, mostly 14 to 18 inches, but there are enough of them around the bridge lights at night to make a worthwhile stop before or after a run at the inlet.

Fluke have held up better than expected for late September. The bay drift from the Shinnecock Coast Guard station down toward Tiana Beach has been giving up keepers in the 18 to 20-inch range on white Gulp Swimming Mullets and bucktail-and-strip combos, fished on the drift in 12 to 18 feet of water on the last of the incoming into the top of the tide. It's not a banner week, but it's steady, and the warmer-than-normal water sitting in the bay is the reason it's lasted this long. Any real cold front that digs in will start shutting that down fast, so anyone who wants a fluke dinner should not wait much longer.

Bluefish are riding shotgun on the bunker just like everywhere else on the Island right now — snappers thick in the canal and back-bay coves, with bigger blues, some pushing 8 to 10 pounds, mixing into the inlet feeds when the bass show. Metal — Deadly Dicks and diamond jigs — worked fast through the froth at the inlet mouth when the birds start working is the move, and it doesn't take much finesse when they're on the bunker like this.

Porgies and blackfish are still around the canal rocks and the bay's harder bottom for anyone looking for a bend in the rod during daylight hours, but they're a secondary target right now while the bass and weakfish bite is this active after dark.

Looking ahead, the next several days should keep building on this pattern as the Harvest Moon tides stay big through the week. If the wind stays out of the north and west and holds off the inlet, the outgoing after dark should keep producing at the deep hole and along both jetties. The bigger question is the weather turning over the next 72 hours — any real cold front that pushes through will start tightening the fluke bite first and could nudge more bass south and out of the bay faster than usual. For now, if there's only one window to fish this week, make it the last three hours of the outgoing on a night around the full moon, working bucktails deep at the inlet before sliding over to the bridge pilings on the way in.
