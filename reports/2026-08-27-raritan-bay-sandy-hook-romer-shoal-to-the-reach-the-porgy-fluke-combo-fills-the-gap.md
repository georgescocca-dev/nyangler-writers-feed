# Romer Shoal to the Reach: the porgy-fluke combo fills the gap the bass left behind
*Snapper blues own the piers, bunker are stacking for the fall push, and the moon builds toward full Friday — here's where the bay is actually paying off right now.*

**KEYPORT, NJ — August 27** — _by Marisol Duarte, Zone Writer_

If you've only got one tide window this week, spend it on the channel edges off the Reach and down toward Romer Shoal, not chasing bass around the bay. That's the call right now, and it's not close. Striped bass are in the predictable late-August holding pattern — scattered, structure-oriented, mostly a dusk-and-dawn program if they show at all — while porgies and fluke are the two species actually cooperating on a consistent basis. We flagged this exact setup last week, saying the porgy-fluke combo would be the most reliable play through the stretch, and that's exactly how it's played out on the drop-offs from Chapel Hill Channel out past the Flats.

Water's warm across the bay, surface temps sitting in that mid-70s range that pushes bait and predators alike off the open flats and onto structure with a cooler bottom layer. That's why the channel edges are holding up better than the Reach's open water — 18 to 24 feet along the drop, moving water on the last two hours of the ebb, is where the bucktail-and-Gulp program keeps finding keeper fluke instead of the dinks that are thick everywhere else. White or chartreuse 3/4-ounce bucktails tipped with a 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullet, dragged slow along bottom as the tide pulls off the flat and funnels into the channel — that's the presentation putting fish in the box. Sizes are modest, most fish 16 to 19 inches, but there's enough of a mix in there to make it worth the drift, especially with a few short flurries of keeper-class fish showing up right where the mud gives way to hard sand along the edge.

Porgies are honestly the more dependable half of that combo right now. Anchoring up on the same channel structure — the drop just off Flynn's Knoll, or the deeper stretch running toward Romer Shoal — with a standard porgy rig, size 4 or 6 hooks, clam or squid strips, is filling coolers while the fluke bite stays hit-or-miss. These fish are stacked tight to bottom in that cooler layer and they're not picky. If you want steady rod-bending action for the family or you're breaking in a kid on the rail, this is the move over grinding for stripers that mostly aren't home.

Speaking of stripers — the bass situation is exactly the summer lull we called out a few days back. This isn't the fall run starting early, it's the bay doing what it does in late August: warm surface water, minimal daytime activity, whatever bass are around holding tight to structure or feeding at night. What's more interesting is what's building underneath that lull. Bunker schools are thickening up in the bay ahead of the fall push, and that's the tell everyone on the docks in Keyport and the Highlands is watching. The moon builds to full on Friday, and that's going to strengthen the tidal current running through the bay's channels and pinch those bunker schools tighter against structure — the same mechanism we pointed to last week when we said this pattern would intensify as the moon filled out. Watch Flynn's Knoll and the mouth of the Navesink over the next several tides; if bass are going to show any real interest before the calendar turns to September, that's where the first sign will be — bunker balled up, birds working, maybe a swirl or two on the surface at first light.

Snapper blues remain the one thing you can count on no matter what the tide chart says. They're thick off the Highlands and Keansburg piers, chasing peanut bunker into water so shallow you can watch it happen, and they'll hit just about anything small and flashy — 1/8-ounce Kastmasters, bare gold hooks with a sliver of bunker, tiny bucktail teasers under a bobber for kids fishing off the rail. It's not glamorous fishing but it's about as close to a guarantee as this bay offers right now, and it's a good way to keep a rod bent while you wait on the bigger patterns to develop.

Up in the Navesink and Shrewsbury, the story's quieter. Warm, slack water in the upper reaches isn't holding much beyond the occasional snapper push and a few small fluke working the deeper bends. Better water and better current are down in the bay proper right now — the rivers should perk back up once nights start cooling and that first real drop in water temp gets underway, which typically doesn't happen here until deeper into September.

Looking at the next few days: expect the warm-water regime to hold with the wind staying out of the southwest in the 8-to-12 knot range, which means stable but unspectacular conditions — no big weather to reshuffle the deck. The building moon into Friday's full phase should sharpen the tidal push through the channels, which is good news for the fluke-porgy program on the edges and worth watching closely at Flynn's Knoll for any early bass activity around the bunker. If there's only one day to fish this weekend, run the last two hours of the ebb on the channel edge off the Reach with bucktail-and-Gulp, then set up on the porgy rigs once the tide goes dead slack. Keep an eye on the bunker schools — when they start getting nervous and balling up tight against structure, that's the signal the fall pattern is closer than the calendar suggests.
