On the gear side, this is still classic late-September south canyon fishing whenever boats can get out and set up. Trolling spreads mixing naked and rigged ballyhoo behind cedar plugs and small Islander-style lures is the go-to for covering water and finding where the yellowfin are holding along the temperature breaks. Where boats can mark bait and get fish fired up, chunking butterfish or bunker on a slow drift has been the better move for converting a sighted school into actual bites — smaller hooks, lighter fluoro, and patience while the chunk line builds. Mahi have been the wildcard — any floating structure or weed line is worth a look with a light spinning rod and a small bucktail or a strip of squid, since a stray patch of grass this late in the season can hold a handful of fish that make the whole detour worthwhile. White marlin sightings have been scattered rather than consistent, which tracks with a spread-out bite pattern rather than a tight, aggressive push.

Route-wise, the call this week has leaned toward starting closer — Spencer or Lindenkohl — and only committing to the longer run out to Poor Man's or Norfolk when there's a specific reason to believe the water or the bait has set up better out there. That's the practical reality of an extended-range warm pattern: the fish are out there, but they're not concentrated enough yet to justify burning fuel past a canyon that's fishing fine.

Looking at the next few days, the warm filament staying intact is the whole ballgame. If it holds, expect the moderate yellowfin activity to persist across the string with occasional wahoo and mahi mixed in for boats willing to put in the trolling miles. If a real cooling signal moves through as September closes out — and there's reason to watch for exactly that late this month — the tighter, more productive fishing should start compressing back toward the true canyon edges rather than staying spread thin across the whole stretch. That would actually be good news for anyone running out this weekend: a tightening pattern usually means better odds per drop, even if it means less real estate to work. Keep an eye on the swell forecast before committing to the long run, and have a Plan B canyon in mind before you leave the dock — because right now, this is a week where flexibility beats stubbornness.
