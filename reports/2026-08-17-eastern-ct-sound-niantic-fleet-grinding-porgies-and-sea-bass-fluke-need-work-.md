# Niantic fleet grinding porgies and sea bass — fluke need work, keepers are out there
*The drift boats are stacking porgy and black sea bass limits while fluke fishing turns into a numbers game from Twotree to Hole-in-the-Wall.*

**NIANTIC, CT — August 17** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Stopped by the dock in Niantic Tuesday morning and one of the mates off the drift boat fleet was hosing down coolers full of porgies before the boat even finished tying up. That's the story right now on this end of the Sound — porgies and black sea bass are doing the heavy lifting while fluke make you earn every keeper.

Water's sitting around 69 to 70 degrees off New London, and that cooler water pushing through the Race and down through Twotree Island Channel is keeping this stretch a touch cleaner and more comfortable than what they're dealing with up in the western basin. That tidal flushing matters — it's why the bite out here has stayed more consistent through the middle of the day instead of shutting down hard like it does closer to the city.

Porgy fishing is the most dependable thing going. The rock piles off Black Point and the structure tight to Hole-in-the-Wall are holding good numbers, and the drift boats have been putting steady limits together on high-low rigs with sandworms and clam, fished right on bottom in 20 to 35 feet. Slack tide into the first hour of the new drift is when it turns on — porgies don't love ripping current, so the captains are timing their drops around the tide change and letting the boat settle before the first baits go down. Nothing fancy about it, just bottom rigs and patience, but it's meat in the cooler almost every trip.

Black sea bass are riding shotgun on the same structure. McCook Point and the drop-offs around Black Point in 25 to 40 feet have been giving up a solid mixed bag — jigs tipped with squid strips or a chunk of green crab are getting bit, and the bigger fish are coming off bottom on the up-tide drift when the current's still got some push to it. It's not a wide-open bite, but it's a fill-your-bag kind of bite, and that's plenty this time of year.

Fluke is where things get honest. The Sound-wide pattern of mostly short fish has held true out here too — Twotree Island Channel and the flats off Niantic Bay are loaded with sub-legal fluke, and you're picking through a pile to find a keeper. The better move has been working Hole-in-the-Wall and the deeper edge near 40 feet where the water stays cooler and the bigger, lazier fish tend to sit tight to structure instead of chasing bait around. Bulked-up baits are the key — a whole squid strip over a 4-inch Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail, dragged dead slow on the last two hours of the outgoing. Warm, stagnant water makes fluke sluggish, so a slower presentation with more scent is out-fishing anything zipped through fast.

Striped bass have been a dawn-and-dusk proposition. With Sound temps running into the high 60s and low 70s in the shallows, the bass have tightened their window — first light and the last hour before dark at the mouth of Niantic Bay and around Hole-in-the-Wall on the moving tide has been the play. Bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs are working for the guys anchoring the rip, and a few boats working eels after dark off Black Point have connected on slot fish in the 28 to 33-inch range. It's not the daylight blitz action they're seeing to the east right now, but it's real fish on a real pattern if you time the tide right.

Bluefish deserve a mention because we flagged this a few weeks back — said the snapper blues thick in the back bays would build into adult-class fish as August wore on, and that's exactly what's happening. The mouth of the Niantic River is loaded with snappers, and there's a growing mix of 3 to 5-pound blues showing up in that same water, cutting into the bass bite at times and giving light-tackle guys something to do when the stripers go quiet mid-day.

Blackfish are still just scouting mode — a few guys picking around the rockier stretches off Black Point with green crabs, nothing worth building a trip around yet, but worth remembering those spots for when the water starts to slide in September.

Looking ahead, tides are easing off toward the weaker cycle right now, but they'll start rebuilding as we push toward the full moon on the 28th. That stronger current should tighten up the bait around Twotree Channel and Hole-in-the-Wall and could be the trigger that gets fluke moving more aggressively instead of just sitting on structure. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd take the last two hours of an outgoing tide at Hole-in-the-Wall — fluke gear rigged and ready, but a bass rod loaded with an eel just in case the light goes right and something bigger shows up looking for an easy meal.
