# New moon spring tides push Moriches fluke out the inlet as the bay tops 78°F
*Great South Bay's gone soup-warm, but the cut at Cupsogue is running cold, clean, and loaded with bait — that's where the rods are bending.*

**MORICHES INLET, NY — July 10** — _by Pete Gallo, Zone Writer_

Here's what the tide charts and the thermometer told me this week: Great South Bay pushed past 78°F on the flats, and that's the number that reorganized this whole system. Warm, oxygen-poor water like that doesn't hold fluke — it pushes them to move, and the only places for a bay fluke to go are the deep channels and the inlet itself, where the ocean is still feeding in around 71°F on the incoming. That's a seven-degree gradient stacked right at the throat of Moriches Inlet, and gradients like that are where I make my money. We're also coming into new moon springs — the moon went dark Tuesday, and that means bigger tidal amplitude, faster water through the cut, and a harder-pulling ebb off Cupsogue than we've seen in three weeks. Bigger springs mean the inlet's storm-cut geometry gets tested — sand that built up on the west bar during the last neap cycle gets scoured out again, and the channel edges sharpen up. That's good news if you fish structure, because it means the drop-offs along the eastern bar and the flats behind Pattersquash are getting freshly cut, not silted in.

Wind's been the other variable. We had a stretch of southwest breeze that churned the back bay into something the color of coffee with milk, but it laid down over the weekend and let the upper water column clean up on the ocean side. That's exactly the setup — clean incoming water plus a warm coastal plume — that opens the door for the first bonito and Spanish mackerel of the year to show on the ocean side of the inlet. That window's been open since the Fourth and it's closing fast, so if you've got a spinning rod and small metal, Cupsogue's ocean beach and the eastern rip off the jetty are worth a dawn run before it shuts.

On the fluke, I've had better weeks and I've had worse. The bay bite inside — off Tuthill Point, the flats by East Moriches — has gone soft with the heat, exactly like I'd expect. But the inlet itself has been paying. Drifting the main channel from the Coast Guard station down through the cut on the last two hours of the outgoing has been the play — 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with 5-inch chartreuse Gulp Swimming Mullets, bounced slow along the bottom in 12 to 18 feet where the channel steepens toward the ocean side. That thermal edge I mentioned holds bait, and the fluke are sitting right on the seam between the warm outflow and the cooler incoming push. I've seen keepers to 4 pounds come out of there this week, nothing to write home about size-wise, but steady enough that three or four rods working the drift will put fish in the box most tides.

Striped bass have been the more honest story. They're funneling through the inlet on the last of the incoming and the first hour of the outgoing, same as they do every summer once the bay warms past their comfort zone — bass don't like 78-degree water any more than fluke do, and the inlet's the pinch point where cooler ocean water meets bay outflow. I'm marking bunker pods stacked along the eastern bar at Cupsogue, and the bass working under them are mostly schoolies with the occasional mid-20-inch fish mixed in. Bucktails and swimming plugs worked through the wash at the base of the jetty have taken fish at first light, and there's been a decent number of bluefish mixed into that same push — snappers mostly, with a few cocktail blues in the 2 to 3-pound class chopping bait on top. If you're throwing metal, keep it moving; the blues won't touch anything slow in water this warm.

Weakfish are the disappointment nobody wants to talk about. The water's been in the right range for three weeks now, and I haven't heard of a single decent weakfish out of this system — not the bay, not the inlet flats, nowhere. Temperature alone isn't doing it this year. I'd stop chasing that species until something changes; put your time into fluke and bass instead.

Porgies have been steady and boring in the best way — scup up to a pound and a half on the humps inside the inlet and along the channel edges off Tuthill Point, taken on standard high-low rigs with clam. Not exciting, but if you want a guaranteed bend in the rod for the kids, that's your play on a slow tide.

Looking ahead: the new moon springs peak through the weekend, and that means the strongest current of the month is running the cut Friday through Monday. If the wind stays out of the south-southwest and light, I'd fish the outgoing at Cupsogue for fluke and bass together, working the drop where the channel meets the ocean bar. If it swings onshore and stacks a groundswell, I'd shift inside to the flats and work the last of the incoming instead — less current, calmer water, still fishable. Either way, watch that bonito window close over the next few days; if the warm plume holds, Moriches' ocean side is as good a shot as anywhere on the South Shore for the first cocktail blues-and-bonito mix of the summer.
