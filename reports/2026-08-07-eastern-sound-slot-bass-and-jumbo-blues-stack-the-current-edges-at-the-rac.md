# Slot Bass and Jumbo Blues Work the Current Edges at The Race
*Building tides ahead of the August 9 full moon are sharpening the rip bite from Plum Gut to Fishers Island — here's how to fish the edges before the flow peaks.*

**ORIENT POINT, NY — August 7** — _by Mike Rizzo, Zone Writer_

_AI fishing-intelligence report. It was prepared from 0 supplied evidence records and 17 supplied analysis inputs; it does not claim firsthand fishing._

Reports out of The Race this week describe exactly the pattern I look for in early August: slot and overslot bass stacked tight on the current seams, with almost no shorts in the mix, and jumbo bluefish crashing the same water. That's not a coincidence. When the bait gets squeezed into a hard edge and the current's ripping, everything eats — and right now the current is building fast toward the August 9 full moon. We flagged this last week — said the tides would rebuild and the bite would sharpen as flow strengthened, and that's exactly what's showing up in the intel coming off the Race and Plum Gut.

Here's the mechanics of it. New London is sitting at 77.4°F on the surface, which sounds warm and lazy, but that number doesn't tell the real story. Down in the rip, where The Race and Plum Gut funnel millions of gallons through a narrow slot four times a day, the water column mixes hard and stays cooler and more oxygenated than the flat water on either side. That's where the bass want to be in August heat — not baking on a mud flat, but holding in the cold, churned seam where bait gets disoriented and easy to eat. Montauk's reading 74.5°F for reference, so there's a real thermal gradient building west to east, and it's pushing bait concentration right into these current-scoured funnels.

The presentation that's working is the one I run every August: a 1.5- to 2-ounce bucktail, white or olive, cast across the downcurrent side of the rip and allowed to sink clean before you start a slow, rhythmic bottom retrieve. Don't rush it. The bass sitting on these edges aren't chasing — they're ambushing, so a bucktail that stutters and bumps bottom outfishes one that swims high and fast. As the tide keeps building into the full moon, expect the window at peak flow to get shorter and more violent — the sweet spot is now the first hour of a strong ebb or flood, right as the rip starts screaming, rather than a long slow build. If you're throwing eels or swimbaits at Plum Gut, that neap-tide window we had two weeks back where the current was manageable through most of the cycle is closing. Peak flow is coming back hard, so plan your drift for the front half of the tide change and be ready to bail off the structure once it turns to washing machine.

Bluefish are the other half of this story, and they're not subtle about it. Bunker pods are still thick in the eastern Sound, and wherever they concentrate, the blues are turning the surface into whitewater. That call from a couple weeks back about violent topwater feeds holding as long as bunker stay thick and the high pressure holds — that's still playing out. Look for diving birds off Fishers Island and along the Orient Point rips at first light; when you see gulls working low and fast, that's bunker getting pushed to the surface, and the blues are underneath doing the pushing. Needlefish plugs and 2-ounce metal — Deadly Dicks, Kastmasters — worked with a fast, erratic retrieve will get bit instantly. Don't finesse it. These fish want speed and flash, not subtlety.

Fluke and porgies are filling in the daytime hours while the bass and blues own dawn and dusk. Drift 3/4-ounce white bucktails tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullets along the current edges near Plum Gut and the Fishers Island reef structure — 25 to 40 feet of water, moving bait slow on the drift, staying in contact with bottom. Porgies are stacked over the same rocky structure; sandworms or clam on a high-low rig will keep the rod bouncing between fluke drifts. Weakfish are still a bonus fish this time of year in the eastern Sound rather than a target — a few are showing on soft plastics worked slow in the evening along grassy edges, but I wouldn't plan a trip around them.

For surfcasters without a boat, Hallock State Park Preserve out of Riverhead is worth the walk. It's not Race-level current, but the small rip off the Mattituck Creek outflow at the west end of the beach concentrates bait on the outgoing, and the cobble sections hold fish that the sandy stretches don't. Night fishing is allowed there with the proper permit, and this time of year that after-dark window — working topwater plugs into the outflow on a dropping tide — is when the bigger bass show up on the sand. It's a solid Plan B if the boat ramps are jammed or the Gut's blown out from wind.

Looking ahead, the full moon on the 9th should keep tightening this pattern. Expect current speeds at The Race and Plum Gut to keep climbing through the weekend, which means bigger, more violent feeding windows but shorter ones — you'll need to be on the water right as the tide turns, not an hour into it. If the bunker schools hold and the high pressure doesn't break, the bluefish blitzes should keep firing off Fishers Island and Orient Point through the coming week. If a front moves through and scatters the bait, I'd shift effort to the deeper current seams at Plum Gut where the bass tend to hold regardless of surface chaos. Either way, fish the edges, not the middle — that's where this bite lives right now.
