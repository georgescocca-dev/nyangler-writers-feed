# Jumbo Blues Own the Rips at Shagwong While Montauk Waits on the Squid
*Slot bass and blues to double digits are stacked on the current edges, and the water's cooling just enough to make you believe the albies are close.*

**MONTAUK, NY — August 17** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

There's a specific week every August when the Point stops feeling like summer and starts feeling like Montauk again. This is that week. The tourist traffic on the Circle is still bumper to bumper, but out on the water the light's changing angle earlier in the evening, the bunker schools are getting nervous, and the fish know something the beach crowd doesn't. I've fished this transition my whole life and it never gets old — the sense that something bigger is loading up behind the curtain.

Right now the story at the Point is bluefish, and I mean real bluefish — not the summer snappers that have been thick in the harbor and up in Fort Pond Bay all month, but grown adult blues pushing eight, nine, ten pounds, chopping bait on top at Shagwong Reef and along the boulder field off the Lighthouse. We flagged this building back when the snappers first showed thick in the back bays — said the adult class would keep filling in through late August, and here they are, right on schedule. These fish are holding tight to the hard-running current edges, the visible boils where the rip pushes against the reef structure, and they're not subtle about it. If you see white water and diving gulls off the Lighthouse on the building tide, get a bucktail or a diamond jig in there fast, because that window can close in twenty minutes once the bait clears the seam.

The bass are mixed in with those blues, and the pattern that's working is almost identical for both species. Cast a 1.5-ounce bucktail — bare or dressed with a white or chartreuse tail — across the far edge of the rip, let it sink clean off the drop where the flat falls into the channel, then work it back with a slow, deliberate bottom hop. That's the North Bar program right now on the incoming, and it's producing slot fish consistently, with the occasional overslot mixed in for guys willing to grind through short fish to find them. Turtle Cove has been quieter for bass specifically but still worth a look on the last two hours of the outgoing, when bait gets flushed out of the cove mouth and stripers stage to ambush it in the deeper pockets along the rock line.

Water at the Point is sitting right around 69 degrees, which is a number I like this time of year — not so warm that the fish get lazy and scattered, cool enough that the bigger bass are comfortable holding shallow on the rocks instead of sulking in deep water during daylight. That's part of why the boulder field bite has stayed dependable even through the midday sun, which is unusual for August. I had a father-son team out earlier this week who worked the incoming at the Lighthouse rocks with bunker chunks on fish-finder rigs and iced two bass in the high twenties, plus enough blues to lose count, before nine in the morning.

South side surf and the fluke drifts have been more of a grind, and I want to be straight about that instead of dressing it up. The fluke bite off the ocean beaches has been mixed — plenty of shorts mixed with the occasional keeper to five or six pounds, and you're covering water to find the better fish rather than anchoring on one drift and waiting. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on three-quarter-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the sand-to-structure transitions, has been the most consistent producer. Porgies have been steadier and less glamorous — working the deeper structure off Fort Pond Bay with clam bait on standard porgy rigs will fill a cooler without much drama, which some days is exactly what you want.

Now, the question everyone's asking me at the dock is the albies. We called the arrival window around the August 12 new moon, and honestly, the timing hasn't quite lined up yet — I haven't had a confirmed albie or bonito push through the rips at the Point this week. But I'm not backing off that call, because the signs are building in exactly the direction you'd want. Squid have been pushing down hard off Rhode Island and into Nantucket Sound as that water cools, and historically when that squid line starts creeping toward the Race and into Block Island Sound, it's the trigger that supercharges our rip patterns here at Montauk — bait gets funneled through Shagwong and around the Point, and albies and bonito follow it in behind the blues. If that squid keeps sliding our way over the next week or two, I'd expect the first real showing right around the Point, probably working the rips on the last of the incoming when the current is still ripping hard enough to hold bait tight.

Looking ahead, the moon's building toward the full on August 28, and that's usually when this whole pattern sharpens up — bigger tides, harder current, bait pushed with more authority through the rips. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd be at North Bar on the building incoming with bucktails, working the seam methodically, and I'd keep a bonito outfit rigged and ready in the rocket launcher just in case that squid line beats my prediction. Worth planning around, not worth betting the farm on just yet.
