This has been a strong week for the Block Island corridor, and it's tracking almost exactly how it should this time of year. We flagged this pattern last week — that this zone would ride alongside Block Island's strong fluke and albie numbers through the next several days — and that's exactly what's playing out. Fluke are holding shockingly well for late September. Warmer-than-normal water offshore is keeping them shallower and more active than a typical year, and boats working the drift off Block in 40 to 60 feet are still putting keepers in the box on white Gulp Swimming Mullets tipped with squid strips, fished on 3/4-ounce bucktails bounced slow along the bottom. That bite tends to fade fast once the first real cold front pushes through and knocks nearshore temps down, so if fluke is the target, this is closer to the end of the window than the middle of it.

The surface story right now belongs to bonito and false albacore. The invasion that started here in Rhode Island waters has become the defining story of the fall — Block Island, Narragansett Bay, and Fishers Island Sound are all holding fish, and the push is actively working west into Long Island Sound and Connecticut. Off Sandy Point and around the rips toward North Light, small metal — half-ounce to one-ounce Deadly Dicks, Albie Snax in bone or pink/white — is getting bit hard on the retrieve when the surface erupts, but the frustrating truth about this fishery hasn't changed: when they're up, they're up, and when they slide under the bait balls, casting into empty water does nothing for you. Patience on the drift, watching for diving birds and nervous water, still beats blind-casting.

Striped bass are underneath all of this, not necessarily leading it. The classic fall bunker pattern is building from Delaware Bay up through Maine, and the bigger Harvest Moon tides are accelerating bait movement everywhere, including here. Around SW Ledge itself, the better bass have been coming on the bottom third of the drift through slack, on 1.5 to 2-ounce bucktails dressed with white or chartreuse pork rind, worked with a slow lift-and-drop right through the rip's edge where the current folds back on itself. It's not a blitz pattern yet at Point Judith the way it is at Montauk or the Canal, but the ingredients — bait, tide push, and cooling water — are lining up for that to change over the next couple weeks as more forage gets funneled south and east.

Bluefish are riding along wherever the bunker shows, and they've been thick enough at times to be a nuisance on bucktail retrieves meant for bass — not the worst problem to have, especially with jumbo blues mixed into the eastern Long Island surf reports this week too. If you want bluefish specifically, wire leader and anything shiny and fast-moving will get bit; if you're trying to avoid them to get to bass, slowing the retrieve down and staying tighter to bottom structure helps some, though not always.

Black sea bass remain the steady, unglamorous piece of the puzzle — structure around the Ledge and the deeper humps off Block continues to hold keeper-sized fish on squid and clam belly fished on standard high-low rigs, with no real change expected in that pattern through the near term.

Looking ahead, the next 48 to 72 hours should keep the current setup intact: bigger Harvest Moon water sharpening the SW Ledge tide windows, bonito and albies continuing their push both locally and westward into the Sound, and fluke holding steady off Block as long as the warm filament offshore doesn't get disrupted. The real thing to watch is the first legitimate cold front of the season — when it comes through, expect the fluke bite to tighten up fastest, the bass pattern to sharpen and possibly turn more aggressive as fish start shadowing bait tighter to structure, and the albie window to start compressing toward peak tide changes only. Until that front shows up on the charts, this remains one of the more productive stretches of the fall calendar for this stretch of water — worth planning around the slack at SW Ledge rather than fighting the full push.
