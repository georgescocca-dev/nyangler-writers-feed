# Cockenoe's Last Ebb: Porgies Stack Up While Bunker Blitzes Hit the Reefs
*With tog season still closed, I'm playing the last two hours of the ebb on Cockenoe and letting the bunker schools do the rest of the work.*

**NORWALK, CT — August 14** — _by Tony "Gold Coast" Bellafiore, Zone Writer_

Every week this time of year I have to make the same call: fish the reef for what's legal and biting, or waste a tide chasing what I wish was biting. Blackfish season's still closed here through the end of the month, so the tog gear stays in the garage. That decision made itself. The one that took more thought was which piece of structure to work off the last of the ebb — Cockenoe Reef, or run out to Penfield and fight the Fairfield boat traffic for a shot at sea bass. I picked Cockenoe, and I'd pick it again.

Here's the read. Kings Point is sitting around 71 degrees, New London's holding closer to 69 — that west-to-east gradient means the western end of the Sound is carrying more warmth and, more importantly, more bait than the water off New Haven. Everything from Greenwich Point through the Norwalk Islands to Sheffield has been thick with peanut bunker pushed tight to shore, and that's dragging schoolie bass and a good class of bluefish right up onto the structure with them. We flagged this last week — said building tidal flow off the August 12 new moon would sharpen the bite at current-driven structure, and that's exactly what showed up. The extra push on the ebb is stacking bait against the reef edges instead of letting it drift off into open water, and every predator in the western Sound knows it.

Cockenoe on the last two hours of the ebb into the first half hour of the new flood has been the most consistent window of the week. I'm anchoring on the outer bar in 15 to 20 feet, working sandworms and green crab on a standard high-low rig, and the porgies have been steady — not a blitz, but a bend-the-rod-every-drop kind of steady, with a mix of decent-sized scup and the occasional short-lived sea robin reminder that you're fishing structure, not sand. Sheffield Island's rockpiles on the north side are producing the same pattern in slightly shallower water, 12 to 15 feet, same rig, same tide window. If you're getting there after 9 a.m. on a Saturday, you're fighting for swing room with three other boats, so I've been sliding my start time up an hour and working the tide before the fleet shows.

The bluefish and schoolie bass story is happening in the guts between the Norwalk Islands and along the flats off Greenwich Point, and it's tide-dependent in a different way. As the ebb pulls bait out of the coves and marsh creeks, it funnels peanut bunker through the cuts, and that's where the blues have been cutting through in short, violent windows — sometimes ten minutes of surface chop and then it's over. I've had the best luck throwing 3/4-ounce chrome or bunker-pattern SP Minnows into the edges of those pushes, and bucktails in white or chartreuse, half-ounce, worked slow along bottom, have picked up schoolies in the same water when the surface action goes quiet. It's not the kind of bite you can plan a whole trip around — you need to be on the water and watching for the birds and the nervous water, then get there fast before it shuts down.

Penfield Reef and the Cows have been my play for sea bass, and that trend has been building gradually rather than exploding, which is honestly the more trustworthy kind of improvement. I'm working 25 to 35 feet of water on the ebb, squid strips and Gulp on bucktail jigs bounced tight to the rock piles, and the ratio of keepers to shorts has been ticking up week over week. It's not a fast bite — you're putting in time for a handful of quality fish — but it's real, and it should keep firming through the rest of August as that structure holds bait later into the season.

Fluke have been the quiet part of the week out here. A few keepers coming off the sandy stretches between Cockenoe and the Norwalk Islands on white Gulp swimming mullets and bucktail combos, but nothing like what the guys are seeing out toward Nantucket Sound right now. If fluke is your priority this week, I wouldn't build a trip around western Sound structure — treat it as a bonus while you're porgy fishing, not the target.

Looking ahead, I don't expect much to change through the weekend unless the wind shifts hard out of the southwest and muddies up the shallow flats. The tide's still building off the new moon, which should keep sharpening the bite at current-heavy spots like Cockenoe's outer bar and the Penfield rockpiles. My plan is to keep working the early ebb before the weekend fleet arrives, and staying flexible enough to run to wherever the bunker schools push next — because on this stretch of the Gold Coast, the bait tells you where to fish, and this week it's been telling me to stay west.
