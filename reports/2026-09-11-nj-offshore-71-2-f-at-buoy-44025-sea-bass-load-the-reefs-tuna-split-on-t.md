# 71.2°F at Buoy 44025: Sea Bass Load the Reefs, Tuna Split on the Ridge
*The mid-range grounds are holding two different fisheries right now — a stacked bottom bite inside and a patchy troll bite out on the lumps, and the book says both should hold through the weekend.*

**MANASQUAN, NJ — September 11** — _by Capt. Rey Santos, Zone Writer_

Buoy 44025 has been reading 71.2°F with 3.9-foot seas on a 5-second period, and that number matters more than it looks. That's warm enough to keep bait pushed up and holding tight to structure, but it's a short, choppy period — wind-driven swell, not a clean groundswell — which means the ocean's been lumpy enough to keep some of the smaller boats tied up this week. That's the story in one number: good water, uncomfortable ride.

Inshore of the 20-fathom curve, the black sea bass bite on the reef and wreck network has been the most consistent thing going. Axel Carlson Reef, Sea Girt Reef, Shrewsbury Rocks, and the Triple Wrecks are all holding fish in the 60 to 90 foot range, and the September pattern is textbook — sea bass pushed off the shallower structure by summer pressure are re-grouping on the deeper pieces as the water starts its slow slide down from summer highs. Green crab and squid combos on a double-hook bottom rig, fished with just enough sinker to hold bottom on the drift, has been outproducing bucktail-and-Gulp combos this week, though a 6-ounce diamond jig tipped with squid strips has taken its share of jumbo bass when the boat's sitting still over a piece. Keepers are running mixed, plenty of 14 to 16 inch fish in the mix, but the Triple Wrecks and the deeper end of Shrewsbury Rocks have been kicking out the occasional 3 and 4 pound class fish for anyone willing to sort through numbers to find them. Blackfish are starting to show as a bycatch on these same pieces — nothing worth targeting yet, but it's the first sign that the tog bite is building underneath the sea bass action, and that's worth remembering once the water drops another five degrees.

Further out, the tuna picture is more complicated, and it's worth being straight about it. Barnegat Ridge has been giving up bluefin, but the word from the docks is that they're running slow and scattered rather than in tight, workable pods — the kind of bite where you might mark fish on the meter and never get a rod bent, or find one small pod holding on a lump for twenty minutes and then nothing. Klondike has been the better bet for yellowfin and mahi. The mahi have been stacking around anything floating — pot buoys, weed lines, the odd piece of debris — in that zone, and boats working green and gold spreader bars or naked ballyhoo behind a bird have connected on fish in the 3 to 8 pound range, with the occasional bull mixed in around structure. Yellowfin have been coming on the troll in the same general area, mostly on the black/purple and green machine combos, with the bite windows tightening up around first light and again in the last two hours before dark.

The reason both of those bites are still alive is the same reason we flagged last week — the warm water sitting off this stretch has held its position for better than two weeks now, and that stability is exactly what's kept the yellowfin and mahi numbers respectable even while the bluefin stay scattered. We said the bite would hold as long as that warm pocket stayed put, and here we are — it hasn't broken down, and the fish that key off warm-water edges are still finding structure to hold on. If that filament starts to slide or fracture, that's the first sign the Klondike bite backs off, so anyone planning a run out there should be checking recent sea surface reports the morning of, not the night before.

Tide and moon are worth a mention too. We're still riding out the tail end of a strong new-moon push, which means better current on the reef pieces for the sea bass drift — stronger current means better scent dispersion off the crab and squid, and that's part of why the bite inshore has stayed strong through a week of lumpy seas. As that tidal energy eases off over the next several days, expect the bottom bite to soften slightly in raw numbers but improve in quality, as sea bass settle back into a more predictable feeding rhythm rather than getting pushed around by hard current.

Looking at the next 48 to 72 hours: if the sea stays manageable, Sea Girt Reef and the Triple Wrecks are the safer inshore play for anyone who wants a mixed bag of sea bass with a shot at an early tog. For the tuna run, Klondike is still the better numbers game over Barnegat Ridge as long as that warm water holds its ground — worth checking a same-day water temp chart before making the run, since a two- or three-degree drop on that edge would be the tell that the mahi and yellowfin are sliding with it. Bluefin chasers should treat the Ridge as a lottery ticket right now, not a sure thing — mark bait, work the meter, and don't be afraid to pick up and run if the first pass shows nothing. The structure's there. The fish are there. The only question this week is whether the warm water stays where it's been sitting.
