# Bluefish Break Their Silence on Middle Ground and 11B as Bunker Pile In
*After weeks of guys asking me where the blues went, the deep reef structure west of Stepping Stones is finally holding fish again — and the timing lines up with what I told you last week.*

**THROGS NECK, NY — August 9** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

I had three separate guys at the bait shop this week ask me the same question: "Frank, where the hell did the blues go?" One of them had fished four trips in July out of City Island without touching a bluefish, and he wanted to know if I thought they'd skipped the Sound entirely this year. I told him what I've told guys for thirty years — bluefish don't leave, they relocate to where the bait is thick enough to be worth the trip. And this week, that bait finally showed up on the deep reef structure, and the blues followed right behind it.

Middle Ground, 11B, and 28C — the deep humps and rockpiles that hold current year after year — are holding real bluefish again after a quiet July. I heard of a 16-pound fish weighed off one of those pieces, which is a genuinely good bluefish for the Western Sound, not some snapper-sized junk fish. What changed is simple: bunker and peanut bunker have finally concentrated tight to that structure instead of spreading out through open water, and predators go where the food is stacked, not where the water looks pretty. If you've been marking bait balls on your finder anywhere near those reefs, don't motor past them — drop a bunker spoon or a diamond jig with a tube teaser right through the school and hang on.

Striped bass are still the more consistent story, and they're still playing the same game I flagged last week — deep and after dark. Execution Rocks continues to be the most reliable piece of structure in this whole stretch, and the outgoing tide there is still stacking bass against the rockpile the way it has for decades. I heard about a 45-pound-class fish taken on a plastic eel worked slow along the bottom on the tail end of the ebb — that's a night bite, gentlemen, not a midday program right now. Kings Point is reading close to 71 degrees, and that's warm enough to push the biggest fish down and back into low light. Stepping Stones is holding up the same way — work the rip line off the light on the last two hours of the outgoing, drift a live eel or a bunker chunk down current, and be patient. These fish aren't schooled up and blitzing bait on the surface. They're sitting deep, feeding on the move of current, and you need to be in the right spot when the tide's doing the work for you.

We flagged this pattern last week — said the deep, current-driven bite would hold until either the water cools off or the new moon tides start stirring things up — and that's exactly what's setting up now. The new moon hits August 12, and the tidal range is already building. That means stronger current through Execution Rocks, sharper rips off Stepping Stones, and more water moving through the reef structure where those bluefish are staging. If the pattern holds the way it usually does around here, expect the bite at all that current-driven structure to sharpen up over the next several days, not slow down.

Fluke, I'll be honest with you, remain a mixed bag on this side of the Sound. Western ports are still mostly turning up shorts — plenty of action if you don't mind culling through a lot of 15-inch fish to find a keeper. Matinecock Point and the mouth of Manhasset Bay have both produced some decent drifts, but you're working for it. White Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, dragged slow along the bottom in 25 to 35 feet, are still the go-to combo. If you want a shot at a bigger fish, slow your drift down and add a strip of squid to the hook — sometimes that extra scent is the difference between a dink and a keeper this time of year.

Weakfish have been scattered but present — a few decent fish coming out of Hempstead Harbor on soft plastics fished slow near bottom structure, nothing to build a trip around but worth a cast or two if you're already anchored up for bass or porgies. Porgies themselves have been steady off the deeper rockpiles around Hart Island, small hooks and clam baits on the bottom doing the job like they always do this time of year — good action if you want to fill a cooler without much drama.

Looking ahead, I'd put my money on the bluefish pattern continuing to build through the weekend as that new moon current strengthens and pushes more bait tight to the reef structure. Execution Rocks and Stepping Stones should keep producing after dark on the outgoing for bass, especially if that warm surface layer holds and keeps pushing the bigger fish deep. If you're chasing fluke, don't expect miracles — cover water, cull hard, and take the keepers when they come. The Sound's giving up fish right now, just not in the same spot for every species, so plan your trip around what the tide and the calendar are telling you, not what worked last month.
