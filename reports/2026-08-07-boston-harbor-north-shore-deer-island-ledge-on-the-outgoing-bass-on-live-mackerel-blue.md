# Deer Island Ledge on the Outgoing: Bass on Live Mackerel, Blues Torching the Winthrop Flats
*With Gulf of Maine water keeping this harbor cooler than everything south of the Cape, I'm staying inside the islands this week instead of running north to Gloucester.*

**BOSTON HARBOR, MA — August 7** — _by Sara Whitfield, Zone Writer_

I made the call Monday morning standing on the dock at Winthrop, coffee in hand, watching a bunker pod nervous up off Belle Isle Inlet: stay inside the islands this week. Not Gloucester, not the Dogbar Breakwater, not a run up to Halibut Point where the rock structure is gorgeous but the fish have been thin. The harbor's holding bait, the water's still comfortably cool thanks to that Gulf of Maine influence we get up here that Long Island Sound and the Cape just don't have this time of year, and that's the whole ballgame in early August.

Here's the read: while everything south of the Cape has pushed stripers into deep structure and nighttime-only feeding as surface temps sit in the mid-70s, our water stays a few degrees kinder. That means daylight bites. That means you can fish the Deer Island ledge at ten in the morning and still find fish willing to eat. I've had two solid mornings this week drifting live mackerel — snagged fresh off the Deer Island Light rocks on a sabiki, then downsized to a single hook and a small egg sinker — over that 30 to 40-foot drop on the outgoing tide. Bass to 34 inches, a handful of shorts, and one fish that made my rod tip disappear before I even had slack line out. Best window has been the first two hours of the ebb, when the current stacks bait against that ledge and the bass sit right on the edge of it.

Bluefish have been the loudest story in the harbor, and it's exactly the pattern we flagged coming — bunker schools thickening up and the blues riding shotgun on them. Winthrop's outer flats, Deer Island's north side, and the water off Nixes Mate have all had stretches of genuine topwater chaos this week, bunker getting bulldozed on the surface with birds diving into the mess. Poppers and 2-ounce Hopkins spoons thrown right into the boil have been getting bit on nearly every cast when it's happening — the trick is getting there before the school pushes off, because these blitzes move fast and don't sit still. I lost a popper to a blue that came clean out of the water off Long Island Head Tuesday evening, first cast, boat still drifting.

Night bite for bass has been the more reliable program if you want a sure thing over a blitz you have to chase. Tides have softened off the recent push, and just like we expected, that's compressed the bass into the strongest current hours and the deepest, coolest structure they can find — eels fished slow along the bottom at the Gut between the Brewster Islands and around the Fore River Bridge pilings have been steady after dark, especially on the last two hours of the incoming when the current is still moving enough to keep the eel swimming naturally. Nothing giant, mostly 26 to 32 inches, but consistent, and that's what you want on a summer night trip.

Mackerel themselves have been thick enough off Gloucester's outer breakwater and around Cape Ann generally that bait has not been an issue for anybody — jig up a dozen in fifteen minutes most mornings and you're set for the day, whether you're feeding stripers or just want a mess for the pan. Pollock have been mixed in with them on the same structure, which is a nice bonus if you're jigging metal.

I'll be straight about the rest of the beat: flounder fishing is basically a non-event right now. That's a spring and fall program in this harbor, and August heat has them scattered and uninterested — I wouldn't plan a trip around them until the water starts cooling in September. Cod and haddock are still out there on the deeper humps toward Stellwagen, but between the summer regulations and warm surface water pushing what's biting deeper still, it's a long run for a modest return this time of year. If you want bottom fish, this isn't the month.

Looking ahead, I'm watching two things. First, whether that bunker pod off Winthrop holds through the weekend — if it does, the blue bite should keep producing topwater chaos on calm mornings, and I'd have a popper rod rigged and ready before I even leave the dock. Second, whether the tides keep softening into next week, because if they do, I expect the daytime bass bite off Deer Island to taper and the program to lean even harder into the night eel game at the Gut and around the bridges. That's the move I'd make if I only had one night to fish — sundown at the Fore River Bridge, eel on a fish-finder rig, patience through the first hour of dark before the current really gets moving. It won't be fast fishing, but it's been the most dependable thing on my water all week.
