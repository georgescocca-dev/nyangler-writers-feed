# Point bass crash the boulder fields as 67-degree water stacks bait on the flood
*Diamond jigs and live bunker producing keeper stripers to 26 pounds in the wash.*

**MONTAUK POINT, NY — June 19** — _by Rich Martin, Zone Writer_

The Point is alive. Water temps hit 67 degrees this week and the bass have responded like someone flipped a switch. The boulder fields from the Lighthouse east to North Bar are holding fish — real fish, not the schoolies we've been picking at since May. I'm talking bass to 26 pounds coming over the rail on diamond jigs, with most of the action happening on the flood tide when that 67-degree water pushes bait tight against the rocks.

The technique is classic Montauk: 4 to 6-ounce diamond jigs worked vertically in the wash. White and blue seem to be the colors, though I've seen plenty of fish taken on plain chrome. The key is staying tight to structure — these fish are relating to specific boulders, not just cruising open water. Work the jig with sharp lifts, let it flutter back down, and be ready. The hits come hard and fast in this current.

Live bunker is also producing, especially for the bigger fish. Rig them on a fishfinder with just enough weight to stay in the zone — 2 to 3 ounces depending on the current. The peanut bunker are thick right now, concentrated by that temperature gradient between the 67-degree inshore water and the cooler 62-degree water showing on the Block Island buoy. That 5-degree difference is creating a feeding lane that's stacking bait and drawing bass from miles around.

Fluke fishing has been solid on the south side drifts. The party boats are finding keeper fish in 30 to 50 feet, with white Gulp and chartreuse bucktails doing most of the damage. The bite has been best on the start of the outgoing tide, when the current begins to move bait off the structure. I'm hearing reports of fish to 5 pounds, with most keepers running 18 to 22 inches. The shorts are numerous, but that's typical for this time of year.

Sea bass are also in the mix, with keeper fish showing on the deeper structure. Diamond jigs are taking them along with the stripers, and the size has been impressive — fish to 3 pounds are not uncommon. The limit is still 7 fish at 15 inches, so check your regs before you head out.

The water conditions tell the story of why the fishing has turned on. That 67-degree reading represents a solid 4-degree jump from where we were two weeks ago, and it's pushed us over the threshold where baitfish become active and predators start feeding aggressively. The southwest wind pattern we've had this week has also helped, keeping the water clean and the bait concentrated along the south-facing structure.

Looking ahead, the full moon on Friday means big tides and strong currents. That should only improve the action, especially for the bigger bass that like to feed in moving water. The forecast shows continued southwest winds, which will keep conditions fishable and maintain that temperature gradient that's been so productive.

The Point is fishing like the Point should fish in mid-June. Get out there while the conditions hold.
