For route and tactics, this is a troll-first week, not a chunk-first week, unless you can confirm bait is stacked tight on the edge before you commit to anchoring up. The warm eddy is diffuse enough right now that covering water with a spread beats sitting on one piece of structure hoping the current brings fish to you. A standard mixed spread — naked ballyhoo on the long riggers, Green Machines and cedar plugs in the shotgun and short corner, a spreader bar down the center — is picking up both yellowfin and the occasional bluefin working the color changes and temp breaks along the canyon edge. Where the troll connects with a weed line or a patch of sargassum, slow down and work it; that's where the mahi have been mixing in, and a naked ballyhoo or a small skirted jet head dropped back into the shade line will usually get a reaction if fish are holding under the grass.

If you want to chunk, don't commit until you've marked bait and confirmed a temp break on the sounder — this isn't the time of year where blind chunking pays the fuel bill. Butterfish chunks fished on circle hooks, with a lighter fluorocarbon leader than you'd run in August, have been the better bet for anglers targeting bigger bluefin holding deeper under the warm layer. Wahoo have been a secondary target rather than a primary one — the occasional fish is coming on high-speed trolled Ilanders and cedar plugs run well back of the pattern, but nobody's targeting them exclusively right now with the tuna bite still the more reliable draw.

Be honest with yourself about the ROI here. This is not a slam-dunk week. The bite is real but it's mixed — some boats are coming back with a well-rounded box of yellowfin, bluefin, and mahi, others are grinding through long stretches with one or two fish to show for a full day on the troll. That's consistent with a bite that's holding on fading structure rather than a fresh, aggressive push. If you've got the fuel budget and the weather window, it's still worth the run. If you're tight on either, this is a week where the inshore striper bite might give you a better return for less diesel burned.

Sea state matters for the ride out more than it has the last few weeks. Nearshore buoy readings are showing building wave heights in the two-and-a-half to three-foot range with a short period, which won't stop anybody from running offshore but will make for a sloppier transit than the glass-calm mornings from earlier in the month. Check the forecast for your specific window before committing to an overnight.
