# Execution Rocks fires as Western Sound bass hit 50 pounds on outgoing tides
*Trophy stripers stack on the ledges while bunker schools trigger feeding sprees from Throgs Neck to Matinecock.*

**EXECUTION ROCKS, NY — June 24** — _by Frank "The Sound Pro" Marinaro, Zone Writer_

The Western Sound is absolutely on fire right now, and I'm telling you this after 30 years of reading these waters — we're in the middle of something special. The bass bite has exploded over the past week, with fish pushing 50 pounds coming off the ledges at Execution Rocks and the Stepping Stones. Water temps have climbed to that magic 68-degree mark, and the bunker schools are finally where they should be for late June.

The standout story is happening at Execution Rocks on the outgoing tide. I've been watching boats work the ledge structure there, and when that water starts moving, it's lights out. The key is positioning yourself over the drop-off in 35 to 45 feet and working large Mojos or bunker spoons through the water column. The morning bite has been slow, but once that afternoon outgoing gets cranking, the bass turn on like someone flipped a switch. We're talking 40-inch-plus fish, with several pushing that magical 50-pound mark.

The technique that's been money is trolling large Mojos — 3 to 4 ounces — over the structure on the outgoing. Keep your speed around 2.5 knots and watch your fishfinder. When you mark fish staged on the ledge, that's when you want to be there. The bass are using that moving water to ambush bait getting swept off the rocks.

Bunker schools have finally moved into the sound proper, and that's changed everything. I'm seeing good concentrations from Matinecock Point all the way west to the Throgs Neck Bridge. The fish are following these schools, and when you find the bait, you find the bass. Live-lining peanut bunker on a fishfinder rig has been deadly, especially around the bridge pilings where the current breaks the schools apart.

The Stepping Stones area has been producing consistent action, particularly on the flood tide when bait gets pushed through the gap. Work the edges where the current creates that seam — that's where the bass set up to feed. Bucktails in the 1 to 2-ounce range tipped with Gulp Swimming Mullets have been taking fish in the 28 to 35-inch range consistently.

Fluke fishing has picked up significantly as bay temperatures climb past the 66-degree mark. The gradient between the cooler sound water and the warming bays is stacking bait on the flood and holding bass on the ebb. I've been finding good fluke action in 25 to 30 feet off the Matinecock area, particularly on white Gulp and chartreuse bucktails dragged slow on the drift.

Porgies have moved into their summer haunts around the rocky structure. The docks and pilings from Little Neck Bay to Hempstead Harbor are holding good numbers of keeper-sized fish. Standard porgy rigs baited with clam are producing, but I've been doing better with small pieces of squid on size 4 hooks.

The water clarity has been excellent, which means the fish can see your presentation clearly — but it also means they can see you. Light leaders are critical right now. I'm running 20-pound fluorocarbon for the bass and dropping down to 15-pound for the fluke.

Looking ahead, we're coming into the new moon on Friday, which means big spring tides and strong currents. That's going to flush even more bait out of the bays and concentrate the feeding. The bass bite should only get better as we move into the weekend. I'm watching for the inlet drains at sunset — that's when the first real push of weakfish typically shows up, and the conditions are setting up perfectly for it.

The Western Sound is fishing as well as I've seen it in years. The combination of proper water temperatures, abundant bait, and strong tidal movement is creating ideal conditions. Get out there while it lasts.
