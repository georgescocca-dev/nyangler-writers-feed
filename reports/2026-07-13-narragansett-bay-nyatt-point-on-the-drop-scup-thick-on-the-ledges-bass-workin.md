# Nyatt Point on the drop: scup thick on the ledges, bass working after dark
*The herring run's long gone, but the bay's settled into its mid-summer rhythm — bass gone nocturnal, fluke sliding onto the flats, scup piled on every rock pile from Rocky Point to Prudence.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — July 13** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

I was down at the India Point pier Tuesday morning watching a kid with a Zebco reel haul scup over the rail two at a time, and his old man turns to me and says, "Where'd all the bass go?" That's the question I keep hearing up and down the river this week. Buckeye Brook's herring run wound down back in June, and once that bait ran dry, the bass that stacked up on it for weeks scattered. They didn't leave the bay — they just changed their address and their clock.

That's the story of mid-July here. The easy money bite off the herring is done. What's left is a bay that's warmed up top to bottom, tides running big off this new moon, and fish that have gone smart and nocturnal. If you want stripers now, you're fishing bridges after dark or you're chasing bunker schools that are holding off Prudence Island and down around the mouth of Greenwich Bay. I had a good hour Thursday night under the Mount Hope Bridge on the last of the outgoing, drifting a live eel down current past the third and fourth piling. Two fish in the 28-30 inch class, both right on the seam where the light meets the shadow line. That's been the pattern all week — small light, tight current, and patience. Bunker chunks fished on a fish-finder rig off the rocks at Rocky Point have also been picking off keeper bass on the last two hours of the ebb, right around midnight to 2 a.m.

Fluke are the better daytime bet right now, and they're pushing hard into the bay off the warm flow coming out of the upper river. Greenwich Bay has been holding fish in 10 to 18 feet on the drop of the tide — I'm dragging a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a half-ounce bucktail, tipped with a strip of squid, nice and slow along the bottom off the Warwick side. Conimicut Point is doing the same thing on the outgoing — that current sweeping around the point concentrates bait, and the fluke sit right on the edge of it in 12 to 20 feet. Nothing giant this week, mostly 16 to 19 inches with a few keepers mixed in, but steady enough that a couple hours on the drift will put fish in the cooler if you keep moving and don't marry one spot.

Scup are the sure thing right now, and that's not a knock — that's just where we are in July. Nyatt Point and the rock piles down toward Rocky Point are loaded. High-low rigs with size 6 or 8 hooks, sandworm or squid strips, dropped into 15 to 25 feet right on the rocks. The bigger tides we're getting off this new moon are moving more water through those ledges, and the scup respond to it — best bite has been the first two hours of the ebb when that current really starts to rip past the structure. If you want a mess of fish for the pan without overthinking it, that's your spot this week.

Bluefish have shown up in patches, mostly chopping bait on top out toward the mouth of the bay and around Prudence Island in the early morning. I haven't seen the kind of blitz that stops traffic, but there's enough bluefish mixed in with those bunker schools that if you're throwing poppers or SP minnows at first light off Prudence, don't be shocked if something with teeth ends your leader. Black sea bass are still holding tight to the rock structure around Jamestown and the deeper edges off Prudence — small jigs tipped with squid, fished slow right on bottom, still producing a few keepers, though the season's ticking and the bigger ones have thinned out some.

Weakfish — I'll be honest, I haven't heard a real report on those this week. A few years back you could count on some around Greenwich Bay this time of year, but that fishery's a shadow of what it used to be around here, so I'm not sending anybody chasing ghosts.

Looking ahead, these new moon tides should keep building through the weekend, which is good news for the ledge bite — more water movement means more bait getting pushed around, which means scup and fluke both should stay active. If the wind stays out of the southwest and lays down at night like it has been, I'd put my money on the bridges again after dark for bass — that's been the more consistent program than daytime blind casting. Worth watching the bunker schools off Prudence too; if they hold position through the week, that's where the next real bass and blue action is going to break loose.
