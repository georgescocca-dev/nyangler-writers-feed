# Nyatt Point after dark: bass sliding deep, snappers thick in Greenwich Bay
*Warm surface water has pushed the bay's striper bite into the shadows, but the bluefish and scup more than make up for it.*

**PROVIDENCE, RI — August 7** — _by Vinny Ciervo, Zone Writer_

I was down at Sabin Point around six the other morning, coffee in one hand, watching a guy from Warwick load a cooler with snapper blues he'd picked off the pilings in twenty minutes with a bucktail jig and a bobber. That's the tell right now — the small stuff is everywhere close to shore, and the big stuff has gone quiet during the day. Anybody who's fished this bay more than a summer knows what that means. The surface water's sitting in the mid-70s, the bass have wised up, and if you want the good ones you're fishing the edges of the light — first hour, last hour, or full dark.

Conimicut Point on the last of the ebb is still your best shot at a keeper striper in daylight, but I'm not going to lie to you — it's a grind right now. The fish that are there are holding tight to the channel drop where the current keeps things cooler, and they want it slow. Eels on a fish-finder rig, or a swimming plug worked dead slow along the rip line where the flat water meets the moving water. I had two fish there Tuesday morning on eels, both mid-20s, nothing to write home about but better than nothing on a summer morning in the upper bay.

Where the real bass are is Nyatt Point and the rocks off Rocky Point after the sun goes down. That stretch holds bait all summer — menhaden schools have been thick off the point for two weeks now, rolling up against the rocks on the incoming, and the bass are underneath them working the shadow line. Live-lined bunker on a 5/0 circle hook, no weight, just let it swim naturally into the current seam. Guys are also doing well soaking chunks — cut bunker on a fish-finder rig, 20-30 feet off the point, fished right on the bottom during the last two hours of the incoming tide. That's when the current pushes the bait tight against structure and the stripers stack up underneath it. It's a night bite now, plain and simple, and if you're not out there after 9pm you're missing the best of it.

The bluefish, though — that's where the bay's actually fun right now. Greenwich Bay is stuffed with peanut bunker, and both the snapper blues and the cocktail-size adults are all over them. We flagged this a few days back — said the peanut bunker spreading through the back coves would pull the cocktail blues in tight within a couple weeks, and it didn't even take that long. Anchor up off the Greenwich Bay Yacht Club moorings or drift the flats near Warwick Cove with a small Kastmaster or a Deadly Dick, and you'll get bit almost every cast on the smaller blues. Mix in a few bigger cocktail fish, two to three pounds, chasing the same bait — those are the ones worth keeping for the smoker. Light braid, small wire leader, and don't overthink it. The blues are doing the work for you.

Fluke have backed off the shallow flats with the warm water and pushed onto the deeper structure where the bottom stays cooler — that's classic August behavior in this bay. The channel edge off Prudence Island, particularly the drop near the north end toward Potter Cove, has been holding fish in 25 to 35 feet. Same story on Ohio Ledge — work a white Gulp Swimming Mullet on a 3/4-ounce bucktail, bounced slow along bottom on the drift, and you'll pick through some shorts to find keepers in the 18 to 20-inch range. The bite's better on the last two hours of the outgoing when the current thins out and you can actually feel the bottom.

Scup are the most honest fish in the bay right now — they're exactly where they're supposed to be. Rocky Point ledges and the rock piles off Nyatt Point are stacked with them in 15 to 25 feet. Squid strips or sandworms on a simple high-low rig, small hooks, and you can fill a bucket in an hour if you find the right rock. It's not glamorous but it's about as sure a thing as this bay offers in August, and it's a good way to keep a kid interested while you wait for the tide to turn for bass.

Weakfish are still a rumor more than a pattern — a few scattered fish have shown up mixed in with the bluefish around the peanut bunker schools in the upper bay, which tracks with what we expected as that bait pushed in. Nothing to plan a trip around yet, but worth a cast or two if you're already working a bunker school with light tackle.

Looking ahead, this stable stretch of weather should hold through the week — light wind, steady tides, nothing to shake the pattern loose. That means more of the same: night bass on bunker at Nyatt and Rocky Point, steady blues in Greenwich Bay, and fluke holding on the deep edges around Prudence. If a front does roll through and stirs the upper bay, I'd expect the bass bite to actually improve for a day or two — a little turbulence gets them moving during daylight again. Until then, set your alarm for the dark hours if you want a real striper, and let the kids have fun with the blues in the meantime.
