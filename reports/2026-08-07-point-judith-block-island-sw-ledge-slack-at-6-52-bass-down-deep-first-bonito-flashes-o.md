# SW Ledge slack at 6:52: bass down deep, first bonito flashes off Block's breachways
*Bunker still rules the rip after dark, but the real story this week is warm water pushing the first tuna-family fish into range off the island.*

**GALILEE, RI — August 7** — _by Ava Thomas, Zone Writer_

Six fifty-two this morning was the slack at SW Ledge, and I had the boat sitting right on the west edge of the drop in 55 feet, watching the current lay down before it turned to build east. That window — the fifteen minutes either side of slack — is when the bigger bass show themselves on the ledge in August. Once the current gets moving hard again, those fish drop off the structure and slide into the deeper cuts where the water stays cooler and the bait gets funneled tight. We marked stripers stacked from 60 to 75 feet on the second half of the outgoing, and they wanted eels — not bucktails, not tins, live eels dropped down on a three-way rig with just enough weight to hold bottom in the current.

That's the pattern right now south of the Judith and out toward the island: stripers have gone nocturnal and deep. Surface water is sitting in the mid-70s — Montauk read 74.5 this week, and the western Sound is pushing into the high 70s — which is warm enough that the better fish don't want to feed up top in daylight anymore. The night bite has been the more honest bite. We've had clients doing well drifting bunker chunks over SW Ledge and the humps off Sandy Point starting an hour before dark, running into the top of the flood. Fish to 35 pounds have come off that pattern in the last ten days, but it's not a blitz — you're working for two or three quality bites a trip, not a dozen. I'd call it a solid, honest bass bite right now, not a red-hot one.

Fluke have been the more reliable daytime fish, and that's typical for this stretch of summer. We're finding keepers on the drift south of Block in 35 to 50 feet, working white Gulp Swimming Mullets on ¾-ounce bucktails, tipped with a strip of squid to give it some scent in current that's been running stronger than normal the last few days. The better drifts have been on the last two hours of the ebb, when the current slows enough to keep bottom contact without a two-ounce sinker dragging you sideways. Sizes have been decent — 18 to 22 inches with a few better fish mixed in — but you've got to move around to find them. We've burned through three or four drops before locking onto a stretch holding fish.

Sea bass are exactly where they should be this time of year — parked on structure and not going anywhere. The reef edges off Block and the rock piles inside SW Ledge have been giving up steady numbers on green and white Gulp jigs and standard sea bass rigs baited with squid, worked right on bottom in 40 to 60 feet. It's the most dependable box-filler in the zone right now. If a client wants a guaranteed dinner, that's where we go first.

Bluefish are running true to form, chasing bunker schools that have been thick off the Judith and around the mouth of the Harbor of Refuge. Adult blues in the 6 to 10 pound range have been busting bait on the surface some mornings, and when that happens it's a top-water program — poppers or metal-lipped swimmers thrown right into the froth. Inside the breachways, snapper blues are stacked up thick enough that it's been the easiest way to keep a kid on a rod bent all afternoon — small Kastmasters or Gotcha plugs under a bobber, nothing fancy required.

Now here's the part I've been watching closest. We flagged this last week — said the early albie-family activity would start showing off the island within days — and it's here, though it's early and it's thin. We've had two separate reports of small bonito flashing bait on the surface off Sandy Point and out past North Light, chasing what looked like silversides pushed tight against the beach. Nobody's boated one yet that I can confirm firsthand, but the water's right for it — squid have been building around the island and Nantucket Sound, and that's exactly the forage signature that pulls bonito in first, ahead of the false albacore. The one thing working against us right now is a leftover ocean swell — 3 to 4 feet at a short period off the outer buoys — which has kept the south side of Block a little sloppy and made it tougher to spot those surface flashes cleanly. If that swell lays down over the next few days like the forecast suggests, I expect confirmed bonito catches off the breachways and Clay Head within the week, with Block and the outer rips following close behind.

Looking ahead: if this warm, stable pattern holds, I'd keep the night program on SW Ledge and Sandy Point for bass, lean on fluke and sea bass for the guaranteed box, and start carrying a bonito rod rigged with small epoxy jigs or Deadly Dicks every trip out to the island — because when that bite tips over, it tips fast, and you don't want to be the guy who left the light stuff at the dock.
