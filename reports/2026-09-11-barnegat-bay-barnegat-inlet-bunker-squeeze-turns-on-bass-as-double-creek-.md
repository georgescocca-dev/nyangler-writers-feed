# Barnegat Inlet Bunker Squeeze Turns On Bass as Double Creek Fluke Thin Out
*The new moon tide packed bunker against Island Beach and the inlet rocks, and the stripers finally leaned into it — while the bay's better fluke have already slipped toward deeper water.*

**FORKED RIVER, NJ — September 11** — _by Gus Papadakis, Zone Writer_

I've watched this happen every September since Nixon was in office, and it never gets old — a big bunker school gets pinned against the beach at Island Beach State Park or squeezed into the throat of Barnegat Inlet on a strong new-moon tide, and for about ten days the whole bay wakes up. The old-timers at the dock in Forked River used to call it "the squeeze," and that's exactly what's been setting up this week. The new moon on the 10th delivered the hardest tidal pull we've had since the spring runoff, and it — it tightened bunker schools from the back bays clear down through the inlet mouth, and it got the bass to finally commit instead of just picking at stragglers. We flagged this coming last week, saying the bait would tighten under the building moon and the first real blitz activity wasn't far off. That's exactly the pattern showing up now.

The better bass activity has been concentrated right where you'd expect — the rips off Barnegat Light, the deep edge of the inlet channel on the last two hours of the outgoing, and along the surf line at Island Beach where the bunker have been getting pinched against the sandbars. Guys working bunker snagged fresh off the schools and drifted back on a fish-finder rig have been doing the most damage, with slot fish and a scattering of overslot bass coming on the harder tides. If you don't want to snag your own, chunked bunker on a fishfinder rig with 5/0 to 7/0 circle hooks, fished on bottom in 15 to 25 feet along the edges of the inlet channel, has been drawing strikes on the last of the ebb and the first hour of the flood. Bucktails in white or chartreuse, 1 to 2 ounces depending on current, worked along the rock jetties at Barnegat Light on a slow bounce, have also connected — mostly with fish holding tight to structure rather than roaming open water.

Fluke are a different story, and it's not a great one right now. Double Creek Channel, which has carried the bay's best fluke action most of the summer, has thinned out considerably. The keeper-grade fish that were stacking on the channel edges through July and August have already started their push toward the inlet and beyond, staging for the offshore run as the water eases out of the mid-70s. What's left inside are mostly short fish — plenty of bites, not much to show for a cooler. If you're set on working the bay for fluke, the deeper bends of Double Creek near the mouth, and the channel edges closer to the inlet itself, are still worth a few drifts with white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails, but treat it as a numbers game for shorts rather than a meat run. The better odds for a keeper right now are closer to the inlet structure itself, where deeper water and stronger current are holding what's left of the mature fish before they push out.

Snapper blues are thick in the shallows all along the bay — Tices Shoal, the marsh creeks off Oyster Creek, the docks around Forked River — and that's the tell every year that adult bluefish aren't far behind. Small Ava jigs or gold Kastmasters on light spinning gear will keep a rod bent for anyone bringing kids down to the dock, and it's worth paying attention, because those snappers thicken up right before the bigger blues show in force behind the bunker schools. Expect that adult bluefish push to build over the next week as the bunker keep getting squeezed through the inlet.

Kingfish have been showing along the ocean side beaches at Island Beach on the high-low rig with squid strips or bloodworms, fished in the wash on the last of the incoming — a solid September pattern that should hold as long as the surf stays relatively calm. Weakfish have gone quiet, as they typically do this time of year; the sedge bank bite that was strong back in May has long since faded, and there's been nothing worth chasing there recently. Blowfish, likewise, are past their summer peak in the Oyster Creek shallows, though a few stragglers are still coming up on strip baits fished off the docks on quiet evenings.

Looking at the next several days, the tidal push from the new moon is still working its way through the system, and that should keep bait — and predators — tightening through the weekend. The bunker schools funneling through Barnegat Inlet are the story right now, and as long as they keep holding position against the beach and the inlet structure, expect striper and bluefish activity to build rather than fade over the coming tide cycles. If you've only got one window this week, fish the last two hours of the outgoing tide at the inlet, right where the channel edge drops off — that's where the bunker are getting pinned and where the bigger bass have been showing. Keep an eye on wind out of the northeast, which can churn the inlet mouth uncomfortably fast on a strong ebb; if that sets in, shift toward the calmer water inside Barnegat Light or the deeper stretches of Double Creek Channel as a backup. The fluke window inside the bay is closing fast, so anyone still hunting a flatfish dinner should make the trip sooner rather than later, before the last of the mature fish clear out for deeper water.
