# Stellwagen Edge holding at 110-130 feet: cod steady on the gravel, haddock working the mud in the Basin
*Bluefin are pinning mackerel schools along Tillies Bank as the pattern we called last week holds through the Labor Day run.*

**GLOUCESTER, MA — August 31** — _by Capt. Donna Shaw, Zone Writer_

One-ten to one-thirty feet of water along the Stellwagen Edge, hard sand-to-gravel transition, and the fleet is marking cod stacked tight to the bottom third of the column. That's the number that matters this week. We flagged this pattern last Friday — said stable offshore conditions would hold through the near-term window — and that's exactly what's playing out. No fronts pushed through, the wind's been light out of the southwest most days, and the water column hasn't turned over. That kind of stability is what keeps groundfish locked onto structure instead of scattering.

The cod bite is concentrated on the gravel patches along the southwest corner of the Bank and down the Edge itself, where the bottom breaks from soft sand into that rougher, cobbled stuff cod like to sit tight against. Jigging diamond jigs — the 12 to 16-ounce range depending on drift speed — tipped with a squid strip is still outproducing bait rigs for anyone willing to work the jig actively. Boats anchoring up and fishing clam or squid on a standard cod rig are doing fine too, but the jig guys are covering more bottom and finding the better fish, several in the 15 to 20-pound class mixed with a lot of school-size fish in the 5 to 8-pound range. Bottom line: fish the transition zones, not the flat sand. The gravel edges are where the bait funnels and the cod know it.

Haddock have settled into their late-summer pattern on the mud in Stellwagen Basin, running in that 170 to 200-foot range where the bottom softens up and holds the worm beds haddock like to root through. This has been the more consistent bite of the two groundfish species this week — haddock don't seem to care much about the moon phase or the light southwest breeze, they're just on the mud and feeding. Standard high-low rigs with clam bait or squid strips, fished right on bottom with just enough weight to hold in the light current, have been steady. Sizes have been solid, plenty of fish in the 3 to 5-pound range with some better ones mixed in. If you want a full box without burning half a tank running around, the Basin mud has been the more dependable stop over the gravel edges the last several days.

The bluefin story is the one everybody at the dock wants to talk about. Mackerel schools have been thick on top of the Bank and pushing up toward Tillies Bank, and where the mackerel balls tighten up, the giants have been showing right underneath them. This is textbook late-August, early-September behavior — the bait gets nervous, balls up tight near the surface, and the big fish come up under it working it from below. Boats running the troll spread with cedar plugs and small tuna feathers have had shots when they crossed through active bird activity, but this has been more of a sight-fishing and jig bite than a straight troll program. Vertical jigging with Shimano Butterfly-style jigs in the 200 to 300-gram range, dropped right through the bait ball, has connected on fish when the mackerel pushed deep enough to hold them down. It's not an every-boat, every-day bite — more spotty than the groundfish pattern, with the better windows coming early morning before the wind fills in and pushes the bait balls apart. Anyone heading out for bluefin should plan to run and gun a little, working the edges of bait concentrations rather than sitting on one mark and waiting.

Water's been holding warm for this time of year, and there's a broader pattern worth watching — warmer filaments of Gulf Stream water have been nosing farther north than usual this summer, and while that's mostly a story for the canyon approaches and points south of the Cape, it's part of why the mackerel and the tuna that follow them have been staying active later into the season up here. If that warm signature holds through September, expect the bluefin window on the Bank to stay open longer than a typical year. If a tropical system tracks up the coast in the next couple weeks, that's the wildcard that could shut it down fast and push everything back to a more typical early-fall pattern.

Looking at the next few days: conditions should stay manageable through the weekend, light southwest wind, no major swell in the forecast, and the moon working toward a phase that should tighten up the tidal push a bit without blowing out the drift. That's good news for the gravel bite on the Edge and the mud bite in the Basin — both should stay productive as long as the wind stays cooperative. For bluefin, keep an eye on bird activity and mackerel schools working the surface between the Bank and Tillies; that's where the next shot is most likely to develop. With Labor Day traffic building, expect more boats on the water and more competition for the better marks, so getting out early and getting your jigs down before the fleet arrives is worth the extra effort. If you've only got one day this week, split it — work the gravel or mud first thing while it's calm, then swing up toward the bait schools once the sun's higher and the birds start working.
