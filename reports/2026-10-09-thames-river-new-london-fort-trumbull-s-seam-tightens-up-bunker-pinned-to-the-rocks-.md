This bite tracks the bass closely and should keep doing so as long as the bunker schools stay concentrated.

The bottom bite is where the real shift is happening. Porgy and scup action is closing out fast as water temperatures slide, and word out of the local tackle counters is that effort is pivoting hard to blackfish. Green crabs and Asian crabs fished on structure around Fort Trumbull's riprap and the harbor's rockier edges are starting to produce tautog, and that bite should only build from here as the porgies thin out and anglers redirect their bottom-fishing time. It's early in that window — don't expect a full-blown tog bite yet — but the structure is holding fish and the pattern is starting.

Looking at the next few days, the setup favors sticking with the current seams rather than searching open water. If the cooling trend continues as expected, bait stays pinned tight to structure and the bass stay underneath it — that's the easiest read on this river right now.
