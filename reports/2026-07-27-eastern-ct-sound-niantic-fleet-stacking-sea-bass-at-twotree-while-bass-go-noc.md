# Niantic Fleet Stacking Sea Bass at Twotree While Bass Go Nocturnal on Black Point
*The party boats are grinding out limits of black sea bass on the reef structure, but if you want a striper you better be fishing after the sun goes down.*

**NIANTIC, CT — July 27** — _by Danny Colón, Zone Writer_

Ran into one of the Niantic charter guys at the bait shop Tuesday morning loading coolers with squid and green crabs, and he told me flat out — "the sea bass are the story right now, everything else you gotta work for." That's about as honest a read as you're gonna get from a guy who's on the water five days a week, and after checking around with the rest of the fleet, he's not wrong.

Black sea bass have been the most dependable thing going in this zone. The drift boats working out of Niantic have been putting anglers on solid catches at Hole-in-the-Wall and out along Twotree Island Channel, working the rock piles and rubble bottom in 40 to 60 feet. Squid strips and Gulp Sea Bass Bites on a double-hook bottom rig are getting it done, and there's enough porgies mixed in on the same drops — sandworms and clam bits on a high-low rig — that nobody's coming back with an empty cooler even on the days when the bigger sea bass aren't cooperating. This has been the one bite in this zone you can set a plan around, tide stage doesn't seem to matter as much as just finding the right piece of structure and staying anchored tight to it.

Fluke is a different conversation, and it's the mixed bag kind of week where you gotta be honest about it. The open flats inside Niantic Bay have gone quiet as that water sits in the high 60s to low 70s — New London's reading around 69.4°F, which isn't brutal, but it's enough to push the fluke out of the shallow sand and into the deeper channel bends where the water keeps moving. Twotree Channel and the mouth of the Niantic River, right where the current squeezes through, have been the more consistent producers — drifting 4-inch white Gulp Swimming Mullets on 3/4-ounce bucktails in 25 to 35 feet on the last of the outgoing has put keepers in the boat, but you're covering water to find them, not just parking and waiting. If you're used to easy summer flatty fishing inside the bay, adjust your expectations and follow the deeper structure.

Stripers are the toughest read right now, and it's not because they're not around — they are — it's because the daytime bite has essentially shut off. With surface temps sitting in that low-to-mid 70s range, the bass have gone nocturnal, holding deep on current seams and structure through the day and only committing after dark. Black Point and the rocks off McCook Point have been the spots where the guys willing to fish the night tide are connecting — bunker chunks fished on fishfinder rigs along the current edges, or eels swum slow through the rip at Hole-in-the-Wall on the incoming, starting an hour before dark and working through the change. We flagged this pattern last week and said it would hold as long as the warm, stable weather stuck around — it has, and the fish are behaving exactly the way that forecast said they would. If you're a daylight fisherman right now, striped bass is going to frustrate you. If you can fish dusk into the night tide, you've got a real shot.

Bluefish have been riding shotgun with whatever bait shows up in the bay. When bunker push into Niantic Bay on a moving tide, the blues show up right behind them, and there's been a couple of legitimate blitz windows this past week right off the river mouth and along the beach toward McCook Point — poppers and metal, anything shiny thrown into the melee, gets bit. It's not a sure thing day to day since it depends entirely on where the bait sets up, but when it happens it happens fast and it's worth having a bag of Deadly Dicks in the truck just in case.

Blackfish are off the table right now with Connecticut's summer closure in effect, so don't waste bunker crab bait on tog until the fall season opens back up — save that gear for October.

As for what's coming, the moon's building toward full in early August and that means current should be rebuilding through this whole stretch, the Race, Fishers Island Sound, right on down into Twotree Channel. Stronger flow is going to sharpen up every current-dependent bite in this zone — the sea bass fishing at Hole-in-the-Wall should stay steady or even improve as the water starts moving with more authority, and the fluke bite in the channel bends should tighten up too as the current does more of the work pulling bait past the structure. If I had one day to fish this week, I'd take an evening tide at Black Point for stripers and follow it with a morning drift at Twotree for sea bass and porgies — that's about as complete a day as this zone offers right now. Just don't expect the stripers to cooperate before the sun goes down.
